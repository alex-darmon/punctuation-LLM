MARCUS
Hark, Leon! A fair day, is it not?
And fairer still for those whose ventures thrive,
Whose ships return with holds of spice and silk.

LEON
Aye, Marcus, a fair day for those who do not
Mistake the sun for their own shining wit.
Thy ships, I hear, did brave a gentle tide,
Whilst others fought the tempest.

MARCUS
Dost thou imply my fortune is but chance?
Or that my wit, though sharp, lacks proper worth?
My coffers swell, good Leon, whilst thine... well,
Thy counting house doth echo somewhat thin, I trow.

LEON
My house doth echo with an honest gain,
Not built on whispers spread in shadowed lanes,
Nor gain that smells of deals too swiftly made.
Thou art too proud, Marcus, of thy sudden rise.

MARCUS
Proud? Nay, but truthful! My repute is sound,
My word is bond, my ledger clear as day.
'Tis envy, Leon, that doth sting thy tongue
And set thy brow in such a sullen frown.

LEON
Envy? Why, should I envy one whose speech
Doth trumpet forth his praise so loud and long?
A peacock's strut holds little charm for me,
Though 'tis a fine display.

MARCUS
Thou art a knave to mock my honest name!
My diligence hath built this enterprise,
While thou dost sit and murmur in the shade,
Like some old owl, content with ancient dust.

LEON
Better an owl than a puffed, cackling hen,
Whose boastful clamour doth disturb the peace.
I tell thee, Marcus, keep thy gains close by,
Lest fortune, like a fickle lover, flee.

MARCUS
Flee? My fortune is as solid as this stone!
And thou, my friend, art brittle as dried straw.
One day thy envy shall consume thy soul,
And leave thee nought but bitterness and bile.

LEON
And thou, perchance, shalt drown in thine own pride,
Thy swollen ego burst like summer's bubble.

MARCUS
Enough! I will not suffer such insult!
Thou darest call me proud, a knave, a hen?
Prepare thy hand, old dog, for I will teach thee

LEON

With lessons learned from many a sharper foe!

Then we would come in, and with Marcus's gold and our sweat, we would make it bloom. It happened time and time again. Farmers would leave, weeping, convinced their land was worthless, only to see it bear fruit for Marcus a year later. It made us wonder, the men on the ground. It made us wonder why they were told such dire tales, when we knew the truth of the soil.

MARCUS

(Steps forward, his voice firm.)

Brutus, your role was to execute. My agents' role was to assess and acquire. The two are distinct. The farmers were incapable of realizing the land's potential. My capital made it possible. This is not deception. It is simply the reality of modern agriculture. I offered them fair market value for what was, at the time, underperforming land, which I then, through massive investment, transformed into high-yield assets. Are you suggesting I should have paid them for the *potential* I alone saw and created?

BRUTUS

(Shakes his head slowly.)

I am simply saying, Marcus, that the land was not as dead as they were led to believe. And that many of those farmers, with a fraction of the investment you poured in, could have saved their livelihoods. You saw potential, yes. But you also made sure no one else could see it, or realize it, before you took possession.

VALERIUS

(Looks intently at Marcus.)

Marcus, you mentioned 'fair market value.' These witnesses suggest that the market value was artificially depressed by your agents' assessments, creating a false narrative of barrenness. How do you respond to this direct accusation?

MARCUS

(His composure begins to show a slight crack, a tightening around his jaw.)

Valerius, this is absurd. My agents provided objective assessments. The market dictates value. If land is not producing, its value is low. If I invest to make it produce, that is my risk, my endeavor. No one was forced. No one was misled. These are disgruntled individuals, one a sentimental farmer clinging to the past, another a clerk with a bruised conscience, and a foreman who perhaps now seeks a pension from some sympathetic patron. They offer no proof. No signed documents confirming a conspiracy. Only their subjective impressions and convenient memories.

VALERIUS

Their testimony, Marcus, speaks to a pattern. A pattern of behavior, a consistent narrative presented to many farmers. The court cannot ignore the cumulative weight of such accounts. Theron spoke of "hundreds like him." Lyra corroborated the systematic approach. Brutus, from the practical side, confirmed the underlying fertility of the "doomed" lands.

MARCUS

(Scoffs.)

A pattern of success, Valerius. A pattern of transforming unproductive land into a thriving enterprise. This is precisely what a modern economy needs. Farmers cannot expect to cling to ancient practices and simultaneously complain when progress leaves them behind. I created jobs, wealth, security. These individuals, consumed by nostalgia and resentment, seek to undermine legitimate enterprise.

VALERIUS

(Leans forward, his voice firm.)

The question before this court, Marcus, is not whether your enterprises are successful, but whether that success was built upon ethical foundations. Whether the means justified the ends. Whether the "progress" you champion was achieved through honest dealings or through systematic deception and dispossession, as these witnesses suggest.

(Valerius pauses, looking from Marcus to the three witnesses, then surveys the hushed courtroom.)

The testimonies heard today paint a troubling picture. A picture of vulnerable farmers, facing genuine hardships, being systematically convinced their lands were worthless, only for those same lands to flourish under new ownership. The defense asserts these were fair dealings, market forces at work, and the triumph of innovation. The plaintiffs and witnesses assert a calculated strategy of misrepresentation, designed to acquire valuable assets at negligible cost.

MARCUS

(Steps forward, attempting to reclaim the narrative.)

It is a distortion, Valerius. A malicious slander. I built my empire through hard work, ingenuity, and the courage to invest where others feared. These accusations threaten not just me, but the very spirit of enterprise.

VALERIUS

(Raises a hand, cutting Marcus off.)

That is enough for this session, Marcus. The court has heard enough for today to understand the nature of the allegations and the initial responses. The arguments are complex, touching upon the very foundations of land ownership, economic progress, and moral responsibility. This is not a simple dispute over a contract. This is a question of public trust and the integrity of our economic systems.

(Valerius looks around the court, his gaze thoughtful.)

The court will adjourn for today. I will reconvene tomorrow at the first hour. Both sides should be prepared to present further arguments, and I will then call for any further witnesses who can shed light on the specifics of these land acquisitions, particularly any direct evidence that might substantiate or refute the claims of deliberate misrepresentation. Marcus, you will be expected to produce any relevant internal documents or communications pertaining to the valuation and acquisition processes during the period in question, particularly those which Lyra referenced. Failure to do so will be viewed most unfavorably.

MARCUS

(His face is now grim, the earlier smirk entirely gone.)

Valerius, those documents are proprietary.

VALERIUS

(His voice cuts through Marcus's protest.)

Proprietary or not, Marcus, if they bear upon the truth of these allegations, this court demands their review. Justice demands transparency. The court stands adjourned.

(Valerius strikes his gavel, a sharp, decisive sound that echoes through the now murmuring chamber. Theron, Lyra, and Brutus exchange glances, a fragile hope dawning on their faces, while Marcus stands motionless, a storm brewing in his eyes as the crowd begins to disperse.)
