VALERIUS.
By my faith, good Leo, must this bickering ever plague our peaceful mornings? What fresh wound hath Cassian inflicted now?

LEO.
Wound, good uncle? Nay, 'tis a gaping chasm he digs betwixt our houses! Did he not yesterday pass our gates, and seeing old Master Tobias, your own faithful steward, nod his greeting, did he not turn his head as if he saw a dog upon the street? A dog, uncle!

VALERIUS.
Perchance he saw him not. The sun was in his eyes.

LEO.
The sun was in his eyes? And yet he spied young Beatrice with her basket of plums, and offered her a sweet compliment, though 'twas but an hour before he looked upon her with a sour face. No, uncle, 'twas a deliberate slight, an affront to our name, and I will not let it lie.

SERAPHINA.
Brother, thy temper doth outrun thy reason. To deem a simple averted gaze a cause for swords? The whole town speaks of thy quickness to quarrel. Didst thou not yesterday churl thy cap at the cobbler for a loose stitch in thy shoe, and then, finding no fault, didst blame the very daylight for showing thee a flaw that was not there?

LEO.
Peace, sister, peace. Thy tongue is sharper than my wit, and I am not afeared to use either. Cassian thinks us cowards, fit only for turning the other cheek. He smiles at maidens and scorns honest men.

SERAPHINA.
And thou wouldst give him cause to scorn thee truly, by making mountains of each molehill.

LEO.
Mountains, Seraphina? This molehill hath a viper coiled within it, I warrant thee. He approaches!

[Enter Cassian and Marco.]

CASSIAN.
Good-den, Leo. I did not expect to find thee lurking so near the public square. Have thy quarrels dwindled so low that thou must seek them in broad daylight?

LEO.
My quarrels, sir, are but a reflection of the ill humour I find abroad. And wherefore should I not be here? Is this not as much my street as thine?

CASSIAN.
It is a street for all men, truly. And for all beasts, I grant thee.

LEO.
Beasts, sayst thou? Dost thou cast thine own shadow upon my path, then, and call it beastly?

MARCO.
Nay, Cassian meant no such offence, Leo. His words are oft like runaway horses, they bolt ere he can rein them in.

LEO.
Bolted, sayst thou? I have heard his words bolt many a time, Marco, and each time they aim their hooves at us. Consort with him if thou wilt, but do not defend his viper's tongue.

CASSIAN.
Viper's tongue? Methinks my tongue hath not yet stung thee so much as thine own pride. Thou art too quick to take offence, Leo, a very tinderbox, ready to spark at a puff of wind.

LEO.
And thou, sir, art the very puff that fans the flame. I have heard enough of thy snide remarks and thy disdainful glances. Thy insolence hath grown too fat for my patience to bear.

CASSIAN.
Patience? Thou hast no patience for aught but thyself. And for thine insolence, I'll return it with steel!
[Draws his sword.]

SERAPHINA.
Hold, gentlemen! In the Prince's name, forbear this outrage! Think of the shame, the punishment!

LEO.
Shame is for those who suffer insult without reply!
[Draws his sword.]

VALERIUS.
Leo, I charge thee, put thy rapier up! Cassian, respect this place, and my grey hairs!

CASSIAN.
Your grey hairs have no jurisdiction over my just anger, old man!

LEO.
And thy braggart's heart hath no power to fright me, boy! Come, let's see whose blade is keener!
[They fight.]

SERAPHINA.
Stop them, Uncle! Marco, help!

MARCO.
I cannot step betwixt two such furies.

VALERIUS.
[Rushing between them.]
Hold, I say! For the love of peace!

[Cassian's blade, intended for Leo, glances off Valerius's arm as he steps in the way. Valerius cries out.]

LEO.
Uncle!
[Pushes Cassian back.]

CASSIAN.
A pox on't! I struck not at thee, old man.

VALERIUS.
[Clutching his arm.]
A scratch, a mere scratch. Yet 'tis enough to show the folly of your wrath. My blood, spilled for your petty pride!

SERAPHINA.
Uncle, thou art hurt indeed! A surgeon, quickly!

LEO.
Thou wretched villain! To strike an old man!
[Advances on Cassian again.]

CASSIAN.
'Twas not my intent! Blame thine own hotheadedness for pushing him to intervention!

VALERIUS.
Nay, blame me, children. My foolish hope that words might mend what honour thinks it must break. Go, both of you. Haste ye home. I will not have this quarrel stained with my old age.

LEO.
But Uncle...

VALERIUS.
Go! And may the heavens forgive this blindness that sees not a brother in every man.

[Exeunt Leo, Seraphina, and Valerius. Cassian and Marco remain, looking at the blood.]

MARCO.
This was ill-done, Cassian. An old man's blood on thy blade.

CASSIAN.
Curse Leo's temper! He pushes all to folly. Yet 'tis true, the strike was mine. What will the Prince say of this? This shall not end here, Marco, I warrant thee. I feel a chill creep through me.

MARCO.
This was ill-done, Cassian. An old man's blood on thy blade.

CASSIAN.
Curse Leo's temper! He pushes all to folly. Yet 'tis true, the strike was mine. What will the Prince say of this? This shall not end here, Marco, I warrant thee. I feel a chill creep through me.

MARCO.
A chill well earned, my friend. The Prince holds Lord Valerius in high esteem. To injure him, even by mischance, in a public brawl spurred by a private feud, is a matter of grave import. Thou must needs prepare thy plea, or thy flight.

CASSIAN.
Flight? And be branded a coward by all of Verona? Never! My intent was not upon the old man. I aimed for Leo's heart, yes, but not Valerius'. The heavens be my witness, I swear it.

MARCO.
The heavens may see thy heart, but the Prince will see the blood. And the cause. This enmity betwixt thee and Leo hath festered too long. It was bound to erupt, and now it hath taken a venerable man as its first casualty.

CASSIAN.
He stepped betwixt us. Was I to hold my hand in the midst of a combat? Leo's blade was out, his eyes aflame. It was self-defense, Marco, naught else!

MARCO.
Self-defense after thou didst draw first, and challenged him with such vehemence. Come, Cassian, let us not twist the truth with words. Thou art hot-blooded, proud, and quick to take offence. As is Leo. This is the heart of the matter.

CASSIAN.
And thou, my friend, art too quick to condemn. Where is thy loyalty? Dost thou stand with me, or against me?

MARCO.
I stand for reason, Cassian, and for thy welfare. The Prince will not tolerate such disorder. His city thrives on peace, not on the clash of swords in broad daylight. Go, clean thy blade, and think on what thou hast done. I will seek out news, and see if I may mitigate the Prince's anger.

CASSIAN.
Go then, Marco. And pray the news be not as grim as my heart forebodes. This chill deepens within me.
[Marco exits, leaving Cassian alone. Cassian looks at his sword, then wipes it on his sleeve with a troubled expression. He paces for a moment, then throws the sword aside and sinks into a chair, head in hands.]

[SCENE II]

[VALERIUS'S study. VALERIUS is seated, his arm bandaged. SERAPHINA kneels beside him, distraught. LEO paces angrily.]

SERAPHINA.
Uncle, how fares thy wound? Does it pain thee greatly? The surgeon said it was deep, yet clean.

VALERIUS.
The pain is but a sting, my child. Lesser than the sting of shame that two noble youths should bring such dishonour upon themselves and upon our house.

LEO.
Shame is upon Cassian, Uncle, not upon us. He is a villain, a scurrilous knave, to strike at thee! I swear, I will not rest until he pays dearly for this outrage.

VALERIUS.
And what payment dost thou seek, Leo? More blood? More sorrow? Will that heal my arm, or mend the fractured peace of this city?

LEO.
Justice, Uncle! Justice for this insult! The Prince must hear of this, and swiftly. Cassian must be punished, severely.

SERAPHINA.
But Uncle said he stepped in the way. Might it not have been an accident?

LEO.
An accident born of malice! His blade was meant for me, and had it found its mark, would it have been less of a crime? He meant murder, Seraphina, murder!

VALERIUS.
Hold thy tongue, Leo. Such words, spoken in haste, breed only more hatred. Cassian is rash, but I doubt he is a murderer. He is a hothead, much like thyself. The blame, if blame must be assigned, lies with both your prides.

LEO.
My pride was defending our honour, Uncle! His was merely insolence, as always.

VALERIUS.
And how much honour is there in drawing steel in the marketplace, causing fear and injury? Our honour, Leo, is not in the swiftness of our blades, but in the wisdom of our hearts. It is in the peace we uphold, and the respect we show our neighbours.

SERAPHINA.
Uncle speaks true. This fighting will bring ruin upon us all.

LEO.
And what wouldst thou have me do, Seraphina? Let him mock us, insult us, then wound our kin, and walk away unchastised? Is that honour? No! I will see him brought low.

VALERIUS.
Thou wilt await the Prince's judgment, Leo. And thou wilt heed it. I will not have my kinsmen turn this city into a private duelling ground. I will not have our family brought to ruin by a childish quarrel. My blood was spilt to show you the folly of such rage. Let it not have been spilled in vain.

LEO.
But Uncle...

VALERIUS.
No "buts", Leo. My will is firm in this. Whatever the Prince decrees, we shall abide. And if he asks for my testimony, I shall speak of circumstance, not of malice. For though the act was grievous, the intent, I pray, was not truly foul. Now, leave me. Seraphina, stay a moment.

[Leo hesitates, then bows stiffly and exits, still seething. Seraphina remains, looking at Valerius with concern.]

SERAPHINA.
Uncle, why dost thou defend him so? He wounded thee!

VALERIUS.
I do not defend his act, child, but I defend against the greater wound of unending vengeance. If I accuse him with bitterness, then Leo's fury will find justification, and this feud will consume us all. I have seen such things tear families, cities, and kingdoms asunder. One scratch leads to a cut, a cut to a mortal wound, and so on, until naught but ashes remain.

SERAPHINA.
But what if the Prince decides against Cassian? Will Leo be satisfied then?

VALERIUS.
Satisfaction is a fleeting mistress, Seraphina. Peace is the true prize. The Prince is a wise man. He will weigh the matter carefully. But whatever his judgment, we must be prepared to accept it, and to heal. Not just my arm, but the rift between these families.

SERAPHINA.
It seems an impossible task, Uncle. Their hatred runs so deep.

VALERIUS.
Nothing is impossible, child, where there is will to mend. Go now, and calm thy brother if thou canst. His rage does him no good, nor us.
[Seraphina nods, kisses Valerius's cheek, and exits. Valerius sighs, looking at his bandaged arm with a wistful expression.]

[SCENE III]

[The PRINCE'S audience chamber. PRINCE NICOLAS is seated on his throne. LORD CHANCELLOR ALARIC stands beside him. A MESSENGER kneels before them.]

MESSENGER.
Your Highness, I bring ill tidings from the city streets. A public brawl, most grievous, has erupted between Lord Cassian and Lord Leo.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
A brawl? Between those two hotheads again? I had thought my edicts on public peace were clear. What was the cause this time? And who was injured?

MESSENGER.
The cause, Your Highness, appeared to be their longstanding animosity. But in the midst of their combat, Lord Valerius, who sought to intervene, was struck by Lord Cassian's blade.

ALARIC.
Lord Valerius? Injured? This is most grave, Your Highness. He is a pillar of this city, and a man of great wisdom.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Indeed. Is his wound mortal?

MESSENGER.
The surgeon declared it a deep cut, but not immediately life-threatening, Your Highness. Though he lost much blood.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
This is an outrage. To draw swords in my city, to disturb the peace, and to injure a man of Valerius's standing, who sought to quell their madness. Such insolence cannot stand unpunished. Alaric, send guards immediately to apprehend Lord Cassian and Lord Leo. Bring them before me at once. And send a physician to Lord Valerius, though I understand one has already attended him. Ensure his comfort. I would also have him brought here, if he is able, to speak on this matter.

ALARIC.
It shall be done, Your Highness.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
These young lords test my patience. Their petty squabbles threaten the very fabric of the harmony I have striven to build. This will serve as a lesson for all who think my laws are but suggestions.
[Alaric bows and exits. Prince Nicolas rises from his throne, his face etched with concern and anger.]

PRINCE NICOLAS.
(To himself)
Valerius, injured. A good man, a true servant of the realm. This folly cannot be permitted to continue. It festers, growing larger, until it consumes all.

[SCENE IV]

[CASSIAN'S dwelling. MARCO returns, looking grim. Cassian sits despondently.]

MARCO.
Cassian, the news is ill. The Prince is furious. He has commanded your immediate apprehension, and Leo's. Guards are on their way to bring you before him.

CASSIAN.
(Rising)
Apprehension? So quickly? He means to make an example of me.

MARCO.
And of Leo. But the injury to Valerius has inflamed his wrath most of all. He holds the old man in high regard.

CASSIAN.
What hope have I then? Even Valerius's kindness may not avail me.

MARCO.
You must plead intent, or lack thereof. You must stress that your blade was not meant for him. And perhaps, pray for Valerius's mercy in his testimony.

CASSIAN.
Mercy? From a man whose blood I have spilled? A bitter hope. My name will be ruined, my lands confiscated perhaps, my honour stripped. This chill, Marco, it becomes an icy grip.

MARCO.
Do not despair yet. The Prince is stern, but just. He will hear both sides. But you must compose yourself. Your usual bravado will serve you ill here.

CASSIAN.
Bravado? Is it bravado to stand by one's honour? Is it bravado to defend oneself against an unceasing tormentor? Leo's slights are like poison, slowly seeping into one's very soul.

MARCO.
And your responses are like fire, consuming everything in their path. There are other ways, Cassian. Words, diplomacy, appeals to the Prince.

CASSIAN.
Words have failed me with Leo. They always have. He delights in my anger, in seeing me roused.

MARCO.
Perhaps. But now is not the time for recriminations. Now is the time for a clear head, and a humble heart.
[A loud knocking is heard at the door.]

CASSIAN.
They are here.
[He straightens his tunic, a flicker of defiance returning to his eyes, quickly veiled by a resigned weariness.]

MARCO.
Remember my words, Cassian. Humility.
[Two GUARDS enter, their faces stern.]

FIRST GUARD.
Lord Cassian, by order of Prince Nicolas, you are commanded to come before him.

CASSIAN.
I expected no less. Lead on.
[Cassian casts a glance at Marco, then follows the guards out. Marco shakes his head, then exits in the opposite direction, clearly agitated.]

[SCENE V]

[The PRINCE'S audience chamber. PRINCE NICOLAS is on his throne. ALARIC stands nearby. LEO is brought in by two guards, his face sullen. Shortly after, CASSIAN is brought in by another pair of guards, looking more composed but pale. VALERIUS enters slowly, supported by SERAPHINA, his bandaged arm evident. The Prince rises slightly in respect for Valerius.]

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Lord Leo. Lord Cassian. You stand accused of violating the public peace, of engaging in a brawl in my city streets, and of injuring a man of the highest esteem. Your actions have brought shame upon yourselves, and dishonour upon this court.

LEO.
Your Highness, I...

PRINCE NICOLAS.
(Raising a hand)
Silence. I will hear from Lord Valerius first. Lord Valerius, speak of what occurred, and of your injury.

VALERIUS.
(Bowing slightly, though with difficulty)
Your Highness, I thank you for your concern. My wound is but a flesh wound, though it bled freely. As for the incident, I fear my own eagerness to prevent further strife placed me in harm's way. I stepped between the two young lords as their blades were drawn. Lord Cassian's strike, intended for Lord Leo, glanced off my arm as I intervened.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
And for what purpose did they draw steel, Lord Valerius?

VALERIUS.
(Sighing)
Alas, Your Highness, the same old quarrel. Words exchanged, slights perceived, and pride inflamed. It is a sorrowful tale of hot tempers and stubborn hearts.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
(To Cassian)
Lord Cassian, did you indeed strike Lord Valerius?

CASSIAN.
Your Highness, with a heavy heart, I confess my blade did connect with his arm. But I swear upon my honour, it was not my intent to harm Lord Valerius. I saw him not until my blade had already moved. My target was ever Lord Leo, whose insolence had pushed me beyond all reason.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
(To Leo)
Lord Leo, is it true your words provoked Lord Cassian to such an extreme?

LEO.
Your Highness, Cassian's tongue is a viper's, as always. He speaks with disdain, and casts slights upon my family. My words were but a response to his ongoing provocations. He drew first, Your Highness! He challenged me to combat!

CASSIAN.
And you were swift to accept, Lord Leo, were you not? Your blade was out as quickly as mine! You revel in conflict!

LEO.
I revel in defending my honour, sir, which you continually assail!

PRINCE NICOLAS.
(Banging his fist on his armrest)
Enough! This is precisely the childish bickering that led to this lamentable state. You both blame each other, yet neither accepts the full burden of your own recklessness. You have turned my city into a theatre for your private vendetta, and in doing so, have endangered innocent lives.

ALARIC.
Your Highness, the public peace has been grievously disturbed. Such an act demands severe punishment to deter others.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Indeed, Alaric. Lord Cassian, your blade struck an innocent. Lord Leo, your fury fueled the fire. Both of you are culpable. The severity of your punishment must reflect the gravity of your actions. But before I pronounce judgment, I must consider the greater good of Verona. This feud between your houses has been a thorn in my side for too long. It breeds disquiet, and weakens the bonds of our society.

SERAPHINA.
Your Highness, if I may speak?

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Lady Seraphina, you may.

SERAPHINA.
My uncle, Lord Valerius, would desire peace above all. He risked his own safety to prevent further harm. I believe he would wish for a resolution that might heal this division, rather than deepen it with more harshness.

VALERIUS.
My niece speaks my heart, Your Highness. Though I bear the mark of their folly, I bear no malice. My only wish is for these two young men to see the light of reason, and for this city to know true amity.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Your wisdom, Valerius, is as ever a guiding star. And Lady Seraphina, your plea for peace is well-heard. I cannot, however, overlook the breach of law. Public fighting will not be tolerated. The injury to Lord Valerius, however accidental, cannot be dismissed.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Therefore, my judgment is thus: For a period of one year, Lord Cassian and Lord Leo, you are both banished from the city of Verona. You shall not set foot within its walls. You shall surrender your swords and any other weapons to my guard, and you shall repair to your country estates. There you will reflect upon your actions, and the consequences thereof. Should either of you violate this decree, the punishment will be imprisonment and confiscation of all lands.

LEO.
Banished, Your Highness? For a year?

CASSIAN.
(Stunned into silence)

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Indeed, Lord Leo. And should this feud continue to fester upon your return, I will consider more permanent measures. Furthermore, I decree that upon the expiration of your banishment, you shall both present yourselves before me, and before Lord Valerius, and publicly swear an oath to cease all hostilities. Should you refuse, or should you break that oath, the consequences will be severe. This is not merely a punishment, but an opportunity for both of you to learn the value of peace. Now, surrender your weapons.

[Reluctantly, Leo unbuckles his sword belt and places his rapier on the floor. Cassian hesitates for a moment, then, with a heavy sigh, does the same. Guards approach to collect the weapons.]

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Alaric, ensure their safe conduct to their estates. Lord Valerius, I regret the pain you have suffered. Seraphina, tend to your uncle. This court is dismissed.
[The Prince rises, a weary look on his face. Leo and Cassian are led away by guards, not daring to look at each other. Valerius and Seraphina remain.]

VALERIUS.
A harsh judgment, perhaps, but a just one. It forces them apart, and offers a chance for reflection.

SERAPHINA.
But a whole year, Uncle. And what if they return as bitter as they left?

VALERIUS.
Then we must pray that time, and absence, might soften their hearts. The seeds of hatred are strong, but the desire for peace, if given fertile ground, can sometimes prevail. Come, child, let us return home. I feel the chill of the afternoon air settling deep within my bones.
[Valerius and Seraphina exit slowly. The audience chamber is left empty, save for Alaric.]

ALARIC.
(To himself)
Banished. A bold move, Your Highness. May it truly bring the peace you seek, and not merely postpone a storm.
[Alaric exits.]

[SCENE VI]

[A country estate. One month later. CASSIAN sits alone in a rustic study, staring out a window. His demeanor is restless, his usual arrogance replaced by a brooding quietness.]

CASSIAN.
A year. A year of exile. The Prince's justice, they call it. My punishment for daring to defend my name. This quiet gnaws at me, Marco. This solitude, it makes my thoughts turn inward, and find no comfort there.

MARCO.
(Entering, carrying a letter)
And what comfort wouldst thou seek, Cassian? The clang of swords? The shouts of conflict? Is that truly where thy soul finds solace?

CASSIAN.
It is where I know my place, Marco. In the fray, in the challenge. Here, I am naught but an exile. A boy sent to his room without supper.

MARCO.
A boy who wounded a respected elder. A boy who brought the Prince's wrath upon himself. This is an opportunity, Cassian, not a mere punishment. An opportunity to reflect, to learn temperance.

CASSIAN.
Temperance? I am weary of such talk. Leo, no doubt, enjoys his own exile, finding fresh grievances to nurse. He will return with renewed spite, I warrant you.

MARCO.
Perhaps. Or perhaps he too will find this solitude a harsh master. I bring you news from Verona.

CASSIAN.
News? Of what? Is the city celebrating my absence?

MARCO.
(Handing him the letter)
This is from Lord Valerius. He writes to both you and Leo, expressing his hope for a peaceful return. He also makes an offer of guidance, should you seek it.

CASSIAN.
(Takes the letter, reads it quickly, then scoffs.)
Guidance. From the man whose wound caused my banishment. What irony is this?

MARCO.
He bears no malice, Cassian. He truly seeks reconciliation. He speaks of healing, of unity for Verona. His words are gentle, yet firm.

CASSIAN.
Gentle words from an old man. They do not sting as deeply as Leo's jibes, nor do they cut as fiercely as his blade. I doubt they will change the nature of things.

MARCO.
And what is the nature of things, Cassian? Eternal conflict? Endless bitterness? Is that truly what you desire for your life, for your house?

CASSIAN.
I desire respect, Marco. I desire that my name not be dragged through the mud by that arrogant fool.

MARCO.
And how much respect do you gain by being banished? By being known as one half of a quarrel that shames our city? True respect, Cassian, is earned not by the sword, but by wisdom, by integrity, by grace.

CASSIAN.
(Turns away, paces restlessly.)
Easy words, Marco. Easier to speak than to live by, especially when one's blood runs hot, and one's honour is constantly challenged.

MARCO.
But challenge it will be, Cassian. Always. The world is full of slights, real and imagined. How you choose to meet them defines you. Do you wish to be known as the tempest, always brewing a storm, or as the oak, standing firm against it?

CASSIAN.
I am no oak, Marco. I am a flame. And some flames cannot be quenched. This banishment does not quench me, it merely confines me. It makes the desire for justice, for vindication, burn all the hotter. This chill I felt, it was not of fear, but of anticipation. The calm before a greater storm, perhaps.

MARCO.
That is a dangerous thought, my friend. A very dangerous thought indeed.

[SCENE VII]

[LEO’S country estate. Also one month later. Leo is training with a wooden sword, his movements sharp and aggressive. SERAPHINA watches him with a worried expression.]

SERAPHINA.
Brother, must you exert yourself so? The day is warm, and your anger seems to fuel your every stroke.

LEO.
(Pauses, wiping sweat from his brow)
Anger? Nay, Seraphina. This is merely preparation. A year of idleness will not dull my blade, nor my resolve. When I return to Verona, I shall be stronger, swifter, and more ready to meet any challenge that odious Cassian may present.

SERAPHINA.
But Uncle wishes for peace. He hopes this banishment will bring you both to reason.

LEO.
Reason, Seraphina, is often merely another word for weakness. Cassian understands only force. He will return as he left, brimming with his usual insolence. And I shall be ready.

SERAPHINA.
But if you both return with the same hostile hearts, then what purpose has this banishment served? The Prince's decree will have been in vain, and the shame will be deeper.

LEO.
The shame, sister, is Cassian's alone. He wounded Uncle. He instigated the brawl. I merely defended our honour. He is the villain.

SERAPHINA.
And are you so without fault, Leo? Did your words not cut as deeply? Did your fury not match his?

LEO.
He deserved every word! And more! He delights in mocking our family, in questioning our lineage! How can I stand idly by while such slander is uttered?

SERAPHINA.
There are ways to answer slander without drawing blood. There are courts, there are appeals to the Prince. Uncle speaks of these. He writes to both of you, hoping for reconciliation.

LEO.
(Scoffs)
Reconciliation with Cassian? A viper cannot be reconciled with its prey. He is a scoundrel, and he will remain a scoundrel. This banishment is a mere pause in our conflict, not its end. It is a time for me to sharpen my resolve.

SERAPHINA.
And what of Uncle's wishes? His letter spoke of unity, of healing the city's divisions. He risks his own well-being for this dream of peace.

LEO.
Uncle is too gentle, too forgiving. He sees the best in all men, even when it is not there. I see Cassian for what he is: a threat. And I will protect our family, and our honour, from him. This banishment has only hardened my resolve. I have had a month to dwell on his every insult, his every sneer. The list of grievances grows longer, not shorter.

SERAPHINA.
This cannot be the path, Leo. This bitterness will consume you. It will consume us all. I fear for what the future holds if you both return with such hate in your hearts. The Prince will not tolerate a renewed conflict.

LEO.
Then let him deal with Cassian. My blade will be clean. But my spirit will be unbroken. I will not yield an inch to that man. Never.

SERAPHINA.
Oh, Leo.
[Seraphina sighs, her face filled with despair. Leo turns back to his training, his movements becoming even more furious, as if battling an invisible foe.]

[SCENE VIII]

[PRINCE NICOLAS'S chamber. Six months into the banishment. The Prince is in conference with ALARIC. He looks tired.]

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Six months, Alaric. Half their term of banishment. Have you received any reports from their estates? Do they show any signs of remorse, of changed hearts?

ALARIC.
Your Highness, the reports are scant. Lord Cassian is said to be withdrawn, spending his days in study, but allowing few visitors. Lord Leo, conversely, is reported to be relentless in his martial exercises, training with a fervor that suggests little peace of mind. Both maintain a strict isolation from city life.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
And Valerius? How does he fare?

ALARIC.
Lord Valerius's wound has healed, Your Highness. He continues his work, advising the council, and quietly urging reconciliation wherever he can. Lady Seraphina, too, is often seen visiting the sick and poor, her spirit a balm to the city.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Good. Their virtue shines brighter for the shadow cast by the others. But I confess, Alaric, I had hoped for more profound change from the banished lords. This silence, this isolation, it could be a brooding quiet, not a contemplative peace.

ALARIC.
Indeed, Your Highness. Absence can sometimes make the heart grow fonder, but it can also harden it, allowing old grievances to fester without distraction.

PRINCE NICOLAS.
Precisely my fear. I had hoped that separated, they might see the folly of their shared anger. But if they merely stew in their own resentments, then my efforts will have been in vain.

ALARIC.
