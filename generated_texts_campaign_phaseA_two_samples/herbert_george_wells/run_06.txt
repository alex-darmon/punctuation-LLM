I had often considered the peculiar trajectory of our species, our ceaseless ascent towards an imagined zenith of intellectual and physical refinement. It is a common conceit, I think, to believe that evolution’s current manifest form represents its ultimate intention. But in nature, as in all things, purpose is often subverted by exigency, and the grand design may yield to the urgent imperative of mere survival. We had built our towering cities, our pristine domes, believing we had transcended the grimy mechanics of existence; yet, the machinery still churned beneath, out of sight, out of mind. What, then, of those who remained tethered to its operation?

It was on a particularly acrid day, one of those rare moments when the outer atmosphere’s filtration systems faltered and the ancient industrial exhalations were permitted to seep through, that I found myself compelled to investigate a rumour. Legends spoke of forgotten maintenance passages, vast arteries of conduit and cabling, now mostly disused, leading deep into the urban substrata. Seeking both refuge from the stinging air and a verification of these unsettling tales, I descended through a forgotten service hatch, whose rusted hinges shrieked a protest against my intrusion.

After a long and treacherous climb downwards, I found myself in a cavernous space, a forgotten artery of the city’s lifeblood. The air was heavy, warm, and thick with the scent of ozone and stale metallic dust. Groping through the Stygian gloom, my hand finally brushed against a vast, cold surface, its texture oddly organic, yielding slightly under pressure. A low, rhythmic thrum vibrated through the floor, a sound that seemed to have no beginning or end. Then, in the profound obscurity, a flicker of movement caught my attention. A pair of eyes, luminous with an internal, phosphorescent gleam, regarded me from what seemed an impossible distance.

My first thought was of some engineered surveillance unit, a remnant of forgotten protocols. But as I held my breath, the eyes shifted, and a faint, almost imperceptible rustle reached my ears. The old, primal instinct to flee warred with an overpowering scientific curiosity. I remained still, attempting to discern the form behind the glowing points. It was then that the creature stirred, detaching itself from the dark wall, a sinuous, elongated silhouette against the faint residual glow of distant junction boxes. It moved with a disturbing fluidity, its limbs far too numerous, its head a mere nodule between broad, flat shoulders.

My impression of it is, of course, terribly imperfect; the light was poor, and my mind was clouded by a nascent dread. But I observed that its skin was a mottled grey, almost translucent in places, and its movements were less like walking and more like a slow, deliberate undulation. It possessed no discernible mouth, only a small, puckered aperture that might have been vestigial. The luminous eyes, large and unblinking, were its most striking feature. It seemed to navigate not by sight, but by some other, more arcane sense, its progress measured and careful, almost hesitant, as if the very air offered resistance.

After an interminable moment, it continued its passage along the wall, disappearing into a fissure in the concrete. I stood there, stunned, the oppressive silence of the chamber amplifying the thrumming beneath my feet. What was this Thing? A new species evolved in the urban depths? A biological machine, somehow reanimated? But gradually, the truth, as unsettling as it was compelling, began to dawn on me: that Man, in its relentless pursuit of an increasingly weightless existence, had created a shadow ecosystem; that our sleek, sun-drenched domes were not the sole inheritors of our technological prowess, but that this pale, multi-limbed, sub-human Thing was also a product, a consequence, of all our advancements.

I thought of the automated nutrient distributors, of the vast, unlit server farms, of the endless conduits carrying power and data beneath our feet. I began to suspect their true purpose. And what, I wondered, was this creature doing in my scheme of a world perfectly optimized for comfort and leisure? How was it related to the effortless grace of the Upperworlders, who floated on grav-carts between their crystalline towers? And what untold adaptations lay hidden in the deeper, unexplored levels of this labyrinthine underworld? I sat upon a discarded conduit, telling myself that, at any rate, there was nothing to fear, and that there I must descend for the solution of my difficulties. And withal I was absolutely afraid to go! As I hesitated, a distant, automated drone passed overhead, its whirring a stark reminder of the world I had left behind.

There were three circumstances in particular which made me think that its rare emergence towards the periphery of the light was the outcome of a long-continued adaptation to its environment. In the first place, there was the peculiar translucence of its skin, reminiscent of deep-sea organisms adapted to immense pressure and utter darkness. Then, those luminous eyes, with their self-generating light, are common features of creatures in lightless realms: witness the bioluminescent fish of abyssal trenches. And last of all, that sinuous, almost liquid movement, that ability to cling and undulate along rough surfaces, and that apparent lack of urgency, all reinforced the theory of a life lived in constant darkness, where speed is less important than precision and resilience.

Beneath our polished plazas, then, the city must be honeycombed enormously, and these hidden arteries were the habitat of the New Race. The presence of these forgotten access points and the ceaseless hum of machinery showed how universal were its ramifications. What so natural, then, as to assume that it was in this artificial Underworld that such maintenance and unseen work as was necessary to the comfort of the daylight race was done?
