Kael, who had been staring at Shard with a mix of fear and awe, knelt beside Zephyr again, checking his vitals. "His pulse is still weak, Commander, but it's stronger than it was. The bleeding has stabilized completely. It's... it's a miracle."

A miracle born of desperation, Shard thought, of a connection forged in fire and blood. She had used the mirror not as a weapon of light, but as a weapon of shadow, a conduit for raw, primal fear, twisted through the deep bond with Zephyr and the Spire's own ancient power. It had worked. For now.

The night had fully fallen, but it was no longer a symbol of dread. It was a canvas for a tentative victory, painted in the distant flashes of energy fire and the receding roars of the leviathans.

Roric's voice, now triumphant, filled the comm. "Shard! You did it! They're retreating! The main force is pulling back! We've bought ourselves more time! What was that? How did you do it?" His questions tumbled out, eager and amazed.

"Later, Roric," Shard said, exhaustion finally catching up to her. "Just... hold the line. And get Zephyr to safety. I'm done."

She closed her eyes, the familiar darkness threatening to consume her. But this time, it was not the darkness of despair, but the welcome embrace of utter fatigue. She had pushed beyond her limits, beyond anything she had thought possible. She had faced the overwhelming might of the leviathans, and she had, for a precious few moments, turned their own primal instincts against them.

But the victory was momentary. The leviathans would return. The alpha, though terrified, would likely adapt again, perhaps learning to shield its mind, or to redirect its terror into something even more devastating. This was just a reprieve, a battle won, not the war.

As Kael gently began to prepare Zephyr for transport, Shard allowed herself to lean back against Zephyr’s still body, drawing comfort from his warmth. She had found a new way, a different power within the mirror, a darker side to its ancient lore. But the cost had been immense. Her mind felt hollowed out, her spirit stretched thin.

The Spire still stood. Zephyr was still alive. And Shard, though broken, was not defeated. Not yet. She had glimpsed a deeper, more dangerous path to victory, one that relied not on dazzling light, but on the manipulation of shadow and primal fear. It was a path that frightened her, but one she knew she would have to walk again. The true horror was not just the leviathans, but the depths she might have to plumb within herself, and within the mirror, to stop them. The fight was far from over, and the darkness, though temporarily repelled, held secrets she had only just begun to uncover. The Spire, the last bastion, depended on her continued resilience, and her terrifying newfound understanding.
