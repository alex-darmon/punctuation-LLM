Noah, ever the pragmatist, consulted a small, worn notebook he always carried. “Commander, including ourselves, we have thirty-seven. Seven of those are support personnel, primarily medics and communications specialists, not frontline combatants. That leaves thirty operational soldiers. All are well trained, but lightly equipped. We’re designed for reconnaissance and limited engagement, not disrupting a company strength unit setting up a bridgehead.” He paused, then added, “We have ample small arms ammunition, some grenades, and a few anti tank rockets, but nothing for sustained heavy fire. Our night vision equipment is sufficient for about half the team.”

Andrei nodded in agreement. “Morale is high, Commander. But Noah is right. We are not equipped for a direct assault. Any engagement would need to be surgical, hit and run, designed to maximize disruption and minimize direct confrontation.”

Anya paced a small circle within the cramped command post. The air felt thick, heavy with the implications of their discussion. Thirty soldiers against what Elias had described as a company strength unit, possibly reinforced, with heavy equipment support en route. The odds were grim.

“Surgical, hit and run,” Anya repeated, testing the words. “That means stealth is paramount. Surprise is our only real advantage. We cannot afford to be drawn into a prolonged firefight. Our objective is not to annihilate Richter’s unit, but to render their preparations useless. Disrupt their communication, destroy their forward observation posts, sabotage any bridge building materials, and delay the crossing of heavy equipment for as long as possible.”

She stopped pacing and fixed her gaze on Noah. “Noah, I want a detailed assessment of the Mill itself. Access routes, potential blind spots, cover and concealment, and any natural choke points. How much do we know about its structural integrity? Could it be collapsed or rendered impassable?”

Noah pulled a map from the table, unfolding it carefully. “The Mill is an old structure, Commander. Built of stone and heavy timbers. It spans the Velikaya River, a relatively narrow but deep channel at that point. The bridge itself is stone and concrete, sturdy. Collapsing it would require significant demolition charges, more than we possess. But the approaches to it, particularly on the enemy side, are less robust. There are several old outbuildings, remnants of the mill operations, which could offer concealment or be used as demolition targets to block the road.”

Andrei interjected, “The river itself is swift flowing there, especially this time of year with the snowmelt. If we could disable the bridge, even temporarily, and push any heavy equipment into the water, it would set them back days, perhaps even weeks, clearing that channel.”

Anya’s mind raced, processing the information. “Good point, Andrei. So, sabotage is key. Not just the bridge, but their means of working on it. What about our approach? How close can we get without detection? Elias said they’d have forward observation posts. Where would they logically place them?”

Noah traced a finger along the map. “Given the terrain, the high ground to the east and west of the Mill offers the best vantage points. They’d likely have small teams there, possibly with thermal or night vision optics. The river valley itself is relatively open for about a kilometer in either direction, but dense forest begins about a kilometer and a half back from the Mill on our side. We could use that for concealment until we are ready to move.”

“So, a night operation,” Anya concluded. “Standard procedure for this kind of infiltration. We approach under the cover of darkness. But we need to move quickly. ‘Imminent’ means tonight, or early tomorrow morning. We don’t have time for extensive preparation.”

She looked at her two most trusted lieutenants. “This is a high risk operation. We will be outnumbered, outgunned, and operating deep behind enemy lines. There’s a high probability of casualties. Anyone who is not absolutely committed, speak now. No shame in it. This is a volunteer mission, in all but name.”

Both Noah and Andrei met her gaze with unwavering determination. “Commander, we are with you,” Andrei stated, his voice firm. Noah simply nodded, his jaw tight.

Anya felt a surge of grim satisfaction. Her team was ready.
