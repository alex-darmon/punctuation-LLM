"Reynolds, you ready to go to work?" Ben turned to the Sergeant Major in the back.

"Born ready, Lieutenant. Just point me at the big toys," Reynolds replied, checking the straps of a heavy pack containing the JDAM warheads, now armed with sophisticated fuzing mechanisms and remote detonators. He also had a smaller satchel of shaped charges and plastic explosives.

Ben took one last look at the chaotic but efficient enemy staging area. This was it. The point of no return. The fate of their entire defensive line, possibly the sector, hinged on their actions in the next few hours. The image of Ghost Two falling, broken and burning, flashed through his mind. He would not fail. He would make them pay. The silent promise was a cold comfort, steeling his resolve.

"Alright team. Phase one: infiltration. Jack, get us as close as you can without drawing attention. We'll approach on foot from there. Sharma, maintain comms silence until we reach our target positions. Keep eyes and ears open. Reynolds, you're with me. We're going to give those bridge layers a taste of our hospitality."

The cargo van slowly, meticulously, edged its way through the dense underbrush, finally finding a hidden spot behind a thick copse of trees, overlooking the enemy's staging area. The hum of their activity was now a dull roar. Ben and Reynolds, equipped with their packs and night vision goggles, slipped out of the van, melting into the shadows. Jack remained behind the wheel, engine idling softly, ready for an immediate extraction or to provide support. Sharma monitored her comms, a silent sentinel.

The air was thick with the scent of diesel and newly disturbed earth. Ben moved with a predator's stealth, his senses heightened. Every snapped twig, every distant cough, was a potential threat. He and Reynolds moved like ghosts through the forest, using the natural cover to their advantage. They had trained for this, but training was never truly the same as the cold, hard reality of enemy territory.

They observed the enemy for precious minutes, getting a clearer picture of their routines, their patrol routes, the gaps in their security. The enemy was confident, almost complacent in their industrial-scale conquest. Their focus was entirely on the river, on getting those bridgelayers into position.

"They have a small patrol moving along the riverbank every ten minutes," Reynolds whispered, his voice barely audible over the enemy’s din. "And another, more irregular, through the forest further back. We need to time this perfectly."

Ben nodded. "We'll wait for the river patrol to pass, then make our move. We'll use the shadows of the vehicles themselves as cover. Our priority is the lead bridgelayer first. Then the second. We plant the charges, set the timers, and get out."

The wait was agonizing, each second stretching into an eternity. Ben felt the familiar thumping in his chest, a rhythm of apprehension and anticipation. He thought of all the men and women depending on them, all the sacrifices already made. He thought of Ghost Two. And then, he saw his opening. The river patrol passed, their chatter fading into the background.

"Now," Ben breathed, and they moved.

They moved with practiced efficiency, low to the ground, using the dense cover, then darting across open patches of ground when the sentries’ backs were turned. The roar of the machinery, meant to intimidate and dominate, now served as their cloak, muffling their movements. They reached the behemoth of the first bridgelayer, its metallic skin cold and imposing in the night. It was an enormous machine, like a giant articulated snake, its segments designed to unfold and span the river.

"Lieutenant, targeting the main drive gears and hydraulic systems here," Reynolds indicated, pointing to a complex array of machinery near the base of the bridgelayer, partially obscured by a heavy track. "And then the primary load bearing points of the bridge itself, here and here." He had a small, handheld thermal imager, allowing him to see the heat signatures of the active components.

They worked quickly, silently. Ben stood guard, his rifle ready, sweeping the area for any sign of detection. Reynolds, with deft, experienced hands, began placing the first of the repurposed JDAM warheads. The devices were heavy, cumbersome, but the Sergeant Major handled them with practiced ease, securing them to the vital structural points with specialized clamps and magnetic attachments. He carefully wired the fuzing systems, ensuring a precise, delayed detonation.

"First charge set, Lieutenant," Reynolds whispered, wiping sweat from his brow. "Fuzed for a ten minute delay after manual arming. Gives us time to hit the next one and get clear."

Ben gave him a grim nod. "Let's move to the second."

They repeated the process with the second bridgelayer, a slightly more challenging placement due to its position closer to the river, with more active personnel nearby. Ben had to suppress a passing patrol with silent, precise movements, a brief, brutal encounter that left two enemy soldiers silently neutralized and dragged into the deep shadows. This wasn't clean aerial combat; this was messy, visceral, and utterly unforgiving.

With the second charge set and armed, they began their extraction. The adrenaline was pumping now, a potent cocktail of fear and triumph. They had done it. Two targets hit. Now, they just had to get out alive.

"Lieutenant, Jack, this is Sharma. Enemy comms are suddenly much more active. They've detected something. Patrols are being redirected. You need to move fast," Sharma’s urgent whisper came over their earpieces.

"Understood, Sharma. We're on our way. Jack, prepare for a hot extraction. We might have company." Ben picked up his pace, Reynolds close behind him. The hum of the enemy machinery now seemed less of a cloak and more of a suffocating blanket. The distant shouts were closer now, more agitated.

Flares suddenly erupted in the sky, illuminating the forest in a stark, blinding white light. Sirens began to wail, piercing the night. They had been detected.

"Move! Move! Move!" Ben yelled, breaking into a sprint. Bullets began to tear through the trees behind them, splinters of wood flying.

"Jack, where are you?" Ben barked into his comms, ducking behind a thick tree as laser sights danced across the foliage.

"Almost there, Ben! They're on to me too! Coming in from your seven o'clock!" Jack's voice was strained, the roar of the van's engine now clear, tires screeching on the rough ground as he pushed it through the trees, creating a diversion.

Ben returned fire, laying down suppressive bursts, buying Reynolds precious seconds to keep pace. The sound of explosions ripped through the air – smaller charges, perhaps a tripwire they'd missed, or enemy munitions hitting something volatile. The forest was turning into a maelstrom.

Suddenly, the ground beneath them shuddered violently. A deafening roar tore through the night, followed by a brilliant, blinding flash of orange and red that momentarily eclipsed the flares. The first JDAM warhead had detonated.

A wave of intense heat washed over them, and a shower of debris rained down, leaves, dirt, and twisted metal. The ear splitting explosion was quickly followed by another, even larger, as the entire structure of the first bridgelayer, compromised by the blast, buckled and tore itself apart, triggering secondary explosions from its own fuel tanks and hydraulics. The monstrous machine, moments before a symbol of relentless progress, was now a mangled, burning wreck, blocking the river and sending colossal sections of its superstructure crashing into the Kael with a thunderous splash.

"Direct hit! The first one is down!" Ben roared, a surge of adrenaline mixed with primal satisfaction coursing through him. The enemy’s screams of panic were now audible, mingling with the ongoing gunfire.

But the fight wasn't over. Not yet. They still had to get out. The second JDAM warhead was moments from detonation. The ground vibrated again, a deeper, more resonant thrum, as the second bridgelayer too met its fiery end, its massive sections collapsing into the river, creating a choke point of twisted steel and blazing fire.

"This is our ride, boys and girls!" Jack’s voice crackled over the comms, and the cargo van burst through the tree line, skidding to a halt right beside them, its side door already flung open.

"Get in! Get in!" Ben yelled, pushing Reynolds into the back, then diving in himself as Jack slammed the accelerator, tires spitting dirt and gravel. Enemy fire raked the back of the van, but the basic armor held, deflecting most of the rounds.

"Sharma, extraction point now! And tell Command what we did!" Ben shouted over the din, the roar of the van, the explosions behind them, and the distant sounds of battle.

"Understood, Lieutenant! Coordinates updating!" Sharma replied, her voice firm despite the chaos.

They sped away from the inferno, leaving behind a scene of destruction and confusion. The mission had been a success, brutal and costly, but a success nonetheless. The enemy's relentless march across the Kael River had been halted, at least for now. They had bought time. The Ghost Bat might have fallen, but Ben had found another way to fight, another way to make the enemy pay for what they had taken. The war was far from over, but for one night, against impossible odds, they had pushed back. And the memory of Ghost Two, burning a hole in Ben's soul, had been partially avenged.
