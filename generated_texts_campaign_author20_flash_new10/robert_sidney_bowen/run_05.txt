Others were stumbling, groaning, their regeneration working overtime to knit together immense wounds. They were not dead, but they were certainly incapacitated, stunned, and severely damaged. The surge had worked.

Liam was still on the platform, slumped over the console, motionless.

"Liam!" Mark yelled, his voice raw, ignoring the throbbing pain in his head and the surviving, groaning aliens. He began to scramble back towards the walkways, his heart in his throat. Had Liam pushed it too far? Had he sacrificed himself for their escape?

He reached the platform, his energy pistol, somehow still clutched in his hand, ready to finish off any lingering threats. Liam was breathing, thankfully. His face was pale, streaked with sweat and dust, but he was conscious. He slowly pushed himself up, groaning.

"You alright?" Mark asked, helping him stand.

"Alive," Liam gasped, his voice raspy. "Barely. Overload almost fried me with it. But it worked. The primary conduit is stabilized, but the discharge... it created a momentary instability across the local energy grid. It should clear us a path for now." He pointed towards the main chamber. "The energy levels there have plummeted. We have a window."

Mark nodded, his eyes sweeping across the damaged cavern. The remaining aliens, though slowly recovering, were too disoriented and damaged to pose an immediate threat. They had bought themselves time. And they had the energy.

"Let's go," Mark said, helping Liam navigate the now partially collapsed walkway. "We have a ship to power, and a door to open. And then we leave this place to its ancient, slumbering, very angry inhabitants."

They made their way back through the ravaged power cavern, the air still thick with the acrid scent of the discharge. As they reached the broken tunnel entrance, Mark risked a glance back. The colossal crystal in the heart of the cavern was still pulsing, but its amber light was dimmer, more subdued. It had given them what they needed, but at a cost. The alien complex had been shaken, awakened, and now it knew they were there. The path ahead was still unknown, but at least now, they had a fighting chance. They had the power of a star.
