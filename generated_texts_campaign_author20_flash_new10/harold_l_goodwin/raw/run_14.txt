"Very well," Thorne said, his voice losing some of its earlier detached formality, tinged now with a new, heavier sense of responsibility. "Professor Vance, Commander Roberts. You will both remain here at Lunar Command. We will establish a secure research facility for you. Dr. Aris, you will lead a team to verify the empirical claims of Professor Vance's data. Colonel Richter, you will conduct a full threat assessment, focusing on the security implications of this 'data pipeline' and the broader 'universal data stream'. General Kael, Captain Valdez, you will begin immediate strategic planning for potential global and extraterrestrial ramifications."

His gaze swept over the faces of his subordinates. "This information, as Professor Vance has presented it, has the potential to redefine humanity's place in the universe. It also has the potential to tear us apart. Until we fully understand what we have, this information remains classified at the highest possible level. No unauthorized access, no leaks, no external communications without my express approval. Is that understood?"

A chorus of "Understood, Admiral" rippled around the table. The atmosphere in the room had shifted, coalescing into a unified, albeit apprehensive, resolve. The initial shock was giving way to the cold, calculated machinery of military and scientific investigation.

"Professor Vance," Thorne continued, his eyes returning to Leo, "I appreciate your candor and your apparent conviction. But understand this: Lunar Command's primary directive is the security and stability of Earth and its interests. Every piece of information you provide, every claim you make, will be scrutinized under that lens. We will operate with extreme caution."

"I understand, Admiral," Leo replied, his voice barely a whisper, the last vestiges of his strength draining away. "And I welcome the scrutiny. The truth, ultimately, will speak for itself." He felt utterly exhausted, physically and mentally drained, yet a strange sense of relief washed over him. The burden, which he and Sam had carried for months in the lonely expanse of space, was now shared. The real work, the confrontation with established thought, the bureaucratic inertia, and the sheer human fear of the unknown, had indeed begun. And it was just as difficult, just as formidable, as he had always known it would be.

Sam, sensing Leo's near collapse, discreetly placed a hand on his arm, a gesture of quiet support. She knew this was only the first battle in a war for humanity's future. The initial presentation had gone as well as could be expected, perhaps even better, considering the radical nature of their findings. There was skepticism, yes, and fear, but also a nascent scientific curiosity and a pragmatic military approach to assessing the unknown. It was a start. The sterile, high tech environment of Lunar Command, designed for control and precision, would now become the crucible for humanity's greatest paradigm shift. The true struggle would be not in understanding the data itself, but in getting humanity to accept and integrate its profound, challenging truths. The quiet hum of the command center now seemed to resonate not just with machinery, but with the silent, immense weight of an unfolding cosmic revelation.
