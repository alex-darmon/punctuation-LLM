Leo and Sam had spent the better part of the morning questioning old Mr. Henderson, the groundskeeper at the abandoned textile mill. His stories of the "Whispering Wall" were legendary in their small town, but always dismissed as local folklore. Now, after a peculiar incident involving a missing drone, they were reconsidering.

"So you say you've heard it, Mr. Henderson?" Leo asked, scribbling in a small notebook.

"Heard it, boy, and seen it," the old man rasped, spitting tobacco juice onto the dusty path. "Not seen *it* directly, mind you, but seen the light. And heard the whispers, clear as a bell."

"How many times have you witnessed this?" Leo pressed.

"Oh, a dozen times maybe, over the years. Always the same. Nobody around, just the light and the whispers." Henderson squinted at them. "You boys ain't thinkin' of messin' with it, are you?"

"Just curious," Sam said quickly. "About our drone. It went down somewhere near there."

Henderson chuckled, a dry, rattling sound. "Wouldn't surprise me if the Wall took it. It's a hungry thing, that wall."

Leo and Sam exchanged glances. Henderson's description was vague, yet consistent. The drone's last signal had indeed come from the vicinity of the old mill's eastern wall, a crumbling brick structure that had stood for over a century.

"Did the light ever move?" Leo asked.

"Sometimes. Like a spotlight, sweepin' across the brick. But then it would just hold steady. And that's when the whispers got loudest."

"And you never saw anyone setting up equipment, or anything like that?" Sam asked.

"Son, if I saw anything like that, I'd have called the sheriff," Henderson said, affronted. "Don't take me for a fool. I know what's natural and what ain't. This ain't natural."

After thanking Mr. Henderson, the boys walked back towards town, their minds buzzing. "He's certain there's no human agency," Sam commented.

"He's certain *he* hasn't seen any human agency," Leo corrected. "Big difference. A projector could explain the light. And the whispers could be audio."

"But who would do it?" Sam pondered. "And why? For years?"

"That's the question," Leo agreed. "And more importantly, *how*? Where's the power source? Where do they hide the equipment?"

They decided their best course of action was to stake out the area themselves. They had brought binoculars, a flashlight, and a small audio recorder. Insects buzzed around them, a constant annoyance.

"He has to," Leo replied. "This is prime time, according to Henderson. Besides, we need to know what happened to the drone."

A flash of green light erupted behind them, followed by another explosion of glass and a wet, slithering sound that indicated another one of Finch's creations had been freed. The ground trembled again. This place was truly falling apart.

The creature emerged from the shadows, its massive, serpentine body slithering between the pods, its glowing eyes fixed on them. The first volley of fire erupted from the lead drones. Not projectiles, but concentrated beams of crimson light that lanced across the short distance, superheating the air. Sarah didn't flinch. The crimson beams hit the shimmering field around her, dissipating into showers of sparks, like static electricity on a grand scale. The force of the impact pushed her back a step, but the field held. It was absorbing the energy, redirecting it, somehow. Her eyes glowed even brighter, reflecting the onslaught.

“They are adapting,” she said, gritting her teeth. The effort was immense. “They are attempting to overwhelm the field. They are trying to find a frequency to bypass it.”

But nothing like this. This was a living, breathing shield, powered by Sarah’s own essence. It was beautiful and terrifying.
