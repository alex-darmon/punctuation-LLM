She spread the papers out on the polished mahogany table, her finger tracing columns of numbers, figures that represented her dwindling fortune.

He looked at the reports, his heart aching. He knew these figures, had seen them months ago, had even made suggestions to Clara's advisor, guided by Julian's subtle instructions, to retain certain holdings or to sell others at specific, disadvantageous moments. He was a direct accomplice.

"It does seem... unusually severe," he said, feigning a thoughtful frown, carefully choosing words that would reinforce her suspicions without revealing Julian's hand. "Market downturns can be brutal, of course. But for so many diverse investments to suffer such similar fates... it is indeed curious."

"Curious? Or deliberate?" she asked, her eyes sharp, looking at him intently. "Could someone be... manipulating the market to target me?"

His blood ran cold. She was dangerously close to the truth. He had to deflect. "Manipulation of the market is a complex and often impossible thing to prove, Clara. Only the most powerful, most unscrupulous individuals could even attempt such a feat. And why you?" He added the last question, trying to sound genuinely perplexed.

She sighed, running a hand through her hair. "I don't know, Thomas. I honestly don't. But everything seems to be conspiring against me lately. The garden, the whispers about the orphanage, and now this. It feels... personal." She looked up at him, a desperate plea in her eyes. "What do I do, Thomas? The orphanage relies on my contributions. Without them..." Her voice trailed off, fear clouding her face.

He resisted the urge to reach out and comfort her. Julian's instructions echoed in his mind: *offer comfort, sympathy, false hope*. "You must be cautious, Clara," he advised, his voice grave. "Perhaps consolidate your remaining assets. Be very careful with any new investments. And certainly, do not speak of your suspicions to anyone. Such accusations, without proof, could cause further damage to your reputation." He delivered Julian's veiled warning, disguised as protective advice.

She nodded slowly, her gaze distant, lost in thought. "You're right, of course. I must be careful. But I cannot simply let this continue. I will not. There has to be a way to fight back."

He watched her, a mix of admiration and dread warring within him. Her resilience, her refusal to break, was both inspiring and terrifying. Julian would not tolerate such defiance. This meant the next blow would be even harder, more direct. And Thomas, the man who was supposed to be her shield, would be forced to wield the sword himself. He was sinking deeper into the mire, and the threads of Julian’s web were tightening, drawing him irrevocably towards a confrontation he desperately wished to avoid. The house, once a sanctuary, now felt like a gilded cage, and Thomas, Julian’s unwilling prisoner, knew that Clara, too, was slowly but surely becoming entrapped within its walls. The game was far from over; it was merely entering its most brutal phase.
