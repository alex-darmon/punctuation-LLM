At least then, the suffering would cease, and the insistent demands of Barnaby would finally be silenced.

He finished the current branch, pushing it mechanically onto the growing pile with a dull thud. "Another, Pip!" For the aerial spheroid threats do not pause for tea breaks or leisurely contemplation of sunsets! They are relentless! They are unyielding! They are, above all, opportunists, ever seeking the slightest vulnerability in our defenses!"

Pip sighed, a dry, raspy sound that barely disturbed the night air, a sound more akin to the rustle of dead leaves than a living creature's exhalation. He reached for another branch, his paws clumsy and slow, their movements lacking any of their usual grace. This one was thicker, knottier, its surface more resistant to the flaker's bite. A fresh wave of despair washed over him, chilling him more deeply than the night air. Each unique imperfection of the wood presented a new challenge, a new opportunity for Barnaby's eagle eyed scrutiny, a new reason for Pip to falter and be reprimanded. He pressed the flaker against the resistant grain, feeling the shudder run up his arm, a jolt that went through his entire fatigued body. It was like trying to carve stone with a blunt stick, an exercise in futility. The wood groaned, a faint, almost inaudible protest, mimicking Pip's own internal anguish, but refused to yield its stubborn form.

"Firmness, Pip! But not brute force! There is a delicate balance, a subtle interplay of pressure and angle! The art of chamfering is not merely a test of strength, but a testament to finesse! Like the intricate dance of the lunar cycles, or the precise migratory patterns of the nectar seeking moths as they navigate by starlight! It requires understanding of the material, an almost intuitive communion with the very essence of the branch, a silent dialogue between creator and created!" Barnaby's lecture continued, weaving its way through celestial mechanics and entomological phenomena, all to impress upon Pip the critical importance of a clean cut, a perfect chamfer.

Pip grit his teeth, an unconscious reaction to the physical pain and the relentless barrage of Barnaby's rhetoric, a futile attempt to hold back the tide of his mounting frustration. He shifted his weight, trying a different angle, a different pressure. Finally, with a small, reluctant groan, a sliver of wood detached itself, leaving behind a rough, uneven edge, a testament to his failing concentration. He knew instantly it was wrong, a glaring imperfection. Barnaby, too, seemed to sense it, his keen vision detecting the flaw even in the dim light. He materialized at Pip's side, his long claw pointing directly at the offending cut. "Irregularity! A jagged edge! This is not a chamfer, Pip, it is an atrocity! A gaping vulnerability! Imagine the wind resistance! The catastrophic turbulence! The aerodynamic nightmare this would present to our defensive structure! It would be like inviting the aerial spheroid threats to a grand feast of vulnerability, laying out a banquet of our strategic weaknesses for them to exploit!"

Pip said nothing. He couldn't. His throat felt parched, his tongue thick and unresponsive, a dead weight in his mouth. He merely stared at the offending cut, then at Barnaby's exasperated face, which seemed to glow with a righteous indignation, then back at the wood. His mind was a blank slate, wiped clean of all capacity for explanation or apology, of all will to defend himself. All he felt was a profound, aching emptiness, a void where his spirit used to be.
