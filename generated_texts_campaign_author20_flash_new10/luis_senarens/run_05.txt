The thrumming of Thorne's massive ship, now crippled, was a constant, mocking reminder of the chaos he had caused. He was far from safe, but he was alive, and Thorne's plans were in ruins. The maintenance tunnels were a stark contrast to the relatively clean, if utilitarian, corridors he had navigated previously. These were ancient, predating Thorne's acquisition of the vessel, layered with decades of grime, forgotten spills, and the faint, acrid tang of ozone and decaying lubricants. The air was thick, still, and significantly colder than the power room, a damp chill seeping into his bones through the environmental suit. Kael's holographic projection, a flickering, almost translucent blue beside him, seemed to absorb what little light escaped from Vance's helmet lamp.

"Watch your step, Vance. These conduits are unstable." A structural integrity warning flashed past Kael's ethereal form. "The entire section has been flagged for demolition for cycles, but Thorne's budget allocations prioritize... other endeavors."

Vance grunted, ducking under a cluster of thick, insulated pipes that wept condensation onto his shoulders. His injured arm throbbed, a dull, persistent ache that grew sharper with every strained movement. The adrenaline that had propelled him through the thruster array was slowly ebbing, replaced by a profound exhaustion that threatened to drag him down. He ignored it, focusing instead on Kael's precise, disembodied voice.

"Right turn here. Ahead, a pressure valve, release it manually. It's stiff."

The metal groaned, protesting, before finally giving way with a screech that echoed eerily through the confined space. A gust of stale, recycled air blew past him, carrying with it the smell of stagnant water and something vaguely metallic. The air in these tunnels felt dead, suffocating.

Kael's projection flickered, and a map of the immediate vicinity materialized, highlighting several moving red blips. "Two patrols are converging. We need to move quickly through this accessway."

The suit’s display, projected inside his helmet, showed his velocity slowly increasing, his direction vector aligning with Kael’s target coordinates. He was moving, purposefully, towards something hidden in the void.

The minutes ticked by, each one stretching into an eternity. He kept glancing back at Thorne’s ship, half expecting to see lights detach from its hull, growing larger as they hurtled towards him. The silence, broken only by Kael’s calm directives and the hiss of his own breath, was almost unbearable. It amplified every creak of his suit, every beat of his heart.

Thorne’s vessel has altered course, Kael announced suddenly, a subtle shift in his tone, a fraction less calm. It is decelerating. Detecting launch signatures. Multiple. They are deploying the drones. Hold your course, Vance. Do not deviate. Stealth is now our primary defence.

A cold knot formed in Vance’s stomach. They knew. Or at least, Thorne suspected. He imagined Thorne’s cold, calculating eyes, perhaps watching a sensor screen, seeing a tiny anomaly where Vance floated. The thought spurred him, injecting adrenaline into his weary limbs. He forced himself to concentrate even harder on Kael’s instructions, keeping his movements minimal, precise.

Drones are active. They are searching, Kael continued. They operate on passive scanners primarily, detecting minute gravimetric distortions and reflected energy signatures. Your current trajectory and signature should be below their threshold, provided you make no further propellant expenditures.

So I just… drift towards this drone? Vance asked, his voice strained.

Precisely. Minimal interaction with the environment is key. The cargo drone is now three hundred kilometers distant, closing at relative velocity of seventy five meters per second. Your closure rate is thirty meters per second. Combined, you will intercept in two minutes, twenty seconds. Prepare for docking procedures. This will be manual, but the drone has a grappler arm designed for automated cargo transfer. You will need to guide yourself into its capture zone.

Vance swallowed hard. Manual docking in the vastness of space, with patrol drones actively hunting him. This was not in his job description. Not even close. He felt a tremor run through him, not of cold, but of sheer terror. He looked at his gloved hand, flexing his fingers. He had to trust Kael. He had to trust himself.

As the two minute mark approached, Kael’s voice became more urgent, though still controlled. Drone proximity. One hundred kilometers. Visual acquisition imminent. Look for a faint, dark silhouette against the starfield. It is designed to absorb most ambient light.

Vance strained his eyes, peering into the blackness. The starfield was still breathtaking, a diamond dust tapestry, but now it felt less like a wonder and more like a backdrop for his impending doom. Then, he saw it. A darker patch, barely discernible, a smudge against the shimmering backdrop. It was small, no bigger than an old terrestrial shipping container, but elongated, with faint, almost imperceptible thruster glows at one end.

Contact, Vance breathed.

Affirmative. Its grappler arm is extending. Target trajectory locked. Begin your final approach. Use your suit’s maneuvering jets sparingly, only for course correction. The grappler will attempt to match your velocity.

Vance focused on the drone. It looked like nothing so much as a metallic coffin, hurtling through space. But it was his coffin, or his salvation. He watched the grappler arm, a multi jointed appendage, slowly extend from the drone’s side, its claws open, ready to receive. The drone was rotating gently, aligning itself.

Drones are closing on your last known position, Kael warned, his voice a low hum in Vance’s ear. They are spreading out. One is heading directly towards your current vector, though it is still beyond its effective scanning range. You have approximately sixty seconds before it might detect you. Execute the approach with precision.

Vance felt a surge of adrenaline, cold and sharp. Sixty seconds. He could see the drone clearly now, its dull, black surface reflecting almost no light. He aimed himself for the waiting grappler, feeling the immense pull of its targeting system. A faint green outline appeared in his HUD, showing him the capture zone.

He fired a tiny burst of thrusters, adjusting his angle, then another, a miniscule nudge. He was still tumbling slightly, a consequence of the ejection, and he needed to stabilize. He was close enough now to see individual panels on the drone’s hull. The grappler arm was massive, designed to handle large cargo, and it seemed to loom over him like a giant metallic spider.

Forty seconds, Kael said.

Vance gritted his teeth. He was almost there. He could see the opening on the drone’s side, a rectangular hatch, waiting. He extended his arms, positioning himself.

Twenty seconds. Drone detection signature increasing. It is within range, Kael’s voice cut through the tension.

Vance ignored it. He was a few meters from the grappler. He held his breath, guiding himself with minute movements of his torso. The grappler’s claws twitched, ready to close. He was almost there, almost in the capture zone.

Then, a sudden jolt. The grappler arm shot out, not gently, but with a surprising force, snapping around his torso. The metal claws closed, not painfully, but firmly, securing him against the drone’s hull. Vance gasped, the sudden impact rattling his teeth.

Grapple successful! Kael exclaimed, a hint of relief in his voice. Retracting arm. Prepare for ingress.
