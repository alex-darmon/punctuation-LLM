Cryptic marginalia. Aris recalled Finch’s propensity for scribbling notes in the margins of almost every book he owned, not just his own journals. Two meters high, one meter wide, one meter deep. That was exactly the dimensions of his hiding spot. Aris felt a wave of nausea. They knew. They knew the space was there. They just did not know he was in it. Not yet.

"Empty," the higher voice announced, a flicker of disappointment in his voice. "Just a small, irregular cavity. No obvious contents. Dust, cobwebs, some rat droppings."

Aris held his breath, his muscles tensed, ready to bolt, to fight, to simply disintegrate. He felt a moment of dizzying relief. They had not seen him. He had been behind a small support beam, or a particularly dark shadow, his dark clothing blending with the grime. He was safe, for a moment. But the deeper voice was not so easily dissuaded.

A different hum, lower and more resonant, filled the air. Aris knew about thermal imagers. They picked up body heat, even through layers of clothing or light concealment. His heart plummeted. This was it. There was no escape from this. His carefully constructed invisibility was about to shatter.

"Beta," the higher voice said, his voice now hushed, laced with a mixture of surprise and something else, something akin to morbid fascination. "There is a thermal signature. Small. But distinct. In the far corner, behind the old pipe conduit."

The deeper voice made a low sound, a wordless grunt of confirmation or acknowledgment. One of them held a small, angular device that cast the revealing beam. The other, presumably the deeper voice, stood slightly behind him, his posture radiating authority.

Aris remained silent, his mind racing, desperately trying to formulate a plan, an excuse, a plea. But no words came, only the frantic drumming of his pulse against his eardrums. He looked at the two figures, trying to gauge their intentions, to read something in their impassive stances.

The higher voice, who Aris now saw was taller and leaner than his companion, took a step forward, the light beam unwavering. Aris’s mind raced. They had analyzed his scent? Detected disturbed dust patterns? Their level of forensic detail was terrifying. He was dealing with professionals of the highest caliber, an organization with resources beyond his comprehension. There was no point in denying his presence, or his prior activity.

"I took nothing," Aris lied, a desperate gamble. His hand still rested protectively on the journal in his pocket, a silent testament to his deception. "I merely… explored. Aris felt a knot of fear tighten in his stomach. He looked from one man to the other, seeing only cold, implacable resolve. He was trapped. Aris felt a chill that had nothing to do with the cold air. The deeper voice’s words resonated with a terrifying truth, a confirmation of his own gnawing fears about Finch’s work. A cup that inexplicably fell from a stable shelf. A faint, almost imperceptible hum that would occasionally permeate the air, a sound he had attributed to faulty wiring or distant machinery. He had dismissed them as quirks, anomalies, scientific curiosities. Now, he understood. These were the faint ripples of Finch’s terrifying experiments, the tremors of a reality slowly being warped.
