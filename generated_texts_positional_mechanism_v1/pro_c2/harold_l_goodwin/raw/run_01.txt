Rick took a small pencil flashlight from his pocket, the one the man had used on them, and switched it on. The thin beam cut through the darkness, revealing a scene of chaos in the hotel's lobby. Chairs were overturned. A table was on its side. In the center of the room, the dusty floorboards were scuffed and scarred, as though something heavy had been dragged across them.

"They weren't here for a quiet meeting," Scotty observed.

"They were searching for something," Rick agreed. He played the light around the room. It fell on a section of the floor near the far wall. "Look."

He pointed the beam at a floorboard that was pried up at one end. It looked like someone had tried to lift it and given up. But next to it, another board was slightly askew, as if it had been put back in a hurry.

Rick knelt down and worked his fingers under the edge. It came up easily. He shined the light into the dark cavity below.

"What is it?" Scotty crowded next to him.

The light revealed a small, dark gray box. It was made of metal and had no visible handle or lock, just smooth, bolted sides. It looked incredibly dense and solid.

"I don't know," Rick breathed. He reached in and tried to lift it. It was surprisingly heavy, far heavier than its small size suggested. He grunted with the effort, getting a better grip with both hands, and hauled it out onto the floor. It landed with a solid, leaden thud.

"Lead-lined," Scotty guessed, tapping it with his knuckle. "To block radiation? Or maybe a signal?"

"Whatever it is, they must have been after it," Rick said. "Maybe they thought they had it, or couldn't find it and had to leave on schedule." He looked at the box, then at Scotty. "We can't leave this here."

Scotty nodded. "We take it with us."

"It's going to be a long walk," Rick said, looking at the heavy object.

"Then we'd better get started," Scotty replied.

They figured out a way to carry the box between them, each gripping an edge. It was awkward and heavy, and it slowed them down considerably. They left the hotel and started out of the town, not toward the road, but directly across the open desert. It was a longer route to the base, but it was safer. They didn't want to be seen.

The trek was grueling. The moon provided just enough light to see the menacing shapes of cholla cactus and jagged rocks. Every shadow seemed to hold a threat. The box grew heavier with every step, its edges digging into their fingers. They had to stop frequently to rest and switch their grip.

"My arms are about to fall off," Scotty gasped during one of their breaks. He sat on a rock, flexing his aching fingers.

"Mine too," Rick admitted. He looked back the way they had come. The ghost town was just a collection of dark smudges on the horizon. "Do you think they'll come back for it?"

"If they realize they missed it, you bet they will," Scotty said. "Let's keep moving."

They had been walking for what felt like hours when Rick suddenly stopped, pulling Scotty to a halt with him. "Listen."

At first, there was only the wind. Then Rick heard it again, a low, distant hum. The sound of a car engine. And it was getting closer.

"Headlights!" Scotty hissed, pointing to their left.

Far across the desert, two beams of light were sweeping across the landscape, moving slowly along the dirt road that led from the main highway to the ghost town.

"Down!" Rick ordered.

They dropped the box and flattened themselves behind a thick clump of creosote bush, pulling the box in after them. They lay motionless, their hearts pounding. The car was moving slowly, as if searching. It was the sedan. Rick was sure of it.

The headlights grew brighter, illuminating the desert in a stark, white glare. The beams swept past their hiding place, not ten yards away. Rick held his breath, pressing his face into the dirt. He could hear the crunch of the tires on the gravel road. The car passed their position and continued on toward the town.

They waited until the red taillights had vanished completely.

"That was too close," Scotty whispered, his voice shaky. "They came back."

"Which means this box is even more important than we thought," Rick said, getting to his feet. He felt a new sense of urgency. "And they'll be coming back this way when they don't find it. We have to move, now."

They picked up their heavy burden and pushed on, moving faster now, fueled by adrenaline. The terrain grew rougher, and they stumbled often in the darkness. Rick's thirst was a dry fire in his throat.

"Next time we go exploring," Scotty panted, "remind me to pack a canteen. And maybe an armored car."

Rick managed a weak grin. "It's a deal."

They walked in silence for a long time after that, conserving their energy. The landscape was a monotonous blur of rock and sand and shadow. Just when Rick felt he couldn't take another step, he saw it. A faint, steady glow on the distant horizon.

"Look," he said, his voice hoarse. "Lights."

It was the base. It was still miles away, but it was there. A beacon of safety in the vast, dark desert. The sight gave them a fresh burst of strength. They pushed on, their eyes fixed on the distant glow.

The last mile was the worst. Their legs were numb, their arms screamed in protest, and the box seemed to be filled with lead anchors. But they kept going, step after painful step, until they were stumbling up the embankment to the paved perimeter road of the base.
