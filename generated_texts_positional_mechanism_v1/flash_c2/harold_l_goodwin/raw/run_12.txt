The pictures were grainy, as expected, but they clearly showed figures in the gully, a generator, and, most importantly, the unmistakable, otherworldly glow of the Viridium. One photograph, though blurred by the low light, even captured the outline of the Frostola man's distinctive profile.

With the developed photographs carefully secured, they waited for a reasonable hour to contact Sheriff Thompson. The decision weighed heavily on them. They were about to step into a world far more complex and dangerous than chasing a projected ghost. They were about to challenge a grown man, a pillar of the community, who was engaged in serious, illegal activities. The consequences could be unpredictable, even perilous. But they knew they couldn't turn back now. The truth, glowing green in the hidden depths of Mason's Hill, demanded to be revealed.

The afternoon sun slanted across Rick's room, throwing long shadows that danced with the dust motes in the air. Scotty sat on the edge of the bed, nervously tapping his foot. Rick carefully placed the stack of developed photographs into a large envelope, his hands steady despite the tremor in his stomach. The Viridium glowed faintly in the pictures, an eerie green that seemed to defy the ordinary world. The indistinct figures, the crude generator, the shadowy depth of the gully—it was all there, tangible evidence of their wild theories.

"Ready?" Scotty asked, his voice a little tight.

Rick took a deep breath. "As ready as we'll ever be. Remember, we stick to the facts. No ghosts, no spooks, just illegal mining and potential danger."

"Got it," Scotty affirmed, though his eyes darted to the window as if expecting a blue glow to materialize outside.

They walked the mile and a half to the sheriff's office in town, the envelope of photographs feeling heavy in Rick's hand. The usual afternoon bustle of Harmony Creek seemed muffled, distant. Every passing car, every person they saw, felt like a potential observer, a subtle reminder of the secret they carried. Rick found himself scrutinizing faces, wondering if any of them knew about the goings-on at Mason's Hill, if any were connected to Collins or the Frostola man. The thought made his jaw tighten.

Sheriff Thompson’s office was in the old stone courthouse, a solid, dependable building that exuded an air of quiet authority. Inside, the air was cool and smelled faintly of old paper and furniture polish. Mrs. Henderson, the sheriff’s secretary, looked up from her typing, her spectacles perched on her nose.

"Well, Rick and Scotty, what brings you boys here on such a fine afternoon?" she asked, her voice kindly.

"We need to speak with Sheriff Thompson, Mrs. Henderson," Rick said, trying to sound as adult and serious as possible. "It's important."

Mrs. Henderson gave them a once-over, noting the solemn expressions on their young faces. "He's just finishing up with Deputy Barnes. Go on in, I'll tell him you're here."

They exchanged a quick glance of encouragement and pushed open the heavy oak door. Sheriff Thompson, a man whose broad shoulders seemed to fill his uniform, was leaning back in his chair, a half-empty coffee mug on his desk. Deputy Barnes, younger and leaner, stood by the window. Both turned as the boys entered.

"Rick, Scotty," Sheriff Thompson said, a friendly smile creasing his weathered face. "To what do I owe the pleasure? Not in trouble, are we?" He winked, but there was an underlying current of official curiosity.

"No, sir," Rick replied, stepping forward. "Not in trouble, exactly. We have something important to discuss with you. Something serious."

The sheriff’s smile softened a little, replaced by a more attentive look. He waved Deputy Barnes out with a subtle nod, indicating privacy was needed. Deputy Barnes gave the boys a brief, unreadable look and exited, closing the door behind him.

"Alright, boys, sit down," Sheriff Thompson said, gesturing to the two chairs in front of his desk. "Serious, you say? What's on your minds?"

Rick placed the envelope on the desk, not opening it yet. "Sheriff Thompson, we've been investigating the 'Blue Ghost' up at Mason's Hill."

The sheriff’s eyebrows rose slightly. "Oh? Still on that, are we? Thought you boys had that sorted out. A projector, wasn't it?" He chuckled, a deep rumble in his chest.

"Yes, sir, a projector," Rick confirmed. "But it's much more than just a ghost. We believe it's a cover for an illegal mining operation."

The sheriff’s amusement faded. He leaned forward, his gaze sharper. "Illegal mining? Where did you get an idea like that?"

"We've seen it, sir," Scotty interjected, feeling a surge of bravery. "Men working in the gully at night, under the cover of the ghost. And we have photographs."

Rick unsealed the envelope and carefully laid out the pictures on the sheriff's desk. He picked up the one showing the distinct green glow. "This, sir, is what they're mining. We believe it's Viridium, a rare and valuable mineral, possibly even radioactive." He remembered fragments from Dr. Thorne's letter, trying to recall the exact terminology. "Dr. Thorne, the geologist from the university, mentioned it in a letter to Mr. Miller at the historical society. He was looking for geological formations that might indicate its presence in this area."

Sheriff Thompson picked up the photograph, his brow furrowing as he studied the eerie green light. He then looked at the blurry but discernible figures, the generator. The smile was completely gone now, replaced by a deep concern. "Viridium, you say? Radioactive? Boys, this is quite a claim."

"We saw them hauling it out, sir," Rick continued, emboldened by the sheriff's change in demeanor. "They use a cart or wagon, just like Mr. Belsely, the tenant farmer, described. He thought they were ghosts collecting the dead, but we now think they were collecting the mineral. The 'dark shadows' he saw were probably the miners, and the 'Blue Ghost' himself is actually Jethro Collins, the real estate agent."

The sheriff slowly put the photograph down. His eyes narrowed. "Jethro Collins? Are you sure about that?"

"We've seen him at the mine, associated with the ghost's appearances," Rick stated. "And the Frostola man, the ice-cream vendor who rents from Collins, he’s involved too. He was at the site multiple times. Mr. Belsely saw him there, and we recognized him in one of our photos."

Scotty pointed to a slightly blurred image. "That's him, sir. His profile is quite distinct. He drives a scooter."

Sheriff Thompson picked up the photo with the Frostola man. He looked at it, then at the other images of the mining activity. His gaze remained steady, but a thoughtful silence filled the room. The implication of a respected local businessman like Collins being involved in such an operation was clearly weighing on him.

"Mr. Belsely told us he saw the Blue Ghost alone only once, the night we chased it," Rick added. "Every other time, when they were 'searching the fields,' he said, the ghost had men with him. We believe those were the mining operations, and the ghost was acting as a lookout or a deterrent. The ghost's appearance at the Sons of the Old Dominion feast was likely a distraction from the real work happening in the gully. He uses the superstitions of the town to keep people away."

"And the mine hill, where your plane was? That's where some of the sightings were?" the sheriff asked, connecting the pieces.

"Yes, sir," Scotty confirmed. "And the cornfield on top of the hill. They were looking for something, Belsely said. We think they were prospecting, looking for more veins of the mineral."

Sheriff Thompson leaned back in his chair, folding his hands across his chest. He looked from the photographs to Rick, then to Scotty. "Boys, this is a serious accusation. If what you're saying is true, we're not just talking about some boys chasing a prankster. We're talking about grand larceny, potential environmental hazards, and maybe even endangerment if this Viridium is as dangerous as you suggest."

"We understand, sir," Rick said. "That's why we came to you. We thought you'd take us seriously, and we didn't know who else to trust with something like this."

The sheriff nodded slowly. "You were right to come to me. These photos, while a bit grainy, certainly show… something. And your testimony, combined with Mr. Belsely's observations, paints a troubling picture. Viridium, though. I've heard the name before, usually in scientific journals, never in Harmony Creek. How do you know about it?"

Rick explained about Dr. Thorne's letter, how they had found it at the historical society, and how it had detailed the properties and rarity of the mineral. He recounted their own observations of the glowing material in the gully.

"So you actually saw the mineral itself, glowing green," the sheriff clarified.

"Yes, sir," Rick replied. "And we almost touched it. It gave off a strange heat and a faint, almost metallic smell."

Sheriff Thompson stood up and walked to the window, looking out at the quiet street. His back was to them, but they could sense the weight of his thoughts. The silence stretched, thick with unspoken possibilities.

Finally, he turned. His expression was grave. "Alright, boys. This is what we're going to do. I need to verify some of this. First, I'll contact Dr. Thorne discreetly. He's a reputable scientist, and if he confirms the geological potential for Viridium here, that gives us a strong lead. Second, I'll look into Mr. Collins and the Frostola man more closely. Not overtly, just background checks, see if anything unusual comes up. Third, and this is important, I need you boys to stay away from Mason's Hill. Completely. Do you understand?"

Rick and Scotty exchanged a look. They understood the necessity of it, but the thought of simply waiting felt like a betrayal of their own efforts.

"Yes, sir," Rick said, though he knew keeping that promise would be difficult.

"This is not a game anymore," Sheriff Thompson continued, his voice firm. "If Collins is indeed involved in something like this, he's not just a prankster. He's a criminal, potentially a dangerous one. He's clearly gone to great lengths to keep this secret. If he suspects anyone is onto him, he could react unpredictably. I don't want you boys putting yourselves in harm's way."

"We understand the danger, sir," Scotty said. "We've thought about that."

"Good," the sheriff replied, though he didn't look entirely convinced. "For now, you've given me enough to go on. These photographs are crucial. I'll keep them safe. I'll need you to come in and give formal statements at some point, but let's take this one step at a time. I need to build a case that holds up."

He picked up the phone. "Mrs. Henderson, hold all my calls for a few minutes, please. And then see if you can get me Dr. Alistair Thorne at the university. Extension seven-two-four, if I recall." He hung up and looked at the boys again. "One more thing. Has anyone else seen these mining operations? Besides Belsely?"

"Not that we know of, sir," Rick answered. "Belsely mentioned others seeing 'ghosts' in the fields, but his interpretation was that they were spirits. He didn't connect them to mining."

"So, as far as we know, you two and Belsely are the only witnesses to the actual activity," the sheriff mused. "That makes your testimony even more vital, but also potentially more challenging to corroborate without direct evidence." He tapped his fingers on the desk. "I'll also pay Mr. Belsely a visit, casually, see what he has to say when I ask some pointed questions without giving too much away."

He stood up, signaling the end of the meeting. "Thank you, boys. You've brought a very serious matter to my attention. I'll be in touch."

As they walked out of the sheriff's office, the setting sun cast long shadows across the town square. The previous lightness in the air was gone, replaced by a sense of foreboding. They had done what they set out to do, but the relief was mixed with a heavy apprehension. The problem was now out of their hands, yet somehow felt more personal, more dangerous.

"Well," Scotty said, breaking the silence, "that went better than I expected. He believed us."

"He believed the evidence," Rick corrected. "And he took it seriously. But he also told us to stay away. That's going to be hard."

"You think he'll really handle it?" Scotty asked, a hint of doubt in his voice.

"He's Sheriff Thompson," Rick said, a note of conviction in his tone. "He's a good man. He won't let illegal activity like this stand, especially if it involves something dangerous like Viridium. But it won't be quick. Collins is a local figure, and he's clearly cunning."

They continued their walk home in silence, each lost in thought. Rick pictured Collins, the smooth real estate agent, now transformed in his mind into a shadowy figure orchestrating a dangerous, illegal enterprise. The quiet streets of Harmony Creek suddenly seemed to hold more secrets, more hidden dangers than he had ever imagined. The comfortable world of summer adventures had irrevocably shifted. They were no longer just boys chasing a ghost; they were embroiled in a criminal investigation.

The next few days passed with agonizing slowness. Rick and Scotty tried to act normally, playing baseball, reading comics, but their minds constantly drifted to Mason's Hill. Every time a car slowed near Rick's house, they wondered if it was the sheriff, or worse, someone connected to Collins. They adhered, somewhat, to Sheriff Thompson's instruction to stay away from the mine, but their curiosity was a constant, nagging itch. They discussed contingency plans, what they would do if the sheriff found nothing, or if Collins somehow discovered their involvement. The thought made them both nervous.

One afternoon, a week after their visit to the sheriff, Rick's phone rang. It was Sheriff Thompson.

"Rick, can you and Scotty come down to the office? I have some news." The sheriff's voice was calm, but Rick detected an undercurrent of seriousness.

"Yes, sir, we'll be right there," Rick said, his heart beginning to pound. He immediately relayed the message to Scotty, who was helping his dad in the garden. They quickly made their way back to town, a mix of excitement and dread churning in their stomachs.

Mrs. Henderson greeted them with a less cheerful demeanor than usual. "The sheriff is waiting for you, boys. Go right in."

Sheriff Thompson was at his desk, a file open before him. He looked up as they entered, his expression unsmiling. "Sit down, boys. I've been busy since our last talk."

He paused, collecting his thoughts. "First, I contacted Dr. Thorne. He was quite interested in the possibility of Viridium in the area. He confirmed that the geological conditions around Mason's Hill align with known deposits of the mineral. He also expressed significant concern about its potential radioactivity and the dangers of improper extraction. He even offered to come down and survey the site, under official escort, of course, if we had reason to believe it was there."

Rick felt a surge of validation. Dr. Thorne's confirmation was a huge step.

"Second," the sheriff continued, his voice growing more serious, "I've been looking into Jethro Collins. He's been buying up a lot of land around Mason's Hill over the past year, more than his usual real estate dealings would suggest. He acquired the original mine property from a distant relative of the Mason family just six months ago, using a shell corporation registered out of state. That raises a red flag right there. And the Frostola man, a Mr. Silas Croft, has a record. Minor stuff, mostly petty theft and some grifting in other towns, but enough to show a pattern of shady dealings. He's been living in Harmony Creek for less than a year, renting from Collins."

"So he’s involved," Scotty whispered, a sense of grim satisfaction in his voice.

"It certainly looks that way," Sheriff Thompson agreed. "And here's the most troubling part. I paid Mr. Belsely a visit, just a friendly chat about local matters. I steered the conversation to the ghost and the strange activities he'd seen. He recounted everything you told me, about the figures, the wagon, the Blue Thing. Then, I casually asked him if he'd ever noticed anything odd about the land he was farming, any unusual soil or rocks. He mentioned that in the gully area, where your plane landed, the soil had started to look... different. A bit discolored, sometimes with a faint sheen, especially after a rain. And once, he said, he'd accidentally broken a piece of rock there with his plow and noticed it had a peculiar greenish tint inside, almost like the color of emeralds, but duller. He thought nothing of it at the time, just odd rock. He said he'd seen a few of those pieces lying around that gully at different times."

Rick and Scotty stared at each other. Belsely had unwittingly confirmed their Viridium sighting.

"That's enough," Sheriff Thompson said, closing the file with a decisive thud. "That's enough for me to act. We have corroborating testimony, the geological possibility, the suspicious behavior of Collins, and now Mr. Belsely's unwitting description of the mineral itself. This is no longer just a theory."

"What are you going to do, sir?" Rick asked, leaning forward eagerly.

"I can't just raid the place based on a farmer's casual observation and some grainy photos, not for something this serious," the sheriff explained. "Especially if radioactivity is involved. That brings in federal agencies. But I can request a warrant to investigate the property for illegal mining activity and potential environmental hazards. Given Collins's recent land acquisitions and the nature of the potential find, I should be able to get it. And I'll be calling in Dr. Thorne to accompany us, along with some specialists for hazardous materials, just in case."

"When will you go?" Scotty asked, his eyes wide.

"I'll work on getting the warrant first thing tomorrow morning," Sheriff Thompson said. "These things take time, but given the potential danger, I'll push for an expedited process. I aim to be up there with a team within the next two or three days, under the cover of night, when they're most likely to be active. That way, we can catch them in the act."

He looked at them sternly. "And I want you two nowhere near that hill when we go in. Do you understand? This could be dangerous, boys. Very dangerous. We don't know what Collins and Croft might do if they're cornered."

Rick and Scotty nodded, the thrill of their success tempered by the stark warning. The sheriff was clearly serious. The game was truly over, and the real stakes were higher than they had ever imagined. The wait would be excruciating, but the wheels of justice were finally turning. The Blue Ghost’s reign was about to come to a very real, very human end.

The next three days stretched before Rick and Scotty like an endless, sun-baked road. Every minute seemed to drag, every hour an eternity. Sheriff Thompson’s warning echoed in their ears, a stern, unwavering command that grated against their natural inclination to be in the thick of things. “Nowhere near that hill when we go in,” he had said, and the words were a barbed wire fence around their burgeoning excitement. Yet, despite the prohibition, an unspoken understanding passed between them. They were Rick and Scotty, the sleuths of the Sons of the Old Dominion. Staying completely out of it was simply not in their nature.

Their usual haunts, the dusty pathways, the creek banks, even the old swimming hole, offered no solace. Every conversation turned, inevitably, to the mine hill. They replayed every detail of Belsely’s testimony, every nuance of Collins’s evasiveness. They discussed Dr. Thorne’s probable role, imagining the learned professor, armed with scientific instruments, descending upon the Viridium lode. The thought of hazardous materials specialists, men in strange suits, added a layer of grave reality to their adventure, a reality far more potent than any ghostly apparition.

On the first day, they attempted to distract themselves. They tried fishing in the creek, but their lines lay slack, their minds far away. The glint of sunlight on the water, the lazy hum of insects, all seemed dull and inconsequential. Rick found himself constantly glancing towards the distant silhouette of the mine hill, a dark hump on the horizon, imagining the strange greenish glow of the Viridium beneath its surface. Scotty, ever the more practical of the two, tried to map out possible approaches for Sheriff Thompson’s team, tracing imaginary lines through the woods with a stick in the dirt. “They’ll need to come in from the west,” he mused, “the road’s too open on the east, and the gully’s too rough from the south.” Rick nodded, only half listening, his thoughts still caught on the image of Collins and Croft, cornered and dangerous.

The second day brought a fresh wave of restless energy. They rode their bicycles far out into the countryside, ostensibly to explore, but their route invariably led them within sight of the mine hill. They saw no unusual activity, no telltale signs of hurried preparations. The Frostola truck was absent from Collins’s property, a fact Rick noted with a pang of disappointment. He had hoped for some last-minute clue, some slip-up that would confirm their theories beyond all doubt. Instead, an unsettling calm hung over the area, a calm that felt almost sinister. It was the calm before the storm, Rick thought, the tense silence before the breaking of a dam.

They spent hours poring over geological maps that Dr. Thorne had lent them, tracing fault lines and rock formations. The maps, dry and technical as they were, suddenly held a fascinating new meaning. They were not just lines and symbols, but a hidden language, revealing the secrets of the earth beneath their feet. Viridium, the professor had explained, was often found in specific types of igneous rock, in veins or pockets. They marked potential locations on their own crude map, based on Belsely’s descriptions of the gully and the mine shaft. It was a game, but a serious one, with very real stakes.

“What if they move it?” Scotty asked suddenly on the afternoon of the second day, startling Rick from his contemplation of a geological cross-section. “What if they load it all up and drive away before the sheriff gets here?”

Rick frowned. “That’s a possibility, of course. But Thompson said they’d go in at night, when they’re most active. That suggests they’re processing it or digging it then. And where would they take it? They’d need a secure place, probably nearby. Besides,” he added, “Thompson said it was potentially radioactive. You can’t just load radioactive material onto any old truck and drive off. It requires special handling.”

“Unless they don’t know it’s radioactive,” Scotty countered. “Or don’t care.”

That thought was a chilling one. It underscored the potential danger not just to the villains, but to anyone who came into contact with the Viridium. It hardened Rick’s resolve. He had to be there, somehow, even if only to watch. The sheriff’s warning, while well-intentioned, felt like an unacceptable constraint on their duty to see this through.

On the morning of the third day, a quiet determination settled upon them. The wait was almost over. Sheriff Thompson had mentioned “two or three days.” This was the third. Rick woke early, the pre-dawn light barely seeping through his window, and knew that today was the day. He met Scotty by unspoken agreement at their usual rendezvous point behind the old barn. There was no need for words. Their eyes met, a shared understanding passing between them. They would go.

They spent the day in a state of heightened alert, trying to appear normal, trying not to draw attention. They helped Mr. Harrison with some chores, mended a fence, and even practiced their pitching arm in the field, but their focus was elsewhere. Every car that passed on the road, every distant sound, made them jump. They ate dinner in a perfunctory manner, barely tasting their food. As dusk began to settle, painting the western sky in hues of orange and purple, they made their preparations. A flashlight, a compass, a small canteen of water. Nothing to give away their intentions, nothing that would make them look like anything other than two boys out for a late evening walk.

“We’ll go from the north,” Rick whispered as they slipped out of Scotty’s backyard, navigating the familiar paths of the woods behind his house. “It’s the most wooded approach, and we can keep the mine hill between us and Collins’s place.”

Scotty nodded, his face grim in the deepening twilight. The air was still and humid, promising a night full of buzzing insects. The moon, a sliver of silver, offered little light through the dense canopy of leaves. They moved with the stealth of experienced woodsmen, a skill honed by countless games of hide-and-seek and imaginary adventures. Their destination was a small, rocky outcrop on a ridge overlooking the mine hill, a spot they had discovered weeks ago, offering a commanding view without exposing them too much.

As they drew closer, the familiar topography of the mine hill began to emerge from the shadows. The old mine entrance, a gaping black maw in the hillside, seemed to loom larger, more ominous, than usual. They could just make out the outlines of Collins’s house and outbuildings in the distance, dark shapes against the fading sky. No lights were on. Everything was quiet, perhaps too quiet.

An hour passed, then another. The waiting was agony. Every rustle of leaves, every snap of a twig, made them tense. They strained their ears, listening for the sound of approaching vehicles, for the telltale signs of the sheriff’s team. Nothing. Just the sounds of the night. Doubt began to creep in. Had Sheriff Thompson changed his mind? Had he been unable to get the warrant? Or perhaps they were simply too early.

Then, a faint, almost imperceptible hum reached their ears. It was distant, but growing steadily louder. It was the sound of multiple vehicles, not just one. Rick nudged Scotty, his heart pounding a frantic rhythm against his ribs. Scotty pointed with a trembling finger towards the far end of the valley road, where a faint glimmer of headlights briefly pierced the darkness, quickly vanishing behind a bend.

“They’re here,” Scotty whispered, his voice hoarse with excitement.

The vehicles, moving without lights for the final approach, were now little more than dark shapes merging with the shadows. Rick estimated they were at least three or four. He imagined the Sheriff, Dr. Thorne, the specialists, perhaps even some deputies, all moving with a grim determination. They were a force, an official, scientific force, coming to unveil the carefully guarded secret of the Blue Ghost.

The operation was executed with a precision that impressed Rick, even from his distant perch. The vehicles stopped well short of Collins’s property, disgorging their occupants in silent, efficient movements. Small, shielded flashlights flickered briefly, like fireflies, as the team fanned out. They approached the mine hill from multiple directions, encircling it like hunters stalking their prey. Rick watched, mesmerized, as the shadowy figures melted into the landscape.

He saw two figures, larger than the others, peel off towards Collins’s house. That would be the Sheriff, Rick surmised, and perhaps a deputy, to secure the property and, if possible, apprehend Collins and Croft. The others, a group of five or six, headed directly for the mine entrance and the area of the gully where the Viridium was suspected to be.

The minutes that followed were excruciating. Rick imagined the confrontation, the surprise, the desperate attempts of Collins to explain away his illicit activities. He wished he could hear, could see more clearly. But his vantage point, while safe, was also limiting.

Suddenly, a beam of bright, steady light pierced the darkness near the gully. It wasn’t a flashlight. It was a powerful, almost unnatural green glow, a ghostly green. Rick gasped. It was the Viridium. He remembered Dr. Thorne describing its luminescence, its strange, internal light. The team must have found it.

Then, chaos erupted.

From within the mine shaft, a shrill, piercing alarm blared, echoing across the valley. It was the same alarm that had startled them the night they chased the ghost. Simultaneously, a figure detached itself from the darkness near Collins’s house. It was a man, running with a desperate urgency, not towards the house, but away from it, towards the mine hill. Behind him, two other figures, clearly the Sheriff and a deputy, gave chase, their flashlights bouncing wildly as they navigated the rough terrain.

The sudden blare of the alarm, a jarring contrast to the quiet tension of the night, snapped Rick and Scotty to full attention. It was a piercing, mechanical shriek that echoed off the mine hill and across the valley, announcing to everyone within a mile that something was terribly wrong. Rick felt a jolt of adrenaline, cold and sharp, shoot through him. The figure running from Collins's house, pursued by the Sheriff and his deputy, seemed to redouble his efforts, a frantic scramble over the uneven ground. It had to be Collins, Rick thought, his heart hammering against his ribs. Who else would be so desperate to escape?

The chase was a chaotic ballet of bobbing flashlights and desperate footfalls. The fleeing man was fast, surprisingly agile for what Rick imagined was a real-estate agent, leaping over rocks and brush with a practiced ease that suggested more than a little experience in illicit nocturnal activities. The Sheriff, a larger man, was slower but relentless, his voice booming commands into the night, though the alarm largely swallowed his words. The deputy, younger and leaner, kept pace, gaining on the fugitive with every stride.

Down at the gully, where the green glow of the Viridium was now a steady, almost alien beacon, the scene was equally frenzied. The specialists, caught by surprise by the alarm, were shouting, their flashlights sweeping frantically across the mine entrance. Dr. Thorne, identifiable by his slightly stooped posture and intense focus, seemed to be trying to maintain order, waving his hands, pointing towards the green light, then towards the mine opening. It was clear their covert operation had just become very, very public.

Rick nudged Scotty again. "This is it, Scotty. The whole thing's blowing up."
Scotty, his eyes wide, could only nod, mesmerized by the unfolding drama. "What do we do?" he whispered, but Rick barely heard him above the din.

Rick’s mind raced. The projector. The ghost. They had planned to wait, to observe, but the carefully laid plans of the Sheriff had been shattered. This was no longer a stealthy investigation; it was a full-blown emergency. If Collins was running, he was likely heading towards his escape route, or perhaps towards whatever mechanism he used to produce the ghost. That mechanism, Rick was now convinced, would be located somewhere within the mine itself, or very close to it.

"We move," Rick decided, his voice firm despite the tremor in his hands. "We get closer to the mine entrance. We need to see what they find, what he's trying to hide."
Without waiting for a reply, Rick began to descend the gentle slope from their hiding place. The screen of foliage, once a secure shield, now felt like a barrier holding them back from the critical action. Scotty followed without question, his steps light and quick. They moved carefully, trying to stay low, hoping their dark clothes would blend them into the shadows. The noise of the alarm, while chaotic, also offered them a perverse kind of cover, masking the sounds of their approach.

As they drew nearer to the gully, the green glow of the Viridium intensified. It seemed to emanate not from a single source, but from multiple points within the rock face, a network of glowing veins that pulsed faintly. Dr. Thorne and one of the specialists were now huddled directly in front of the mine entrance, their faces illuminated by the eerie green light, talking excitedly, pointing. It looked like they had found more than just a single piece of the strange material.

Suddenly, another figure emerged from the mine shaft itself, a man Rick recognized as Croft, the Frostola man. He was carrying something, a bulky, metal object wrapped in dark canvas, cradling it carefully as if it were incredibly fragile or precious. He didn't seem to notice the alarm at first, or perhaps he was too focused on his burden. He was moving quickly, not towards Collins, who was still being chased across the hill, but in the opposite direction, towards a less conspicuous path that led away from the mine and into a dense thicket of trees.

"Croft!" Rick exclaimed, pointing. "He's got something! That must be it, the projector!"
Scotty saw him too. "He's trying to get away with it!"
The Sheriff and his deputy were still engaged in the pursuit of Collins, who had now reached the higher ground of the mine hill, dodging rocks and low-hanging branches. They hadn't seen Croft emerge from the mine, or if they had, they were too preoccupied with their primary target. Dr. Thorne and the specialists were too absorbed by the Viridium, too close to the source of the green light to have a clear view of the mine entrance.

This was their chance, Rick realized. Their moment. They had intel the Sheriff didn't have, a vantage point that offered a wider view of the unfolding scene.
"Scotty, we have to stop him!" Rick said, his voice urgent. "If he gets away with that, they might never figure out how it was done!"
Without another thought, Rick broke into a run, heading straight for Croft. Scotty, always a step behind but never hesitant, followed, drawing a deep breath. They were leaving their role as observers behind. They were now participants.

Croft, hearing their approach, spun around, his face a mask of surprise and irritation. He hadn't expected anyone to be coming from that direction. He clutched the canvas-wrapped object tighter. It was heavy; Rick could tell by the way Croft struggled to hold it steady.
