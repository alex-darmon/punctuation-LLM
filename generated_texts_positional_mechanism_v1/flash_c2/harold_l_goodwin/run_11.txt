A new surge of hope, mixed with a healthy dose of fear, flooded Rick. They had not only escaped, they had stumbled upon something truly significant. This device, whatever it was, was clearly important to their captors. This was their chance to find out something of importance, just as he had hoped earlier.

“We have to get a message back to the base,” Rick said, his voice firm with renewed purpose. “They need to know about this. And about those men.”

Scotty nodded, his eyes still fixed on the glowing, mysterious device. “But how do we get out?” he asked. “We still haven’t found an exit.”

Rick looked around the small storage room. There was no other door. No window. Only the single corridor leading back to the main entrance of the jail.

“There has to be another way,” Rick insisted, his gaze falling upon the ceiling. It was low, made of rough-hewn timbers, thick with dust and cobwebs. He noticed a faint, irregular patch in one corner, near the stone wall. It looked like a makeshift repair, or perhaps a hidden hatch.

“Up there,” Rick said, pointing with the flashlight. “See that patch?”

Scotty followed his gaze. “Yeah. Looks like an old trapdoor, maybe. Or a section of the ceiling that was replaced.”

They dragged one of the broken chairs from the junk pile, a rickety wooden contraption, and placed it under the suspicious patch. Rick climbed onto it, carefully, testing its stability. It creaked ominously under his weight, but held. He reached up, feeling the timbers. They were loose. One section of the ceiling, a plank, seemed to be held in place only by gravity and a prayer.

He pushed upwards, trying to dislodge the plank. It resisted, but he could feel it shift. He pushed harder, using all his upper body strength. Dust and cobwebs rained down on him. The plank groaned, then, with a soft crack, it lifted.

A gap appeared, revealing a black void above. The faint smell of dry desert air, mixed with a hint of old wood and something else, something that smelled of bat guano, wafted down. It was an attic, or a crawlspace, above the jail.

“There’s a space up here,” Rick whispered, excitement bubbling in his chest. “It’s tight, but it’s a way out.”

Scotty quickly joined him on the chair. He looked up into the darkness. “We can crawl through there. It might lead to the roof, or another part of the building.”

They had found their escape route. But before they left

But before they left, Rick paused. His eyes went back to the blinking lights of the strange machine. “We can’t just leave it,” he whispered, a new resolve hardening his voice. “We have to know more. What if we could disable it?”

Scotty shook his head. “We don’t know what it is. Messing with it could be dangerous. And we need to get back. That’s the most important thing right now.” He was right, Rick knew. Their primary mission was to get word back to the base. But the device, the men, it all felt so critical.

“Just one more look,” Rick conceded, taking a last mental snapshot of the blinking lights, the thick cable disappearing into the wall. He climbed back onto the chair, Scotty steadying it. The opening in the ceiling was just large enough for them to squeeze through. Rick reached up, found a purchase on a sturdy beam, and pulled himself into the cramped darkness above. Dust and cobwebs brushed against his face, and he had to fight a cough. The air was stale, thick with the scent of decay and something else, a faint, dry aroma that he couldn’t quite place, but which reminded him vaguely of very old, dried wood, perhaps mold.

“It’s tight,” he whispered down to Scotty, his voice muffled by the close quarters. “Give me the flashlight.” Scotty handed it up, and Rick switched it on. The beam cut through the blackness, revealing a low, dusty space. Rough, unfinished timbers formed the framework, crisscrossed with ancient wiring and pipes that seemed to go nowhere. He saw the faint outlines of more cobwebs, hanging like ghostly curtains. “Okay, come on up.”

Scotty, younger and a bit smaller, had an easier time of it. With a grunt, he pulled himself through the opening, joining Rick in the confined space. They lay on their bellies, unable to properly sit up, their heads brushing against the rough underside of the roof. The air was heavy, still, and surprisingly cold despite the stuffiness.

“Which way?” Scotty whispered, his voice tinged with a mixture of excitement and apprehension.

Rick shone the flashlight beam around. The space extended in two directions, following the length of the old jailhouse. One direction seemed to lead over the main room, the cells, and then, presumably, the front entrance. The other direction, however, seemed to lead towards the rear of the building, and perhaps, more directly, towards the exterior wall or the roof.

“Let’s try this way,” Rick decided, pointing the beam towards the rear. “It seems more likely to have an exit to the outside, maybe a ventilation shaft or a loose board.”

They began to crawl, Rick leading, his elbows and knees scraping against the rough floorboards. The dust was thick, rising with every movement, making them sneeze and cough in quiet, stifled bursts. The darkness beyond the beam of the flashlight was absolute, swallowing everything. Rick had to focus on the small circle of light ahead, navigating around old, forgotten pieces of lumber, what felt like desiccated animal droppings, and the occasional skeletal remains of some desert creature, long past its time. He kept expecting to encounter something alive, a rat or a bat, but the space seemed eerily empty, save for the dust and the ghosts of its past.

The going was slow, painstakingly careful. Every creak of the old wood beneath them sent a jolt of anxiety through Rick. What if they made too much noise? What if their captors were still in the hotel, or worse, had sent someone to check on them? The thought of being recaptured, after all their efforts, made his stomach clench.

“Hold on,” Scotty whispered from behind him, his voice strained. “There’s something here.”

Rick stopped, shining the light back. Scotty was pointing to a section of the wall, where a faint glimmer of moonlight, or starlight, seemed to penetrate the darkness. It was a narrow crack, high up, barely visible.

“A window, maybe?” Rick mused softly. He pushed forward, inching his way towards the light source. As he got closer, he saw it was not a window, but a loose section of the wall, a few planks that had come away from the main structure, leaving a gap just wide enough for a person to squeeze through, if they were careful. The planks were old, weathered, and looked as if they might crumble at a touch.

“This could be it,” Rick breathed. He cautiously pressed against one of the planks. It gave slightly, swaying outward. He peered through the gap. Below him was the dark, open desert. Above, he could see the clear, star-studded sky, vast and silent. It looked like they were near the rear corner of the jail, perhaps the roof of a smaller, attached storage shed, or just a lower section of the jailhouse roof.

“We can get out here,” he confirmed, his voice a little louder with the fresh air and hope. “But we need to be quiet. This looks rickety.”

He pushed the planks outwards a little more, making the gap wider. A thin shower of dust and splinters rained down. He then carefully, slowly, began to slide his body through the opening, feet first. The edge of the hole was rough, scraping against his clothes. He lowered himself, dangling for a moment, before his feet found purchase on a sturdy, though slippery, surface. He was on a lower section of the jail’s roof, a flat, tarred area that had once been patched and repaired countless times.

“Your turn,” he whispered up to Scotty.

Scotty followed, making a little more noise as he wriggled through. He dropped down beside Rick with a soft thud. They crouched low, surveying their surroundings. The air was cool, a sharp contrast to the stuffy attic. The desert wind carried the faint scent of creosote and dust. The moon, now higher in the sky, cast long, distorted shadows across the ghost town.

From their vantage point, they could see almost the entire settlement. The hotel, a blocky silhouette against the starry sky, stood out. Rick could make out the faint glow of light from one of its windows, confirming the men were still inside. The sedan, their captors’ vehicle, was parked nearby, partially obscured by the hotel’s corner. He couldn’t see any activity around it, but he knew it was there, a silent sentinel.

“They’re still in the hotel,” Rick murmured, pointing subtly. “See the light?”

Scotty nodded, his eyes scanning the area. “No one outside, though. That’s good.”

“We need to get to the jeep,” Rick said, his mind already formulating a plan. “It’s still parked outside of town, on the road, near the wash where we slept. That’s south-west from here. We’ll have to circle wide, past the town, and then cut back.”

The distance wasn’t great, perhaps a mile, but it was a mile over rough, unpredictable desert terrain, in darkness, with unknown dangers lurking. And the men were still a formidable threat.

“Alright,” Scotty agreed, his voice firm. “Let’s move. Stay low, and stick to the shadows.”

They crept along the edge of the roof, moving with silent precision that came from long practice in their many unsupervised adventures. They found a small, rusted ladder descending to the ground at the far side of the jailhouse. It groaned under their weight, but held. They reached the ground and immediately melted into the deeper shadows cast by the jail’s thick walls.

The first challenge was to get past the town itself. They couldn’t risk being seen. The men might come out at any moment. They decided to make a wide detour, circling around the ghost town from the west, using the scattered mesquite and scrub bushes for cover.

Moving across the desert at night was a different beast than moving through it during the day. The familiar landmarks dissolved into indistinct shapes. Every rustle of dry leaves, every scuttling sound, made them freeze, hearts pounding. The air grew colder, biting at their exposed skin. Rick pulled his jacket tighter around him, wishing he had remembered to grab his gloves.

They moved in a crouching run, then a slow crawl, then a bent-over walk, adapting their pace to the terrain and the available cover. Rick kept a constant vigil on the hotel, watching for any sign of movement, any change in the faint glow from its window. He imagined the men inside, perhaps plotting, perhaps securing their strange device. The thought spurred him on, reminding him of the urgency of their mission.

“How far do you think we’ve gone?” Scotty whispered after what felt like an hour, though it was likely much less. He was breathing heavily, his slight frame tiring from the exertion.

“Maybe half a mile,” Rick estimated, his own breath coming in ragged gasps. “We need to cut east soon, to get back to the road.”

They paused behind a particularly dense clump of creosote, scanning the desolate landscape. The silence of the desert was profound, broken only by the chirping of crickets and the distant hoot of an owl. It was a beautiful, dangerous silence.

Suddenly, a faint beam of light cut through the darkness from the direction of the town. Rick and Scotty dropped instantly, pressing themselves flat against the sandy earth, their bodies almost camouflaged by the low bushes.

“They’re coming out,” Rick breathed, his voice barely audible.

The light moved erratically, sweeping across the ground, growing brighter as it approached. It was a powerful flashlight, clearly in the hands of one of their captors. Rick could hear faint voices now, carried on the desert wind, too indistinct to make out words, but unmistakably human.

The light was heading in their general direction, though still far off. It seemed to be sweeping the perimeter of the town, perhaps checking for anyone else. Or perhaps, Rick thought with a sickening lurch, they had discovered their escape and were hunting them down.

“Stay absolutely still,” Rick hissed, pressing his face into the rough sand. He felt Scotty’s arm brush against his, a silent acknowledgment.

The beam of light came closer, closer, seeming to dance directly over their hiding spot. Rick squeezed his eyes shut, trying to make himself as small and invisible as possible. He could hear the crunch of footsteps now, closer than the light suggested, indicating more than one person. He held his breath, his heart hammering against his ribs, a frantic drumbeat in the otherwise silent night.

The voices were clearer now, just fragments. “...around here somewhere…” and “...no signs…” Then the light passed over them, its brilliant beam illuminating the very bush they were under for a terrifying second, before moving on. Rick felt the heat of it on his back. He didn’t dare move. He waited, an eternity measured in seconds, until the light had passed and the voices had faded into the distance.

Slowly, carefully, he lifted his head. The light was now receding, still sweeping, but moving away from them, back towards the town. The footsteps grew fainter.

“They didn’t see us,” Scotty whispered, his voice shaky with relief.

“No,” Rick agreed, pushing himself up to a crouch. “But they’re looking. We have to be faster.” The close call had invigorated him, replacing his fatigue with a surge of adrenaline.

They resumed their trek, moving with renewed urgency. The incident had burned into their minds the absolute necessity of speed and stealth. They ran when they could, crawled when necessary, always keeping their eyes peeled for any sign of movement or light from the ghost town.

They found the road again after another long, winding detour, carefully crossing it and then following it towards the wash where they had hidden the jeep. The familiar landmarks began to reappear,  a distinctive rock formation, a gnarled Joshua tree, a patch of unusually dense sagebrush. Hope flickered within Rick. They were almost there.

“There!” Scotty whispered, pointing ahead.

Rick saw it too,  the familiar outline of the jeep, nestled in its hiding spot in the dry wash. It was a sight more beautiful than any sunset. They scrambled down the bank, their legs aching, their clothes torn and dusty.

Rick fumbled for the keys in his pocket, his fingers stiff with cold and nerves. He unlocked the driver’s side door, threw it open, and slid behind the wheel. Scotty quickly got into the passenger seat.

Rick inserted the key into the ignition, took a deep breath, and turned it. The engine sputtered once, twice, then caught, roaring to life with a satisfying rumble that seemed deafening in the still desert night. He quickly checked the lights. Off. Good. He didn’t want to give away their position just yet.

“Let’s go,” Scotty urged, peering anxiously into the rearview mirror.

Rick put the jeep in gear, and slowly, carefully, began to back out of the wash. He kept the jeep’s lights off, relying on the moonlight and the faint glow from the dashboard to guide him. He drove slowly, carefully, not wanting to attract attention, until they were a good distance away from the ghost town. Only then, when he felt they were truly safe, did he flick on the headlights, illuminating the deserted road ahead.

The road stretched out before them, a long, dark ribbon leading back to civilization, back to the base. Rick pressed down on the accelerator, and the jeep surged forward. The wind rushed past the open windows, a refreshing blast after the stale air of the attic and the dust of the desert floor.

“We made it,” Scotty said, a grin finally breaking through the dirt on his face. “We actually made it.”

Rick nodded, a feeling of immense relief washing over him. “Yeah. And we found something too.” His mind went back to the blinking device, the hidden cable, the hushed voices of the men. “They’re up to something big, Scotty. Something important. And we need to tell the Colonel. Immediately.”

The long drive back to the base was quiet, punctuated only by the hum of the engine and the occasional bump of the road. Both boys were exhausted, but the adrenaline still coursed through their veins, keeping them awake and alert. The near capture, the close call with the snake, the terrifying moments in the jail, the escape through the attic, the anxious crawl across the desert,  it all played back in their minds, a vivid, harrowing reel.

As the first faint streaks of dawn began to paint the eastern sky, casting long, purple shadows across the desert, the familiar lights of the military base appeared on the horizon. Rick felt a profound sense of accomplishment, mingled with the lingering anxiety of the unknown. They had faced danger, escaped, and, crucially, discovered something significant. Their unplanned adventure had turned into a critical reconnaissance mission.

They drove directly to the main gate. The guard on duty, a sleepy-eyed corporal, looked surprised to see them, especially in their dishevelled state.

“Hey, boys,” the corporal said, leaning out of his booth. “Where have you two been? We almost sent out a search party.”

Rick cut him off, his voice firm and urgent. “We need to see the Colonel. Now. It’s an emergency. Top priority.”

The corporal, sensing the seriousness in Rick’s tone and the weary, determined look on both their faces, straightened up. “Alright, alright. Pull over there. I’ll make the call.” He pointed to a small parking area.

Within minutes, a staff car pulled up, and a stern-faced military police officer emerged. He quickly assessed the situation, his eyes taking in their dirt-stained clothes and their tense expressions.

“Follow me,” he ordered, his voice crisp. “The Colonel is already awake. He received a preliminary report when you didn’t return by dawn.”

They followed the MP to the main administration building, their footsteps echoing in the quiet corridors. They were led into a large office, where Colonel Davis, a man with a weathered face and sharp, intelligent eyes, sat behind a desk, a stack of papers before him. He looked up as they entered, his expression serious.

“Rick. Scotty. I was just about to have you declared missing. What in blazes happened?” he demanded, his gaze sweeping over them.

Rick stepped forward, his voice still a little hoarse, but clear with purpose. “Sir, we were captured last night. By some men who were operating out of the old ghost town. They took us to the jail, but we escaped.”

The Colonel’s eyes narrowed. “Captured? By whom? And what were you doing in the ghost town, I might ask?” There was a hint of irritation in his voice, but also genuine concern.

“We were just exploring, sir,” Scotty interjected, choosing his words carefully, knowing they were technically out of bounds. “But then we saw a car, no lights, going into the town. We followed it.”

Rick took over. “They were in the hotel, sir. And they had some kind of machine. Blinking lights, connected by a thick cable into the wall. It was important to them, sir. We think they might be spies, or smugglers, operating right under our noses.” He recounted their experience in the jail, the discovery of the device, their escape, the close call with the snake, the men searching outside the town. He spoke quickly, concisely, trying to convey the gravity of the situation.

The Colonel listened intently, his expression growing grimmer with each detail. He leaned back in his chair, his fingers drumming lightly on the desk. “A device, you say? Blinking lights, hidden cable? And they were planning to leave in fifteen minutes?” He looked from Rick to Scotty, his gaze piercing. “This sounds… significant. Are you certain of what you saw?”

“Yes, sir,” Rick affirmed. “It was definitely clandestine. They were very careful not to be seen. And they were going to use the jail to hold us so we couldn’t report it.”

“And you say they mentioned they wouldn’t be back, that they couldn’t use the place again?” the Colonel pressed.

“Yes, sir,” Scotty confirmed. “One of them said that, right after they tied us up.”

The Colonel stood up, walking over to a large map of the surrounding area on the wall. He tapped a finger on the location of the ghost town. “If this is true, boys, you’ve stumbled onto something potentially very important. A clandestine operation, perhaps. Or worse, espionage.” His face was grim. “We’ve had reports of unusual signals in the area, but nothing concrete. This might be the break we needed.”

He turned to an intercom on his desk. “Sergeant O’Malley, get Captain Hayes in here. And alert the reconnaissance team. We have an immediate priority mission.” He paused, then added, “And send for the medical team to check these two boys over, then get them some food and rest. They’ve earned it.”

He looked back at Rick and Scotty, a rare smile touching his lips. “You two showed incredible initiative, and courage. Despite your unauthorized excursion, your actions may have uncovered something vital to national security. I’ll make sure that’s reflected in your records. For now, tell Captain Hayes everything, every single detail, no matter how small.”

Rick felt a profound sense of relief and accomplishment. He was tired, bruised, and his muscles ached, but they had done it. They had gotten the message through. The blinking device, whatever it was, would no longer be a secret. And the men who had captured them would soon face the full might of the base.

As the MP led them out of the office towards the waiting medical team, Rick glanced at Scotty. Scotty caught his eye and grinned, a wide, tired, triumphant grin. “Well,” Scotty said, a little breathlessly, “at least we didn’t get bitten by that blasted snake.”

Rick chuckled, a genuine laugh bubbling up from deep within him. “No,” he agreed. “And we got a good story out of it too.” He knew, though, that this was more than just a story. This was an experience that would stay with them, a testament to their bravery and resourcefulness, and a thrilling reminder of the hidden dangers that sometimes lurked just beyond the ordinary world. The desert, they had learned, held more secrets than they had ever imagined. And they had just helped unlock one of its most dangerous ones.

The MP led them to a small, sterile room where a medic, a young man with kind eyes and a no-nonsense demeanor, quickly began his examination. Rick felt the medic's hands probe gently along his ribs and check the bruise forming on his cheekbone. Scotty was undergoing a similar inspection.
“Just some minor contusions, boys,” the medic pronounced, applying a cool balm to Rick’s cheek. “You’ll be a bit stiff tomorrow, but nothing serious. You were lucky.”
Lucky, Rick thought. That was one way to put it. He still felt the phantom slither of the snake, the rush of adrenaline as the men grabbed him, the suffocating rope around his wrists. He knew luck had played a part, but so had courage, and Scotty’s ingenious discovery of the wire.
From the medical bay, they were ushered into a small mess hall, surprisingly empty at this late hour. A sergeant behind the counter handed them each a plate piled high with what looked like a Thanksgiving dinner. Roast beef, mashed potatoes, green beans, and thick slices of bread. And a tall glass of milk.
“Eat up, boys,” the sergeant said gruffly, but with a hint of a smile. “Orders from the Colonel himself. Said you earned it.”
Rick hadn't realized how ravenous he was until the aroma hit him. He dug in, Scotty doing the same. The food was warm and comforting, a stark contrast to the cold fear and dust of the ghost town. They ate in silence for a few minutes, the tension slowly draining from their shoulders.
“This is a lot better than stale rations in a jail cell,” Scotty mumbled around a mouthful of potatoes.
Rick chuckled. “You can say that again. I’ll never complain about base food ever again.”
The quiet meal was a welcome respite, a chance for their minds to catch up to their bodies. The weight of their adventure, the danger they had faced, and the immense responsibility of their discovery, settled in around them. But so did a deep sense of satisfaction. They had faced fear, acted decisively, and perhaps, just perhaps, made a real difference.

As they finished their meal, the mess hall door opened and a man in a crisp uniform entered. He was tall, with a lean build and an air of quiet authority. His eyes, though tired, held a keen intelligence. This, Rick instinctively knew, was Captain Hayes.
“Rick, Scotty?” he asked, his voice calm and even.
They pushed back their chairs and stood up. “Yes, sir,” Rick replied.
“Colonel Sanders briefed me briefly. I understand you two have quite a story to tell.” Captain Hayes gestured for them to sit again, then pulled up a chair across from them. He didn’t seem to be in a rush, his manner reassuringly unhurried despite the urgency of the situation. “Take your time. Start from the beginning, everything you remember, no matter how insignificant it might seem.”
Rick took a deep breath, and began to recount their unauthorized excursion, starting from their decision to go exploring and the discovery of the car. He explained the peculiar silence of the vehicle, the sudden stop in town, and their stealthy approach through the desert behind the abandoned buildings. Scotty interjected occasionally, adding details Rick might have missed, or emphasizing a particular point, like the way the men’s voices had been muffled, making it hard to discern their words clearly. They described the mysterious vehicle, the men’s voices from within the hotel, the forced entry into the hotel, and the chilling encounter with the rattlesnake that had nearly given them away.
“They tied us with our knees bent at an acute angle, sir,” Scotty explained, demonstrating with his hands. “And our shoulders pulled back. It was really uncomfortable, designed to make it hard to move.”
Captain Hayes nodded, making a note on a small pad he had pulled from his pocket. “And the device? Describe it again, with as much detail as you can recall.”
Rick explained the metal box, the small blinking light, the wire trailing into the ground. “It wasn’t very big, sir, maybe a foot square, made of a dark, dull metal. The light was a small red pinpoint, blinking slowly, steadily.” He tried to recall the precise shade of the metal, the texture. “It was tucked away in a corner of the main room, almost hidden under some debris.”
“And the cable?” Captain Hayes pressed, his pen poised.
“It went through a small hole in the floor, sir,” Scotty added, his brows furrowed in concentration. “It looked like they had drilled it recently. It was a fresh cut, not an old one.”
“They said they wouldn’t be back,” Rick reiterated, remembering the chilling words. “And they couldn’t use the place again. As if it was compromised.”
Captain Hayes listened without interruption, his gaze fixed on them, his expression unreadable. He asked about the men’s voices, their accents, any distinguishing features, though Rick admitted the gloom and fear had made it hard to see much. “Just vague shapes, sir. And we hadn’t heard their voices before. They sounded like… ordinary men, though. Nothing to make them stand out, no obvious accents.”
“And the car? Did you see anything distinctive about it, a model, a color, anything?”
Rick recounted his desperate attempt to see the license plate as he was carried to the jail. “I strained to turn my head, sir, but couldn’t catch a glimpse. It was just a dark shape against the night. A sedan, they called it.”
“A sedan,” Scotty supplied. “They mentioned a sedan, saying the rope was in its trunk.”
Captain Hayes scribbled more notes. “And the jail? You were able to escape yourselves?”
Rick and Scotty explained their painstaking work with the wire and the knots. “Scotty found a sharp end of wire under his hands, sir,” Rick said, giving his friend credit. “He slid his hands down the frame and worked on the knots. It took a while to loosen them enough for him to move.”
“I was able to untie the first knot between my wrists and ankles,” Scotty piped up, a hint of pride in his voice, “and then Rick got to the second one, which was even tighter. That’s when the MP found us, just as the last knot was coming undone.”
Captain Hayes permitted himself a small, almost imperceptible smile, the corners of his mouth barely twitching upwards. “Ingenious. Very resourceful, both of you. You put yourselves in considerable danger, but your quick thinking, and the information you’ve provided, could be invaluable.” He closed his notebook. “Alright, boys. I think I have a good picture now. Get some rest. You’ve earned it.”
He stood up. “I’ll be briefing the reconnaissance team now. We’ll be moving out within the hour.”

Captain Hayes left, and an MP came to escort them to a small, Spartan room with two cots. But sleep was far from Rick’s mind. The adrenaline was still pumping, albeit a little less furiously. He found himself pacing the small room, his mind replaying the events of the night. He imagined the device, blinking silently in the hotel, and the men, their faces still unseen, carrying out their dark work. Scotty, surprisingly, seemed calmer, already sprawled out on one of the cots, staring at the ceiling.
“Think they’ll find anything?” Scotty murmured, his voice a little hoarse, but with an underlying current of excitement.
“They have to,” Rick replied, stopping his pacing to look at his friend. “With all the information we gave them, they can’t miss it. Every detail we remembered, every word we heard, it all points to that one spot.”
A sudden thought struck Rick, a desperate hope blossoming in his chest. “Do you think they’ll let us go along? Just to point things out, to make sure they don’t miss anything.”
Scotty sat up, a flicker of excitement in his eyes, quickly followed by a shake of his head. “To the ghost town? Are you crazy? No way, Rick. This is military business now, serious stuff. We’re just kids. We got lucky once.”
“But we know the layout,” Rick insisted, his voice low. “We know where the device was. We know how they got in. We could be helpful. We could guide them directly to it.”
Just then, the MP returned, his face grim. “The Colonel wants you two in the briefing room. Immediately.”
Rick’s heart leaped. Could it be? Were they going to be allowed to join the mission, to see the culmination of their accidental discovery?
They followed the MP down a series of brightly lit corridors, the base humming with a new, urgent energy. The briefing room was a hive of activity. Uniformed personnel moved purposefully, their faces set in determined lines. Maps were spread across a large table, and specialized equipment was being checked and rechecked. In the center, Captain Hayes stood, gesturing towards a schematic of the ghost town.
He saw Rick and Scotty enter and nodded briefly to them, acknowledging their presence without breaking his focus on the assembled teams. “Alright, gentlemen, listen up. We have a confirmed target of interest. Two young men, who I’ve just debriefed, stumbled upon a clandestine operation in the abandoned settlement code-named ‘Dust Devil’,  the old ghost town. They describe a device, approximately one foot square, with a blinking light, connected by a cable trailing into the ground. It was located in the main room of the old hotel, specifically in the northwest corner, partially concealed by debris.”
He pointed to a spot on the detailed map, then swept his hand over the broader area. “Our intel suggests it could be a signal relay, a sensor array, or possibly a communications jammer. Whatever it is, it’s not ours, and it’s operating in a restricted zone, actively transmitting. The individuals involved were careful, traveling without lights, and mentioned compromising the location, suggesting a planned withdrawal. They are considered hostile.”
Rick and Scotty stood quietly at the back, absorbing every word. They felt a strange mix of pride and apprehension. This was their story, now being translated into precise military jargon, becoming part of a serious operation. Their fear and confusion were being transformed into actionable intelligence.
Captain Hayes continued, detailing the approach route, the perimeter security, and the team assignments. “Alpha team will secure the immediate area around the hotel. Bravo team will sweep the

surrounding structures, including the livery stable and the jail, to ensure no other personnel are present. Charlie team will provide overwatch from the ridges overlooking the town. We are operating under extreme caution. These individuals are believed to be armed and dangerous. We do not engage unless fired upon, but we are prepared for any contingency. Our primary objective is to secure the device, photograph it in situ, and then extract it for analysis. Secondary objective is to identify and apprehend any suspects. Any questions?”

Captain Hayes paused, scanning the room. Several hands went up, and he addressed each question with precision, reiterating protocols and confirming assignments. Rick and Scotty watched, a knot forming in Rick’s stomach. This was real. Not just a story they were telling, but a full-scale military operation. It was far more intense than he had imagined. The professionalism, the careful planning, it was all so much bigger than them.

Finally, Captain Hayes dismissed the teams to their final preparations. The room began to empty, the purposeful hum of activity shifting to the corridors as men moved towards their gear. Captain Hayes turned to Rick and Scotty, a slight smile touching his lips.

“Well, gentlemen, you’ve certainly given us a busy night.”

Rick stepped forward. “Captain, sir, we know the town. We know the layout of the hotel. We could lead Alpha team directly to the device. We know exactly where it is.”
