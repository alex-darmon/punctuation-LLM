“It’s bolted from the outside,” Rick muttered, his voice laced with frustration. “And there’s no way to reach it.”
Scotty moved beside him. “Let me see.” He stood on his tiptoes, trying to peer through the high window, but it was too dark to see anything beyond the immediate frame. “They said we could get loose, but it would take a while,” Scotty reminded him, his voice grim. “They must have known we’d escape the ropes, but not the cell.”
Rick felt a fresh wave of despair. They were free from the ropes, but still trapped. He leaned his head against the rough wood of the door, listening. Silence. The men had been gone for a while.
“We have to find something,” Rick said, his mind racing. “A tool, anything.” He began to feel around the cell, his hands sweeping over the floor, along the walls. The cell was bare, a few ancient, dusty spiderwebs the only inhabitants. He felt under the bunks, behind the rusted frames, but there was nothing.
“Wait,” Scotty said suddenly. “The window. Maybe the bars are loose.”
Rick looked up. The window was small, covered with thick iron bars. It looked impenetrable. “It’s a jail, Scotty. Those bars are meant to hold.”
“Doesn’t hurt to try,” Scotty insisted. He jumped up, grabbing one of the bars with both hands, and pulled. It was solid. He pulled again, putting all his weight into it, his muscles straining. The bar didn’t budge.
Rick watched him for a moment, then moved to the opposite end of the window. “Try the one next to it,” he suggested. “Sometimes they get corroded in different places.”
Scotty nodded, moving to the next bar. He pulled, grunted, strained. This time, there was a faint, metallic creak.
“Again!” Rick urged. “Harder!”
Scotty pulled again, and the creak was louder, more pronounced. He put his foot against the wall for leverage, and pulled with a grunt that echoed in the small cell. With a sharp groan of rusted metal, the bar twisted slightly in its mountings.
“It’s giving!” Scotty exclaimed, his voice filled with renewed hope. He pulled again, and this time the bar moved more freely, bending slightly outward.
Rick joined him, grabbing the adjacent bar. “Together!” he said.
They pulled, their combined strength slowly but surely bending the thick iron. The creaking and groaning of the old metal was loud in the silence of the jail. Rick worried it would attract attention, but they had to risk it. They were making progress.
After several minutes of arduous effort, one bar was bent out enough to create a gap, and the other was loose enough to wiggle.
“It’s not wide enough,” Rick panted, his breath coming in ragged gasps. “We can’t fit through.”
“We need to break one of the vertical bars,” Scotty decided. “If we can get enough leverage, we might be able to snap it.”
They worked on the most bent bar, pushing it back and forth, trying to stress the metal at its base. It was slow, agonizing work, their hands raw and blistered. The metal was old, but incredibly tough.
Then, with a sudden, sharp crack that seemed deafening in the stillness, the bar snapped. A shower of rust and old mortar flew, and a jagged piece of iron clattered to the floor.
“Yes!” Scotty whispered, triumph in his voice.
Now, there was a hole large enough for them to squeeze through. It was still a tight fit, but it was possible.
“You first,” Rick said, stepping back. “You’re smaller.”
Scotty nodded, and with a careful push, began to squeeze through the opening. He scraped his shoulder, then his hip, but slowly, painfully, he worked his way through the gap. He dropped to the ground outside, landing with a soft thud on the dusty earth.
“Your turn,” he whispered back through the hole.
Rick took a deep breath, and began to force himself through. He was larger than Scotty, and it was a tighter squeeze. The jagged edges of the broken bar tore at his clothes, and he felt a sharp pain as it scraped against his side. He pushed, grunted, and with a final heave, he slid free. He landed beside Scotty, gasping for breath.
“We’re out,” Rick whispered, looking around.
They were in a small, enclosed yard behind the jail, surrounded by a dilapidated wooden fence. Overhead, the sky was a deep, inky black, dotted with countless stars. It was late, well past midnight.
“Now what?” Scotty asked, his voice low. “Where do we go?”
Rick looked around. The jail was a small, stone building, separate from the main town. They were in the middle of nowhere, surrounded by the vast, silent desert. He remembered the faint outline of the town, the old hotel.
“The hotel,” Rick said, making a decision. “It’s the tallest building. We might be able to see something from there. And if those men are still there, we need to know.”
They scaled the rickety fence with ease now that they were free, and moved silently through the darkness, skirting the edge of the town. The air was cool, carrying the faint scent of sagebrush. The only sounds were the soft crunch of their footsteps on the dry ground and the distant cry of a coyote.
As they approached the hotel, Rick felt a knot of apprehension tighten in his stomach. Had the men left? Or were they still inside, waiting? He remembered the cold voice, the man who had ordered them tied. They were dangerous.
They moved with extreme caution, using every shadow, every abandoned vehicle, for cover. The town was eerily quiet, a ghost town in truth, except for the lingering presence of the men who had brought them here.
They reached the back of the hotel, the same place they had been before their capture. The broken rear door hung precariously, a testament to their unplanned entry.
“Let’s try the other side,” Rick whispered, pointing towards the front of the hotel. “We need to see if

“We need to see if their car is still there, if they left any trace of their plans.” Rick’s voice was barely a whisper, lost in the vast silence of the desert night.

They moved away from the dilapidated rear of the hotel, keeping close to the shadows cast by the moon. The front of the building, with its wider porch and larger windows, offered both more visibility and more risk. The air was cool and still, carrying the faint scent of creosote and dust. Every rustle of dry leaves, every distant hoot of an owl, made them flinch. The experience in the jail had sharpened their senses, made them acutely aware of the vulnerability of their situation. Rick’s heart pounded a rhythm against his ribs, a frantic drumbeat of caution and a strange, thrilling sense of purpose. This was not just about escape anymore. It was about discovery.

They skirted the edge of the abandoned general store, its wooden walls leaning inward as if in perpetual exhaustion, then moved past the ghostly shell of the old saloon. Each building was a dark silhouette against the star-dusted sky, a silent witness to a time long past. The main street, a wide expanse of packed dirt, stretched out before them, shimmering faintly under the moon. At the far end, they could see the darker mass of the hotel. No lights. No sound. It was as if the town held its breath, waiting.

“Where do you think they parked the car?” Scotty whispered, his eyes scanning the empty street.

Rick remembered the brief glimpse he’d had when he was being carried to the jail. “Near the front, I think. But I couldn’t tell exactly where. It was dark, and I was… preoccupied.” He managed a weak grin that faded quickly. “They said they were traveling without lights. So it wouldn’t have been obvious.”

They reached the corner of the general store, peering cautiously around it. The street was empty. The hotel stood sentinel, its dark windows like vacant eyes. There was no sedan parked directly in front. Rick felt a flicker of disappointment, then relief. Perhaps the men had indeed left. But then, a thought struck him.

“They wouldn’t leave it out in the open,” Rick murmured. “Not if they didn’t want to be seen. They said they were traveling without lights. Maybe they pulled it around the side, or behind one of the larger buildings.”

Scotty nodded slowly. “Smart. So we have to check the whole perimeter again.”

They started moving along the main street, sticking to the deeper shadows of the buildings on the left side. Each step was deliberate, silent. Rick’s eyes darted from shadow to shadow, searching for any anomaly, any shape that didn’t belong. The wind picked up slightly, whistling through broken windowpanes, a mournful sound that seemed to fit the desolation of the town.

They passed the old assay office, its small, iron-barred windows like tiny, unseeing eyes. Then the barbershop, still holding the faint, phantom scent of old tonics and dust. Finally, they reached the hotel. They paused at the edge of the porch, a long, wooden structure that spanned the entire front of the building. The porch roof cast a deep shadow, offering excellent cover.

Rick took a deep breath. “Okay. We split up. You go one way, I go the other. We check around the sides. Meet back here if we don’t find anything. If you see something, signal me, but don’t do anything stupid.”

Scotty’s eyes were wide in the gloom. “What if they’re still inside?”

“Then we don’t go in,” Rick said firmly. “We’re looking for information, not a fight we can’t win. We just need to know what they were up to, and if possible, who they are.” He remembered the phrase, “We can’t use this place again.” It suggested they were done with whatever they were doing here. But they also mentioned, “We’ll be out of here in fifteen minutes, if the others keep to schedule.” That implied more than one group, or at least a scheduled departure.

They moved to opposite ends of the porch. Rick flattened himself against the rough wood siding of the hotel, inching his way toward the far corner. The wood creaked faintly under his hand, and he froze, listening. Nothing. Only

the whisper of the wind around the crumbling eaves of the old hotel. He released the breath he hadn’t realized he was holding. The rough wood pressed against his cheek, smelling of sun-baked dust and decay. He resumed his painstaking progress, moving one deliberate foot in front of the other, his hands tracing the contours of the wall.

He reached the end of the porch and rounded the corner of the hotel. Here, the ground was uneven, strewn with rocks and prickly desert brush. The darkness was thicker away from the main street, almost absolute. Rick squinted, trying to discern shapes in the gloom. He moved slowly, cautiously, stepping over roots and avoiding loose stones that might announce his presence. He was acutely aware of the vulnerability of his position. If one of the men came out, Rick would be exposed, caught between the building and the open desert.

He pressed himself against the hotel wall, his heart thumping a frantic rhythm against his ribs. The side of the hotel was long, punctuated by a series of boarded-up windows. He checked each one, peering through cracks where the wood had rotted or split. All he saw was inky blackness, the faint, acrid smell of stale air and something metallic, like old machinery. He strained his ears, listening for voices, for any sound at all. But there was nothing. The building seemed utterly deserted, a hollow shell. It was hard to believe the men were still inside.

He continued his stealthy circuit, moving toward the back of the hotel. He remembered the rear door, the one Scotty had crashed through, the one from which the voices had come. He imagined the men inside, perhaps gathering their gear, preparing to leave. The thought spurred him on, a new urgency prickling at his skin. They had to find the car before it was too late.

As he neared the back of the building, a different scent reached him, faint but distinct. It was the smell of gasoline, fresh and sharp, overlaying the dust and decay. Rick froze, his senses on high alert. Gasoline meant a vehicle. And where there was a vehicle, there were likely the men.

He peered around the next corner, his eyes slowly adjusting to the profound darkness. There, partially concealed by a dilapidated shed that had once served as a storage annex, was a vehicle. It was indeed a sedan, dark colored, blending almost perfectly into the shadows. He could just make out its bulky outline, a silent, menacing presence in the deserted town. Its hood was facing away from him, toward the open desert, as if poised for a quick getaway.

Rick dropped to a crouch, his eyes glued to the car. He saw no movement around it, no sign of the men. They must still be inside the hotel. But the car was here. This was it. He had found it. He felt a surge of triumph, quickly tempered by a renewed sense of danger. He had to signal Scotty.

He pulled back slowly, pressing himself against the hotel wall again. He needed to get back to the front porch where they had agreed to meet. He wondered if Scotty had found anything. His return trip along the side of the hotel felt agonizingly slow, each step measured, each shadow scrutinized. He kept his eyes fixed on the path ahead, but his mind raced, replaying the image of the dark sedan, the smell of gasoline. What were they transporting? What was so important they had to hide it in a ghost town and tie up two kids?

He finally reached the front corner of the hotel and peered around it, scanning the porch. Scotty was already there, a dark, motionless shape pressed against the front wall. Rick moved quickly, silently, to join him.

"You found it?" Scotty whispered the moment Rick was within earshot, his voice barely audible.

Rick nodded, his own voice hushed. "Yes. Around the back, partially hidden by the storage shed. A dark sedan. It smells like gasoline."

"Good," Scotty breathed, a hint of satisfaction in his tone. "I didn't see anything on my side, just the wind blowing through the old bank. Any activity around the car?"

"No," Rick replied. "Not a thing. But they have to be in the hotel still. The car was just sitting there, waiting."

"So, what do we do?" Scotty asked, his eyes darting toward the hotel’s main entrance. "We can't just stand here. They could come out at any second."

Rick thought quickly. "We need to get closer to the car. We need to see what's in it, or at least get the license plate number. They said they wouldn't use this place again. That means they'll be gone soon. And they said something about 'the others keep to schedule.' That might mean more people coming, or another vehicle."

"More people?" Scotty's voice was tight with renewed alarm.

"Or just their departure," Rick corrected. "Either way, time is running out. We need to know what they're up to. If we can get information, we can tell someone. But if we just run, they get away with whatever it is."

"True," Scotty agreed, his gaze fixed on the corner of the hotel. "But we have to be careful. They know we're here, or at least they knew we were inside."

"They tied us up and left us for dead, practically," Rick muttered, a cold anger stirring within him. "They don't think we'd escape so easily."

"That's our advantage," Scotty said, a grim smile playing on his lips. "They're overconfident. So, plan?"

Rick took a moment, his mind sifting through the possibilities. "We approach from the rear, from the desert side. That's where the car is. We use the same route we took to get to the hotel, staying in the shadows. You're better at stealth. You go ahead, scout it out. See if anyone is standing guard, or if there's any activity. I'll follow, but stay back a bit. If you see something, signal me. Two quick taps on the arm if it's safe to come closer, three if it's dangerous, and stay put."

Scotty nodded, his eyes bright with determination. "Got it. Let's move."

They retraced their steps, moving away from the hotel porch and back into the open desert behind the town. The wind was a constant companion now, swirling dust devils around their ankles, rustling through the dry brush. Rick kept his eyes on Scotty, a dark, fleeting shape moving with a dancer’s grace from one patch of cover to the next. He marveled again at his friend's natural ability to move through the environment almost unseen. Rick, for his part, tried to emulate Scotty's fluid movements, bending low, planting each foot carefully, trying to merge with the night.

They reached the storage shed near the back of the hotel, and Scotty paused, melting into the deep shadow cast by its leaning wall. Rick took cover behind a clump of creosote bushes, his eyes fixed on Scotty. He waited, his ears straining, hearing only the distant sigh of the wind and the rapid beat of his own heart.

After a long moment, Scotty gave two quick taps on his arm, a signal Rick felt through the air itself, not through direct contact. Rick moved forward, joining Scotty in the shadow of the shed.

"Still clear," Scotty whispered, his voice barely a breath. "The car's right here. It's an old model, dark blue, I think. Hard to tell in this light. No plates visible from this angle, but I can get closer."

"Wait," Rick said, putting a hand on Scotty's arm. "Are there any lights on inside the hotel now? Any sounds?"

Scotty shook his head. "Still dark. Still silent. It's eerie, like they just vanished."

"That's what they want us to think," Rick said grimly. "They're probably just waiting. They might have a lookout inside, or they're just being extremely cautious. They know someone was listening at the door."

"So, what do we do about the car?" Scotty asked.

"We need that license plate," Rick said, his mind racing. "And if we can get a look inside, even better. Is there a way to approach it from the other side, away from the hotel?"

Scotty peered into the darkness. "There's a broken-down wagon here, and some more brush. We could use that for cover. But it's risky. That's a clear shot from the back door of the hotel if they come out."

"But if they're coming out the back, they'd be heading straight for the car," Rick countered. "It's a calculated risk. They wouldn't expect anyone to be this bold, to approach their vehicle. They think we're still tied up, or ran away."

"You're right," Scotty conceded. "Okay, I'll go first. You follow, just like before. Stay low."

Scotty moved with incredible swiftness and silence, a fleeting shadow darting across the open ground behind the shed. He crouched behind the skeleton of the old wagon, almost invisible. Rick followed, heart pounding, his body tense. He reached the wagon, pressing himself against its rotting wood, his eyes scanning the hotel's back door. It remained dark, lifeless.

From his new vantage point, Rick could see the sedan more clearly. It was a 1940s-era model, a classic, but dark and dusty, giving it a sinister air. He could just make out the rear license plate, partially obscured by dirt and the deep shadows. He needed to get closer, just a few more feet.

Scotty, closer still, was already moving, inching his way around the wagon. He reached the rear of the sedan, dropping to one knee, his head tilted. Rick saw him reach out, his fingers brushing against the license plate. Scotty drew back, a quick, almost imperceptible shake of his head. He looked back at Rick, his face a mask of frustration.

Rick understood. The plate was too dirty, too obscured by darkness and grime to read. They needed more light. But a light would give them away immediately.

Just as Rick was contemplating their next move, a faint, metallic clang echoed from within the hotel. It was brief, sharp, and unmistakably from inside. The sound seemed to reverberate through the silent night.

Both boys froze. Their eyes locked on the hotel’s back door.

Then, a low murmur of voices, indistinct but definitely present, filtered through the darkness. The men were still in there. And they were moving.

"They're coming out," Scotty whispered, his voice urgent. "We have to go."

"No," Rick whispered back, a sudden stubbornness seizing him. "Not yet. We need that plate. And I need to know what they're carrying." He remembered the comment about the "rope in the car trunk." It was a simple statement, but it hinted at preparedness, at a planned operation.

Scotty looked at him, his eyes wide with disbelief, but Rick’s expression was resolute. There was no time for argument.

Rick quickly scanned their surroundings. The wagon offered some cover, but not enough if the men came out fully armed and looking. He spotted a shallow depression in the ground a few feet to their left, near a pile of discarded lumber. It wouldn’t hide them completely, but it was better than nothing.

"The ditch, by the lumber," Rick hissed, pointing with his chin. "Crawl. Fast."

Scotty nodded and moved, a blur of motion in the darkness, slithering toward the slight hollow. Rick followed, scrambling on hands and knees, his breath catching in his throat. He reached the ditch, pressing himself flat against the dusty earth, the rough wood of the lumber pile scraping against his back.

He heard the creak of the hotel’s back door, more distinct this time, then the soft scuff of boots on dirt. Muffled voices, now closer, exchanged a few words. He couldn't make out the specifics, but the tone was low, conspiratorial.

Then, a thin beam of light pierced the darkness. A pencil flashlight, just like the one that had momentarily flicked on in the hotel after they were captured. It swept across the ground, briefly illuminating patches of sand and scrub, then settled on the rear of the dark sedan.

"Check the trunk," a gruff voice commanded. Rick recognized it as the first voice, the leader.

Another man, a heavy-set figure, moved toward the car. His silhouette was bulky against the faint ambient light. He walked around to the trunk, the small flashlight still held by the first man, casting an unsteady beam. There was a click, then the trunk lid slowly lifted, revealing a dark, cavernous space.

Rick held his breath, his eyes straining in the gloom. He could see shapes inside the trunk, dark and indistinct. The second man leaned in, his back to Rick and Scotty, obscuring much of the view. He seemed to be adjusting something, perhaps arranging items.

"Everything looks secure," the second man grunted, his voice muffled by his posture. "Ready to load."

"Good," the first voice replied. "Get the cases. Don't waste time. The others will be here any minute. We can't be found with this stuff still in here."

"Cases?" Rick thought, a cold dread washing over him. What kind of cases? And what did they contain?

The second man closed the trunk with a soft thud. He then turned and moved back toward the hotel door, which was still slightly ajar. He re-emerged carrying two identical, dark metal cases, about the size of large briefcases. They looked heavy, cumbersome. He carried one in each hand, his arms bulging with the effort.

He returned to the car and carefully placed the cases into the now open trunk. The first man, still holding the flashlight, watched intently. Rick caught a glimpse of the cases as they were lowered. They were plain, unremarkable, but the way they were handled, the hushed urgency, spoke volumes.

The second man went back for more. He emerged with another two cases. And another. Rick counted six cases in total. Each one was placed with extreme care into the sedan's trunk.

"That's all of them," the second man said, wiping his brow. "Let's get out of here. I don't like this place."

"Agreed," the leader said. "Let's secure the car. And double-check the hotel. Make sure we didn't leave anything behind, especially not those kid's ropes. We don't want anyone following our trail."

Rick's blood ran cold. They were going to check the hotel again. This meant they might discover the boys’ escape.

The second man moved to the driver's side door, and Rick saw him fiddle with something inside. Then he straightened up, giving the car a final once-over. The first man, meanwhile, was walking back toward the hotel’s open door. He paused, shining his flashlight in a sweeping arc along the ground, directly toward Rick and Scotty's hiding place.

Rick squeezed his eyes shut for a split second, pressing himself deeper into the dirt, praying the small depression and the lumber pile would be enough. The beam of light danced tantalizingly close, illuminating the desert floor just feet from their faces. He could feel the warmth of the light, hear the crunch of the man's boots. The light lingered, then moved on, continuing its arc around the perimeter of the hotel.

Rick let out a silent sigh of relief. They hadn't been spotted. But they were still in immense danger.

The first man re-entered the hotel, and the back door slowly swung shut, then clicked. The light inside vanished. Darkness enveloped the hotel once more.

"Now," Scotty whispered, his voice trembling slightly with adrenaline. "Now is our chance."

"No," Rick contradicted him. "They're just going back in to check for anything left behind. They'll be out again. We need the license plate. And we need to know what those cases are. This is more than just some petty crime."

"But what if they leave before we can do anything?" Scotty protested. "We heard him say 'any minute' for the others. What if the others are the ones who drive the car away?"

"Then we act fast," Rick said. "But we can't just run. Not yet. We have to be smart. If we can get just a single piece of evidence, like that license plate number, it will be worth it."

He saw the second man, the one who had loaded the cases, walk back to the driver's side of the sedan. He opened the door, and Rick heard a soft click. The man slid into the driver's seat. The engine coughed to life, a low rumble that vibrated through the ground. The sound was surprisingly quiet for an old car, almost as if it had been specially modified.

"This is it," Scotty breathed. "They're leaving."

"Not yet," Rick insisted. "The leader is still inside. The driver is just preparing. He'll wait for the other one. This is our chance. Scotty, you're faster. Can you get to the rear of the car, get the plate, and get back to the wagon before the other guy comes out?"

Scotty looked at Rick, his eyes glinting in the dark. "I can try. But if he comes out, I'm exposed."

"You'll have to be quick," Rick urged. "As soon as you have it, get back here. Then we run. We make for the high ground overlooking the town. We watch them go. And we remember everything."

Scotty nodded, determination hardening his features. "Okay. On your count."

Rick watched the hotel door intently. He heard faint noises from inside, movement. The leader was nearing the door. "Now!" Rick hissed.

Scotty launched himself from the ditch, a dark streak against the darker night. He darted around the back of the car, his movements fluid and silent. Rick’s eyes were glued to the hotel door, waiting, watching.

Scotty reached the license plate. Rick saw him lean down, his fingers quickly brushing away the dirt, his eyes scanning the numbers and letters etched into the metal. It seemed to take an eternity.

Just as Scotty straightened up, his mission accomplished, the hotel’s back door creaked open. The first man stepped out, his figure silhouetted against the inner darkness. He carried nothing, but his eyes swept the area, a final, cautious glance.

Scotty, already turning to run, saw him. The man also saw Scotty. For a split second, they were frozen, staring at each other across the twenty feet separating them.

Then, the man yelled, a guttural shout that shattered the night. "Hey! There's one of them! The kid!"

Scotty didn't hesitate. He was already sprinting, a blur of motion heading toward the cover of the desert.

Rick swore under his breath. "Run, Scotty, run!" he muttered.

The leader pulled something from his jacket, and Rick recognized the glint of metal in the ambient light. It was a pistol. He raised it, pointing it at the fleeing figure of Scotty.

"Get in the car!" the leader yelled to the driver. "Don't let him get away!"

The car engine revved, and the sedan started to move, slowly at first, then picking up speed, its tires crunching on the loose gravel. It was heading toward Scotty, cutting off his escape route into the open desert.

Rick couldn't just stay hidden. His friend was in danger. He had to create a diversion. His eyes darted around, searching for anything. He spotted a loose rock, about the size of his fist, among the pile of lumber. He snatched it up.

As the car gained speed, closing in on Scotty, Rick took aim. He didn’t aim for the men, but for the car. With all his might, he hurled the rock.

It flew through the air, a dark missile, and struck the rear window of the sedan with a sharp, resounding CRACK! The glass didn't shatter completely, but a spiderweb of cracks instantly spread across it.

The driver swerved, startled, and the car momentarily veered off course, slowing slightly.

The leader, distracted by the sudden impact, turned his head toward the car, his pistol still raised. "What was that?" he roared. "Another one?"

That moment of distraction was all Scotty needed. He ducked behind a large boulder, vanishing from the man's direct line of sight.

Rick, seeing his chance, scrambled out of the ditch and began to run in the opposite direction, toward the main street. He heard the leader's furious bellow. "He's going the other way! Get him!"

The leader fired his pistol. The shot cracked through the night, a sharp report that made Rick's ears ring. He didn't know if the bullet was aimed at him or just meant to scare him, but he ran faster than he ever had before. His legs pumped, his lungs burned. He could hear the car's engine revving again, the tires spitting gravel as it reversed, turning to pursue him.

He dodged around the general store, his heart hammering. He heard another shot, closer this time, and the distinctive whine of a bullet ricocheting off something nearby. They were actively shooting at him now.

He ran for the cover of the old livery stable, the same one where Scotty had first reconnoitered. Its dark, shadowy interior offered a temporary refuge. He burst through the wide, swinging doors, gasping for breath, and plunged into the hay-scented darkness. He tripped over something unseen, falling hard onto the dusty floor, sending up a cloud of old hay and dirt. He scrambled to his feet, trying to orient himself in the pitch blackness.

He could hear the car outside, turning, its engine idling, then moving slowly, cautiously, toward the stable entrance. The leader was yelling orders. "He's in here! Search the stable! Don't let him escape!"

Rick pressed himself against a support beam, his body trembling, trying to control his ragged breathing. He could hear footsteps approaching, crunching on the loose dirt outside the stable. A narrow beam of light, the pencil flashlight, probed through a crack in the stable door, then quickly swept inside, illuminating patches of the interior for a split second.

He knew he couldn't stay here. The stable was a trap. He had to find another way out, or another hiding place. His hand brushed against something cold and metallic in the darkness. It was a pitchfork, leaning against the beam. Not much of a weapon, but it was something.

He heard the heavy stable doors begin to open, creaking ominously. The light intensified, casting long, distorted shadows. He could hear the men’s voices, low and dangerous, as they entered the stable.

"Spread out! He can't have gone far!" the leader growled. "Check the stalls, the haylofts. He's just a kid, he's probably hiding in the darkest corner."

Rick clutched the pitchfork, his knuckles white. His mind raced. He remembered the layout of the stable from when they had first been here. There was a back door, a smaller one, that led out to a narrow alley between the stable and the next building. It was likely bolted, but it was his only chance.

He moved silently, carefully, deeper into the stable, using the stalls and hay bales for cover. The men were moving through the stable, their flashlights cutting through the darkness, their heavy boots thudding on the dirt floor. He could hear them getting closer. One of them was moving down his aisle.

Rick took a deep breath, steeling himself. He knew he had to be quick, or he was finished. He burst from behind a stack of hay, ducking low, and sprinted for the back door.

"There he is!" one of the men shouted, his voice right behind Rick. "Stop him!"

A shot rang out, and Rick heard the wooden wall beside him splinter. He dove for the door, his hand fumbling for the latch. It was old, rusty, stiff. He yanked at it frantically. It gave way with a groan of metal, and he pulled the door open, throwing himself through it. He landed hard in the narrow alley, scraping his knees, but he didn't stop. He scrambled to his feet and ran, blindly, into the deeper shadows of the town.

Rick remembered their previous location, the base. The base wasn't far. "What if this has something to do with the base?" he murmured. "What if they're planning to disable the base? Or some other important facility?"

"We have to stop them," Scotty declared, his voice unwavering.

"But how?" Rick asked. "There are three of them, maybe more when the 'other team' arrives. We're just two kids."

"We're two smart kids," Scotty countered. "And we have information they don't know we have. They think we're tied up in the jail. We have the element of surprise."

Rick thought quickly. The odds were stacked against them. But letting these men get away with EMP devices, whatever their purpose, felt wrong. It felt dangerous. He looked at Scotty, his friend's face determined. He knew Scotty was right. They couldn't just walk away.

"Okay," Rick said, a plan starting to form in his mind. "Here's what we do. They're focused on assembling these things. They're on a tight schedule. That's our advantage. We need to create a distraction, something big enough to make them leave their post, but not so big that they come after us immediately."

"Like what?" Scotty asked.

Rick looked around the decaying hotel interior visible through the window. "This place is old, dry wood. Flammable. There must be something here we can use. Maybe the old kitchen, if it has any gas lines or something similar, or just some old paper and rags."

"Arson?" Scotty asked, a hint of unease in his voice.

"Not to burn the whole place down," Rick clarified. "Just enough to create smoke, maybe trip a fire alarm, if there's one that still works. Something to make them think twice about staying here to finish their work."

"And while they're distracted?" Scotty pressed.

"We go for the cases," Rick said. "We grab one of those cylindrical objects, or at least a circuit board, anything that can prove what they're doing. And then we run for it. We go back to the jeep, if it's still there, and we get help."

"That's a huge risk," Scotty said, but his tone was more thoughtful than hesitant.

"It is," Rick agreed. "But what's the alternative? Let them drive off with these EMP devices and let them use them wherever they're going?"

Scotty was silent for a moment, then nodded. "You're right. We have to do something. So, distraction first. Where's the kitchen?"

"Look," Scotty whispered, pointing to a corner. "Old newspapers, rags. A whole pile of them."

Rick nodded. Perfect. And next to them, a broken wooden chair. He could snap off a leg. He also spotted an old, rusty can of what looked like kerosene, used perhaps for an old lamp. It was a dangerous thought, but they needed impact.

"Okay," Rick whispered. "Scotty, you gather the papers and rags. Make a small pile, but don't set it off yet. I'll get this." He moved toward the kerosene can. It was heavy, but thankfully not completely empty. Enough to make a good, smoky fire.

He carefully picked up the can. He could feel the slosh of liquid inside. Scotty, meanwhile, was silently gathering the dry, brittle newspapers and tattered rags into a small, loose pile near an old, broken stove.

"Got it," Scotty whispered, indicating the pile.

Rick poured a small amount of the kerosene onto the pile of combustibles. The pungent smell immediately filled the air. "Now for a spark," Rick said, patting his pockets. "Do you have a match, Scotty?"

Scotty rummaged in his own pockets. "I think so. I usually carry some for campfires." He produced a small, waterproof container. He opened it, revealing a few dry matches.

"Good man," Rick breathed. "Okay, here's the plan. We light this, get a good smoke going. The smell will carry. They'll have to investigate. While they do, we loop back around to the lobby, grab a component, and get out."

"What about the 'other team'?" Scotty asked. "They'll be here any minute."

"That's why we have to be fast," Rick said. "We make our move as soon as the first one or two of them come to investigate the smoke. We can't wait for all three to leave the lobby, or for the other team to arrive."

Scotty nodded, his face grim. He took a match, struck it on the rough surface of the stove. A tiny flame flared in the darkness. He carefully touched it to the kerosene-soaked paper.

The dry paper caught quickly, then the rags. A small, orange flame flickered and grew, sending tendrils of smoke curling upward. The kerosene added to the intensity, and the fire began to crackle and pop. The smell of burning paper and fuel became more pronounced.

"Now," Rick said, his voice low and urgent. "Back to the lobby, through the main corridor. Stay low, move fast."

They crept out of the kitchen, back into the main corridor. The smoke was already beginning to drift from the kitchen, a grey plume snaking its way down the hall. Rick listened intently. He could hear raised voices from the lobby now, a distinct change from the earlier murmur.

"What's that smell?" one of the voices barked.

"Smoke!" another exclaimed. "It's coming from the back!"

Rick and Scotty exchanged a quick, meaningful glance. This was it. One man was coming to investigate. Two would remain in the lobby, guarding the cases. The other team was imminent. Their window of opportunity was tiny.

They moved into the main lobby, pressing themselves against the wall just inside the doorway. The cases, with their intricate contents, gleamed under the faint light from a single battery-powered lantern on the table.

Rick pointed to the table, then to one of the cylindrical objects sitting in the open case. That was their target.

"You get the component," Scotty whispered. "I'll watch the door. If the others come, I'll distract them."

Scotty, meanwhile, had grabbed a circuit board, its intricate wiring a web of delicate connections.

"Intruders!" the man at the table yelled, his voice booming. He lunged for Rick, but Rick was already turning, dodging past him.

The man facing the door pulled a pistol from his belt. It glinted ominously in the dim light. "Don't move!" he roared.

But Rick and Scotty were already running, heading for the partially open back door. The pistol fired. The shot echoed deafeningly through the old hotel, a splintering sound as a bullet slammed into the wall near Rick's head. He didn't look back. He just ran.

They burst through the back door, out into the cool night air. The acrid smell of smoke was stronger now, billowing from the kitchen windows. Behind them, they heard shouting, footsteps, and then another gunshot.

"Which way?" Scotty panted, running alongside Rick.

"To the jeep!" Rick gasped. "If it's still there! Head for the wash!"

They ran, fueled by pure adrenaline, across the open desert behind the town. The desert floor was uneven, and they stumbled occasionally, but they kept going. The sounds of pursuit were still behind them, but seemed to be fading slightly as they put distance between themselves and the hotel.

They reached the top of the wash, sliding down its sandy slope. They had parked the jeep at the bottom, hidden from view. Rick prayed it was still there. He had visions of it being gone, or disabled.

They reached the bottom of the wash. And there it was. Their old, reliable jeep, sitting just as they had left it. Rick fumbled for the keys in his pocket, his hands shaking slightly. He unlocked the door, and they scrambled inside.

"Start it!" Scotty urged. "Hurry!"

Rick jammed the key into the ignition, twisted it. The engine coughed, sputtered, then roared to life. He slammed it into reverse, spun the wheel, and floored the accelerator. The jeep lurched backward, then sped forward, kicking up a plume of dust.

As they tore out of the wash and onto the rough desert track, Rick glanced in the rearview mirror. He saw figures emerging from the hotel, silhouetted against the glow of the growing fire. They were shouting, pointing.

Then, headlights appeared in the distance, speeding toward the ghost town. The "other team" was arriving.

"We made it!" Scotty yelled over the roar of the engine. "We've got proof!" He pulled the circuit board from his pocket, holding it up.

Rick patted his own pocket, feeling the cylindrical object he had snatched. "Now we just need to get away, and get this to someone who can do something about it," he said. He pushed the jeep harder, hurtling across the desert, leaving the burning hotel and the angry men behind. The moon cast long shadows before them, guiding their escape into the vast, silent desert. They were free, for now, but the true danger, the real implications of what they had discovered, had only just begun. The EMP device, the "other team," the fueled car,  it all pointed to a larger, more sinister plan than they could have imagined. And they were now right in the middle of it. The desert stretched before them, a canvas of endless possibilities and untold perils.

"Somewhere safe," Rick replied, "and somewhere we can contact someone without being traced. We need to get this information to the authorities, but we can't trust just anyone now." He thought of the base. It was secure, but what if these men had infiltrated it? What if the "other team" was part of an even larger conspiracy?

He drove away from the main road, veering off into an even more desolate section of the desert, following a barely visible jeep track that he remembered from an old map. It led to a remote, abandoned prospector's shack, miles from anywhere. It wasn't much, but it offered shelter, and a chance to think.

The jeep bumped and jostled, the wind whipping through the open windows. The moon was high now, casting a silver sheen over the landscape. Rick kept an eye on the rearview mirror, but there were no lights pursuing them. For now, they were safe.

After what felt like an hour, Rick spotted a faint outline in the distance. The shack. A small, crumbling structure made of rough-hewn timber and corrugated iron. He pulled the jeep to a stop a hundred yards away, cutting the engine. The sudden silence was deafening after the roar of the jeep.

Someone completely outside of whatever these guys are connected to."

"Like who?" Scotty asked. "The police? But they're so far away. And if these guys are as organized as they seem, they could have contacts everywhere."
