There was no mistaking it. “It is a tiger!” he exclaimed, lowering the glasses with a sharp intake of breath. “And a large one, by the look of it. Murdock, if he is still down there, is in grave danger.”

“Faith, an’ ‘tis a fine way for a villain to meet his end,” muttered Barney, clutching his crucifix. “A bit of a mauling from a wild beast might be just what he deserves, begorra!”

“That may be so, Barney, but we are not here to dispense jungle justice,” Frank replied, his brow furrowed in thought. “Our mission is

to capture Murdock and bring him to justice, not to abandon him to the maw of a wild beast, however deserving he might be of such a fate,” Frank declared, his voice firm. He turned from the rail, his gaze sweeping over his companions. “We must descend. We cannot simply leave him there.”

“Descend, Massa Frank?” Pomp’s eyes widened, his dark face paling slightly. “Into dat jungle? Wid dat… dat roarin’ beast?”

“Aye, Pomp,” Frank affirmed, a resolute glint in his eyes. “It is dangerous, but we have faced dangers before. We shall take our rifles, and proceed with the utmost caution. Barney, prepare the Pegasus for landing. Find a clearing, if you can, near the edge of this dense growth. We will land on the clear ground and approach the jungle on foot.”

“Begorra, Masther Frank, ye’ve got the pluck of a dozen men, ye have,” Barney muttered, though he made no move to disobey. He lumbered towards the controls, his brow furrowed in concentration. “But a tiger, now, that’s a different breed o’ cat than any we’ve met before. They say they’re cunning as the Devil himself, and strong as a steam engine.”

“We are armed, Barney, and we are not easily intimidated,” Frank assured him, though a flicker of unease crossed his own features. The tiger’s roar had been truly fearsome, a sound of primal power that spoke of savage strength and untamed wilderness. He knew that even with their modern rifles, a direct confrontation with such a beast would be a perilous affair. “Besides, we have the advantage of surprise, and perhaps, the stealth of our approach will keep us from drawing its attention immediately.”

The Pegasus began its slow, majestic descent. Barney, with practiced skill, guided the airship towards a patch of open ground that bordered the thick, emerald wall of the jungle. The morning sun was now fully risen, casting long shadows and painting the scene in vibrant hues, but the jungle itself remained a place of mystery, dark and foreboding. As they drew closer, the air grew thick with the scent of damp earth, decaying leaves, and strange, exotic blossoms. The sounds of unseen creatures began to filter up to them, a symphony of chirps, buzzes, and distant calls that only heightened the sense of alien danger.

The helices whirred to a stop a few feet above the ground, and Pomp quickly lowered the gangplank. Frank was the first to step out, rifle in hand, his eyes scanning the periphery. Barney followed, gripping his own weapon with an almost white-knuckled intensity. Pomp, though visibly nervous, also descended, bringing up the rear. Tom Reynard, who had been in the cabin poring over charts, emerged with a concerned expression.

“Are you truly going in there, Frank?” Tom asked, his voice betraying a hint of worry. “That’s a foolhardy venture, even for you. A tiger in its own domain is not to be trifled with.”

“We have no choice, Tom,” Frank replied, adjusting his grip on the rifle. “Murdock is still our quarry. You will remain with the Pegasus. Keep a sharp lookout, and if you see any sign of trouble, do not hesitate to raise the alarm or even fire a warning shot. But do not leave the ship.”

“Understood, Frank,” Tom acknowledged, his hand already moving towards the signal flare pistol he kept on a rack near the gangplank. “Be careful, my friend. And do not let that rogue Murdock escape you again, not even to the jaws of a beast.”

With a final nod, Frank turned and led Barney and Pomp towards the edge of the jungle. The transition from the open, sunlit field to the shadowy, tangled undergrowth was abrupt. Giant trees, their branches laden with vines and epiphytes, formed a dense canopy overhead, filtering the sunlight into shifting patterns of light and shade. The air immediately became cooler, heavier, and humid, filled with the intoxicating perfume of unseen flowers and the damp musk of rich earth. Every rustle of leaves, every distant bird call, seemed amplified, taking on a sinister quality in the oppressive silence.

“Keep your eyes peeled, both of you,” Frank whispered, his voice barely audible above the faint sounds of the jungle. “And listen. Tigers are masters of stealth. They hunt by ambush.”

They proceeded slowly, Frank in the lead, his rifle held ready. Barney, behind him, periodically wiped sweat from his brow with the back of his hand, his eyes darting nervously from side to side. Pomp, bringing up the rear, tried to match their quiet footsteps, but every now and then a twig would snap beneath his boot, sending a jolt of anxiety through the small party.

After what seemed an age, they came upon a patch of ground where the vegetation was trampled. Large paw prints, unmistakably feline, were pressed into the soft earth. They were immense, far larger than any domestic cat’s, and spoke of a creature of formidable size and power.

“Look here,” Frank murmured, pointing with the barrel of his rifle. “Fresh tracks. It has passed this way recently.”

Barney knelt, examining the prints with a grimace. “Faith, an’ that’s no house cat, Masther Frank. That’s a beast that could swallow a man whole, buttons and all.”

“It would certainly have no trouble with Murdock,” Frank observed grimly. “The question is, did it find him?”

They followed the tracks, which led deeper into the jungle. The air grew stiller, the sounds of smaller creatures seeming to recede, as if in deference to a greater presence. A heavy, musky odor, distinct and animalistic, began to permeate the air. Frank’s senses were on high alert. He knew this smell; it was the scent of a large predator.

Suddenly, a choked cry, raw with terror, echoed through the dense foliage ahead of them. It was a human sound, undeniably so, but filled with an agony that sent shivers down their spines.

“Did you hear that?” Pomp gasped, his voice barely a whisper.

“Aye,” Frank replied, his jaw tight. “That was Murdock, or I’m much mistaken. And he is in trouble.”

They quickened their pace, pushing through thickets of bamboo and thorny bushes, their rifles now held even more firmly. The cries did not repeat, but a series of low, guttural growls, followed by a heavy thrashing in the undergrowth, reached their ears. It was the unmistakable sound of a struggle, a brutal, primal conflict.

They broke through a curtain of hanging vines and emerged into a small, relatively open clearing. The scene that met their eyes was one of savage horror. On the blood-soaked ground lay Martin Murdock, his clothes torn, his body badly mauled. He was alive, barely, his chest heaving with ragged breaths, but his eyes were wide with uncomprehending terror, fixed upon something beyond their view.

Standing over him, its massive striped body partly obscured by a fallen log, was the tiger. It was an enormous male, its coat a vibrant orange-gold with stark black stripes, its eyes glowing with a predatory intensity. Its powerful jaws were stained red, and a low growl rumbled in its chest, a sound of supreme dominance. It had Murdock pinned, and was clearly in no hurry, to finish him off. It was playing with its prey, asserting its power.

“Holy Mother of Saints!” Barney exclaimed, his face ashen.

Frank raised his rifle, taking aim. But even as he did so, the tiger shifted its enormous head, its intelligent eyes fixing upon them. It did not immediately charge; rather, it regarded them with a chilling stillness, as if assessing this new intrusion into its hunting ground. A low, menacing snarl escaped its throat, a warning that vibrated through the very air.

“Do not move suddenly,” Frank commanded in a low, steady voice, his finger resting lightly on the trigger. “Pomp, Barney, aim for the head or the heart. Do not miss. This is our only chance.”

The tiger, as if understanding the threat, crouched lower, its muscles tensing. It let out a short, explosive cough-growl, a terrifying sound that bespoke immense power about to be unleashed. Murdock gave a weak moan, a sound of abject despair.

“Now!” Frank roared, and simultaneously three rifles cracked, their reports echoing deafeningly through the jungle.

The bullets struck the tiger with sickening thuds. One hit it squarely in the shoulder, another in the flank. The third, from Frank’s rifle, struck it near the base of the neck. The great beast roared, a sound of pain and fury that seemed to shake the very trees. It lurched, its massive body convulsing, but it did not fall. Instead, with a burst of incredible speed, it launched itself at Frank, its eyes burning with savage vengeance.

Frank barely had time to react. He dropped his spent rifle and drew the heavy hunting knife from his belt, bracing himself for the impact. But before the tiger could reach him, Barney, with a desperate yell, fired his second shot. The bullet struck the tiger in the side of its head, just behind the eye.

The immense animal stumbled, its charge faltering. It shook its head violently, a guttural shriek tearing from its throat, and then, with a final, shuddering gasp, it collapsed to the ground, its massive form lying motionless.

A stunned silence fell over the clearing, broken only by the ragged breathing of Murdock and the distant chirping of birds. Frank stood frozen for a moment, his knife still clutched in his hand, his heart pounding like a drum. He had faced many dangers, but none had been so raw, so primal, as this confrontation with the king of the jungle.

“Is it… is it dead, Masther Frank?” Barney asked, his voice trembling as he reloaded his rifle with fumbling fingers.

Frank cautiously approached the fallen beast. He prodded it with the barrel of his knife. There was no movement. The magnificent animal was indeed dead, its ferocious power extinguished by their desperate shots.

“Yes, it is dead,” Frank confirmed, a strange mixture of relief and regret in his voice. It was a magnificent creature, and he felt a pang of sorrow at its demise, despite the mortal threat it had posed.

He then turned his attention to Murdock. The villain lay groaning, his eyes fluttering. He was a pitiable sight, his escape attempts thwarted not by human ingenuity, but by the brutal impartiality of the wild.

“He’s still alive,” Pomp observed, cautiously approaching. “But he’s hurt bad, Massa Frank.”

“Indeed,” Frank agreed, kneeling beside Murdock. The man was severely wounded, with deep lacerations and claw marks across his chest and limbs. He had lost a great deal of blood. “He will not be escaping us again, not for a long while. We must tend to his wounds, and then secure him.”

“Tend to his wounds?” Barney scoffed. “After all the trouble he’s caused us? Faith, an’ I’d say leave him to the vultures, Masther Frank. He deserves no better.”

“He is our prisoner, Barney, and as such, he is entitled to proper treatment,” Frank stated firmly. “Besides, we need him alive to answer for his crimes. Pomp, go back to the Pegasus and fetch the medical kit. Bring bandages, antiseptic, and whatever else you think necessary. Be quick about it.”

Pomp, though still nervous about venturing back into the jungle alone, nodded and hastened away. Frank carefully examined Murdock’s injuries, noting the severity of some of the gashes. He tore strips from his own shirt to fashion temporary compresses, attempting to stem the flow of blood. Murdock moaned in pain, his eyes opening to stare blankly at Frank. There was no fight left in him, only agony and the lingering shadow of terror.

Barney stood guard, rifle still at the ready, periodically glancing at the enormous carcass of the tiger. “Never thought I’d see the day we’d be saving the hide of a scoundrel like Murdock from the jaws of a beast,” he muttered, shaking his head. “The world’s a queer place, Masther Frank, a queer place indeed.”

It was not long before Pomp returned, breathless, carrying a well-stocked medical satchel. Under Frank’s direction, they cleaned Murdock’s wounds as best they could, applying antiseptics that made the rogue shriek in pain, and then binding them tightly with clean bandages. The process was slow and painstaking, but eventually, Murdock’s bleeding was largely controlled, and he lay relatively still, though still unconscious from pain and shock.

“He will need proper medical attention soon,” Frank remarked, wiping his brow. “But for now, this will have to suffice. Barney, help me carry him. We will take him back to the Pegasus.”

Lifting the unconscious Murdock was no easy task, but between Frank’s strength and Barney’s brawn, they managed to hoist him between them. Pomp gathered their rifles, and they began the arduous journey back through the dense jungle. The path seemed longer and more arduous on the return, each rustle of leaves making them start, their nerves still frayed from the recent confrontation. The silence of the jungle, once merely ominous, now seemed to hold a memory of the savage struggle they had witnessed.

The return journey proved to be as taxing, if not more so, than their initial foray. The dense undergrowth, which had seemed merely inconvenient before, now snagged at their clothing and threatened to trip them at every step. Murdock’s inert form, though lighter than the tiger’s carcass, was an awkward burden. His head lolled against Frank’s shoulder, a chilling reminder of the proximity of death, even for a scoundrel. Pomp, ever vigilant, scanned the shadows with his rifle ready, his dark eyes wide with apprehension. Barney, muttering prayers and curses under his breath in equal measure, bore the greater share of Murdock's weight, his brawny frame straining with the effort. The air was heavy with the scent of damp earth and exotic blossoms, a perfume that seemed to mock their grim task. Every snapping twig, every rustle of leaves, sent a fresh wave of unease through them, conjuring images of lurking predators or, worse, vengeful natives. Frank, despite his fatigue, kept his wits about him, guiding their path with an unerring sense of direction. He knew that any deviation, any lost moment, could mean disaster in this unforgiving wilderness. The sun, now climbing towards its zenith, pierced the canopy in shimmering shafts, illuminating dust motes dancing in the humid air, but offering little comfort. Their shirts were plastered to their backs with sweat, and their muscles ached with a deep, persistent throb. The jungle, with its myriad sounds and unseen dangers, seemed to press in upon them, a living, breathing entity that resented their

presence. The very ground beneath their feet seemed to conspire against them, roots like gnarled serpents rising from the earth to ensnare their boots, while thorny vines, unseen in the dappled gloom, reached out with malicious intent. The air, thick and humid, clung to their skin, making every breath a shallow struggle against the oppressive atmosphere. The sounds of the jungle, a constant chorus of chirps, buzzes, and distant cries, formed a disquieting symphony, each note a potential harbinger of peril.

Frank, though his brow was furrowed with concern for their present plight, found his thoughts drifting to the predicament of Murdock. Here was the man who had wronged them, the individual whose treachery had nearly brought disaster upon the Pegasus, yet they were now expending their strength and risking their lives to save him from a savage death. It was a curious turn of events, one that spoke volumes of Frank’s inherent nobility, a characteristic that often placed him at odds with the harsher realities of their adventurous life. He pondered the nature of justice, of vengeance, and of mercy, finding no easy answers in the bewildering depths of the Indian jungle. The dictates of humanity, however, were clear, for even a villain deserved succor in such dire circumstances.

“Be careful there, Pomp!” Frank called out, his voice a low warning as the young detective nearly stumbled over a fallen log, his rifle still held ready. “The ground is treacherous. Watch your step, for a twisted ankle here would be a grave misfortune indeed.”

“Aye, Masther Frank, and it’s a right tangled mess this jungle is,” Barney grumbled, shifting Murdock’s weight, which seemed to grow heavier with every passing moment. A bead of sweat, thick and slow, traced a path down his weathered cheek. “Sure, and a man would need the eyes of an eagle and the feet of a cat to traverse this place with any ease. But we’ll make it, we will, for the Pegasus awaits, and a good strong ship she is, unlike this cursed patch of wilderness.”

Their progress was painfully slow. Each footstep was deliberate, each glance around them filled with caution. They navigated around ancient trees, their colossal trunks draped in moss and parasitic plants, and through tangles of bamboo that formed an almost impenetrable barrier. The sun, when it broke through the canopy, was a fiery disc, casting an unbearable glare that shimmered on the leaves, making the air dance with heat. Thirst began to set in, a dry, insistent rasp in their throats, and their limbs grew heavy, protesting against the relentless exertion.

After what seemed an eternity, a faint, almost imperceptible lightening of the overhead canopy announced their approach to the edge of the jungle. Frank, pushing aside a final curtain of dense foliage, emerged into the open ground that surrounded the jungle. The sight of the clear expanse, stretching for several hundred yards to the sea, was a welcome relief, a visual balm after the oppressive closeness of the forest. And there, floating majestically above the waves, was the Pegasus, a beacon of hope and a promise of respite.

“We’re out!” Pomp exclaimed, his voice filled with newfound vigor. “Thank heaven for that!”

“Indeed, Pomp,” Frank replied, a tired smile touching his lips. “Now for the last stretch. Barney, let us hasten, for Murdock requires immediate attention.”

With renewed determination, they covered the open ground, the soft sand yielding slightly under their weary feet. The Pegasus, a marvel of engineering, seemed to beckon them, its gleaming brass and polished metal a stark contrast to the rough natural world they had just conquered. Frank, with a practiced hand, lowered a rope ladder from the airship, and with considerable effort, they managed to hoist the unconscious Murdock aboard, securing him carefully on a stretcher that had been prepared.

Once Murdock was safely inside the main cabin, Frank began a more thorough examination of his injuries. The tiger’s claws had inflicted deep gashes, and while the bandages had stemmed the worst of the bleeding, the wounds were still angry and inflamed. He observed the man, so often their antagonist, now lying helpless and pale, his breathing shallow. It was a sobering sight, and a wave of professional duty, overriding all personal animosity, swept over the young inventor.

“His pulse is weak, and he is still much in shock,” Frank explained to Barney and Pomp, who stood by, their faces etched with concern. “The anti-septic was good, but these wounds will require more than our rough ministrations. He needs a doctor, and he needs one quickly, if we are to save him from infection or worse.”

“A doctor, Masther Frank?” Barney scratched his head, his brow furrowed in thought. “And where would we find one in this godforsaken place, unless it be a witch doctor with his charms and potions, which I doubt would do much good for the likes of Murdock.”

“No, Barney, not here,” Frank said, shaking his head. “We must make for the nearest port, a place where medical science has advanced beyond mere superstition. I believe Colombo, in Ceylon, would be our best option. It is a major port, and we should find competent physicians there.”

“Ceylon, is it?” Pomp interjected, consulting a small map he kept in his pocket. “That is a good distance, Captain. Even at our best speed, it will take us some hours. Can he hold out that long?”

Frank knelt beside Murdock, placing a hand against his forehead. It was clammy and cool, but not excessively feverish, which was a small mercy. “He must hold out, Pomp. We will do everything in our power to ensure it. We will keep him warm, and I will administer what stimulants I possess to aid his circulation. Barney, prepare the Pegasus for immediate departure. Set our course for Colombo. We must make haste.”

“Aye, Masther Frank, consider it done,” Barney replied, already moving towards the control cabin. His movements, though tired, were efficient and precise. Pomp began to gather blankets and extra medical supplies, his usually carefree demeanor replaced with a serious urgency.

As the great helices of the Pegasus began to whir, slowly at first, then with increasing speed, Frank stood over Murdock, a complex mixture of thoughts swirling in his mind. This man, who had tried to steal his invention, who had allied himself with pirates and rascals, was now entirely at his mercy. The irony was not lost on him. He had always believed in justice, but this felt different. It was not a matter of bringing a criminal to court, but of preserving a human life, no matter how flawed that life might be.

The Pegasus rose gracefully into the air, leaving the perilous jungle behind. Below them, the sapphire waters of the Indian Ocean stretched to the horizon, an endless expanse that promised both speed and danger. Frank ordered a high altitude, for he wished to avoid any further encounters with the Rover, should that treacherous steamer still be lurking in these waters. Their primary mission, the capture of Murdock, had taken an unexpected turn. Now, it was a race against time, a desperate dash to save the very man they had pursued.

Hours passed, marked only by the hum of the engine and the rhythmic sway of the airship. Frank maintained a constant vigil over Murdock, changing his bandages, moistening his lips, and monitoring his feeble pulse. Barney, at the controls, kept a steady course, his eyes fixed on the navigational instruments, his mind on the critical journey ahead. Pomp, meanwhile, meticulously cleaned their rifles and checked their ammunition, ever mindful of unforeseen contingencies. The camaraderie amongst the three men, forged in countless adventures and shared dangers, was palpable, a silent understanding that transcended words.

Towards evening, as the sun began its descent, painting the sky in hues of fiery orange and deep violet, Murdock stirred. A low moan escaped his lips, and his eyelids fluttered open, revealing eyes that were dazed and unfocused. He blinked several times, attempting to orient himself, his gaze eventually settling upon Frank, who was bending over him.

“Murdock,” Frank said, his voice gentle but firm. “You are safe. We are on the Pegasus, bound for Colombo. You were attacked by a tiger in the jungle, and you are badly injured.”

A flicker of recognition, then fear, crossed Murdock’s face. He tried to speak, but only a raspy whisper emerged. “The tiger… Frank… you… why?” His voice was weak, barely audible.

“Why did we save you?” Frank completed the question, understanding the confusion in the man’s eyes. “Because it is the right thing to do, Murdock. Even you are entitled to succor when gravely injured. We are not savages.”

Murdock closed his eyes again, a strange expression, perhaps of shame, perhaps of gratitude, passing over his features. He did not speak again for a long time, drifting in and out of consciousness, his body wracked with pain and fever. Frank continued his ministrations, pouring a small amount of water into his parched mouth, offering what little comfort he could.

The night deepened, and the Pegasus sailed on, a silent sentinel against the backdrop of the star-strewn sky. Barney, relieved at the controls by Pomp, came to check on Murdock, his rough exterior softening at the sight of the vulnerable man.

“He’s a bad one, Masther Frank, a truly bad one,” Barney muttered, shaking his head. “But to see him like this… it’s a sorry sight. Even a villain deserves a chance to mend, I suppose.”

“Indeed, Barney,” Frank replied, his gaze fixed on Murdock’s pale face. “And perhaps, through this ordeal, he might find cause to reflect upon his actions. Though I hold little hope for such a transformation in a man of his character.”

“Ah, but you never know, Masther Frank,” Barney said, a hopeful glint in his eye. “A brush with death has a way of changing a man’s perspective, it does. Or so they say.”

Frank merely nodded, unconvinced. He knew Murdock too well, or so he thought. The man’s ambition and greed were deep-seated, qualities that rarely yielded to mere suffering. Yet, a part of him, the idealistic inventor, hoped that Barney might be right.

As dawn approached, painting the eastern sky with soft pastels, land was sighted on the horizon, a faint, dark smudge against the brightening sky. It was Ceylon, and with it, the promise of medical aid for Murdock. The Pegasus began its slow descent, heading towards the bustling port of Colombo, its journey transformed from a pursuit into a mission of mercy.

Upon landing in a discreet area on the outskirts of the city, Frank made arrangements for Murdock to be transported to the finest hospital available. He explained the situation to the local authorities, carefully omitting the more sensational details of their chase, simply stating that the man had been gravely injured in a jungle mishap. The local officials, accustomed to the oddities of foreign adventurers, accepted the story with a minimum of fuss.

Murdock was taken away by a team of local doctors, his fate now out of Frank’s hands. A strange sense of emptiness settled upon Frank as he watched the ambulance depart. The chase had ended, at least for now, but not in the triumphant capture he had envisioned. It had ended with a critical rescue, a testament to Frank’s humanity, even towards his enemies.

“Well, that’s that for now, I suppose,” Pomp said, holstering his pistol with a sigh of relief. “What now, Captain? Do we search for the Rover, or do we await Murdock’s recovery?”

Frank considered the question, his gaze sweeping over the vibrant, bustling city of Colombo, a stark contrast to the quiet wilderness they had just left. The streets were alive with rickshaws, merchants, and the clamor of a thousand voices. The air was thick with the scent of spices and exotic flowers.

“The Rover is still at large, Pomp, and its crew are scoundrels who aided Murdock in his escape,” Frank stated, his voice firm, his resolve hardening once more. “We cannot let them slip away entirely. However, Murdock’s condition means our immediate objective has shifted. We cannot pursue the Rover effectively if we are preoccupied with a critically injured man. For now, our duty is done regarding Murdock. We will remain in Colombo for a short while, to ensure he receives proper care, and to gather supplies. But rest assured, the pursuit of the Rover will resume. They have not yet escaped justice.”

Barney, who had been listening intently, nodded in agreement. “Aye, Masther Frank, that sounds like a proper plan. A bit of rest wouldn’t go amiss, either, for I’m feeling as tired as a dog after a long hunt. And a good hot meal, not to mention a refreshing beverage, would be a welcome change from jungle fare.”

Frank smiled, a genuine smile that reached his eyes. “Indeed, Barney. We have earned a respite. But our vigilance must not waver. While we are in port, we must also be discreet. The Rover’s crew knows we pursue them, and they may yet try to cause trouble, or even seek to retrieve Murdock, however unlikely that may seem given his state.”

The three adventurers spent the next few days in Colombo, a city of fascinating sights and sounds. They replenished their stores, purchased new supplies, and made discreet inquiries about the Rover, though with little success. The steamer, it seemed, had vanished without a trace, a phantom ship swallowed by the vastness of the ocean. Frank made daily visits to the hospital, inquiring about Murdock’s condition. The reports were cautiously optimistic. The man was gravely ill, but the doctors believed he would recover, though his convalescence would be long and arduous.

One afternoon, as Frank was returning from the hospital, a local newsboy thrust a crumpled newspaper into his hand, shouting headlines in a language Frank barely understood. He purchased it out of curiosity, and upon returning to the Pegasus, he and Pomp carefully translated the relevant articles. Their blood ran cold. The newspaper reported a series of audacious piratical attacks on merchant vessels in the Arabian Sea, further to the west. The descriptions of the attacks, the speed of the raiding vessel, and the ruthless efficiency of the crew, all pointed to one terrifying conclusion: the Rover.

“It seems our quarry has not merely fled, but has returned to its old, nefarious habits with a vengeance!” Frank exclaimed, slamming the newspaper onto the cabin table. His eyes blazed with renewed determination. “They believe themselves safe, hidden by the very vastness of the ocean and our current preoccupation. But they are sorely mistaken!”

“Piracy, Masther Frank!” Barney cried, his face clouding with indignation. “The scoundrels! And to think we nearly let them slip away. But we’ll put a stop to their wicked ways, won’t we, and make them pay for their villainy!”

“Indeed, Barney, we shall,” Frank declared, already striding towards the navigational charts spread across a large table. “This changes everything. Murdock’s recovery, while important, can no longer delay our primary objective. Justice demands action, and it demands it swiftly. The lives and livelihoods of honest mariners are at stake.”

He traced a line across the map, from Colombo, across the Arabian Sea, towards the Horn of Africa, where the reported attacks had taken place. “They are heading west, toward the Red Sea, perhaps intending to pass through the Suez Canal and disappear into the Mediterranean. That would be a truly difficult chase. We must intercept them before they reach those narrow waters.”

“The Pegasus is ready, Captain,” Pomp affirmed, his hand resting on the hilt of his revolver. “We have provisioned fully, and the engine is in prime condition. Tell us where to go, and we shall fly.”

“Excellent, Pomp,” Frank responded, a glint of the old adventurous spirit returning to his eyes. “Barney, prepare for immediate departure. Set our course for the Arabian Sea, directly towards the reported areas of these piratical attacks. We will sweep the sea lanes, and we will find the Rover. This time, there will be no escape for Martin Murdock’s crew, and no further delay in bringing them to account!”

With renewed purpose, the crew of the Pegasus sprang into action. The majestic airship, having enjoyed its brief respite, once again rose into the sky, its powerful helices cutting through the tropical air. It left behind the bustling port of Colombo, the recovering Murdock, and the temporary truce with fate, embarking once more on its relentless pursuit of justice across the vast, blue canvas of the Indian Ocean. The sun, a fiery orb in the west, cast long shadows behind them, as if urging them onward, towards the next great adventure, towards the lair of the pirates. Frank, at the helm, felt the familiar thrill of the chase coursing through his veins. The game was afoot, and this time, he vowed, the Rover would not elude them. The very vastness of the ocean might provide temporary refuge for the scoundrels, but it was not large enough to hide them indefinitely from the vigilant eyes and tireless pursuit of the Pegasus and its intrepid crew.

They maintained a high altitude, allowing them to survey a vast expanse of ocean below, their powerful telescope scanning the sparkling surface for any sign of a steamer fitting the Rover’s description. Day and night they flew, the rhythmic hum of the engine a constant companion. Frank, Barney, and Pomp took turns at the watch, their eyes sharp, their senses alert for any deviation from the monotonous expanse of water. The Arabian Sea stretched out before them, an enormous blue desert, seemingly devoid of their quarry. The task of finding a single ship in such an immensity was daunting, yet their determination remained unflagged.

Several days into their renewed search, Frank grew restless. The lack of any definite lead was frustrating, and the reports of piratical activity seemed to have ceased as suddenly as they began. This cessation, he reasoned, could mean one of two things: either the Rover had successfully escaped into a new hiding place, or it was lying low, biding its time. Neither prospect was appealing.

“We need a new strategy,” Frank announced one evening, as they gathered in the cabin over a simple supper. “Sweeping the entire sea is akin to searching for a needle in a haystack, even with the Pegasus. Tom and his crew returned to the Rover, their faces pale with shock. “He’s gone, Frank,” Tom reported, his voice hushed. “The sharks… there was nothing we could do.” Frank stood for a long moment, staring out at the spot where Murdock had disappeared. His reign of terror had indeed ended, but not in the manner anyone had anticipated. The sea, in its cruel and unpredictable way, had claimed him before human justice could.

“Bring the boat aboard, Tom,” Frank said finally, his voice heavy. “There is nothing more to be done here. We will continue our course for India.” The crew, visibly shaken by the gruesome spectacle, slowly resumed their duties. The Pegasus, its powerful searchlight now extinguished, resumed its silent vigil, a shadow against the stars.

The remainder of the journey was somber. The incident with Murdock left a deep impression on everyone, a stark reminder of the unforgiving nature of the sea. Upon reaching the bustling port of Bombay, Frank wasted no time in reporting the events to the local authorities. He presented the captured Rover, the ledger, the stolen gold and jewels, and the testimonies of the now-cooperative crew. The Indian officials, initially skeptical, were soon convinced by the weight of the evidence and the compelling narrative of the young inventor and his intrepid companions. The captured sailors from the Rover were taken into custody, and the ledger, with its intricate details, was deemed a crucial piece of intelligence that would undoubtedly lead to further investigations into Murdock’s vast criminal empire.

With Murdock’s case concluded, albeit tragically, Frank felt a sense of both relief and unease. The primary mission was accomplished, but the manner of its completion left a lingering shadow. The Pegasus, having delivered its unique brand of aerial justice, now needed extensive maintenance and resupply. Bombay, with its vibrant markets and skilled laborers, proved an ideal location for this. Frank, Barney, Pomp, and Tom found comfortable accommodations ashore, allowing them a much-needed respite from the constant vigil of the airship.

While the Pegasus underwent its thorough overhaul, Frank devoted himself to studying the details within Murdock’s ledger. It was a labyrinthine document, detailing transactions across continents, revealing names of shadowy merchants, corrupt officials, and clandestine rendezvous points. He realized that Murdock’s network was far more extensive than he had first imagined, stretching its tendrils from the bustling ports of the Orient to the gilded drawing rooms of Europe. The destruction of this one man, while significant, was merely a single victory in a larger, unseen war against global crime.

One afternoon, as Frank was pouring over a particularly obscure entry, Barney burst into the room, his face alight with excitement. “Masther Frank! Ye won’t believe what I’ve heard down at the docks! Whispers, they are, of a magnificent gem, the ‘Eye of the Serpent,’ rumored to be hidden somewhere in the jungles of Ceylon! Said to possess a cursed power, it is, bringin’ misfortune to all who hold it, save for a chosen few!”

Frank, though accustomed to Barney’s colorful tales, listened with a keen interest. “The Eye of the Serpent, you say? A cursed gem? Tell me more, Barney.” Barney, settling into a chair, launched into a spirited account of the rumors he had gathered. The gem, a colossal emerald, was said to have once adorned the idol of an ancient Ceylonese deity, worshipped by a forgotten civilization deep within the island’s impenetrable forests. Legend had it that the gem was stolen centuries ago, leading to the collapse of the kingdom, and had since been sought by treasure hunters and adventurers, all of whom met with tragic ends.

“And here’s the kicker, Masther Frank,” Barney continued, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper, “they say Murdock himself was tryin’ to get his hands on it! One o’ the sailors we captured, the one with the missing teeth, he swore he heard Murdock mention it. Said it was his ultimate prize, somethin’ that would make him the richest man alive!”

Frank’s mind whirred. Murdock’s ledger. He quickly flipped through the pages, searching for any reference. And there it was, a subtle, almost unnoticeable entry, a coded notation that, in hindsight, clearly pointed to Ceylon and a specific, ancient site. The possibility that Murdock was pursuing this legendary gem, and that his network might have been involved in its recovery or sale, sent a thrill of renewed purpose through him. The hunt for Murdock was over, but perhaps the quest for the Eye of the Serpent was about to begin, a new chapter in the adventurers of the Pegasus.

He looked up at Barney, a glint in his eye. “Barney, this changes everything. If Murdock was indeed after this gem, then it is entirely possible that his associates might still be pursuing it. This ‘Eye of the Serpent’ could be a key to unraveling even more of his criminal activities. We must investigate this further.”

Barney grinned, his eyes sparkling. “Aye, Masther Frank! A grand adventure, it sounds! Better than sittin’ around this dusty port, waitin’ for rivets to be hammered!”

Pomp, who had been quietly cleaning his rifle in the corner, looked up, his dark eyes thoughtful. “A gem of such renown, with a history so steeped in legend, would undoubtedly attract many unscrupulous individuals. It would also be a powerful tool, if the rumors of its 'cursed power' are more than mere folklore. We must approach with caution.”

Tom, entering the room with a fresh set of navigational charts, overheard the last part of the conversation. “The Eye of the Serpent, you say? I’ve read tales of it. It’s said to be guarded by ancient traps and fierce jungle tribes. A perilous undertaking, Frank.”

“Perilous, yes,” Frank agreed, rising from his chair, “but potentially crucial. If this gem truly exists, and if it was connected to Murdock, then it is our duty to prevent it from falling into the wrong hands, or from causing further misfortune. We have the means, and we have a responsibility.” He walked over to the large map on the wall, his finger tracing a line from Bombay to Ceylon. “Once the Pegasus is fully restored, we will set a course for the jungles of Ceylon. The hunt for relics, and the remnants of Murdock’s empire, begins anew.”

The decision was made. The mechanics worked tirelessly on the Pegasus, replacing worn parts, polishing her silver hull, and topping off her fuel reserves. In a matter of days, the magnificent airship was once again ready for adventure, its powerful engines eager to carry its intrepid crew to new and uncharted territories. Frank, with Murdock’s ledger as his guide, began to plot a course for the remote interior of Ceylon, a land known for its lush, impenetrable jungles, ancient ruins, and whispered legends.

The morning of their departure was crisp and clear. The Pegasus, gleaming under the tropical sun, rose gracefully from the harbor, a spectacle that drew gasps of admiration from the onlookers. Barney, at the controls, guided her steadily towards the southeast, while Frank, with Tom and Pomp, consulted charts and pored over Murdock’s cryptic notes. The thought of a cursed gem, guarded by ancient spirits and ruthless men, filled them with a mixture of apprehension and excitement.

As they flew over the azure waters of the Indian Ocean, leaving the bustling port of Bombay behind, Frank felt a familiar thrill. The airship, a marvel of ingenuity, was truly in its element, soaring above the world, a silent sentinel of progress and discovery. Yet, he knew that beneath the tranquil canopy of the Ceylonese jungle, dangers unknown awaited them. The ghost of Murdock’s ambition still lingered, guiding them towards a new and formidable challenge. The Eye of the Serpent, whether cursed or merely a magnificent treasure, promised an adventure unlike any they had encountered before, a test of their courage, their wit, and their unwavering resolve against the dark forces that sought to exploit the world's hidden wonders. Frank looked at his companions, their faces etched with determination. They were ready. The Pegasus was ready. And the jungle of Ceylon, with all its ancient secrets, awaited them.
