All eyes turned to Dave and Freddy. For a moment, a hush fell over the room. Dave felt a renewed surge of adrenaline. This was the moment. This was why they were there. He looked at Freddy, who, despite his lingering fatigue, stood tall, his gaze unwavering as he met the stern stares of the Allied commanders.

“Gentlemen,” Dave began, his voice clear and steady, despite the tremor in his hands. “My name is Dave Harrison, American. This is Freddy Thompson, British. We were shot down over Germany, captured, and have just escaped. While prisoners, we overheard and observed a massive German buildup. We believe the main thrust of the German offensive is not where your intelligence expects it. It is here, through Belgium and the Ardennes, aimed at the Channel coast, to cut off the Allied armies.”

He then launched into a detailed account, recounting everything they had seen, every conversation they had overheard, every piece of German strategy they had gleaned. Freddy, as before, supplemented his narrative, adding precise details about troop numbers, equipment types, and the specific routes of advance, his youthful voice cutting through the tension in the room with surprising authority. He even described the morale of the German soldiers, conveying a sense of their fanatical conviction.

The senior officers listened in stunned silence, their faces growing paler with each revelation. Lord Gort’s jaw tightened, and General Gamelin, a man known for his unflappable demeanor, slowly leaned forward, his elbows on the table, his eyes fixed on Dave with an intensity that was almost unnerving. He frequently interrupted, asking sharp, incisive questions, probing for any discrepancies, any weaknesses in their account. But Dave and Freddy had seen too much, heard too much, for their story to be anything but brutally consistent.

As they spoke, a young aide, a British Captain, began to move the pins on the large wall map, repositioning the German arrows according to their testimony. The map, initially showing a dispersed, more cautious German advance, now depicted a terrifyingly concentrated spearhead, thrusting deep into Belgium, threatening to slice through the Allied lines like a hot knife through butter. The implications were chillingly clear.

When they finally finished, the silence in the room was even heavier than before. The air crackled with tension, with the unspoken dread that hung over every man present. Lord Gort slowly rose from his chair, his expression grim.

“Gentlemen,” he said, his voice low and gravelly, “if what these two young men say is true, then we are in a far more perilous position than we could have ever imagined. Our entire strategy, our entire deployment, is based on a false premise. We have committed the bulk of our forces to the wrong sector. We are about to be outflanked, perhaps even encircled, before we have even begun to fight a proper battle.”
