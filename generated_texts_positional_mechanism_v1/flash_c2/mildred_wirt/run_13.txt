She could try to confront Maxwell, but that would only lead to more shouting and likely her immediate expulsion from the hotel. She needed to get inside that room, or at least discover what lay behind the green door, before Maxwell could further barricade it.

A sudden, desperate plan formed in her mind. It was risky, perhaps even foolhardy, but it was the only chance she had. She took a deep breath, mentally bracing herself for the consequences.

"Don't worry, Mr. Maxwell," Francine was saying, her voice dripping with false reassurance. "I am quite certain we can contain this. We merely need to intercept Miss Parker before she makes any further progress. Perhaps a gentle escort back to her own room, with a stern warning about trespassing, would suffice. Or, if she proves… uncooperative…" Francine let the sentence hang in the air, a silent threat unspoken.

Maxwell didn't reply, his attention focused on the key card in his hand. He was already hurrying down the corridor, his heavy footsteps echoing on the polished floor. Penny pressed herself further into the alcove, waiting for him to pass. As he rushed by, oblivious to her presence, she slipped out, moving swiftly and silently in the opposite direction. She wasn't going to the Green Door;

she was heading for the service elevator, a discreet, gray metal box tucked away in a dimly lit alcove near the laundry chute. She pressed the call button, her heart thumping a quick, urgent rhythm against her ribs. The clatter of Maxwell’s retreating footsteps faded down the main corridor, replaced by the soft hum of the elevator mechanism. Penny didn’t dare breathe until the doors slid open, revealing an empty, utilitarian interior. She stepped inside, punching the button for the ground floor, then quickly changed her mind, pressing the one for the fourth. It was a gamble, but she needed to throw Maxwell off her trail, and a quick, unexpected shift in direction was her best bet.

As the elevator began its descent, then paused, then continued its upward journey, Penny leaned against the cold metal wall, trying to collect her thoughts. Maxwell had her number now, or at least he thought he did. He believed she was merely a reporter, sent by her father to dig up dirt. That was a useful misconception, for it masked her deeper, more personal, reasons for being at the hotel. But he had also taken her green ticket, the key to the mysterious Green Room, and now she was back to square one, only worse. She was a marked girl.

The elevator shuddered to a halt on the fourth floor. The doors opened onto a plush, carpeted hallway, identical in its quiet opulence to the second floor, but currently deserted. Penny peeked out cautiously. No sign of Francine. No hotel staff. The silence was almost deafening, broken only by the distant, muffled strains of a piano from somewhere below. Penny stepped out, letting the doors close behind her. She moved quickly, her soft boots making no sound on the thick carpet, heading towards the fire escape door she had spotted earlier on her way up. It was an emergency exit, but it also offered a less conspicuous route down to the lower floors, bypassing the main elevators and their potential surveillance.

She pushed open the heavy door and found herself in a narrow, unheated stairwell. The air was cold and still, smelling faintly of dust and old metal. She began her descent, her hand sliding along the smooth, wooden banister. One floor, two floors, three. She reached the second floor landing and paused, listening. She heard nothing, only the faint murmur of life from beyond the heavy, fire-rated door. She considered trying the Green Room door again, but without the card, it was useless. And besides, Maxwell would undoubtedly be there now, having likely secured it or replaced the guard. It would be foolish to approach it directly.

Penny continued down to the main lobby, slipping out of a service entrance near the reception desk, hoping to blend in with any incoming or outgoing guests. The lobby was bustling now, with several people checking in, porters carrying luggage, and a few guests lounging in the comfortable armchairs. She scanned the room quickly, her eyes darting between faces. No sign of Maxwell. No sign of Francine. For the moment, she was safe, but the sense of urgency hadn't diminished. She needed a new plan, and she needed information.

Her gaze fell upon the elegant clock above the reception desk. It was almost noon. Lunchtime. The hotel restaurant, "The Alpine Grill," would be busy. Perhaps a discreet table there, close to the kitchen or service areas, would offer an opportunity to observe, to listen. Hotel staff often exchanged snippets of information while going about their duties, information that could prove invaluable to a keen observer.

Penny made her way to the restaurant entrance. A smiling maître d' greeted her. "Table for one, young lady?"
"Yes, please," Penny replied, trying to sound nonchalant. "Something quiet, perhaps near a window?" She hoped for a window, but a table near a service entrance or kitchen door would be even better.
"Certainly," he said, gesturing towards a small, somewhat secluded table near the far wall. It wasn't perfect, but it offered a good vantage point of the room and the various comings and goings.

As she settled into her seat, Penny ordered a simple sandwich and a glass of milk, her mind racing. Maxwell’s outburst had confirmed that the Green Room was no ordinary storage closet. His reference to a "special guest" and Francine's mention of a "transfer" resonated with a vague disquiet in her mind. What kind of guest required such secrecy? What kind of transfer? And why was her father's newspaper, the _Riverview Star_, considered such a threat? She knew her father had a reputation for fearless investigative journalism, but his beat was usually local corruption, not secret hotel dealings in remote mountain resorts. Unless, of course, there was a connection.

She watched the restaurant staff as they moved between tables, clearing plates, refilling water glasses. Most were young, seemingly oblivious to anything beyond their immediate tasks. But then she noticed a busboy, a gangly youth with perpetually worried eyes, who seemed to hover near the kitchen door more often than necessary. He would glance around nervously, then disappear into the kitchen, only to reappear moments later, sometimes without a tray. He seemed to be listening, too, his head cocked slightly, as if straining to catch a conversation.

Penny decided to risk it. When the busboy passed her table, she caught his eye with a small, encouraging smile. He looked away quickly, flustered.
"Excuse me," Penny said softly, just loud enough for him to hear. "Could I trouble you for another glass of water?"
He nodded, still avoiding her gaze, and scurried off. When he returned, placing the glass on her table, Penny leaned forward slightly.
"You seem a little on edge today," she murmured, keeping her voice low. "Is everything alright?"
The busboy started, his eyes widening. "Everything's fine," he mumbled, beginning to back away.
"My name's Penny," she said. "I'm a guest here. I just have a feeling something strange is happening in this hotel. Perhaps you've noticed it too?"
He hesitated, glancing around the bustling room. "I don't know what you mean," he said, but his voice was thin, unconvincing.
"The Green Room, for instance," Penny continued, taking a sip of water. "It seems to be quite the topic of conversation among the staff." This was a bluff, of course, but she hoped it would bait him.
His eyes flickered with something that looked like fear. "I… I can't talk about that," he whispered. "Mr. Maxwell said we'd be fired."
"Fired for what?" Penny pressed gently. "For talking about a hotel room? That seems a bit excessive, don't you think?"
He wrung his hands, his gaze darting to the kitchen door. "He said… he said it was a private matter. And that we weren't to speak of the new guest, or the… the package."
"The package?" Penny repeated, her interest piqued. So, not just a guest, but a "package." That sounded more like an object, something valuable. "What kind of package?"
"I don't know!" he exclaimed, his voice rising slightly. He immediately clamped his mouth shut, glancing nervously at the maître d'. "I really have to go. I'll lose my job."
"Just one more question," Penny pleaded. "Did anyone else seem interested in this guest or this package?"
He wrung his hands again, then quickly bent down as if to pick something up from the floor. "There was a lady," he muttered, so low Penny almost missed it. "Dark hair. She asked a lot of questions. Looked like a reporter."
Francine. Of course. Penny thanked him, and he scurried away, clearly terrified of being seen speaking with her.

Penny finished her sandwich, a new piece of the puzzle falling into place. A "new guest" and a "package" requiring extreme secrecy and a paranoid hotel manager. And Francine was also sniffing around, confirming Penny's earlier suspicion. This was much bigger than just a simple room. It had all the hallmarks of a major scoop, the kind her father loved. But what was it? And why here, in this secluded mountain resort?

After lunch, Penny decided her best course of action was to try a different angle. She couldn’t force her way into the Green Room, and Maxwell would have likely placed a new, more vigilant guard. But perhaps she could find a back way, a service tunnel, or even a maintenance shaft that led to that part of the hotel. These old mountain hotels often had labyrinthine layouts, full of forgotten passages.

She spent the next hour discreetly exploring the less frequented parts of the hotel. She found herself in a maze of service corridors on the lower level, a stark contrast to the luxurious guest areas. Here, the walls were bare plaster, the floors cold concrete, and the air smelled of disinfectant and steam from the laundry room. She passed various storage rooms, the staff locker rooms, and the boiler room, the rhythmic throb of machinery echoing through the walls.

She even tried the laundry room, hoping to glean some information from the busy workers. They were mostly older women, focused on their tasks, folding mountains of white sheets. Penny, feigning interest in the hotel's laundry service, attempted a casual conversation with one of them.
"It must be quite a job, keeping up with all the guests' needs," Penny remarked, watching a woman deftly iron a tablecloth.
"Aye, that it is, lass," the woman replied, not looking up. "Especially with special requests."
"Oh? What kind of special requests?" Penny asked, trying to sound genuinely curious.
"Certain guests prefer their linens changed more often, or special cleaning for their garments," the woman explained. "Nothing out of the ordinary, really. But then, there's always the odd one."
"The odd one?"
The woman paused her ironing, her gaze finally meeting Penny's. "The ones who want no service at all. Who don't want anyone to enter their rooms. We've had a few of those lately, up on the second floor, in that new section." She lowered her voice conspiratorially. "Mr. Maxwell himself gave strict instructions. No cleaning, no entry, just leave fresh towels outside the door."
Penny's heart gave a little jump. "The Green Room, perhaps?" she ventured.
The woman’s eyes widened slightly, then she shook her head, resuming her ironing with renewed vigor. "I wouldn't know the room numbers, dear. Just that it's on the second floor. Now, if you'll excuse me, I have work to do."
Penny got the hint. Maxwell had clearly instilled a fear of speaking about these things. But she had confirmation: the "new guest" was sequestered in the Green Room, and they were trying to keep their presence a complete secret.

Leaving the laundry room, Penny continued her exploration. She found a door marked "Staff Only" that led to a narrow, winding staircase. She ascended it cautiously, wondering where it might lead. It was dark, with bare light bulbs hanging at intervals, casting pools of yellow light on the dusty steps. She climbed for what felt like an eternity, finally emerging onto a small, unadorned landing. Another door, this one unmarked, stood before her. She tried the handle. It was unlocked.

She pushed it open slowly, revealing a short, dimly lit corridor. At the end of the corridor was another door, this one unmistakably familiar. It was green, with a heavy brass knocker. The Green Door. She had found a back entrance, a service passage that led directly to the mysterious room. And there was no guard in sight.

A wave of exhilaration washed over her, quickly followed by a surge of caution. This was too easy. Had Maxwell not thought of this? Or was it simply an oversight, a loophole in his security? She listened intently. No sounds from within the room. No footsteps in the corridor behind her. The only sound was the distant murmur of the hotel, and the steady beat of her own heart.

She reached the Green Door, her hand hovering over the knocker. What should she do? Knock? Try the handle? What if it was locked from the inside? What if Maxwell was waiting on the other side? Her mind flashed back to Francine's words: "The room I am searching for has a green door." And Maxwell's rage. This room held something important, something explosive.

Penny took a deep breath. She had come this far. She couldn't turn back now. Slowly, she reached for the brass handle, twisting it gently. It turned. The door was unlocked.

Her breath hitched. She pushed the door inward, inch by cautious inch. The room beyond was dark, the heavy drapes drawn tight against the afternoon sun. A faint, stale smell hung in the air, a mixture of cigar smoke and old paper. As her eyes adjusted to the gloom, Penny stepped inside, her senses on high alert.

The room was larger than she expected, a luxurious suite, not just a single room. There was a sitting area with plush armchairs and a small desk, and beyond that, she could make out the shape of a bed. But it was the desk that immediately drew her attention. Papers were scattered across its surface, some crumpled, others neatly stacked. A half-empty glass of amber liquid stood beside an ashtray overflowing with cigar stubs.

And then she saw it. Lying prominently on top of a stack of documents, was a small, velvet-covered box. It was perhaps six inches long, four inches wide, and an inch thick. It looked old, possibly antique, and very important. This must be the "package" the busboy mentioned. The "transfer."

Penny moved towards the desk, her footsteps muffled by the thick carpet. She reached for the box, her fingers trembling slightly. As she touched the velvet, a sudden, sharp click echoed through the room.

Penny froze, her hand still on the box. The sound had come from behind her. She whirled around, her heart leaping into her throat.

Standing in the doorway, framed against the dimly lit corridor, was Francine Sellberg. A triumphant, almost predatory smile played on her lips. She held a small, antique camera, still pointed directly at Penny.

"Well, well, Penny Parker," Francine said, her voice a low purr. "It seems you've found your way into the Green Room after all. And just in time for me to catch you red-handed, prying into private property."
Penny glared at her. "You followed me," she accused, her voice tight with anger.
"Clever girl, aren't you?" Francine chuckled, stepping further into the room, her eyes glinting in the dim light. "I saw you sneak out of the main elevator. I also saw you enter the service stairwell. It wasn't hard to guess where a back passage might lead. Especially when one is a seasoned reporter with an eye for detail, unlike some." She gestured pointedly at the velvet box. "Going to open that, are we? Or perhaps you've already found what you were looking for?"
"What do you want, Francine?" Penny demanded, her mind racing for an escape route, a way out of this trap.
"What do I want?" Francine feigned surprise. "Why, the same thing you want, dear Penny. A story. And it seems you've just handed me the lead, neatly tied up with a bow." She adjusted the focus on her camera. "Miss Penny Parker, daughter of the esteemed Anthony Parker, caught trespassing and attempting to pilfer confidential documents from a private hotel suite. Oh, the headlines practically write themselves! The _Riverview Star_ reporter turns common thief."
"I wasn't stealing anything!" Penny retorted, her cheeks flushing with indignation. "I was investigating. And besides, what gives you the right to break in here?"
"Break in?" Francine raised an eyebrow. "I simply followed you through an open door, dear. No forced entry necessary. And I'm merely documenting a crime in progress. Think of me as a public servant." Her smile widened, devoid of warmth. "Now, why don't you step away from the desk, Penny, and let's see what interesting trinkets you've discovered."

Before Penny could reply, a third voice cut through the tense silence.
"What in blazes is going on here?"
Harvey Maxwell stood in the doorway, his face a thundercloud. His eyes, already red-rimmed from stress, darted from Francine to Penny, then to the open door and the velvet box on the desk. He clearly had not expected to find two unauthorized individuals in the highly secret room.
"Maxwell!" Francine exclaimed, her composure wavering for the first time. "I was just… I caught her! Penny Parker, in the act! She was trying to steal something!"
Maxwell ignored her, his furious gaze fixed on Penny. "You! I told you to leave this hotel! What are you doing in here?" His voice was a low growl, barely contained.
"I found a back entrance," Penny said, trying to sound defiant, though her knees felt weak. "And I wasn't stealing. I found this box, and I think it's connected to something important, something you're trying to hide!"
Maxwell took a step into the room, his hand reaching for the velvet box. "This is none of your business, you meddling girl! Get out of here, both of you, before I call the police!"
"Call the police, Mr. Maxwell?" Francine interjected, quickly regaining her professional demeanor. "And explain why you have two reporters in a locked hotel suite, one of whom has photographic evidence of the other's supposed 'crime'? Not to mention whatever is in that box. That sounds like a fascinating story in itself, doesn't it?" She raised her camera again, aiming it at Maxwell. "Perhaps a photo of the hotel manager attempting to confiscate evidence?"
Maxwell stopped, his hand hovering inches from the box. He was caught. Francine had him, and Penny, too, in a web of his own making. His face contorted with frustrated fury.
"You think this is a game?" Maxwell hissed, his eyes narrowed. "You two have no idea what you're dabbling in. This is far more serious than a newspaper scoop. You're putting lives at risk!"
"Whose lives, Mr. Maxwell?" Penny challenged. "The mysterious guest? Or yours? What exactly is so dangerous about this 'package'?"
"I won't say another word," Maxwell declared, his jaw clenched. He turned to Francine. "You, miss. Delete that photograph. And then you will both leave this room. Now."
"I think not," Francine replied, a cool smile returning to her lips. "This photograph is my insurance, Mr. Maxwell. And I haven't even begun to explore the contents of this fascinating room, or the mysterious box." She walked over to the desk, her eyes alight with journalistic zeal, and with a delicate touch, opened the velvet lid.

Penny and Maxwell both leaned forward, their eyes fixed on the small box. Inside, nestled on a bed of crimson satin, lay a single, gleaming, emerald necklace. The gems sparkled even in the dim light, radiating an intense, almost otherworldly green. Each stone was perfectly cut, exquisitely set, clearly of immense value.

"Good heavens," Francine breathed, momentarily forgetting her journalistic detachment. "It's magnificent."
"The 'package'," Penny murmured, understanding dawning on her. It wasn't papers, or documents. It was stolen jewelry, or something equally illicit, being transported under the guise of a secret hotel guest.
Maxwell's face had gone pale, his anger now replaced by a chilling fear. "You fools," he whispered, his voice hoarse. "You don't understand. That isn't just jewelry. That's… that's the Serpent's Eye."
"The Serpent's Eye?" Francine repeated, her brow furrowed in concentration. "I've heard of that. A legendary emerald necklace, said to be cursed, stolen years ago from the collection of an Eastern potentate. It was believed to have disappeared forever."
"It did," Maxwell said, his voice barely audible. "Until now. It was brought here, to be discreetly transferred. A very particular client, a very specific deal. And now you two have stumbled into it. You've blown everything."
"Discreetly transferred by whom?" Penny pressed, her mind piecing together the fragments. "Who is the 'new guest'?"
"That information is not for your ears," Maxwell snapped, trying to regain some semblance of authority. "But I assure you, the people involved in this transfer are not to be trifled with. They will not take kindly to exposure. They will not take kindly to their plans being ruined by two inquisitive reporters." He cast a meaningful glance at the necklace. "Especially if the 'package' is compromised."

Just then, the door to an adjoining room, previously unnoticed by Penny, slowly opened. A tall, impeccably dressed man, with sharp, intelligent eyes and a thin, cruel mouth, stepped into the suite. He held a small, automatic pistol in his gloved hand.
"Compromised, Mr. Maxwell?" the man said, his voice smooth and cold as ice. "I believe that is precisely the word you used. Care to explain why my private suite has been invaded by a pair of amateur snoops and a blustering hotel manager, and why my property is being openly displayed?"
His eyes settled on the open velvet box and the glittering emeralds. Then, they moved to Francine's camera, still clutched in her hand, and finally, to Penny. A faint, humorless smile touched his lips. "It seems we have a problem."

Penny felt a cold dread seep into her bones. Maxwell's fear was entirely justified. This was no mere hotel scandal. This was something far more dangerous, far more sinister. The glint in the stranger's eyes, the casual way he held the pistol, spoke volumes without a single word. They were in deep trouble. Deeper than Penny had ever been before.

"Mr. Ivanov," Maxwell stammered, his face ashen. "I assure you, this is all a misunderstanding. These two young women are merely reporters, curious about the hotel. They will be removed immediately."
"Reporters?" Ivanov chuckled, a dry, rasping sound. "Curious, indeed. And they have just discovered the Serpent's Eye. A remarkable coincidence, wouldn't you agree?" He walked slowly towards the desk, his gaze unwavering, his every movement radiating a quiet menace. "I had heard whispers that the _Riverview Star_ was becoming a nuisance. But to send one of your own, Mr. Parker, into my private affairs? That shows remarkable audacity, or perhaps, profound stupidity." His eyes fixed on Penny. "You are Anthony Parker's daughter, aren't you?"
Penny remained silent, her mind racing, searching desperately for a way out. Her usual quick wit seemed to have deserted her in the face of such chilling composure.
"So, the little bird has flown too close to the sun," Ivanov mused, picking up the necklace from the box. The emeralds shimmered in his grasp. "And now she is caught." He turned the necklace over in his fingers, admiring its brilliance, seemingly unconcerned by the two women and the terrified hotel manager. "This piece is worth a fortune. But its true value lies in its power to influence, to secure alliances, to silence enemies. It is not something to be played with by children."

Francine, ever the opportunist, suddenly snapped another picture, her camera clicking loudly in the quiet room. Ivanov's head whipped up, his eyes narrowing to slits.
"A mistake, Miss Reporter," he said, his voice losing its smooth edge, replaced by a cutting harshness. "A very grave mistake." He took two quick strides and snatched the camera from Francine's hand, crushing it effortlessly in his gloved fist. The sound of splintering plastic and glass was sickening. "That will be the last photograph you take today."
Francine stared at her ruined camera, her face a mixture of shock and anger. "You can't do that!" she cried.
"Oh, but I can," Ivanov replied, a cold smile playing on his lips. "And I can do much more." He gestured with the hand still holding the pistol towards the adjacent room. "I believe it's time for a private discussion, wouldn't you agree, Mr. Maxwell? And you two inquisitive young ladies will join us. We have much to talk about."

Penny looked at Francine, then at Maxwell, whose face was a mask of utter despair. The elegant hotel suite, once a symbol of luxury and comfort, had suddenly become a gilded cage. The "Green Room" had revealed its secret, but in doing so, it had also trapped them in a perilous game, the rules of which were set by a man with cold eyes and a deadly weapon. There was no escape now. Not from this room, and certainly not from the dangerous forces they had unwittingly unleashed. The Serpent's Eye had truly cast its spell.
