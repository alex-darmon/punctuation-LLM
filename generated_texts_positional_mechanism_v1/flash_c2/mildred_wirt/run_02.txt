The sun beat down on the deserted cove, shimmering on the wet rocks. Clara nudged Bess with her elbow. "Look," she whispered, pointing towards the craggy outcrop where the old lighthouse stood, its light long since extinguished. "There's old Mr. Finch again. What do you suppose he's doing?"

Bess squinted against the glare. Mr. Finch, a stooped figure known for his solitary rambles, was meticulously arranging a collection of seashells and smooth stones on a flat section of rock. He placed each object with precise care, stepping back occasionally to survey his work, then leaning forward to adjust a tiny pebble.

"Probably just tidying up," Bess said, shrugging. "He spends half his day collecting bits of rubbish from the tide line."

"Rubbish?" Clara scoffed. "Look at him, Bess. He's not just 'tidying up.' That's a pattern, a very deliberate one. See how the white shells are in a line, and the dark stones form a triangle? It's a code, I tell you!"

"A code for what?" Bess asked, unconvinced. "Who would Mr. Finch be sending secret messages to, the gulls?"

"Don't be silly," Clara retorted, a thrill of adventure tingling through her. "Maybe it's a map. A treasure map! There are old pirate legends about this coast, you know."

They watched for another ten minutes. Mr. Finch finished his arrangement, gave it a final, scrutinizing look, then slowly gathered his basket and walking stick. "Now's our chance!" Clara exclaimed, springing up. "Come on, Bess, let's see what secrets he left behind."

Bess sighed, but her curiosity was piqued despite herself. "Alright, but if it's just a pile of pretty rocks, I told you so."

They scrambled over the rocks to the spot where Mr. Finch had been. His 'code' was a simple pattern indeed: three white shells in a row, then two dark stones forming a small V shape beneath them, followed by another white shell. It looked rather like a stylized bird.

"See?" Bess said, a note of triumph in her voice. "Just a bird. He probably likes birds."

"But why here?" Clara countered, circling the arrangement. "And why so exact? And why was he so careful?" She began to poke around the surrounding rocks, digging her fingers into the sand and loose gravel. "He must have hidden something. A message, perhaps, beneath the bird symbol!"

The ground proved hard and unyielding. They dug for several minutes, overturning loose stones and scraping away at the packed earth, but found nothing more than sand, grit, and the occasional piece of dried seaweed.

"Nothing," Bess stated flatly. "Just like I said. You're wasting your energy, Clara."
