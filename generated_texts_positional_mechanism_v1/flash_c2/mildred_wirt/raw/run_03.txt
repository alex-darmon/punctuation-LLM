It was relatively quiet, a mere trickle of guests heading in or out, none of whom paid any attention to her. Francine, still looking a little pale but resolute, followed closely behind.

Inside the south wing, the corridor was strangely deserted. The usual faint sounds of hotel activity were drowned out by the distant shouts and the lingering echoes of the emergency whistle. This was it. This was the window they had created.

Penny pulled out the green ticket she had found, clutching it tightly. She knew the Green Room was on the second floor, somewhere in this wing. With the hotel's attention diverted, she had a clear path, for now. She pressed the button for the second floor, and the elevator doors slid open.

She remembered Francine's earlier "guess" that she was in the wrong part of the hotel, a clear bluff that now only confirmed Penny's suspicion that the Green Room was indeed on this floor. She reached the unmarked door at the end of the wing, the one she had approached yesterday before Maxwell's furious intervention. Her heart hammered in her chest. She listened intently. No sound. The guard, usually at his post before the Green Door, was conspicuously absent. The bob-sled incident had drawn him away, just as they had planned.

Taking a deep breath, Penny pushed open the door and stepped into the narrow, dimly lit hall. The Green Door itself loomed before her, a solid, imposing barrier. There was no guard, no one to challenge her. The green ticket felt like a heavy weight in her hand.

She approached the door, her fingers tracing the smooth, cool wood. This was the moment. She slid the green card into a slot she now noticed on the side of the doorframe. There was a faint click, and then a soft whirring sound. The heavy Green Door swung inward, revealing a glimpse of the mysteries within.

Penny stepped over the threshold, her pulse racing. The room was not what she had expected. It was not a lavish gambling den, nor a secret meeting place for shadowy figures. Instead, it was a surprisingly simple, functional room, albeit meticulously tidy. A large table dominated the center, covered with maps and blueprints. Various scientific instruments, unfamiliar to Penny, lay neatly arranged on a side bench. Charts filled with intricate diagrams hung on the walls. One wall was entirely taken up by a large, almost futuristic-looking control panel, studded with an array of flashing lights and glowing dials.

Her gaze fell upon a man seated at the main table, his back to the door, hunched over a complex set of calculations. He was middle-aged, his hair streaked with gray, and wore a thick tweed jacket. He seemed utterly engrossed in his work, oblivious to her presence, or to the pandemonium outside.

"Excuse me," Penny ventured, her voice a little shaky in the quiet room.

The man jumped, startled, his head snapping up. He spun around in his chair, his eyes wide with surprise, then narrowed with suspicion as he took in Penny's figure. He had a pair of thick spectacles perched on his nose, which he now removed, peering at her with an intense, academic gaze.

"Who are you?" he demanded, his voice thin but firm. "How did you get in here? This is a private research facility. It is strictly off-limits!" He glanced at the open door, then back at Penny, his confusion evident.

"I... I have a pass," Penny stammered, holding up the green ticket. "I was given this."

The man rose slowly from his chair, his gaze fixed on the ticket, then on Penny's face. He seemed to recognize the card, for his expression softened slightly, though a trace of bewilderment remained. "A pass? From whom? No one should be issuing these. My work requires absolute discretion."

"I... I found it," Penny admitted, deciding honesty, at least in part, was the best policy. "Last night. I was told it was for the Green Room."

The man frowned, running a hand through his graying hair. "Found it, you say? That is highly irregular. This is Dr. Alistair Finch's private laboratory. You must be mistaken. There is no 'Green Room' here in the sense you imply. This is where I conduct my meteorological research, specifically my studies on controlled atmospheric conditions and localized weather patterns." He gestured vaguely towards the control panel.

Penny's mind reeled. Meteorological research? Controlled atmospheric conditions? This was not what she had expected. She had envisioned a secret gambling room, or perhaps a place where Maxwell was conducting illicit business dealings. Not a scientific laboratory.

"Meteorological research?" Penny repeated, trying to process this unexpected revelation. "But... what does this have to do with the Fergus hotel? And Harvey Maxwell? And why all the secrecy? The hotel staff, they seem to think this room is something entirely different."

Dr. Finch sighed, rubbing his temples. "Secrecy is paramount to my work, young lady. The hotel was simply a convenient, secluded location for my equipment and experiments. Mr. Maxwell, the hotel manager, was hired to ensure my privacy and to keep curious individuals, like yourself, at bay. He is, shall we say, rather overzealous in his duties, particularly when it comes to maintaining an air of mystery. He believed that an enigmatic 'Green Room' would be more effective in deterring snoopers than a simple 'No Entry: Private Laboratory' sign. He thrives on such theatricality."

A sudden thought struck Penny. "Then the snowmaking machines... the unusual weather patterns... are they connected to your research?"

Dr. Finch's eyes lit up, his academic fervor momentarily overcoming his annoyance. "Precisely! An astute observation, young lady. My current project involves the localized manipulation of weather systems, particularly snow generation. I have been experimenting with methods to create precise snowfall for recreational purposes, such as ski resorts, without relying solely on natural conditions. The recent unusual snowfalls and sudden thaws are all part of my controlled tests. The hotel's location, high in the mountains, provides an ideal environment for these delicate experiments."

Penny stared at him, dumbfounded. The "big story" Francine was chasing, the strange occurrences at the hotel, the rumors of mysterious activity – all of it boiled down to a reclusive meteorologist and his weather experiments. It was a story, certainly, but not the one they had imagined.

"But... why the extreme secrecy?" Penny pressed. "And why did Mr. Maxwell go to such lengths to keep everyone away, even threatening them? It seemed much more sinister than simply protecting a scientific endeavor."

Dr. Finch's expression darkened again. "The potential applications of my research, if successful, are enormous, both for industry and for... other interests. Certain individuals would pay a great deal for my findings. Mr. Maxwell was tasked with protecting my work, and me, from industrial espionage and potential sabotage. He, unfortunately, has a rather heavy-handed approach to such matters. He probably believed that creating an atmosphere of dark secrets and forbidding mystery was the most effective way to keep people from prying. And he certainly relishes the power it gives him."

A sudden sound from the corridor startled them both. Footsteps, heavy and urgent, were approaching the open door. Dr. Finch's eyes widened in alarm.

"Someone is coming!" he whispered, his voice laced with panic. "Maxwell must have learned about the breach! You must leave immediately! I cannot afford to be discovered like this!"

Before Penny could react, the door burst open. Harvey Maxwell stood there, his face contorted with fury, his eyes blazing. He had clearly rushed back from the bob-sled incident, alerted by a flustered Francine or perhaps by the realization that the Green Room had been left unguarded.

"Parker!" Maxwell roared, his gaze fixed on Penny, then sweeping to the open door and the startled Dr. Finch. "I knew it! I knew you were up to no good! Snooping around, breaking into private property! I'll have you arrested! And you, Dr. Finch, what is the meaning of this? Why is this girl in your lab?"

Dr. Finch, looking utterly terrified, stammered, "Mr. Maxwell, I... I assure you, this is a misunderstanding. She just walked in. I was just about to..."

Penny tried to pull away, but his grip was like iron. She glanced desperately at Dr. Finch, but the meteorologist seemed too overwhelmed to offer any assistance. Her clever plan had certainly worked in getting her into the Green Room, but it appeared to have landed her in an even worse predicament, now caught red-handed by the very man she sought to expose. The thrilling success of the diversion had turned into a terrifying capture. This was definitely not going to be her lucky day, after all.

Maxwell’s grip on Penny’s arm was painful, his fingers digging into her flesh. She winced, but refused to cry out, her eyes blazing with a defiance that only seemed to fuel his rage. Dr. Finch, trembling visibly, cowered against a workbench, his earlier scientific bravado completely evaporated. The air in the Green Room crackled with animosity, thick and suffocating.

"Don't think you can escape the consequences this time, Miss Parker!" Maxwell snarled, giving her arm a sharp tug that nearly unbalanced her. "You have trespassed, you have illegally entered a restricted area, and you have interfered with confidential research! I'll see to it that you face the full extent of the law!"

"I haven't interfered with anything!" Penny retorted, struggling against his hold. "And as for trespassing, I had a ticket to get in! A valid one!"

Maxwell merely snorted, a contemptuous sound. "A ticket you obtained under false pretenses, no doubt, or perhaps even stole! I warned you to stay away from this hotel, but you are as stubborn and meddlesome as your father! And just as intent on stirring up trouble where none exists!"

Her gaze darted towards Dr. Finch, a silent plea for help, but the scientist merely wrung his hands, his face pale with fear. He was clearly too intimidated by Maxwell to offer any aid.

"Dr. Finch, tell him!" Penny exclaimed, hoping to jolt the meteorologist into action. "Tell him I was just asking questions, that I didn't mean any harm! Tell him about your experiments!"

Maxwell paused, turning his furious glare towards the cowering doctor. "Experiments? What experiments is she talking about, Finch? Have you been divulging confidential information to this... this amateur sleuth?"

"No, Mr. Maxwell, no, not at all!" Dr. Finch stammered, his voice barely a whisper. "She... she just came in, and I was explaining the nature of my work, just generally, you understand, nothing specific. I was about to ask her to leave, when you arrived!"

"A likely story!" Maxwell sneered. "You're either a fool or a traitor, Finch. Perhaps both! This whole operation will be compromised if word gets out about your... 'weather control' experiments, as you so grandly call them!"

The implication that Finch's work was vulnerable, or worse, that Maxwell might expose it himself, seemed to galvanize the doctor, if only slightly. "Mr. Maxwell, please! You must not involve the authorities! The media attention, the scrutiny, it would be disastrous for my research! For the hotel's reputation! For everything!"

And you, Finch, will be held personally responsible for this security breach!"

As they passed through the doorway, Penny caught a fleeting glimpse of Dr. Finch, still huddled against the workbench, looking utterly defeated. His scientific passion had given way entirely to fear.

The guard, who had been sitting dutifully at his post, now stood up, looking alarmed by the commotion. "Mr. Maxwell, is everything all right?" he asked, his voice hesitant.

"A forged card, Johnson! Or one she obtained by deception!" Maxwell cut him off, clearly not wanting any further questions. He propelled Penny down the narrow hall, his momentum carrying them quickly towards the unmarked door at the wing's entrance.

The hallway was quiet, devoid of other guests or staff. Francine Sellberg was nowhere in sight. Penny felt a surge of despair. She was alone, completely at Maxwell's mercy, and he clearly intended to make an example of her. She remembered his earlier threat about having her arrested. What if he truly meant it? The ride down was agonizingly silent, punctuated only by the soft hum of the elevator machinery. Penny stood as far away from Maxwell as the small enclosure would allow, her mind racing. She had to think of something, anything, to turn the tables. She had the green ticket, yes, but Maxwell had dismissed it as forged. She had the knowledge of Dr. Finch's experiments, but Maxwell wouldn't believe her, and Finch himself was too terrified to corroborate.

The elevator doors slid open onto the bustling hotel lobby. The sudden change from the quiet upper floors to the lively ground floor was disorienting. Guests milled about, some checking in, others heading towards the dining room. A bellhop was assisting a new arrival with her luggage. Maxwell, however, seemed oblivious to the curious glances he might attract. His focus was entirely on getting Penny out of the hotel. He steered her firmly towards the revolving doors leading outside.

"Now, Miss Parker," he growled, his voice low and menacing, "I am going to release you, and you are going to walk out of this hotel, and you are never going to set foot inside it again. Do you understand?"

Penny glared at him, refusing to answer. She knew that if she left now, she would have failed completely. Her father would be disappointed, Francine would have won, and Maxwell would have gotten away with whatever secrets he was truly hiding, beyond Finch's weather experiments. Maxwell hesitated for a fraction of a second. He clearly did not want to involve Mrs. Downey's staff in this unpleasantness. "No problem at all, Miss Miller. Just escorting an... unwanted guest from the premises."

A hush fell over a small section of the lobby. Heads turned. Maxine Miller's jaw dropped slightly. Maxwell's face turned a dangerous shade of red.

Maxwell, furious and flustered, stepped towards Penny, his hand raised as if to grab her again. "

"I have been trying to save him!" Finch cried, turning back to them, tears streaming down his face. "I have been trying to undo what I inadvertently helped him create! Maxwell was merely providing the funding, the secrecy, for my research. He did not know the full extent of it. No one did."

"That is a lie!" Maxwell suddenly burst out, his voice regaining some of its earlier force, though it was now tinged with panic. "I was told he was researching advanced cryogenics for medical applications! For preserving organs, for reversing disease! Not... not this horror!" He gestured wildly at the chamber containing the transformed professor. "I would never have allowed this! I would never have funded this monstrosity!"

"You were too eager for the profits, Maxwell," Penny said, her voice filled with a quiet anger. "You didn't ask enough questions. You just wanted whatever Dr. Finch promised you, without concern for the ethics or the safety. You wanted to own the secret, didn't you? To profit from a breakthrough, no matter the cost."

Maxwell opened his mouth to retort, but no words came out. He looked at the glowing figure of Professor Albright, then at the furious gaze of Senator Davies, and finally at Penny, his face crumpling. He knew he was trapped. His hotel, his reputation, his entire enterprise was about to crumble.

"Dr. Finch," Senator Davies said, his voice now devoid of any trace of politeness, "this is a matter of profound ethical and scientific concern. It is also a criminal matter. You have, by your own admission, subjected a human being to an unauthorized and dangerous experiment, resulting in his horrific transformation. Mr. Maxwell, you are complicit in concealing this from the authorities and the family. We need to secure this facility immediately. Miss Miller, please contact the local police and then Professor Albright's wife. Tell them everything."

Maxine, her face still pale but her eyes now burning with a fierce determination, nodded. She pulled out a small notepad and pencil, already scribbling notes. "Understood, Senator," she said, her voice clear. "I will also contact a team of independent scientific experts. This story needs to be handled with extreme care and accuracy."

"And Miss Parker," Senator Davies continued, turning to Penny. "Your instincts were remarkably accurate. You have uncovered something truly extraordinary and deeply disturbing. You have served a great public good today."

Penny felt a surge of triumph, but it was quickly overshadowed by a deep sadness as she looked back at the glowing, crystalline form of Professor Albright. He had been a kind man, a brilliant man, lost to a terrible ambition. This was not the kind of story she had imagined breaking, not the kind of victory she had sought.

Finch had collapsed into a chair, burying his face in his hands, muttering incoherently about the pursuit of knowledge and the unpredictable nature of discovery. Maxwell stood beside him, a defeated and broken man, his dreams of secret profits shattered along with his composure.

The heavy silence in the Green Room was broken only by the hum of the machinery, the soft hiss of the cryo-chambers, and Finch's pained whispers. The antiseptic scent now seemed to mingle with the metallic tang of fear and despair.

"This entire facility must be sealed off," Senator Davies instructed, addressing the guard who still stood nervously by the door. "No one is to enter or leave. Secure all data, all equipment. Everything. This is a crime scene, and a scientific emergency."

The guard, looking utterly bewildered but recognizing the authority in the Senator's voice, nodded and hurried out of the room, presumably to alert other staff or contact outside authorities.

Maxine was already dialing the hotel's front desk to request an outside line. "This is Maxine Miller," she said into the phone, her voice regaining its professional edge. "I need to make an urgent call to the Riverview Police Department. And then to Professor Albright's residence."

Penny remained by the large cryo-chamber, gazing at the frozen, luminescent form of Professor Albright. The pulsating light within him seemed to dim slightly, as if mirroring the tragic reality of his fate. She had found her story, a truly monumental one, but the cost was far greater than she could have ever imagined. The "Green Room" had indeed held a secret, but it was a secret of scientific hubris, human tragedy, and profound ethical failure. She knew the days ahead would be filled with investigations, scientific analysis, and heartbreaking news for the Albright family. The tranquil facade of the Fergus Hotel, and indeed the entire scientific community, would be shaken to its core.

She felt a hand on her shoulder. It was Senator Davies. "You're a remarkable young woman, Miss Parker," he said gently. "You pursued the truth, no matter how difficult or dangerous. Riverview, and indeed the world, will be better for your courage."

Penny offered him a small, sad smile. "I only wanted to make sure Professor Albright was safe, Senator," she replied, her gaze still fixed on the transformed scientist. "I never imagined this."

"Sometimes," Davies said with a sigh, "the truth is far stranger, and far more tragic, than any fiction we could invent." He paused, then added, "You should be very proud of what you've done today, Penny."

But pride felt like a distant, almost inappropriate emotion in the face of such profound sorrow. The mystery was solved, but the happy ending was nowhere in sight. Penny knew her green ticket had led her not to a simple scoop, but to a profound reckoning. The quiet hum of the machinery in the Green Room seemed to echo the silent scream of a man lost to his own ambition, a chilling reminder of the unforeseen consequences of unchecked scientific exploration. She would have to write this story, every single devastating detail. It would be the biggest story of her young career, but it would also be the hardest.

CHAPTER 15 A COLD TRUTH

The days that followed were a blur of activity. The Fergus Hotel, once a place of quiet luxury and veiled secrets, was now a hive of police investigation and scientific scrutiny. Detectives swarmed the Green Room, meticulously documenting every piece of equipment, every flask, every glowing component. Experts from various scientific fields, including cryogenics, metallurgy, and cellular biology, were flown in to assess Dr. Finch's experimental setup and, more importantly, the crystalline state of Professor Albright. The hotel’s reputation plummeted, its elegant lobby now frequented by grim-faced officials and distraught family members, rather than ski enthusiasts.

Maxine Miller, true to her word, had ensured the story broke with unparalleled speed and accuracy. Her newspaper, along with Penny’s _Riverview Star_, published front-page exposes detailing the horrifying transformation of Professor Albright and the illicit activities taking place in the hotel’s secret laboratory. The scientific community reeled, grappling with the ethical implications of Finch’s reckless experiments. Penny, alongside Maxine, provided detailed accounts, their reports painting a vivid and chilling picture of a brilliant mind lost to a dangerous quest for scientific immortality.

Penny found herself hailed as a hero, her name on everyone's lips. Her father, initially furious about her independent investigation, was now brimming with a reluctant pride. "You've got a nose for news, Penny," he admitted, a rare compliment. "A better one than most seasoned reporters I know." The "yellow" sheet accusation from Maxwell now seemed ironic, given the stark, factual reporting that dominated their front page.

However, the weight of the story pressed heavily on Penny. The image of Professor Albright, frozen and glowing, haunted her nights. She had expected to uncover corruption or perhaps a gambling ring, but not such a profound and unsettling tragedy. The scientific debate over Professor Albright’s condition raged on. Some experts believed he was irretrievably lost, others clung to the hope that his crystalline form might one day be reversed. Dr. Finch, utterly broken, was taken into custody, facing multiple charges of reckless endangerment and unauthorized human experimentation. Harvey Maxwell, facing ruin and criminal charges for his complicity and attempted cover-up, was quickly ousted from his position and disappeared from public view.

Senator Davies, true to his character, ensured a thorough and transparent investigation. He met with Penny several times, commending her bravery and insight. "You've saved countless others from potentially falling prey to such unethical practices, Penny," he told her during one of their meetings. "This case will set precedents, I believe, for the regulation of experimental research."

Even Francine Sellberg, humbled by the sheer magnitude of Penny's scoop, approached her in the hotel lobby a few days later, a strange look on her face. "Parker," she said, her usual smirk replaced by a grudging respect. "You really did it. A real story. A big one. I... I underestimated you."

"We could have worked together, Francine," Penny reminded her gently.

Francine sighed, running a hand through her perfectly coiffed hair. "I know. My mistake. Next time, perhaps." She offered a rare, genuine smile. "But for now, congratulations. You've earned this one."

Penny appreciated the unexpected olive branch, but her mind was elsewhere. She found herself often visiting the Green Room, now stripped of its working machinery, its secrets laid bare. The large cryo-chamber remained, Professor Albright's glowing form still suspended within, a perpetual, silent sentinel. His wife, a kindly woman with tear-filled eyes, would also visit, simply sitting there, holding his hand through the thick glass, whispering to him.

One afternoon, Penny found herself alone in the now empty corridor leading to the Green Room. The guard was gone, replaced by a police officer stationed further down the hall. The chill still lingered in the air, a constant reminder. She leaned against the wall, staring at the green door, no longer a symbol of intrigue, but of sorrow. The green ticket she had found seemed so insignificant now, a mere catalyst for a much larger, more devastating truth.

Her thoughts turned to Sara Jasko. She hadn't seen Sara since that morning, her own mission having taken such a drastic turn. She wondered if Sara had managed to slip away to Mrs. Downey’s bob-sled run. The carefree world of skiing and youthful adventures seemed a million miles away from the stark realities of the Green Room.

A soft voice broke her reverie. "Penny?"

She turned to see Maxine Miller standing beside her, a cup of coffee in her hand. "Still contemplating the abyss?" Maxine asked, a weary smile on her face.

"Something like that," Penny admitted. "It's a lot to take in."

"It is," Maxine agreed. "But you handled it beautifully. You held your nerve, Penny. You saw through the smoke and mirrors."

"I just wanted to find out what was going on," Penny said, looking back at the green door. "I never intended to stumble upon... this. It feels like such a hollow victory."

"It's not hollow, Penny," Maxine said firmly. "It's a necessary one. Think of the potential for harm if this had continued unchecked. Think of the people who might have been exploited, the lives that might have been ruined, all for a twisted version of scientific glory or corporate greed. You stopped it."

Penny nodded slowly, accepting the truth in Maxine’s words. She had done her job, and she had done it well. But the thrill of the chase was gone, replaced by a profound understanding of the responsibilities that came with uncovering such difficult truths.

"What will happen to Professor Albright?" Penny asked, her voice soft.

Maxine sighed. "No one knows for sure. The scientific community is in an uproar. There's talk of forming a specialized task force, of transferring him to a state-of-the-art research facility. But reversing his condition... it's unprecedented. A long shot, at best."

"And Dr. Finch?"

"He'll face justice, Penny. As will Maxwell. Their careers, their reputations, are over. But the damage they caused, especially to Professor Albright and his family, will last a lifetime."

Penny closed her eyes for a moment, letting the weight of it all settle upon her. She was a reporter, and her job was to tell the story, to expose the truth. She had done that, with courage and tenacity. The adventure had been far darker than she could have ever imagined, but she had seen it through to the end.

"I think I need a break from mysteries for a while," Penny said, finally opening her eyes.

Maxine chuckled softly. "I doubt that'll last long, Penny. It's in your blood, isn't it? The pursuit of the hidden, the unveiling of the unknown. Just be careful, and always, always trust your instincts."

Penny smiled, a genuine, albeit tired, smile. "I will," she promised. The Fergus Hotel, once a place of intrigue, was now a monument to a cold, hard truth. But even in its quiet devastation, a new resolve had taken root within Penny Parker. She might crave a temporary respite from the shadows, but she knew, deep in her bones, that her future would always be found in the relentless search for truth, no matter how unsettling or challenging it might prove to be. The green ticket had opened a door, and through it, Penny had stepped into a world far more complex and dangerous than she had ever envisioned, emerging stronger and wiser, a true reporter forged in the fire of an extraordinary, if tragic, scoop.
