LORD ARTHUR. Nay, Toby, an I could but grasp the notion, I would commend your patience. You are a saint for suffering such a fool. Thou wilt endure a sermon on a radish. Thou wilt smile at a wasp that stings thee.
SIR TOBY. And you, my lord, will snap at shadows. You will pick a quarrel with the air for stirring your hair. Peace, Arthur, peace.
LORD ARTHUR. Peace? I know no peace where folly reigns, where boorish men do strut and puff like pigeons. My patience is a thread, Sir Toby, worn too thin. See, yonder comes a fellow, Master Leo.
SIR TOBY. Your foe again? I pray you, hold your tongue; forbear all strife.
MASTER LEO. Good day, gentlemen. I trust the morning finds you well.
LORD ARTHUR. Well enough to see a hypocrite. Sir, your face is like a book of lies. It promises fair weather, but holds a storm.
MASTER LEO. What insolence is this? I offered greeting, not a challenge. Your wit is ever keen to wound, Lord Arthur.
LORD ARTHUR. And your honour is ever prone to bleed. You borrowed coin from me, a fortnight past. Where is my gold?
MASTER LEO. Gold? What gold, sir? I recall no debt. Your memory plays you false. My purse is empty of your favour.
LORD ARTHUR. Empty? It is as empty as your conscience. You are a cheat, a knave, a thief!


MASTER LEO.
A thief? I am no thief. You lie, Lord Arthur. Your tongue is venomous. It slanders honest men.
SIR TOBY.
Forbear, good sirs. This bickering is ill.
LORD ARTHUR.
Ill? It is the truth. He owes me gold. He denies it now. He is a base-born dog.
MASTER LEO.
Base-born? I am as nobly bred as you. My honour is untainted. Your words are but the wind.
LORD ARTHUR.
The wind? It carries truth. It whispers in the street. You are a debtor, sir.
MASTER LEO.
I owe you nought. My conscience is quite clear. My coffers are my own. They hold no borrowed coin.
SIR TOBY.
Pray, gentlemen, calm your hot blood. This public quarrel shames you. Do not draw the sword.
LORD ARTHUR.
He begs the sword. He tempts my very hand. My patience is quite spent.
MASTER LEO.
Your threats are hollow, sir. I fear them not. What proof have you of debt?
LORD ARTHUR.
Proof? My word is proof. Your promise was my bond. You swore to pay me back.
MASTER LEO.
I swore no such thing. Your mind is full of fancies. You dream of debts that are not.
SIR TOBY.
Perhaps a misunderstanding, sirs. A slip of memory, perchance?
LORD ARTHUR.
No slip. No chance

LORD ARTHUR. No. No. It is no chance. It is a calculated lie. You are a liar, sir. You are a wretch.
MASTER LEO. I am no liar. I am no wretch. I am an honest man. Your words are poison.
SIR TOBY. Gentlemen, I beg you. Think of your repute. The folk do stare.
LORD ARTHUR. Let them stare. Let them know his shame. He is a debtor. He is a knave.
MASTER LEO. My coffers are full. My honour is clear. I owe you nought.
LORD ARTHUR. You owe me gold. You swore it on your honour. That honour is a shadow.
MASTER LEO. My honour is my shield. It guards me from your slanders. You are a madman.
LORD ARTHUR. A madman? I am a man of truth. You are a serpent.
SIR TOBY. Good sirs, for pity's sake. The street grows thick with gazers.
MASTER LEO. Let them gaze. Let them see Lord Arthur's folly. He barks at shadows.
LORD ARTHUR. I bark at thieves. I bark at cheats. You are both.
MASTER LEO. You are a fool. Your rage consumes you.
LORD ARTHUR. My rage is just. My cause is true. Pay what you owe.
SIR TOBY. This is no way. This is no place. Let reason guide you.
MASTER LEO. Reason finds no purchase in his mind. He is lost to

his own spleen.
LORD ARTHUR. My spleen? My spleen is justice, sir. It seeks its due.
MASTER LEO. Your due is nought. Your claim is false. I owe you nothing.
SIR TOBY. Lord Arthur, Master Leo. This dispute grows too loud. It troubles the King's peace.
LORD ARTHUR. The King's peace? He breaks it, sir. He steals my gold.
MASTER LEO. I steal no gold. I am no thief. I am a merchant. I thrive by honest trade.
LORD ARTHUR. Your trade is lies. Your profit is deceit. You are a blot upon the city.
MASTER LEO. A blot? Look to yourself, Lord Arthur. Your name is much diminished. Your temper makes you mad.
SIR TOBY. Peace, I say. Hold your tongues. Or I shall call the Watch.
LORD ARTHUR. Call them, sir. Let them hear my grievance. Let them know his perfidy.
MASTER LEO. His grievance is a phantom. His words are empty air. I welcome the King's justice. It will clear my name.
LORD ARTHUR. Your name is mud. It clings to every lie. I swear upon my sword. I shall have satisfaction.
MASTER LEO. Satisfaction? What satisfaction, pray? A duel? A brawl? I am a man of peace.
LORD ARTHUR. A man of peace? You are a fox. You hide behind your craft.
SIR TOBY. No duels, no brawls. This is a public street

SIR TOBY. No duels, no brawls. This is a public street.
FIRST WATCHMAN. What clamour's this? What brawl is here?
SECOND WATCHMAN. Peace, good sirs. Be still.
LORD ARTHUR. Peace? There is no peace. This man is a thief.
MASTER LEO. He lies. He slanders me.
FIRST WATCHMAN. Hold your tongues. Both of you.
SECOND WATCHMAN. What is the cause? Speak plainly.
SIR TOBY. Good Watchmen, I thank you. This is a dispute. A private matter, sirs.
LORD ARTHUR. No private matter. It is public theft. He owes me gold.
MASTER LEO. I owe him nothing. He is mad.
FIRST WATCHMAN. Mad? Or merely vexed?
SECOND WATCHMAN. Explain yourselves. Who speaks first?
SIR TOBY. Lord Arthur, pray. Let sense prevail.
LORD ARTHUR. Sense? My gold is stolen. That is my sense. This merchant, Leo, he borrowed coin. A sum most weighty, sirs.
MASTER LEO. I borrowed nothing. I swear it.
FIRST WATCHMAN. Silence, merchant. Let him speak.
LORD ARTHUR. He swore to repay me. Upon his honour, sirs. Upon his faith. Now he denies it all. He calls me mad.
SECOND WATCHMAN. And you, Master Leo? What say you to this charge?
MASTER LEO. It is a fancy. A dream. He gave me gold, indeed. A gift. A token, sirs. For services. Rendered long past.
LORD ARTHUR. A gift? You jest. You mock me. It was a loan. A clear, distinct agreement.
MASTER LEO. No. No such thing. I am a merchant. I keep my books. My ledgers are clear.
FIRST WATCHMAN. Where are these books? Where is this proof?
MASTER LEO. At my shop. They rest there. Safe and sound.
LORD ARTHUR. Books? His books are lies. His mind is twisted.
SECOND WATCHMAN. This is a serious charge. You accuse him of theft.
LORD ARTHUR. Yes. I do. He is a rogue. A most villainous rogue.
MASTER LEO. My name is spotless. My trade is honest. I deny his words.
SIR TOBY. Perhaps a compromise? A meeting? A quiet discussion?
FIRST WATCHMAN. No. Not now. This street is blocked. The people stare.
SECOND WATCHMAN. We must take you both. Before the Magistrate.
LORD ARTHUR. Good. Let the Magistrate hear. Let justice be done.
MASTER LEO. I welcome it. My truth will shine.
FIRST WATCHMAN. Come then. No resistance.
SECOND WATCHMAN. Move along, folk. Nothing to see here.
(The WATCHMEN lead LORD ARTHUR and MASTER LEO offstage. SIR TOBY sighs and follows, shaking his head. The street clears.)

(SCENE II. A small, bustling courtroom within the city walls.)

(The MAGISTRATE sits at a high desk. A CLERK sits beside him, writing. LORD ARTHUR and MASTER LEO stand before the MAGISTRATE. SIR TOBY stands nearby. The WATCHMEN are present.)

MAGISTRATE. What is this disturbance? What brings these men before me?
FIRST WATCHMAN. Your Honour, a public quarrel. A grave accusation. Lord Arthur claims Master Leo owes him gold. Master Leo denies it.
MAGISTRATE. Gold? How much gold?
LORD ARTHUR. A thousand crowns, Your Honour. A king's ransom, nearly.
MASTER LEO. A thousand lies. Not a single crown.
MAGISTRATE. Silence. I shall hear you both. Lord Arthur, speak first.
LORD ARTHUR. Your Honour, I am a man of means. I trust my friends. I trusted this man. He came to me. He sought my aid. His business wavered. His ships were late. He needed funds. To bridge a gap.
MAGISTRATE. A loan, then?
LORD ARTHUR. Yes, Your Honour. A loan. For six months, he said. With gratitude. And interest. He promised much.
MASTER LEO. No interest. No loan. He offered help. A kind gesture. To a friend. My ships were due. My coffers were full. I needed nothing.
MAGISTRATE. So you say he gave you this sum? As a gift?
MASTER LEO. Yes, Your Honour. A generous gift. For a service rendered.
MAGISTRATE. What service?
MASTER LEO. I advised him. On a land deal. A property north of the city. He profited greatly. From my counsel. He thanked me then. With this gold.
LORD ARTHUR. Lies. All lies. I sought no counsel. My agents handled it. He merely heard of it. He spins a tale. A tangled web.
MAGISTRATE. Did you sign a bond? A written agreement?
LORD ARTHUR. No, Your Honour. His word was enough. His honour, I thought. He was a friend.
MASTER LEO. A friend. Indeed. Friends do not charge. Friends do not sue. Friends help each other.
MAGISTRATE. No written proof? No witness?
LORD ARTHUR. Sir Toby was there. He heard it.
SIR TOBY. (Stepping forward nervously) Your Honour, I was present. When the gold exchanged hands.
MAGISTRATE. And what did you hear? Was it a loan? Or a gift?
SIR TOBY. I heard talk of a loan. Yes. And repayment. Lord Arthur spoke of it. Master Leo assented.
MASTER LEO. I assented to a gift. A generous one. I thanked him for it.
SIR TOBY. You spoke of repayment. I recall it well.
MASTER LEO. You misremember, sir. Your mind plays tricks.
LORD ARTHUR. See? He lies. He twists words. He is a serpent.
MAGISTRATE. Lord Arthur. Control your tongue. Sir Toby, did Master Leo explicitly state it was a loan? Or did he merely agree to Lord Arthur's terms?
SIR TOBY. Lord Arthur spoke of it. As a loan. Master Leo nodded. He said, "I am obliged." He said, "I shall repay."
MASTER LEO. I said, "I am obliged by your kindness." That is all.
MAGISTRATE. This is a difficult case. One man's word. Against another's. With an uncertain witness.
LORD ARTHUR. My word is true. His is false.
MASTER LEO. My conscience is clear. His is clouded.
MAGISTRATE. Such sums are rarely given. Without document. Or firm agreement. Why was this not written?
LORD ARTHUR. Trust, Your Honour. Pure trust. Between friends.
MASTER LEO. A gift. An act of grace.
MAGISTRATE. It strains belief. That one would gift. A thousand crowns. So casually.
MASTER LEO. Lord Arthur is a rich man. He has much to spare.
LORD ARTHUR. That is irrelevant. It was my gold. It was a loan.
MAGISTRATE. Sir Toby. Can you recall? Any specific terms? Of repayment?
SIR TOBY. Lord Arthur spoke of six months. And a small increase. For his generosity. Master Leo simply nodded. He looked relieved. I thought it settled.
MASTER LEO. I looked relieved. Because of his gift. Not a debt.
MAGISTRATE. Relief is not consent. For a loan. This is vexing.
LORD ARTHUR. My ledger has it. As a loan. A clear entry.
MASTER LEO. His ledger is his own. It proves nothing. Without my hand. Or my seal.
MAGISTRATE. Do you keep a ledger? Master Leo?
MASTER LEO. I do, Your Honour. For all my dealings. I am a careful man.
MAGISTRATE. Bring your ledgers then. Both of you. To this court. Tomorrow morning. I will compare them. I will examine entries.
LORD ARTHUR. My entries are clear.
MASTER LEO. As are mine. They speak the truth.
MAGISTRATE. Then let them speak. Until then. This matter is adjourned.
(The MAGISTRATE strikes his gavel.)
MAGISTRATE. Watchmen. See these men depart. In peace.
FIRST WATCHMAN. Come, sirs. No more words. Until tomorrow.
(LORD ARTHUR and MASTER LEO glare at each other. They are led off in separate directions. SIR TOBY shakes his head again and exits.)

(SCENE III. The next morning. The same courtroom.)

(The MAGISTRATE is again seated. The CLERK is present. LORD ARTHUR and MASTER LEO stand before the MAGISTRATE, each with a large, bound ledger. SIR TOBY is also present, looking uncomfortable.)

MAGISTRATE. Are these your ledgers, sirs?
LORD ARTHUR. Yes, Your Honour. My own hand. My truthful account.
MASTER LEO. And mine, Your Honour. Unblemished. Exact.
MAGISTRATE. Clerk, take Lord Arthur's book. Find the entry. For the thousand crowns.
CLERK. (Takes the ledger and flips through it) Here it is, Your Honour. "To Master Leo. Loan for six months. With interest. One thousand crowns." Dated three months past.
MAGISTRATE. (To Master Leo) And your book, sir? Let the Clerk examine it.
CLERK. (Takes Master Leo's ledger. Flips through it. He looks puzzled.) Your Honour, there is no entry. No record of one thousand crowns. From Lord Arthur. Or any other sum.
MAGISTRATE. (Leans forward) No entry? Not even a gift?
CLERK. None, Your Honour. For a thousand crowns. Or a similar sum. There is a small entry. Two crowns. From Lord Arthur. For some fine silk. A week before.
LORD ARTHUR. Silk? He speaks of silk? This is an outrage.
MASTER LEO. The silk was fine. The price was fair.
MAGISTRATE. (To Master Leo) Why no record? Of a gift so large? Do you not record your gifts? Your profits?
MASTER LEO. Gifts, Your Honour? I do not record them. As income. It was not profit. It was a kindness. From a friend.
MAGISTRATE. But you would record it. As money received? Yes? To balance your books?
MASTER LEO. No. If it is a gift. It is not trade. My books are for trade. And for debts.
LORD ARTHUR. He records his debts. But he has none. He claims. This is a trick.
MAGISTRATE. (To Master Leo) You received a thousand crowns. You admit it. But you show no record. Of its receipt. In your official ledger. How do you explain this?
MASTER LEO. It was outside my commerce. A private matter. Not for my accounts. Of business.
LORD ARTHUR. A private matter? My money? His gain?
MAGISTRATE. This omission is curious. For a careful merchant.
MASTER LEO. I am most careful. For my business. Not for personal gifts.
MAGISTRATE. (To Sir Toby) Sir Toby, you saw the exchange. When the gold passed. Was it counted? Was it checked?
SIR TOBY. Yes, Your Honour. Lord Arthur counted it out. A full thousand. Master Leo took it. He placed it in a purse.
MAGISTRATE. In a purse? Did he look at it? Did he examine it?
SIR TOBY. He did. He smiled. He seemed pleased.
MASTER LEO. I was pleased. By his generosity.
LORD ARTHUR. He was pleased. By his new debt.
MAGISTRATE. (Sighs) This evidence. It is circumstantial. Your ledger, Lord Arthur. It states a loan. But Master Leo's ledger. It is silent. Silence is not proof. Of either claim.
LORD ARTHUR. But his denial. His outright lie. Does that not condemn him?
MASTER LEO. My denial is truth. Your claim is false.
MAGISTRATE. It is your word. Against his. Sir Toby's testimony. It leans to Lord Arthur. But is not decisive.
LORD ARTHUR. What more do you need? His confession?
MAGISTRATE. I need certainty. Not accusation.
LORD ARTHUR. He took my gold. He broke his word. What sort of justice is this?
MAGISTRATE. The justice of law. Not of emotion. Where are your witnesses? Lord Arthur? Who else heard this pact?
LORD ARTHUR. No one else, Your Honour. It was a private trust. Between friends.
MAGISTRATE. And you, Master Leo? Any witness? To this grand gift?
MASTER LEO. No, Your Honour. It was a private gesture. A token of thanks.
MAGISTRATE. So we have. No witnesses. No written bond. Conflicting ledgers. One asserting a loan. The other showing nothing. This court cannot compel repayment. On such flimsy grounds.
LORD ARTHUR. Flimsy? My honour. My gold. My trust.
MAGISTRATE. I understand your frustration. Lord Arthur. But the law requires more. It demands proof.
LORD ARTHUR. He admits receiving it. He cannot deny that.
MASTER LEO. I admit a gift. Not a debt. There is a difference.
MAGISTRATE. Indeed. A crucial difference. Without a bond. Without clear witnesses. Without a corresponding entry. I cannot rule for you. Lord Arthur. The case is dismissed.
LORD ARTHUR. Dismissed? No. This cannot be. This is an outrage.
MASTER LEO. (Smiling faintly) Justice prevails. My name is clear.
LORD ARTHUR. Your name is mud. Your soul is black. This is a travesty. Of all justice.
MAGISTRATE. Lord Arthur. Your language is intemperate. I have made my ruling.
LORD ARTHUR. You have made a mockery. Of my trust. Of my good nature. You reward a thief.
MAGISTRATE. I reward no thief. I uphold the law. Now, leave my court. Both of you.
(LORD ARTHUR stands stunned. MASTER LEO bows slightly to the Magistrate, a smug look on his face. LORD ARTHUR glares at him with pure hatred.)
FIRST WATCHMAN. Come, Lord Arthur. The court has spoken.
SECOND WATCHMAN. Master Leo, you may go.
(MASTER LEO turns and begins to walk away. LORD ARTHUR suddenly lunges at him, grabbing his arm.)
LORD ARTHUR. You will not escape this. You rogue. You cheat.
MASTER LEO. Unhand me. I am free.
FIRST WATCHMAN. Lord Arthur. Desist.
SECOND WATCHMAN. Do not touch him.
(The WATCHMEN quickly move to separate them. LORD ARTHUR struggles against them, his face contorted with rage.)
LORD ARTHUR. He steals from me. He laughs at me. This is not over.
MASTER LEO. It is over. The law has spoken.
MAGISTRATE. (Rising) Watchmen. If Lord Arthur persists. In this behaviour. Take him to the cells. Let him cool his temper.
LORD ARTHUR. Cells? For seeking justice? For pursuing truth?
FIRST WATCHMAN. Your Honour's word. Is law.
(The WATCHMEN forcefully restrain LORD ARTHUR. SIR TOBY rushes forward.)
SIR TOBY. Your Honour, a moment. Lord Arthur means no harm. He is merely distraught. Permit him to leave.
MAGISTRATE. He is distraught. And dangerous. He threatens the peace. Let him reflect. On his actions. In quiet solitude. For a few hours.
LORD ARTHUR. Solitude? I will not be silenced. I will not be caged. This man is a criminal.
(The WATCHMEN begin to drag LORD ARTHUR away. He continues to shout.)
LORD ARTHUR. Remember this, Leo. Remember this day. Your triumph is brief. My vengeance will come.
MASTER LEO. (Shaking his head, a slight smile still playing on his lips) Vengeance? For a simple truth? He is truly mad.
(LORD ARTHUR's shouts fade as he is removed from the court. MASTER LEO straightens his tunic. SIR TOBY looks utterly dejected.)
MAGISTRATE. Master Leo. You are free to go.
MASTER LEO. I thank Your Honour. For your wisdom. For your fairness.
(MASTER LEO bows deeply and exits the courtroom, his step light.)
MAGISTRATE. (To Sir Toby) Sir Toby. You witnessed this.
SIR TOBY. I did, Your Honour. A sad affair.
MAGISTRATE. Indeed. Friendship broken. By gold. Or the lack of it.
SIR TOBY. Lord Arthur. He is consumed by this. He truly believes.
MAGISTRATE. Belief is not enough. For the scales of justice. Evidence is needed. Hard proof. Without it. We have chaos. Not law.
SIR TOBY. He spoke of vengeance. I fear for Master Leo.
MAGISTRATE. We shall see. Let us hope. That time heals. And reason returns. (He sighs.) Clerk. Next case.
(SIR TOBY remains for a moment, looking towards the door where Master Leo exited, then towards where Lord Arthur was taken. He shakes his head, then slowly departs.)

(SCENE IV. A street near Master Leo's shop. A few days later.)

(MASTER LEO is examining some goods outside his shop. He looks prosperous and content. A few CITIZENS pass by, some nod respectfully. LORD ARTHUR enters, looking dishevelled and pale. He hides in the shadows of an alley, watching Master Leo.)

FIRST CITIZEN. Good Master Leo. Your trade thrives.
MASTER LEO. By honest labour. And fair dealings. Good day to you.
SECOND CITIZEN. The word is out. You won your case. A fine thing.
MASTER LEO. The truth stands firm. It always does.
(LORD ARTHUR clenches his fists. His eyes burn with anger.)
LORD ARTHUR. (Muttering to himself) Honest labour? Honest lies. The city believes him. The fools.
(MASTER LEO enters his shop. LORD ARTHUR emerges from the alley. He approaches a street urchin, a small BOY.)
LORD ARTHUR. Boy. A moment.
BOY. Sir? What is it?
LORD ARTHUR. Do you know this shop? Master Leo's?
BOY. Yes, sir. He sells fine silks. And rich spices.
LORD ARTHUR. Indeed. Do you ever run errands for him? Or for others nearby?
BOY. Sometimes, sir. If the coin is good.
LORD ARTHUR. The coin will be excellent. For a small task.
BOY. What task, sir?
LORD ARTHUR. I need eyes. And ears. On Master Leo.
BOY. You want me to watch him?
LORD ARTHUR. Yes. His comings. His goings. His visitors. His dealings. Everything.
BOY. That sounds tricky, sir. He is a careful man.
LORD ARTHUR. I know. But he has secrets. All men do. Find his secrets. Find his weaknesses.
BOY. What for, sir?
LORD ARTHUR. For justice. For truth. He wronged me greatly. And the law failed.
BOY. And the coin?
LORD ARTHUR. A silver piece. For a week's watching. And more. If you find something. Of true value.
BOY. A silver piece? That's much, sir.
LORD ARTHUR. It is. But I need diligent service. And absolute discretion. No one must know. You work for me.
BOY. I understand, sir. When do I begin?
LORD ARTHUR. Now. Start now. Observe him. And report to me. Tomorrow evening. At the old oak. In the King's Park. Do you know it?
BOY. I know it well, sir.
LORD ARTHUR. Good. Be swift. Be subtle. And be silent.
BOY. I will, sir. I shall be as a shadow.
(The BOY hurries off, casting a glance at Master Leo's shop. LORD ARTHUR watches him go, a grim satisfaction on his face.)
LORD ARTHUR. The law may be blind. But my eyes are open. My patience is spent. I will find a way. I will have my due. He thinks himself safe. He thinks himself wise. But every fox has a den. And every secret. Has a whisper. I will hear that whisper. I will find his truth. I will expose his lies. This is not over. Not by far. He will regret this day. He will rue his perfidy. I swear it. By my father's grave. I swear it. By the stars above. He shall pay. He shall suffer. For his foul deceit. He thinks himself clever. He thinks himself untouchable. But pride comes before a fall. His fall will be great. His ruin will be complete. I will see it. I will taste it. It will be sweet. Sweet as wine. After a long thirst. My spirit thirsts. For his downfall. My heart demands it. My honour compels it. He will know my wrath. He will feel my sting. The sting of betrayal. The sting of a friend. Turned enemy. A vile, base enemy. He shall see. He shall learn. That a man scorned. Is a dangerous foe. More dangerous. Than a common thief. For a thief takes gold. But he took trust. He took my faith. He took my good name. He made me a fool. Before the court. Before the city. But the fool will rise. The fool will conquer. This game is not done. This battle continues. I have lost a skirmish. But the war is mine. The ultimate victory. It will be mine. His shop. His goods. His reputation. All shall crumble. Beneath my hand. I will tear him down. Brick by brick. Lie by lie. He will stand naked. Before all men. His false honour. Stripped away. His vile heart. Exposed to the sun. This is my promise. This is my vow. So help me God. So let it be. He will pay. Oh, yes. He will pay. Every last crown. And more. Much more. For his treachery. For his insolence. For his betrayal. The debt is not merely gold. It is honour. It is pride. It is my very soul. He trampled it. He spat upon it. But it will rise. It will claim him. It will consume him. Let him enjoy his moment. Let him bask in false light. For darkness approaches. A storm is brewing. And it will break. Over his head. His smug, hateful head. He will know fear. He will know pain. He will know what it means. To cross Lord Arthur. To scorn his word. To deny his bond. He will know despair. He will know ruin. I will see it. With my own eyes. I will savour it. With my very soul. He thinks he is safe. He thinks he is free. But no man is free. From a righteous wrath. From a just anger. From a heart set on vengeance. This is my path. This is my purpose. He will not escape. Not from me. Not from this debt. Not from this wrath. He will pay. He will surely pay.

(SCENE V. The King's Park. Evening. LORD ARTHUR waits impatiently by the old oak tree. The BOY arrives, looking excited.)

LORD ARTHUR. Well? What news? Have you seen anything?
BOY. Yes, sir. Much, sir. Master Leo. He is a busy man.
LORD ARTHUR. Busy with what? With whom?
BOY. He met a man. A strange man. In the shadows. Behind his shop. After closing.
LORD ARTHUR. A strange man? Describe him.
BOY. Tall, sir. Cloaked. His face was hidden. But his voice. It was gruff. And low.
LORD ARTHUR. What did they say? Did you hear anything?
BOY. Only whispers, sir. But one word. I heard it clear. "Cargo." And "port." They spoke of it. With much urgency.
LORD ARTHUR. Cargo? And port? This is interesting. Very interesting. What else?
BOY. Master Leo. He seemed anxious. He kept looking about. As if watched. He gave the man a small pouch. Of some kind.
LORD ARTHUR. A pouch? Gold? Or something else?
BOY. I could not tell, sir. It was quick. And dark. But it felt heavy. In his hand. The other man's hand.
LORD ARTHUR. So, secret meetings. In the dark. This does not sound. Like honest trade.
BOY. No, sir. It did not. He looked nervous. More nervous. Than I have ever seen him.
LORD ARTHUR. Good. Very good. You have done well. Here is your silver. (He hands the Boy a coin.)
BOY. Thank you, sir. Will there be more?
LORD ARTHUR. Much more. Continue your vigil. Watch his shop. Watch his house. Watch his movements. Every shadow. Every whisper. I need to know. Who this man is. And what this cargo. Truly means.
BOY. I will, sir. I shall be diligent.
LORD ARTHUR. And if you see the cloaked man again. Follow him. Carefully. Find out where he goes. Who he meets.
BOY. That might be dangerous, sir.
LORD ARTHUR. All great quests. Have danger. But the reward. Will be greater. For you. And for justice.
BOY. I understand, sir. I will be careful.
(The BOY runs off. LORD ARTHUR paces under the oak tree, a new fire in his eyes.)
LORD ARTHUR. Cargo. Port. Secret meetings. He hides something. Something dark. Something illicit. This is not the honest merchant. He pretends to be. This is a crack. In his armour. A flaw in his fortress. I will exploit it. I will expose it. This is my chance. My true chance. To unravel his lies. To reveal his true self. A thousand crowns. That was just the beginning. This new secret. It could be his undoing. His absolute ruin. I can feel it. In my bones. The scent of truth. The taste of victory. It is near. So very near. The law. It failed me. But cunning. It will serve me. My own hands. My own mind. They will bring him down. He will know. What it means. To cross Lord Arthur. He will regret. His insolence. His deceit. His false triumph.
