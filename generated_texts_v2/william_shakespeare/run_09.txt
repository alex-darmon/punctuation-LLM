That Vane seeks swift

redress. So you claim. But tell me, sir.
These honest folk. Who flee in fear.
Their homes laid waste. Their cattle stolen.
Their daughters shamed. Their sons struck down.
Have you yourself. With your own eyes.
Beheld this horror? Seen the proof?
Or do you speak. From Vane's report?
From rumour's breath? From whispered fright?

VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, I have. My eyes have seen.
The smoking ruins. The ravaged fields.
The weeping women. The fatherless.
I rode this morn. Through sorrow's vale.
My heart did ache. My spirit bled.
To see such ruin. In your good land.
The raiders fierce. Their numbers strong.
They sweep the pass. Like winter's storm.
And Baron Vane. He stands alone.
Against this tide. This brutal surge.
He calls for aid. For quick relief.
To stem the blood. To save your folk.

RUPERT
He stands alone? My forces wait.
At my command. Within these walls.
They stand prepared. To guard the peace.
To crush all foes. Both far and near.
Why did not Vane. Send word ere this?
When first the shadow. Of this threat.
Did fall upon. The mountain path?
Why wait till now? Till all is lost?
Till cries of woe. Fill every breeze?
And where, good sir. Is this dire pass?


VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, it lies. Beyond the hills. The Serpent's Tooth. So is it named. A winding track. Through jagged rock. And ancient woods. It leads to lands. Beyond our sway. To wilder folk. To lawless men. It is a choke. A narrow neck. That links our realm. To outer dark. It is the gate. Through which they pour. These savage tides. This cruel invasion.

RUPERT
The Serpent's Tooth? I know the name. A place of dread. Of old renown. For robber bands. For desperate souls. Not mighty armies. Not fierce invaders. Do you now speak. Of petty thieves? Or forces vast? That threaten peace?

VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, they are. No common thieves. They ride in strength. With banners strange. And warlike cries. Their numbers swell. Like winter floods. They do not seek. A purse of gold. But lands to hold. And power to seize. They burn and kill. They take and spoil. With cruel delight. With savage glee.

RUPERT
Banners strange? You say they bear. Such foreign signs. Such unknown marks. Have you beheld. These standards, sir? Or do you speak. Of what you heard? From fearful tongues? From panicked cries?

VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, I saw. Their tattered flag. A raven black. On blood-red field. It flew above. Their wicked camp. As I rode past. To bring you word.

A raven black? On blood-red field?
This is no mark. Of honest men.
Nor simple thieves. Who raid for gain.
This speaks of war. Of cruel design.
A foreign hand. A potent sign.
Are you quite sure? You saw this, sir?
With waking eyes? And clear of blur?
Or was it dim? The twilight's gleam?
A shadowed form? A frightful dream?

VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, I swear. By holy light.
My eyes beheld. It clear and bright.
Though torn and soiled. By dust and fray.
The raven flew. At close of day.
Above their tents. A grim display.
Their spears rose high. A fearful sight.
Their steel did gleam. In fading light.
They moved with purpose. With fell intent.
Their cruel will. On ravage bent.

RUPERT
And where was Vane? This Baron bold?
When you rode past? What did he hold?
His fort, you say? It stands alone?
Against this horde? This foreign sown?
Or was he safe? Within its wall?
While common folk. Did rise and fall?

VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, he fought. With sword and might.
Upon the walls. He joined the fight.
His men were few. His spirit strong.
He held the breach. The whole day long.
I saw him stand. A valiant lord.
With bloody hand. And broken

sword, and broken blade.
He fought, my lord, with might and main.
His men, though few, stood firm in pain.
Against the tide. Of cruel foes.
Their numbers swell. As malice grows.
He called to me, with weary brow,
"Haste, messenger; make promise now.
To Rupert's court, this message bear:
Our fort is lost, to stark despair.
The Serpent's Tooth, that vital pass,
Is overwhelmed, a shattered glass.
Our walls do crack, stone by stone;
While valiant Vane, stands all alone.
Tell him we fall, without his aid;
Our hopes, our lives, quite unafraid.
If he but comes, with swift accord,
To save us all, with righteous sword."
I saw his eyes, a desperate plea,
As shadows fell, on hill and lea.
I left him there. A man of pride.
With few good souls. Close by his side.
I rode through night. Through fear and pain.
To bring this truth. Like winter's rain.
My lord, I swear, by all that's true,
Vane fights for all, for me, for you.
He stands between. Us and the foe.
Will you not strike? A rescuing blow?
His life, his honour, hang in doubt;
Hear his dire call, without a rout.

RUPERT
(Rising, he paces, his brow furrowed with care)
And broken blade? A shattered

glass? The vital pass?
This Serpent's Tooth? A cruel loss.
Vane stands alone. A man forlorn.
His walls do crack. His spirit torn.
He fights for all. He fights for me.
This foreign foe. This raven's plea.
What evil hand. Doth stir this strife?
What savage heart. Would claim such life?
This is no raid. For paltry gold.
A deeper malice. Is here unrolled.
A dark design. A dreadful scheme.
Beyond all thought. Beyond all dream.
My loyal men. My trusty guard.
This news doth strike. My soul full hard.
To let him fall. To watch him bleed.
Would stain my name. A craven deed.
But to rush forth. With hasty might.
Against the unknown. In the dark of night.
Is folly too. A path unwise.
To risk my realm. Before my eyes.
The messenger. He rode with speed.
His urgent words. A desperate need.
My heart is rent. My thoughts are tossed.
A weighty choice. Or all is lost.

(He stops pacing, turns to the MESSENGER)

RUPERT
Tell me, good sir. With honest breath.
What numbers faced. This wall of death?
How many blades. How many men?
Did surge and swarm. Before your ken?

VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, I scarce. Could count them all.
They seemed as leaves. Before the fall.
A

countless host. A surging tide. They filled the plain. From side to side. Their banners flew. A dreadful sight. Against the sun. And fading light. They bore no crest. Of known domain. No lion fierce. No royal crane. But symbols strange. And dark of hue. That spoke of malice. Fierce and new. I saw their ranks. A monstrous stream. As shadows grow. Within a dream. They moved as one. With silent dread. Their purpose fell. Their spirits fed. On conquest's blood. And burning rage. A new dark foe. Upon this stage. Their armour gleamed. A somber grey. They brought despair. To light of day. They had no drums. No battle cry. But moved like wolves. Beneath the sky. Their numbers swelled. With every gaze. Beyond the count. Of mortal days. I swear, my lord. By sun and moon. They numbered tens. Of thousands soon. Their front stretched wide. For miles I think. Beyond the verge. Beyond the brink. Of what a man. Could well behold. A tale of fear. A story cold.

RUPERT
(His hand flies to his sword hilt, then drops it, shaking his head slowly)
Ten thousands soon? No battle cry? Like wolves they came. Beneath the sky? This is no force. Of common breed. No petty prince. Sows such a seed. This is an army. Well arrayed. A power vast. A fear arrayed. Against our peace. Our ancient right. To claim this land. To steal our light.

(His voice lowers, a tremor in its depth)
This shadow falls upon our peaceful land.
A heavy hand from foreign hostile sand.
No common foe, no mere brigand we find.
This evil tide sweeps all before its kind.
My heart is sick, my mind grows ever dark.
To hear such news, it leaves a dreadful mark.
Upon our age, a great and coming doom.
To shroud our hope within an endless gloom.
They bear no crest, you say, my worthy sir?
No known device, no friendly stirring blur?
This anonymity, it chills my very blood.
A faceless threat, a vast and rising flood.
What powers move behind this awful veil?
What dark intent, what hidden, cruel trail?
Speak, messenger, with truth's sharp, bitter sting.
What saw you more, what further tidings bring?
What strange array, what awful, grim-faced form?
Beyond our count, beyond the rising storm?
Did you behold their cruel and hardened leaders grim?
Or did they move from some far distant, shadowy brim?
Did any word, did any single sound, good man,
Betray their cause upon this ravaged, bleeding land?

VANE'S MESSENGER
My lord, I saw no chieftain standing bold.
No single voice, no story to be told.
Their ranks were deep, their silent purpose vast.
A chilling sight, it held my spirit fast.
They moved as one, a living, marching wall.
