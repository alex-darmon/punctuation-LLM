Objects on the workbench vibrated. Glassware rattled ominously. He heard a faint, high-pitched scream. Or was it merely the machine?

The timer clicked down. Ten seconds remained. Thorne's hand ached. His muscles screamed in protest. The blue light was blinding. He could barely see the chamber now. Only a maelstrom of light. And shadows that danced. Shadows that were not shadows. But something else entirely. Something ancient and terrible.

Five seconds. Four. Three. Two. One.

Thorne slammed the lever up. The light vanished. The thrum ceased. A deafening silence descended upon the lab. The air hung heavy and still. Thorne gasped for breath. His heart hammered. He stumbled back from the console. His eyes fixed on the chamber. It was empty. The door remained closed. Finch was gone.

"Elias?" Thorne whispered. His voice was hoarse. He rushed to the chamber. He pressed his face against the glass. He saw only the smooth, unblemished interior. No sign of his friend. No dust. No particles. Just emptiness. A cold, stark emptiness. Thorne felt a wave of nausea. He leaned against the chamber door. His knees buckled.

He closed his eyes. He pictured Finch. His intense gaze. His unwavering conviction. His fatal error. Thorne felt a profound despair. "My God, Elias," he muttered. His voice was thick with grief. "What have you done?" He opened his eyes. He looked around the silent lab. The humming machine stood inert. The flickering console lights seemed to mock him.

He was alone. In a lab filled with impossible machines. With a vial of dust. And a shimmering locket. He looked at the coherence screen. It showed a flat line. No signal. Nothing. Thorne felt a chill stronger than any before. Not just a chill of loss. But a chill of existential horror. A fragment of reality had been torn away. And his friend had gone with it.

He walked slowly back to the workbench. He picked up the shimmering locket. Its surface was still cold. He saw the faint pulsation. The almost imperceptible flicker. An echo. An echo of all possible pasts and futures. He looked at the vial of dust. Bartholomew's remains. A warning. A grim, terrible warning.

The world was not as he knew it. Finch’s madness. It was not madness. It was a terrible, horrifying truth. Thorne felt a new kind of fear. A fear for all humanity. He looked at the inert machine. The silent, waiting monster. He knew he could not simply walk away. He could not leave this knowledge. This danger. Unaddressed. He had to understand. He had to contain it. He had to finish what Elias had started. For the sake of everything. For the sake of all realities. His hand trembled. He had to face the unthinkable. He had to face the bleed.
