Enter Lysander and Julian.

LYSANDER
This city sweats with pride, a fever-heat
That boils the blood and makes all reason sick.
I’d rather be a shepherd on the downs
Than a great lord in such a choleric town.

JULIAN
Peace, Lysander, lest your tongue invite a blade
That’s sharper than the wit it has displayed.
You are too fond of plucking trouble’s sleeve.

LYSANDER
And you too fond to let a good jest leave
Before it’s seen the sun. Why, what’s a man
Whose spirit is not wider than the span
Of his own fears? I say the air is foul
With Cassian’s men, that strut and preen and scowl
Like peacocks in a storm.

JULIAN
And here come two.
Let your wit sleep, I pray you.

Enter Marcus and Decius.

MARCUS
Well met, you crows that flock to Valerius’ name.
What carrion do you seek in this day’s shame?

LYSANDER
And you the jackdaws that from Cassian’s table
Steal your small courage. Tell me, are you able
To make a sound that is not but a boast?
Thy voice is like a bell rung for a ghost,
Hollow and promising an end to things.

MARCUS
Your valour is a painted fire, it brings
No heat, but only smoke to sting the eye.
Draw, if you dare to stand behind the lie.

LYSANDER
A lie, sir, that I’ll not lie down withal.
My sword shall give your slanders proper burial.
[_Draws._]

JULIAN
Hold, gentlemen! For pity’s sake, desist!
The Prefect’s law on this is not to be dismissed.
Put up your swords! The public street’s no field
For private hatreds. I pray you, yield!

MARCUS
Away, you milk-fed meddler! This is not
Your quarrel.

LYSANDER
Julian, stand back! The pot
Is at a boil and must have leave to spill.
Come, sirrah popinjay, and get your fill!
[_They fight._]

JULIAN
I’ll not stand back and see this madness done!
Part, fools! In the Duke’s name, I say have done!
[_He steps between them._]

MARCUS
Then take thou this for thy unwelcome pains!
[_He thrusts under Julian’s arm and wounds Lysander._]

DECIUS
He is struck! Marcus, away!

[_Exeunt Marcus and Decius._]

LYSANDER
I am sped.
A curse on all their houses, and their dead.
He has made a window in me for the soul
To fly. Julian, your peace has took its toll.

JULIAN
Lysander! O, the wound cannot be deep.
A surgeon, quick!

LYSANDER
No, I have an appointment I must keep.
‘Tis but a scratch, ‘tis true, but ‘tis a wide one.
Wide enough for life to slip outside on.
Ask for me tomorrow, and you shall find me… settled.
May both your masters’ fortunes be so nettled
They scratch their skin to madness. I am done.
This world’s a stage where I have had no fun.
[_Dies._]

JULIAN
O noble heart! O friend, too rashly slain!
My own good hand, that sought to quell the rain,
Hath brought the thunder down upon thy head.
This gentle soul for my sake now is dead.
But vengeance is not sleeping. It awakes.

Re-enter Marcus.

MARCUS
Where is the prating fool that for his stakes
Did wager his own life, and lost the throw?

JULIAN
He is in heaven, villain, where you’ll not go.
But I will send his butcher after him.
The debt of blood is due, and on the brim
Your cup of life now stands. Take back this death
You gave so foully, on my sword’s cold breath!
Now all my tempered reason flies away,
And bloody fury governs this dark day!
[_Draws._]

MARCUS
Thou couldst not save him, boy, nor save thyself.
[_They fight. Marcus falls._]

JULIAN
So. It is finished. O, I am grief’s own pelf,
Spent in a moment’s rage. What have I done?

Enter Prefect, with Guards, and Lords Valerius and Cassian.

FIRST GUARD
Lay down your weapon, sir. The fight is done.
Here comes the Prefect.

PREFECT
Where are the authors of this mortal strife
That thus profane the city with a life
So cheaply shed? Who is it that lies here?

CASSIAN
My kinsman, Marcus! Slain! O, Prefect dear,
I see the murderer, his sword still red.
Let justice fall upon his guilty head!
For blood of mine, let his own blood be paid.

PREFECT
Julian of Valerius, are you not afraid
To stand before me with such evidence?
Speak! Who began this fatal violence?

JULIAN
I cannot speak. My friend lies at my feet.
My tongue is lead, my sorrow is complete.

VALERIUS
My lord, this boy is not the root of it.
His heart is mild, and ever did he sit
On peace’s side. I doubt not that the fray
Was started by the man he had to slay
In just revenge for a far dearer friend.

CASSIAN
Revenge? He calls this murder a revenge!
This is the Montague tongue, it twists the truth.
I say he is a butcher, without ruth,
And for my Marcus, he must surely die.

PREFECT
My judgement shall not on your passions lie.
I have an interest in your foolish feud;
My own guard bled to keep your rages lewd
From spilling o’er. This day the law is broke,
And one of you hath felt death’s final stroke.
Because you, Julian, took the law’s own sword
Into your hand, against my sovereign word,
You are exiled. Depart from hence today.
If you are found within the walls, I say
The very hour you’re found, that hour you die.
Let this sad day your bitter rancour quell,
Lest you all learn to make your peace in hell.

[_Exeunt Prefect, Guards._]

JULIAN
Banished. The word is like a funeral bell
That tolls for one who is alive and well.
A walking death, a breathing, moving grave.
This is the mercy that the Prefect gave?
To let me live, but tear out all of life,
To take the husband from the yet-wife,
To rob the son from his dear father’s sight,
And plunge my every morning into night.
Exiled from her, is exile from the sun.
My course of joy is ended ere’t begun.

VALERIUS
My son, my son, do not give way to this.
The Prefect’s doom, though harsh, holds a small bliss:
It is not death. You breathe. And while you breathe,
Hope may yet find a way a wreath to weave
About your brows. But we must use the time.
To linger here is a most fatal crime.
You must be gone from here ere the next watch.
A single hour’s delay, and you will catch
The hangman’s noose instead of Padua’s air.
Come, let us haste and for your flight prepare.

CASSIAN
[_To his men._]
Take up my kinsman. Handle him with care.
He was a lion, caught in a fool’s snare.
[_They lift the body of Marcus._]
Valerius, look upon this heavy bier.
Your son’s soft sentence cannot dry this tear.
He breathes, you say? Aye, breathes a freer air
Than I, who choke on this unjust affair.
But let him fly. The world has roads and lanes,
And on those roads, rage travels when it rains.
A storm is coming for your wretched house.
You have made widows, and I’ll keep my vows
To see your line extinguished, branch and root.
This is no end; it is a bitter fruit
Whose seed will blossom into fields of war.
I’ll hear no peace till Julian is no more.

[_Exit Cassian with his followers and the body of Marcus._]

VALERIUS
His threats are wind, but wind can feed a fire.
Let’s not provoke his anger or his ire
By dallying. Julian, you heard my word?
To Padua. There, let your name be heard
In sober trade, not in a bloody brawl.
I’ll send you letters and with them my all,
My funds, my hopes. I’ll work upon the state,
And try to turn the iron hinge of fate.
Perhaps in time the Prefect may relent.
But for tonight, your safety must be sent
On winged heels.

JULIAN
And my Antonio, dead?
Shall he lie here on this cold, stony bed
Without a friend to close his honest eyes?

VALERIUS
The city guard will see to his demise
And give him rites. To stay for him is mad.
His soul is fled. Think on the life you had,
And what you still may have. Now, come with me.
This place is poison for my son and me.

JULIAN
[_Kneeling by his friend._]
O, sweet Antonio, pardon me this flight.
I cannot serve you in death’s endless night,
For I am hunted by the living day.
I’ll think of thee when I am far away,
And in my heart your monument shall be,
More rich than any tomb the world can see.
Farewell, true friend. My own life is the cost
Of your dear loss, and so I too am lost.

[_Julian rises. He and Valerius exit._]

SCENE II.
A Room in Cassian’s House.

[_Enter Lady Portia._]

LADY PORTIA
The house is still. Too still. A heavy dread
Hangs in the air, as if the very dead
Were walking here and breathing out their chill.
My heart beats like a captive with no will,
Awaiting sentence from a judge’s mouth.
Is it the parching heat of this long south
That makes me faint, or is it woman’s fear?
I would my lord and Livia were here.

[_Enter Cassian._]

My lord, at last! Your face is dark as storm.
What is the news? Does Marcus’ body warm
A grave, or does his murderer lie slain?
Speak, Cassian! Free me from this rack of pain!

CASSIAN
The murderer lives.

LADY PORTIA
Lives! O, you mock me, sir!
The law is dead then, and the Prefect’s fur
Is but a sheep’s disguise upon a wolf
That lets the guilty leap across the gulf
Of justice, safe, while we are left to drown.
How can this be? In this our native town?

CASSIAN
He is exiled. Banished from this day.
If he is found, the Prefect means to slay
The boy at once. That is the final word.

LADY PORTIA
Exile? That is a prize, and not a sword!
A holiday from sorrow, time to mend.
He’ll find new lands, new loves, and a new friend,
While Marcus rots and we are left with grief.
This is no justice, but a villain’s relief.
O, had I been a man, with strength to hold
A blade, his heart’s blood would be running cold
Upon the stones where my dear kinsman fell.

CASSIAN
Peace, wife, your rage does but my own rage tell
In shriller tones. I have a better plan.
Revenge is not a dish for a rash man,
But for a patient one, who serves it cold.
Valerius thinks his son has slipped our hold.
He thinks that time will dull the edge of loss.
I’ll teach him how to bear a heavier cross.

LADY PORTIA
What is your mind? My ears are yours to fill.

CASSIAN
We have a daughter.

LADY PORTIA
Livia? She is ill
With all this news. She weeps within her room.

CASSIAN
Let her weep on. Her tears will feed the bloom
Of my design. She is our finest dart,
The one to pierce old Valerius to the heart.
The boy, this Julian, loves her. It is known.
And on this love, my seeds of hate are sown.
I’ll have her married. And I’ll have her wed
Before the moon is twice seen overhead.

LADY PORTIA
Married? To whom? What man of noble state
Would rush so fast to pass the wedding gate?

CASSIAN
Lord Fabius. He has long desired her hand.
He holds great favour, owns a wealth of land,
And, what is more, he is the Prefect’s kin.
An alliance with him, and we shall win
Such power here that Valerius will shrink
To be a shadow trembling on the brink
Of his own ruin. Think on Julian’s pain,
In exile, hearing that his love, in vain,
Was given him. He’ll hear his Livia’s name
Joined to another’s, and the very flame
Of his own soul will be his burning hell.
Yes. This will wound them. This will serve me well.

LADY PORTIA
A master stroke. The girl will surely fight.
Her will is strong.

CASSIAN
Her will shall bend tonight.
She is my daughter, and she will obey,
Or she will learn what price the disarray
Of filial duty costs. Go, fetch her here.
And you, my lady, quell your woman’s fear
And be a wife to me in this design.
Her sorrow is a trifle. Vengeance is mine.

[_Exit Lady Portia._]

He took my blood. I’ll take his son’s whole life.
Not with a sword, a poison, or a knife,
But with the slow and grinding turn of years,
With bitter tidings whispered in his ears,
With the slow theft of every scrap of hope.
I’ll give his heart a universe of scope
To feel its desolation. Let him roam.
He’ll find no peace abroad, nor welcome home.

SCENE III.
A Room in Valerius’s House.

[_Enter Valerius, Julian, and Titus, an old servant._]

VALERIUS
Here is the gold. Take it, and use it well.
Let none in Padua hear you ever tell
The reason for your coming. Say you are
A scholar, come to study from afar.
Be prudent, son. Let your quick temper sleep.
The wounds you carry are both wide and deep;
Do not expose them to the common air.

JULIAN
I thank you, father, for your loving care.
This gold is dust, this counsel is but sound.
My soul is fixed to one small patch of ground
In this same city. How can I depart
Without a final word to ease my heart?
I must see Livia.

VALERIUS
That is suicide!
Her father’s house is where his rages hide
In every shadow. You would walk straight in
To the lion’s den and boast of your own sin.
It cannot be.

JULIAN
Then I will not be gone.
I’ll stay and face the sharpest edge of dawn,
Which is the headsman’s axe. To live, and know
That she believes I fled without the woe
Of a farewell, that I just slipped away
Like some base thief at the approach of day…
No, I will see her, or I will not live.
What life is it this banishment can give?

TITUS
My lord, forgive your servant’s humble tongue.
The boy is right. When hearts are newly wrung
With such a grief, a final, parting word
Is like a balm. And his plea must be heard.
To send him hence with this despair so new
Is to send half a man. His heart, ’tis true,
Remains behind.

VALERIUS
And you, old man, have you a plan
To breach the walls of Cassian’s house? You can
No more do that than fly.

TITUS
My lord, I have.
Love’s messengers are swift, and find a cave
Through any mountain. Livia’s nurse, you know,
Good Celia, who has watched the lady grow,
Is my own cousin. While you spoke your grief,
I sent a boy, as nimble as a thief,
With a short message. And an answer came.
The lady Livia, breathing Julian’s name,
Will be this hour within her garden, where
The old stone wall admits the cooling air.
She waits for him.

JULIAN
O, Titus, you have been
An angel in this black and mortal scene!
Father, you hear? The way is clear and safe.

VALERIUS
Safe is a word I trust not. But to chafe
Against your will, I see, is but to fray
A rope that’s stretched too taut. Go then. But stay
No longer than a quarter of an hour.
The night has eyes, and every drooping flower
May hide a spy. When you have said your piece,
Return at once, that my old heart may cease
Its hammering. Titus will lead you there
And wait nearby. Go now, and have a care.
May all the gods that watch o’er lovers’ plight
Protect you, son, and guide you through the night.

[_Exeunt Julian and Titus._]

O, what a world is this, where honest love
Must steal its meetings, and a gentle dove
Must fear the hawk in its own nesting place.
I have one son, the pride of all my race,
And he is banished. I have one desire,
To see him safe from this consuming fire.
This feud, this plague, this madness of the blood,
It drowns all reason in a raging flood.
And I, an old man, stand upon the shore
And watch the waves, and pray they rise no more.

SCENE IV.
Cassian’s Garden.

[_Livia’s balcony is visible above. The garden is dark, lit by a partial moon. Enter Julian, climbing over a low stone wall._]

JULIAN
This is the place. O, blessed, sacred ground,
Where I so oft a glimpse of heaven have found.
Each flower here, each leaf upon the vine,
Has felt the warming of her light divine.
But now, how cold it seems. The moon is shy
And hides her face behind a cloudy sky,
As if she fears to look on my despair.
Is that a light? It is. She must be there.
Livia! My love!

[_Livia and her nurse, Celia, appear on the balcony._]

LIVIA
Julian? Is it your voice
That makes the sorrowing nightingale rejoice
And sing again? O, speak, if it is you!

JULIAN
It is your Julian, faithful, fond, and true,
Though fortune plays the cruelest of all parts.
I come to take my leave of my own heart’s
Most precious treasure.

LIVIA
Leave? That word is death.
I heard the news, and it did steal my breath.
Banished? O, Julian, say it is not so.
Tell me this rumour is our common foe,
A lie sent out to poison our delight.

JULIAN
It is no lie, but the cold truth of night.
I am exiled. For slaying him, your kin,
I must now pay for my impassioned sin
By leaving all I love. I must be gone
Before the first grey promise of the dawn.

[_Julian climbs the trellis to the balcony._]

CELIA
My lady, quickly. Every sound I hear
Is your father’s footstep drawing ever near.
I’ll stand on watch. But make your parting brief.
A stolen joy is cousin to much grief.

[_Exit Celia from the balcony, into the house._]

LIVIA
O, Julian, your hands are cold as stone.
And look, my cousin’s blood is on your own
Dear tunic still. How can this be? So fast
Our sun has set, our sky is overcast.
He was my kinsman, and I should be sad,
But all my sorrow is for the life we had
And now have lost. I cannot weep for him
When all my world is rendered dark and dim
By your departure.

JULIAN
My sweet, gentle love,
My hand was forced by all the powers above
And those below. Antonio, my friend,
Was slain by Marcus. To a bitter end
His pride did bring him. In my rage and grief,
I sought a vengeance that was all too brief
And brought this ruin. Can you forgive the hand
That shed the blood of one from your own land,
Your family’s house?

LIVIA
It was my family’s blood,
But he was not my heart. I understood
His temper well. He was a man of strife.
My love, you fought to guard a dearer life,
Your friend’s good name, and your own honour bright.
It is the feud I curse, this endless night
Of hate between our houses. I forgive.
How could I not, and have my own heart live?

JULIAN
Then I can go in peace. Or in a kind
Of peace, at least. Take this, and let it bind
Your heart to mine.

[_He gives her a ring._]

This ring my mother wore.
Let it speak for me when I am no more
Beside you. I will send you word, I swear,
From Padua. I’ll find a messenger there
Whom we can trust. And I will live in hope
That soon the Prefect will allow a scope
For my return.

LIVIA
And I will be as true
As Penelope, my thoughts all turned to you.
And wear this token.

[_She gives him a small locket from her neck._]

Let it lie upon
Your heart, and think of me from dusk till dawn.
O, must you go? The night is still so young.

JULIAN
The lark, not nightingale, has just now sung
A warning note. I hear the sound of day
Preparing to chase all our dreams away.
I must descend. One kiss.

LIVIA
A thousand more!
Each one a memory to keep in store
Against the lonely winter of the soul.

[_They embrace. Re-enter Celia._]

CELIA
My lord, away! I hear the morning patrol
Upon the street. My master stirs within.
To be found here would be a mortal sin!
Away!

JULIAN
Farewell!

LIVIA
O, think not that we part,
You take with you the vessel of my heart.
Farewell! Be safe!

JULIAN
And you, my love, be strong.
This separation will not be for long.

[_He climbs down from the balcony and disappears over the wall._]

LIVIA
He’s gone. And all the light is gone with him.
The world’s a cup, filled to its very brim
With bitter poison, and I must drink deep.
O, would that I could fall to endless sleep
And wake only when he returns to me.
Fortune, I beg you, turn your wheel and be
Not blind, but kind, for once. Let my true love
Find safety under your swift stars above.

[_Exit._]

SCENE V.
A Hall in Cassian’s House.

[_Enter Cassian, Lady Portia, and Lord Fabius._]

CASSIAN
My lord Fabius, you honour this poor house.
I trust my message and my solemn vows
Have found you well and pleased you.

FABIUS
Cassian, they have.
Your daughter Livia is a prize I crave
Above all others. Her renowned beauty,
Her modest grace and peerless quality,
Are known throughout the city. To be frank,
I am a man who has achieved a rank
Where I may choose, and it is her I choose.

CASSIAN
A wise decision you will never lose
A moment’s sleep regretting. She is mild,
A dutiful and an obedient child.
My wife has told her of the great good news,
The honour that you do us. She will use
Her sweetest voice to thank you for the gift
Of your good self. Ah, here she is. Be swift,
My dear, and greet your future husband now.

[_Enter Livia, pale and weeping. She ignores Fabius and goes to her father._]

LIVIA
Father, I beg you, on my bended knee,
Do not inflict this sudden misery.
I am not well. My heart is full of dread
From yesterday. My kinsman Marcus, dead…
This is no time for talk of wedding feasts.

CASSIAN
Silence, you fool. Such talk but ill befits
A noble bride. Lord Fabius is here.
Put by your mourning and your childish fear.
He offers you his hand, his name, his wealth.
A union that will bring our house great health
And standing.

LIVIA
Father, I cannot.

CASSIAN
You cannot?
What foolishness is this? Have you forgot
Your duty to your name, your blood, your sire?

LIVIA
I mean no disrespect, but my desire
Is to remain as I am for a time.
To marry now would be a joyless crime
Against myself and this good lord, for I
Can bring no joy. I beg you, let it lie.
I am not ready for a husband’s bed.
My mind is full of sorrow for the dead.

FABIUS
Perhaps the lady speaks the truth, my lord.
A little time her spirits may afford
A chance to rally.

CASSIAN
Time? She has had time enough!
This is a girl’s excuse, a flimsy stuff
Of woven fantasy. She will be wed.
I say it. Livia, lift up your head.
Look on Lord Fabius. He is a man
Of worth and honour. It is my firm plan
You marry him on Thursday next.

LIVIA
On Thursday!
No! Father, no! Do not throw me away!
I will not marry him. I cannot love
A man I do not know! By all above,
I swear I’ll not be brought unto the church
A willing bride.

CASSIAN
You will not? Then you’ll lurch
Your way there dragged! What is this? ‘I will not’?
You disobedient, ungrateful blot
Upon my honour! I have given you life,
And I can make that life a living strife
If you defy me. Thursday is the day.
And you will go, and you will smile, and say
The vows required. Or, by my very soul,
I’ll cast you out. You’ll have no wedding dole,
No home, no name. You’ll beg upon the street
And starve and die, for all the world to greet
As Cassian’s shame. This is your final choice.
Obey me now, or never use your voice
To call me father more.

LADY PORTIA
Husband, be calm.
Livia, my child, you do yourself great harm.
Think what you do.

LIVIA
I think on what is right!
My heart is given. And in heaven’s sight,
I am already promised. I’ll not break
That sacred vow for any power’s sake.

CASSIAN
Promised! To whom? That banished murderer?
That villain Julian? Is it for that cur
You shame your house and spit upon my will?
Now, by the gods, my rage could have me kill
You where you stand! But death is far too kind.
A living misery is what you’ll find.
Prepare for Thursday. I have said my piece.
Your tears are rain upon the angry seas;
They do but make the tempest worse. Come, friends.
We’ll leave this wretch to think on how she mends
Her broken state. Her choice is to obey
Or be a beggar come next Thursday.

[_Exeunt Cassian, Lady Portia, and a displeased Fabius._]

LIVIA
O, I am trapped. A sheep within the fold,
Whose shepherd means for her to be sold
And slaughtered. Where can I turn? My mother’s ear
Is deaf to me, my father’s heart is fear
And rage combined. My Julian is gone,
Too far to help me face this cruel dawn.
What can I do? My nurse? Old Celia’s love
Is great, but weak. What power does a dove
Possess against an eagle? I am lost.
My hope is wrecked, my spirit tempest tossed.
Unless… unless there is another way.
A path of shadows, hidden from the day.
Old Friar Laurence, in his quiet cell,
He knows the secrets of the heart so well.
His counsel might provide a hidden key
To unlock this grim cage and set me free.
To him I’ll go. If his wise arts should fail
To save me from this joyless, forced espial,
Then in my hands I have a final cure.
A sleep so deep it ever will endure.

[_Exit._]

SCENE II. FRIAR LAURENCE’S CELL.

[_Enter LIVIA, her face a mask of desperation._]

LIVIA
Good father, help! I come a soul in need,
Upon whose heart the hounds of sorrow feed.
You are my last recourse, my only guide
In this dark world where horrors now preside.

[_FRIAR LAURENCE looks up from a heavy tome. He is old, his face lined with wisdom and care._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
Peace to you, child. What troubles your repose?
The heart that seeks for aid should all disclose.
I am God’s servant, and my ear is lent
To hear the woes of the sad penitent.
What brings you here with such a storm-tossed mien?
Has some new grief made your existence keen
With misery’s sharp edge? Speak, do not fear.
The holy church gives sanctuary here.

LIVIA
Oh, father, you who joined my hand and heart
To my dear Julian, must now play a part
In tearing us asunder! All is lost!
My father, like a ship by tempest crossed
From its true course, has set his will on me.
He says that I, on Thursday, am to be
The bride of Fabius.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Lord Fabius?
A worthy man, and noble. Why then thus
Do you recoil? The match is one of wealth,
And should secure your happiness and health.

LIVIA
My happiness is Julian! My vow
Was made to him. You heard it. Father, how
Can I commit a sin so great, so deep,
As perjury, while my true promise sleeps
Within my heart? I cannot marry him.
I will not. But my father’s word is grim.
He threatens me with ruin, shame, and scorn,
To be an outcast, wretched and forlorn,
If I do not obey his harsh command.
I have no friend, no aid in all the land.
And Thursday comes apace. It is so near!
What can I do to quell this rising fear?
What counsel can you give? What secret path
Can save me from my angry father’s wrath?

FRIAR LAURENCE
This is a pass of great perplexity.
I do not like this forced fidelity.
A vow to God is not a feather light,
To be blown off by any gust of might.
Your father’s rage is terrible, I know.
He is a man who lets his passions grow
Into a forest where no light can shine.
This haste of his suggests a dark design,
To stamp the memory of Julian out
With wedding drums and a triumphant shout.
Have you no means to plead, to make him wait?

LIVIA
His heart is stone. He has decreed my fate.
My mother weeps, but dare not speak a word.
My will against his own is held absurd.
He calls me blot, and shame, and ungrateful child.
His gentle love has turned to hatred wild.
So if your wisdom cannot find a way,
If you can give no hope to light my day,
Then I have found a remedy myself.
And on this dark and final, desperate shelf,
I’ll place my soul.

[_She reaches into her bodice and reveals a small, gleaming dagger._]

LIVIA
This knife shall play its part.
If I must wed, ‘twill be within my heart
That Fabius shall find his wedding bed.
With my own blood my freedom shall be read.
This point will set me free. If all else fail,
This steel shall cut the knot of my travail.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Hold, daughter, hold! Put up that desperate blade!
Are you so lost, so witless, so afraid,
That you would turn from God’s most sacred gift?
To cast your soul into that lightless rift
Of self destruction? This is not the cure,
But a disease that ever will endure.
Damnation is the fruit of such a tree.
Give me the knife. O, put your trust in me.
There is a hope, if you have strength to dare.
A perilous and strange and wild affair,
That requires a will as strong as death
To see it through. If you can hold your breath
When terror calls, and play a fearful role,
I have a way to save your very soul
And body from this marriage.

LIVIA
Oh, speak on!
I’ll leap from any battlement, upon
The sharpest rocks below; I’ll walk among
The lairs of serpents; I will chain my tongue
To silence, or my limbs to any rack,
Before I’ll follow on this wedding track
With any man but Julian. Tell me what
I am to do, to change this awful lot.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Then listen well. Go home, be calm and meek.
Let not your face of this rebellion speak.
Consent to marry Fabius. Let a smile
Your true and inward sorrows now beguile.
Tomorrow is Wednesday. On that evening, pray
You may be left alone, and send away
Your nurse and mother. When you are secure,
Take this small vial.

[_He goes to a dusty shelf and takes down a small, dark glass bottle, filled with a pale liquid._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
Its contents will ensure
A sleep so like to death, it will deceive
The very doctors. They will all believe
Your life has fled. For when you drink this draft,
Through all your veins a chilling cold will waft.
Your pulse will cease its beat, no breath will show
That life remains. Your cheeks will lose their glow,
And on your lips a pallor will reside.
The lamps of life will seem to have all died.
For two and forty hours you will sleep
In this strange state, this counterfeit so deep.

LIVIA
And when they find me?

FRIAR LAURENCE
On the Thursday morn,
Your bridegroom comes to find his bride new born
Into the realm of death. Then, as our way
Requires for maids of noble houses, they
Will bear you in your finest wedding gown,
Uncoffined, on a bier, through all the town,
And lay you in the ancient family vault
Where all your ancestors, without a fault,
Lie sleeping. There you will remain until
You wake, as from a pleasant dream.

LIVIA
What will
Become of me when I awake in there,
Surrounded by the dead and stagnant air?

FRIAR LAURENCE
But you will not be there alone for long.
I’ll send a message, swift and sure and strong,
By a fast rider to your Julian’s ear,
To tell him of our plan, to make all clear.
He shall be told to come with hurried haste.
No moment of your freedom shall he waste.
And he and I will watch within the tomb
To wait for you to wake from that cold gloom.
And on that night, he’ll bear you far from here,
To live in freedom, with no cause for fear.
This is the way. But does your spirit quail?
Might not your woman’s heart at last then fail,
When you must drink this liquor, all alone?

LIVIA
Give it to me. O, speak not of a stone
Where my heart lies! For I have courage now
To face down death, to keep my sacred vow.
A thousand fears are better than the shame
Of wedding Fabius, and soiling my name
And Julian’s love.

[_She takes the vial._]

LIVIA
My thanks, good, holy man.
I’ll play my part and follow all your plan.
Farewell. My fate is in your hands and his.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Go now in peace. May heaven smile on this.
Remember, Wednesday night. Be strong of will.

LIVIA
My will is iron. I shall be quite still.

[_Exit LIVIA._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
I’ll send a letter to him now by post.
This plan, if it should fail, will cost the most
Of any stratagem I yet have tried.
But heaven, I pray, is on the lovers’ side.

[_Exit._]

SCENE III. A HALL IN CASSIAN’S HOUSE.

[_Enter CASSIAN and FABIUS._]

FABIUS
My lord, I thank you for this generous gift,
This honour you bestow. It is a swift
Advancement for my house, and for my name.
And Livia’s beauty puts the sun to shame.
And yet, a doubt does trouble my content.
Her look, when you declared your firm intent,
Was not of joy. It was a look of dread,
As if she heard a sentence to be dead.
I would not take a bride into my bed
Who brings a heart of ice, a soul of lead.

CASSIAN
Think nothing of it. It is but the way
Of maidens. They must weep and disobey
In little things, to show their modest heart.
It is a role, a well rehearsed new part
They learn to play. She’ll come around. A tear
Or two is just a passing, foolish fear
Of the unknown. A girl’s will is a kite;
It struggles on its string with all its might,
But he who holds the line can guide its flight.
I hold the line, my lord. I know her well.
A little storm, and then a peaceful spell.

FABIUS
But she did speak of promises, a vow
Already made. What truth is in this now?
She mentioned Julian. The banished one.
If her heart follows that rebellious son
Of our great rival’s house, what hope have I
To win her love, however hard I try?
A marriage built on such a shifting sand
Of loyalty, cannot for long now stand.

CASSIAN
A childish fancy. Nothing more, I say.
A secret meeting on a summer’s day,
A whispered word, a promise in the dark.
It has no substance, not a single spark
Of legal fire. It is but smoke and air.
And Julian is gone. He is nowhere
To press his claim. His name is black with shame,
His fortune lost, a blot upon his fame.
Do you, a man of wealth and high estate,
Fear such a ghost, such a poor trick of fate?

FABIUS
I fear no ghost, my lord. But a living bride
Whose spirit looks forever to the side,
To where her banished love in exile grieves,
Is not a prize a man of sense receives
With open arms. My pride could not endure
To be a husband who is never sure
His wife’s affection is his own to claim.

CASSIAN
Then be the man who can her spirit tame!
She is a girl. With jewels and with grace,
You’ll drive the memory of Julian’s face
From out her mind. A woman’s heart is wax,
On which the present a new seal attacks
And quite erases what was there before.
Show her your power, open wide the door
To all the pleasures that your wealth can buy,
And soon enough for your embrace she’ll sigh.
On Thursday, she is yours. The deed is done.
A new campaign for her heart is begun,
And you, my lord, are general of the field.
With such a prize, you cannot, must not, yield
To maiden’s fears. Be strong, and she’ll be strong.
Believe me, it will not be very long
Before she thanks me for my steady hand,
That gave her such a lord in all the land.

FABIUS
Your words have weight. And I do trust your sense.
I will dismiss this foolish diffidence.
Perhaps you’re right. A firm hand and a guide
Is all she needs. I’ll cast my doubts aside.
I’ll be a husband worthy of her grace,
And try to bring a true smile to her face.

CASSIAN
That is the spirit! Now, let us prepare.
The wedding feast must be beyond compare.
We’ll show the world how Cassian’s daughter weds.
Let no expense be spared on foods or beds
For all our guests. This union will confound
My enemies, on this, my solid ground.
Come, Fabius. My son. For so you are
In my heart’s eye. A bright and rising star.

[_Exeunt._]

SCENE IV. LIVIA’S CHAMBER.

[_Enter LIVIA and her nurse, CELIA._]

CELIA
Why, here’s a change! My little bird, my dear,
You look so calm. You’ve shed that awful tear
That sat upon your cheek for this whole day.
Has your good sense returned? Oh, tell me, pray!

LIVIA
Nurse, I have been a disobedient fool.
To set my will against my father’s rule
Was arrogant and wrong. He knows what’s best.
I have put his great wisdom to the test,
And I am now ashamed of my own pride.
I see the truth I foolishly denied.

CELIA
Oh, blessings on you! This is news indeed!
Now you are planting a far better seed
For your own future. Your good father’s love
Was never gone. He is a gentle dove
Behind the eagle’s mask that he puts on.
He will rejoice to see your temper gone.
Lord Fabius is a handsome, worthy lord.
You will be happy, take my honest word.

LIVIA
I know it now. Please, Celia, go to him,
To my good father. Tell him that the grim
And stubborn spirit that possessed me fled.
Tell him I am content, and I will wed
Lord Fabius on Thursday, with a heart
Prepared to play a loyal helpmeet’s part.
And ask for his forgiveness for my sin
Of willfulness.

CELIA
I’ll fly! My feet have wings!
This news will make the very rafters sing
With his delight. Oh, happy, blessed day!
I shall return to you without delay.
You have made your old nurse so very proud!

[_Exit CELIA._]

LIVIA
So, the first thread of this deception’s shroud
Is woven well. Poor Celia. Her kind heart
Is made a tool to help me play my part.
Forgive me, nurse. This lie is not to wound,
But born of love on desperate, stony ground.

[_Re-enter CELIA with LADY PORTIA._]

LADY PORTIA
My daughter, can this be? Does Celia speak
The truth? Have you become so mild and meek?

LIVIA
Mother, I have. I see my foolish ways.
I beg your pardon for these troubled days.
I am prepared to do my father’s will.
I ask you, help me now to be quite still
And choose the robes that I should wear for him.
Let us make ready for the joyful hymn
The choir will sing on my next wedding day.

LADY PORTIA
My dearest child! All clouds have blown away!
This is the daughter that I know and love.
Come, Celia, we have much to think of.
We’ll pick the finest silks, the brightest lace!
Joy shall return to this benighted place!
Oh, Livia, you have lifted such a weight
From my sad heart. You have redeemed your fate.

LIVIA
I wish to be alone tonight, if I
May ask it, mother. For I wish to cry
The final tears for my own maiden state,
And pray to heaven to accept my fate.
My mind is filled with many thoughts, and I
Would seek a peaceful hour before I lie
Down for the night.

LADY PORTIA
Of course, my sweet. Of course.
You must have time to calm this new resource
Of strength and duty. Celia, come with me.
We’ll leave her to her prayers. Lord, we thank thee!

[_Exeunt LADY PORTIA and CELIA._]

LIVIA
Farewell. God knows when we shall meet again.
I have a faint cold fear thrills through my veins,
That almost freezes up the heat of life.
What if this mixture do not work at all?
Shall I be married then tomorrow morning?
No, no. This shall forbid it. Lie thou there.

[_Laying down her dagger on the table._]

LIVIA
What if it be a poison, which the friar
Subtly hath ministered to have me dead,
Lest in this marriage he should be dishonoured
Because he married me before to Julian?
I fear it is. And yet, methinks, it should not,
For he hath still been tried a holy man.
I will not entertain so bad a thought.
What if, when I am laid into the tomb,
I wake before the time that Julian come
To rescue me? There’s a fearful point.
Shall I not then be stifled in the vault,
To whose foul mouth no healthsome air breathes in,
And there die strangled ere my Julian comes?
Or, if I live, is it not very like,
The horrible conceit of death and night,
Together with the terror of the place,
As in a vault, an ancient receptacle,
Where for these many hundred years the bones
Of all my buried ancestors are packed;
Where bloody Tybalt, yet but green in earth,
Lies festering in his shroud; where, as they say,
At some hours in the night spirits resort.
Alack, alack, is it not like that I,
So early waking, what with loathsome smells,
And shrieks like mandrakes torn out of the earth,
That living mortals, hearing them, run mad.
O, if I wake, shall I not be distraught,
Environed with all these hideous fears,
And madly play with my forefathers’ joints,
And pluck the mangled Tybalt from his shroud,
And, in this rage, with some great kinsman’s bone,
As with a club, dash out my desperate brains?
O, look! Methinks I see my cousin’s ghost
Seeking out Julian, that did spit his body
Upon a rapier’s point. Stay, Tybalt, stay!
Julian, I come! This do I drink to thee.

[_She drinks the contents of the vial and falls back upon her bed, stiff and pale._]

[_Enter CELIA, humming to herself, carrying a tray with a small breakfast._]

CELIA
Mistress! What, mistress! Livia! Fast, I warrant you.
Why, lamb! Why, lady! Fie, you slugabed!
Why, love, I say! Madam! Sweetheart! Why, bride!
What, not a word? You take your pennyworths now.
Sleep for a week, for the next night, I trow,
The noble Count Valerius hath set up his rest
That you shall rest but little. God forgive me,
Marry, and amen, how sound is she asleep!
I must needs wake you. Madam, madam, madam!
Aye, let the County take you in your bed,
He’ll fright you up, i’ faith. Will it not be?
[_She pulls back the curtains._]
What, dressed and in your clothes and down again?
I must needs wake you. Lady! Lady! Lady!
Alas, alas! Help, help! My lady’s dead!
O, well a day, that ever I was born!
Some aqua vitae, ho! My lord! My lady!

[_Enter LADY PORTIA, in a state of alarm._]

LADY PORTIA
What noise is here?

CELIA
O lamentable day!

LADY PORTIA
What is the matter?

CELIA
Look, look! O heavy day!

LADY PORTIA
O me, O me! My child, my only life,
Revive, look up, or I will die with thee!
Help, help! Call help.

[_Enter LORD PORTIA._]

LORD PORTIA
For shame, bring Livia forth. Her lord is come.

CELIA
She’s dead, deceased, she’s dead! Alack the day!

LADY PORTIA
Alack the day, she’s dead, she’s dead, she’s dead!

LORD PORTIA
Ha! Let me see her. Out, alas! She’s cold.
Her blood is settled, and her joints are stiff.
Life and these lips have long been separated.
Death lies on her like an untimely frost
Upon the sweetest flower of all the field.

CELIA
O lamentable day!

LADY PORTIA
O woeful time!

LORD PORTIA
Death, that hath ta’en her hence to make me wail,
Ties up my tongue and will not let me speak.

[_Enter FRIAR LAURENCE and COUNT VALERIUS, with musicians._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
Come, is the bride ready to go to church?

LORD PORTIA
Ready to go, but never to return.
O son, the night before thy wedding day
Hath Death lain with thy wife. There she lies,
Flower as she was, deflowered by him.
Death is my son in law, Death is my heir.
My daughter he hath wedded. I will die,
And leave him all. Life, living, all is Death’s.

COUNT VALERIUS
Have I thought long to see this morning’s face,
And doth it give me such a sight as this?

LADY PORTIA
Accursed, unhappy, wretched, hateful day!
Most miserable hour that e’er time saw
In lasting labour of his pilgrimage!
But one, poor one, one poor and loving child,
But one thing to rejoice and solace in,
And cruel death hath catched it from my sight!

CELIA
O woe! O woeful, woeful, woeful day!
Most lamentable day, most woeful day,
That ever, ever, I did yet behold!
O day! O day! O day! O hateful day!
Never was seen so black a day as this!
O woeful day, O woeful day!

COUNT VALERIUS
Beguiled, divorced, wronged, spited, slain!
Most detestable Death, by thee beguiled,
By cruel, cruel thee quite overthrown!
O love! O life! Not life, but love in death!

LORD PORTIA
Despised, distressed, hated, martyred, killed!
Uncomfortable time, why camest thou now
To murder, murder our solemnity?
O child! O child! My soul, and not my child!
Dead art thou! Alack, my child is dead,
And with my child my joys are buried.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Peace, ho, for shame! Confusion’s cure lives not
In these confusions. Heaven and yourself
Had part in this fair maid. Now heaven hath all,
And all the better is it for the maid.
Your part in her you could not keep from death,
But heaven keeps his part in eternal life.
The most you sought was her promotion,
For ‘twas your heaven she should be advanced.
And weep ye now, seeing she is advanced
Above the clouds, as high as heaven itself?
O, in this love, you love your child so ill,
That you run mad, seeing that she is well.
She’s not well married that lives married long,
But she’s best married that dies married young.
Dry up your tears, and stick your rosemary
On this fair corse, and, as the custom is,
In all her best array bear her to church.
For though fond nature bids us all lament,
Yet nature’s tears are reason’s merriment.

LORD PORTIA
All things that we ordained festival,
Turn from their office to black funeral.
Our instruments to melancholy bells,
Our wedding cheer to a sad burial feast,
Our solemn hymns to sullen dirges change,
Our bridal flowers serve for a buried corse,
And all things change them to the contrary.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Sir, go you in, and, madam, go with him.
And go, Sir Count. Everyone prepare
To follow this fair corse unto her grave.
The heavens do lour upon you for some ill.
Move them no more by crossing their high will.

[_Exeunt all but the FRIAR._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
A risky business, and a fearful one.
But if my letters reach young Julian’s hand,
All may be mended, and this sorrow’s sun
Will rise again upon this grieving land.
The potion’s strength, I did precisely measure.
Two nights and days shall she in slumber lie,
Then wake refreshed, to find her heart’s dear treasure,
And from this city’s prosecution fly.
But fate, I know, is a capricious thing.
A moment’s chance, a message gone astray,
Can turn the sweetest song the angels sing
Into the dirge of some disastrous day.
I’ll to my cell, and check my messenger.
God grant my plan has not already failed for her.

[_Exit FRIAR LAURENCE._]

SCENE II. A street in Padua.

[_Enter JULIAN._]

JULIAN
If I may trust the flattering truth of sleep,
My dreams presage some joyful news at hand.
My bosom’s lord sits lightly in his throne,
And all this day an unaccustomed spirit
Lifts me above the ground with cheerful thoughts.
I dreamt my lady came and found me dead,
Strange dream, that gives a dead man leave to think,
And breathed such life with kisses in my lips,
That I revived and was an emperor.
Ah me! how sweet is love itself possessed,
When but love’s shadows are so rich in joy!

[_Enter MARCUS, his servant, looking pale and distraught._]

JULIAN
News from Verona! How now, Marcus?
Dost thou not bring me letters from the friar?
How doth my lady? Is my father well?
How fares my Livia? That I ask again,
For nothing can be ill if she be well.

MARCUS
Then she is well, and nothing can be ill.
Her body sleeps in Portia’s monument,
And her immortal part with angels lives.
I saw her laid low in her kindred’s vault,
And presently took post to tell it you.
O, pardon me for bringing these ill news,
Since you did leave it for my office, sir.

JULIAN
Is it even so? Then I defy you, stars!
Thou know’st my lodging. Get me ink and paper,
And hire post horses. I will hence tonight.

MARCUS
I do beseech you, sir, have patience.
Your looks are pale and wild, and do import
Some misadventure.

JULIAN
Tush, thou art deceived.
Leave me, and do the thing I bid thee do.
Hast thou no letters to me from the friar?

MARCUS
No, my good lord.

JULIAN
No matter. Get thee gone,
And hire those horses. I’ll be with thee straight.

[_Exit MARCUS._]

JULIAN
Well, Livia, I will lie with thee tonight.
Let’s see for means. O mischief, thou art swift
To enter in the thoughts of desperate men.
I do remember an apothecary,
And hereabouts he dwells, which late I noted
In tattered weeds, with overwhelming brows,
Culling of simples. Meagre were his looks,
Sharp misery had worn him to the bones.
And in his needy shop a tortoise hung,
An alligator stuffed, and other skins
Of ill shaped fishes; and about his shelves
A beggarly account of empty boxes,
Green earthen pots, bladders and musty seeds,
Remnants of packthread and old cakes of roses,
Were thinly scattered, to make up a show.
Noting this penury, to myself I said,
‘An if a man did need a poison now,
Whose sale is present death in this domain,
Here lives a caitiff wretch would sell it him.’
O, this same thought did but forerun my need,
And this same needy man must sell it me.
As I remember, this should be the house.
Being holiday, the beggar’s shop is shut.
What, ho! Apothecary!

[_Enter APOTHECARY._]

APOTHECARY
Who calls so loud?

JULIAN
Come hither, man. I see that thou art poor.
Hold, there is forty ducats. Let me have
A dram of poison, such soon speeding gear
As will disperse itself through all the veins,
That the life weary taker may fall dead,
And that the trunk may be discharged of breath
As violently as hasty powder fired
Doth hurry from the fatal cannon’s womb.

APOTHECARY
Such mortal drugs I have, but Padua’s law
Is death to any he that utters them.

JULIAN
Art thou so bare and full of wretchedness,
And fear’st to die? Famine is in thy cheeks,
Need and oppression starveth in thine eyes,
Contempt and beggary hangs upon thy back.
The world is not thy friend, nor the world’s law.
The world affords no law to make thee rich.
Then be not poor, but break it, and take this.

APOTHECARY
My poverty, but not my will, consents.

JULIAN
I pay thy poverty and not thy will.

APOTHECARY
Put this in any liquid thing you will,
And drink it off, and, if you had the strength
Of twenty men, it would dispatch you straight.

JULIAN
There is thy gold, worse poison to men’s souls,
Doing more murder in this loathsome world,
Than these poor compounds that thou mayst not sell.
I sell thee poison, thou hast sold me none.
Farewell. Buy food, and get thyself in flesh.
Come, cordial and not poison, go with me
To Livia’s grave, for there must I use thee.

[_Exeunt._]

SCENE III. Friar Laurence’s cell.

[_Enter FRIAR JOHN._]

FRIAR JOHN
Holy Franciscan friar! Brother, ho!

[_Enter FRIAR LAURENCE._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
This same should be the voice of Friar John.
Welcome from Padua. What says Julian?
Or if his mind be writ, give me his letter.

FRIAR JOHN
Going to find a barefoot brother out,
One of our order, to associate me,
Here in this city visiting the sick,
And finding him, the searchers of the town,
Suspecting that we both were in a house
Where the infectious pestilence did reign,
Sealed up the doors and would not let us forth,
So that my speed to Padua there was stayed.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Who bare my letter, then, to Julian?

FRIAR JOHN
I could not send it, here it is again,
Nor get a messenger to bring it thee,
So fearful were they of infection.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Unhappy fortune! By my brotherhood,
The letter was not nice but full of charge,
Of dear import, and the neglecting it
May do much danger. Friar John, go hence.
Get me an iron crow and bring it straight
Unto my cell.

FRIAR JOHN
Brother, I’ll go and bring it thee.

[_Exit._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
Now must I to the monument alone.
Within this three hours will fair Livia wake.
She will beshrew me much that Julian
Hath had no notice of these accidents.
But I will write again to Padua,
And keep her at my cell till Julian come.
Poor living corse, closed in a dead man’s tomb!

[_Exit._]

SCENE IV. The Portia family monument. A churchyard.

[_Enter COUNT VALERIUS and his PAGE with a torch, flowers, and perfumed water._]

COUNT VALERIUS
Give me that torch. I’ll enter here alone.
Go, stand aloof, and when thou hear’st a foot,
Whistle to me as signal that one comes.
Give me those flowers. Do as I bid thee, go.

PAGE
[_Aside._]
I am almost afraid to stand alone
Here in the churchyard, yet I will adventure.

[_PAGE retires._]

COUNT VALERIUS
Sweet flower, with flowers thy bridal bed I strew.
O woe! thy canopy is dust and stones,
Which with sweet water nightly I will dew,
Or, wanting that, with tears distilled by moans.
The obsequies that I for thee will keep
Nightly shall be to strew thy grave and weep.

[_The PAGE whistles._]

COUNT VALERIUS
The boy gives warning something doth approach.
What cursed foot wanders this way tonight,
To cross my obsequies and true love’s rite?
What, with a torch! Muffle me, night, awhile.

[_He steps into the shadows. Enter JULIAN and MARCUS with a torch and a crowbar._]

JULIAN
Give me the mattock and the wrenching iron.
Hold, take this letter. Early in the morning
See thou deliver it to my lord and father.
Give me the light. Upon thy life, I charge thee,
Whate’er thou hear’st or seest, stand all aloof,
And do not interrupt me in my course.
Why I descend into this bed of death
Is partly to behold my lady’s face,
But chiefly to take thence from her dead finger
A precious ring, a ring that I must use
In dear employment. Therefore hence, be gone.
But if thou, jealous, dost return to pry
In what I further shall intend to do,
By heaven, I will tear thee joint by joint
And strew this hungry churchyard with thy limbs.
The time and my intents are savage wild,
More fierce and more inexorable far
Than empty tigers or the roaring sea.

MARCUS
I will be gone, sir, and not trouble you.

JULIAN
So shalt thou show me friendship. Take thou that.
Live, and be prosperous, and farewell, good fellow.

MARCUS
[_Aside._]
For all this same, I’ll hide me hereabout.
His looks I fear, and his intents I doubt.

[_MARCUS hides._]

JULIAN
Thou detestable maw, thou womb of death,
Gorged with the dearest morsel of the earth,
Thus I enforce thy rotten jaws to open,
And, in despite, I’ll cram thee with more food!

[_JULIAN begins to pry open the tomb._]

COUNT VALERIUS
This is that banished haughty Montague,
That murdered my love’s cousin, with which grief,
It is supposed, the fair creature died.
And here is come to do some villainous shame
To the dead bodies. I will apprehend him.
Stop thy unhallowed toil, vile Montague!
Can vengeance be pursued further than death?
Condemned villain, I do apprehend thee.
Obey, and go with me, for thou must die.

JULIAN
I must indeed, and therefore came I hither.
Good gentle youth, tempt not a desperate man.
Fly hence, and leave me. Think upon these gone.
Let them affright thee. I beseech thee, youth,
Put not another sin upon my head,
By urging me to fury. O, be gone!
By heaven, I love thee better than myself,
For I come hither armed against myself.
Stay not, be gone. Live, and hereafter say,
A madman’s mercy bid thee run away.

COUNT VALERIUS
I do defy thy conjurations,
And apprehend thee for a felon here.

JULIAN
Wilt thou provoke me? Then have at thee, boy!

[_They fight. VALERIUS is slain._]

PAGE
O Lord, they fight! I will go call the watch.

[_Exit PAGE._]

JULIAN
Let me peruse this face.
Ah, the Count Valerius, a noble gentleman.
What said my servant, when my soul refused
To heed his words as we did ride from Padua?
I think he told me Valerius should have married
My Livia. Said he not so? Or did I dream it?
Or am I mad, hearing him talk of Livia,
To think it was so? O, give me thy hand,
One writ with me in sour misfortune’s book!
I’ll bury thee in a triumphant grave.
A grave? O, no, a lantern, slaughtered youth,
For here lies Livia, and her beauty makes
This vault a feasting presence full of light.
Death, lie thou there, by a dead man interred.
[_He lays VALERIUS in the tomb._]
How oft when men are at the point of death
Have they been merry, which their keepers call
A lightning before death. O, how may I
Call this a lightning? O my love! my wife!
Death, that hath sucked the honey of thy breath,
Hath had no power yet upon thy beauty.
Thou art not conquered. Beauty’s ensign yet
Is crimson in thy lips and in thy cheeks,
And death’s pale flag is not advanced there.
Tybalt, liest thou there in thy bloody sheet?
O, what more favour can I do to thee,
Than with that hand that cut thy youth in twain
To sunder his that was thine enemy?
Forgive me, cousin! Ah, dear Livia,
Why art thou yet so fair? Shall I believe
That unsubstantial death is amorous,
And that the lean abhorred monster keeps
Thee here in dark to be his paramour?
For fear of that, I still will stay with thee,
And never from this palace of dim night
Depart again. Here, here will I remain
With worms that are thy chambermaids. O, here
Will I set up my everlasting rest,
And shake the yoke of inauspicious stars
From this world wearied flesh. Eyes, look your last!
Arms, take your last embrace! And, lips, O you
The doors of breath, seal with a righteous kiss
A dateless bargain to engrossing death!
Come, bitter conduct, come, unsavoury guide!
Thou desperate pilot, now at once run on
The dashing rocks thy seasick weary bark!
Here’s to my love!
[_He drinks the poison._]
O true apothecary!
Thy drugs are quick. Thus with a kiss I die.

[_He falls dead beside LIVIA. Enter FRIAR LAURENCE, with a lantern, crow, and spade._]

FRIAR LAURENCE
Saint Francis be my speed! how oft tonight
Have my old feet stumbled at graves! Who’s there?
Ah, Marcus. What light is yond, that vainly lends
Its glow to grubs and eyeless skulls? It burns
Within the Portia monument, it seems.

MARCUS
It doth so, holy sir, and there’s my master,
One that you love.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Who is it?

MARCUS
Julian.

FRIAR LAURENCE
How long hath he been there?

MARCUS
Full half an hour.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Go with me to the vault.

MARCUS
I dare not, sir.
My master knows not but I am gone hence,
And fearfully did menace me with death,
If I did stay to look on his intents.

FRIAR LAURENCE
Stay, then. I’ll go alone. Fear comes upon me.
O, much I fear some ill unlucky thing.
Julian! What, Julian!
[_He enters the tomb._]
Alack, alack, what blood is this, which stains
The stony entrance of this sepulchre?
What mean these masterless and gory swords
To lie discoloured by this place of peace?
Julian! O, pale! Who else? What, Valerius too?
And steeped in blood? Ah, what an unkind hour
Is guilty of this lamentable chance!
The lady stirs.

[_LIVIA wakes._]

LIVIA
O comfortable friar! Where is my lord?
I do remember well where I should be,
And there I am. Where is my Julian?

FRIAR LAURENCE
I hear some noise. Lady, come from that nest
Of death, contagion, and unnatural sleep.
A greater power than we can contradict
Hath thwarted our intents. Come, come away.
Thy husband in thy bosom there lies dead,
And Valerius too. Come, I’ll dispose of thee
Among a sisterhood of holy nuns.
Stay not to question, for the watch is coming.
Come, go, good Livia. I dare no longer stay.

LIVIA
Go, get thee hence, for I will not away.

[_Exit FRIAR LAURENCE._]

LIVIA
What’s here? A cup, closed in my true love’s hand?
Poison, I see, hath been his timeless end.
O churl! drunk all, and left no friendly drop
To help me after? I will kiss thy lips.
Haply some poison yet doth hang on them,
To make me die with a restorative.
[_She kisses him._]
Thy lips are warm.
Yes, noise? Then I’ll be brief. O happy dagger!
[_She takes JULIAN’s dagger._]
This is thy sheath. There rust, and let me die.

[_She stabs herself and falls on JULIAN’s body._]
