LEOFRIC
Nay, good Cassian, put away this humour. The market square is no place for your spleen. The sun is high, and men of note are forth. Let us not find a quarrel here today.

CASSIAN
My humour? Thou speak’st of it as a cloak I might cast off. This humour is my skin, and I am cursed to wear it. Why, thou art so fearful of a quarrel, thou wouldst bow to a stray dog lest it show its teeth. Thy blood runs as cold as a fishmonger’s slab. Thou wouldst not quarrel with the winter’s chill for freezing thee, but rather thank it for the chance to wear a thicker coat.

LEOFRIC
And what good comes of heat? It doth but boil the pot till it spills o’er and scalds the cook. I see them coming. Pray, be calm.

Enter Marius and his servant, Silas.

MARIUS
Look, Silas, what the kennel hath let loose. A man whose name is but a whispered shame. Good morrow, beggar.

CASSIAN
A beggar, am I? Ay, a beggar for the justice that your father stole. My house was built on honour, yours on lies. I’d rather beg in rags than feast in stolen silks.

MARIUS
My father’s name is clean. You’d best not foul it with your breath, you cur, lest I be forced to clean the air.

CASSIAN
A cur? Then I will bite. At least my bark is honest. Your tongue is like a serpent’s, smooth with venom. You speak of honour, yet you wear a traitor’s crest upon your sleeve.

LEOFRIC
Gentlemen, enough. The Watch will hear you. These stones have ears, and every window, eyes.

MARIUS
Let them hear. I will not be slandered by a penniless rogue whose own sire squandered all he had on wine and foolish pride.

CASSIAN
Thou liest! Now draw thy sword and prove thy words have metal in them, or I’ll proclaim thee coward from the fountain to the gates. Here’s my reply to your foul slander.
[_Draws sword._]

MARIUS
So be it. A lesson must be taught to those who have forgot their station. Silas, stand back.
[_Draws sword. They fight._]

LEOFRIC
Hold, in the city’s name! Forbear this madness! Cassian! Marius! Put up your blades!

[_Cassian wounds Marius in the arm. As Marius stumbles back, Silas draws a dagger and stabs Cassian from the side._]

CASSIAN
Ah! Treachery! Two against one! I am undone.
[_He falls._]

LEOFRIC
O foulest deed!

MARIUS
[_Clutching his arm._]
Thou meddling fool! Who bade thee interfere? This was my fight!

[_In a rage, Marius turns and runs Silas through with his sword. Silas falls._]

LEOFRIC
Sweet heaven, what devilry is this?

CASSIAN
My breath grows short. Leofric, come close. A pox on all their gilded houses. They have made a feast for the crows of me. My sun sets now at noon. Remember me…
[_Dies._]

LEOFRIC
His spirit is fled. O Cassian, brave and rash.

Enter the Captain of the Watch with guards.

CAPTAIN
What bloody work is this? Swords drawn and two men dead? Who is the author of this grim report?

MARIUS
I am, sir, though unwillingly. This man, Cassian, did force a quarrel on me with vile words against my family’s honour. We fought. His man did strike him foully, for which I have delivered justice.

LEOFRIC
He speaks not true! My name is Leofric, and I saw all. Brave Cassian was beset by two. He fought Lord Marius, and when he had the better of the duel, this servant struck him from the side, a coward’s blow. Marius then, in some distempered rage, did kill the very man who saved his life.

CAPTAIN
Two dead, and you the only one that stands, Lord Marius. This is a heavy charge.

MARIUS
He was a slanderer, a public nuisance. I did the city service, ridding it of such a pest. My wound cries out for vengeance, not for judgment.

CAPTAIN
This city’s law is not a rusty sword for every angry hand to take and wield. You have usurped its power, shed blood upon its stones, and slain a man who, by this witness’ tongue, was struck by foulest play. For this offence, the Magistrate will hear a fuller tale, but I will have your sword. Lord Valerius’s son or not, you are a prisoner now. Take him. The truth will out. For now, let sorrow weep where fury bled.

CAPTAIN
The truth is a stubborn root. It may be buried deep, but it will find the sun. Guards, see to the dead. Take them to the silent house, that they may be prepared for what rites their families decree. You, boy, stay with me.
[_The guards cover the bodies of Cassian and Silas with cloaks and carry them off._]

LEOFRIC
I am no boy, sir. I am a freeman of this city. My name is Leofric.

CAPTAIN
Your age is green, Leofric, however you may name yourself. Your face is yet unacquainted with a razor. But your tongue speaks with the weight of years. Lord Marius, your wound requires a surgeon. One will attend you in your cell.

MARIUS
A cell? You would put the son of Valerius in a common cage? My father’s name is etched into the very stones of your Magistrate’s hall. He will have this place torn down brick by brick ere I spend a night in chains.

CAPTAIN
Your father may be a titan, my lord, but even titans must bow to the law. The law has no family. It has no name but justice. March.
[_Guards escort the protesting MARIUS away. The CAPTAIN turns back to LEOFRIC._]

CAPTAIN
Now, Leofric. You have put yourself in a perilous place.

LEOFRIC
I have put myself in the place of truth, Captain. I could stand nowhere else. Cassian was my friend.

CAPTAIN
Friendship is a noble cause, but it can cloud a man’s eyes. Lord Valerius is a powerful man. His reach is long, his memory longer still. He will not suffer his son’s name to be stained by the testimony of… well, of a man whose fortune is counted in his friendships rather than his coin.

LEOFRIC
Is wealth the measure of honesty in this city? I saw what I saw. A good man slain by treachery, and his murderer then slain by the very hand he sought to save. If that truth costs me, then I will pay the price.

CAPTAIN
A brave sentiment. Let us hope it is not your last. You will be summoned before Magistrate Caius at first light. You will tell your tale to him, exactly as you told it to me. Add nothing for love of your friend, and subtract nothing for fear of his enemies. Let the facts be your shield and your sword.

LEOFRIC
I have no other weapon.

CAPTAIN
Then guard it well. Go now. Go to your home. Bar your door and speak to no one of this night’s work. The agents of great houses move like shadows in the night, and they can whisper poison as easily as they can wield a blade.

LEOFRIC
I thank you for your counsel, Captain.

CAPTAIN
It is not counsel. It is a warning. The city has wept enough blood today. I would not see it weep more for the sake of a truth that many powerful men will wish had died here on these stones, alongside your friend. Now go.
[_LEOFRIC bows his head and exits. The CAPTAIN looks down at the bloodstains on the cobblestones, a grim expression on his face. He shakes his head slowly and exits with the remaining guards. The stage is empty and silent._]

[_SCENE II. A lavishly decorated hall in the house of Lord Valerius. Tapestries hang on the walls depicting great battles. LORD VALERIUS, a man of sixty with a severe face and an air of absolute authority, is pacing. His daughter, HELENA, sits calmly embroidering, her movements precise and measured._]

VALERIUS
He should have returned an hour since. The boy is all fire and no foresight. He mistakes his temper for a virtue and calls his arrogance honour. Where is he, Helena? Where is your brother?

HELENA
Perchance he dines with friends, Father. Or perhaps he found a new diversion at the fencing school. Marius is a moth drawn to every flame he sees. You should not vex yourself with worry. He always returns, smelling of wine and cheap victory.

VALERIUS
Victory is never cheap. And his choice of company grows thin. That upstart Cassian, son of a debtor and a fool. I have heard Marius trades words with him. The boy has no sense of station. He would argue with a beggar over the quality of his rags.

HELENA
It is a fault of his blood, Father. Your blood. You have never suffered a slight to pass unanswered. Marius is but a poor copy of a grand design. He has your pride, but not your wisdom.

VALERIUS
Wisdom is the harvest of a long and bitter season. He is yet in the spring of his life. But he must learn. He must learn that our name is a fortress. One does not simply walk out of the gates to brawl with the rabble.
[_A servant, LUCIUS, enters, his face pale with fear. He hesitates at the door._]

VALERIUS
What is it? Speak, man! Do not stand there trembling like a leaf in a gale. Has your tongue been cut out?

LUCIUS
My lord… there is news. From the city. It is… it is grave news.

HELENA
[_Putting down her embroidery._]
Grave for whom, Lucius? Speak plainly. Suspense is a commoner’s drama.

LUCIUS
It is Lord Marius, my lady. He… he has been taken by the City Watch.

VALERIUS
[_Stops pacing. His voice is dangerously quiet._]
Taken? Explain this word. Has he been arrested? For what trifle? A tavern brawl? A wager left unpaid? The Captain of the Watch knows better than to lay hands on my son for such petty things.

LUCIUS
It is more than a trifle, my lord. Two men are dead.

HELENA
[_Rises to her feet._]
Dead? Who?

LUCIUS
A young nobleman named Cassian… and a servant, my lord. One of your own. A man named Silas.

VALERIUS
Cassian. So the fool finally pushed my son too far. It was inevitable. Marius was provoked, I am certain of it. And this servant, Silas? Did he fall defending my son’s honour?

LUCIUS
I do not know the whole of it, my lord. The word in the street is that there was a duel. They say… they say Lord Marius is accused of both their deaths.

VALERIUS
Both? That is a lie! A fabrication of his enemies! My son is hot blooded, but he is no butcher. To kill his opponent in a fair duel is one thing. But his own servant? Absurd. Who carries this tale? Who is the witness to this slander?

LUCIUS
They speak a name, my lord. Leofric. A friend to the dead man, Cassian.

HELENA
Leofric. I know that name. His father was a scholar, a quiet man who owned a small scriptorium near the old aqueduct. The family has no wealth, no influence. They are nothing.

VALERIUS
They are a swarm of gnats, and one has chosen to bite a lion. This Leofric. He will recant his story. He will fall to his knees and beg forgiveness for his perfidious tongue. Lucius!

LUCIUS
My lord?

VALERIUS
Send for Quintus. Tell him I have need of his particular talents. And send a message to Magistrate Caius. Tell him that Lord Valerius requests an immediate audience. No, demands one. This farce will end before the sun rises. My son will be home by morning.

HELENA
Father, wait. A heavy hand may crush this problem, but it may also scatter the seeds of a worse one. A demand will set the Magistrate against you. Caius is a proud man, a servant of the city’s process. Let us not make an enemy of the judge.

VALERIUS
The judge? I am the judge of what is right for my own house! My son, my heir, sits in a filthy cell because of the word of a nobody. I will not have it!

HELENA
And you shall not. But anger is a blunt instrument. Let me speak to this Leofric. A woman’s words may persuade where a man’s threats might fail. We must understand the nature of our accuser. Is he driven by grief? Or by greed? Every man has a price, or a weakness.

VALERIUS
You are as cold as winter frost, daughter. It is a quality I admire. Very well. Let Quintus first pay the man a visit. Let him assess this weakness you speak of. If coin can seal his lips, he shall have a river of it. If not… then Quintus will know what other methods are required. But I will see the Magistrate. I will remind him of the long friendship between our houses. I will remind him on whose patronage his own career was built. The law may be a shield for the common man, but it is a tool for men like me. Now go, Lucius! Do as I have commanded!
[_LUCIUS bows and hurries out._]

HELENA
Marius has made a ruin of his day, Father. We must be the architects of his salvation. Be careful. Justice is a performance, and the crowd is watchful. We must not be seen as villains in their play.

VALERIUS
I care not for the judgment of the crowd. I care for the honour of my name. And it will not be bought low by a boy witness and a dead rogue. It will not.

[_SCENE III. A small, sparsely furnished room. LEOFRIC sits at a simple wooden table, a cup of untouched wine before him. A single candle flickers, casting long shadows. He holds a small, carved wooden bird in his hand._]

LEOFRIC
He carved this for my nameday, two summers past. ‘It is a hawk, Leo,’ he said, his laugh like ringing steel. ‘For you are ever watchful, while I am the foolish sparrow that flies too close to the sun.’ And so you did, Cassian. So you did. Your heart was too great for your station, your spirit too bright for this grimy world. You saw honour in all things, and could not believe that others saw only advantage.
[_He looks around the empty room._]
And now I am alone with your memory. A ghost to keep me company. The Captain’s words echo in my mind. ‘A perilous place.’ He does not know the half of it. To speak against the house of Valerius is to stand on the shore and command the tide to retreat. They will come for me. They will not send guards or men in uniform. They will send whispers first. Then gold. Then steel. What shield have I? The truth. A flimsy thing, it seems, against a mountain of coin and a legacy of power.
[_A soft knock at the door._]
So soon. The whispers have arrived.
[_He rises, places the wooden hawk on the table, and unbars the door. QUINTUS stands on the threshold. He is an older man, dressed in fine but sober clothing. His smile is pleasant, but his eyes are cold and assessing._]

QUINTUS
Leofric, son of Marcus?

LEOFRIC
I am he. What is your business with me at this late hour?

QUINTUS
My name is Quintus. I am a humble servant of the house of Valerius. May I enter? The night air is filled with sorrow and ill humours. It is better to speak indoors.

LEOFRIC
I have no business with the house of Valerius, save what I must give before the Magistrate.

QUINTUS
Ah, but that is the very business I have come to discuss. A sad affair. A tragedy for all. Lord Marius is beside himself with grief and regret. A young man’s passions, you understand. They flare up like dry tinder. He held no true malice for your friend.

LEOFRIC
The point of his sword held malice enough. And what of his servant, Silas? Did he too die of a young man’s passion?

QUINTUS
A terrible misunderstanding in the heat of the moment. Lord Valerius, the boy’s father, is a man of deep feeling. He wishes to make amends. He knows that no amount of silver can replace a friend, but it can perhaps ease the burdens of life. He has authorized me to offer you a gift. A token of his profound sorrow. Five hundred pieces of gold. A new life, perhaps, in some distant city where the sun is warmer and memories are less keen.

LEOFRIC
You call it a gift. I call it a bribe. You would have me sell my friend’s memory for five hundred pieces of gold. You would have me trade my voice for a pouch of coin and call it a fair exchange.

QUINTUS
I would have you be practical. What is done is done. No testimony will bring your friend back from the shades. But your words could well destroy a great house, and bring ruin upon a young man who acted foolishly, but not with a villain’s heart. Is that a worthy legacy for your friend? To be remembered as the cause of such strife? Lord Valerius is a generous man. He could be persuaded to make it a thousand. Think of it. You could travel. You could become a merchant. You need never want for anything again.

LEOFRIC
I want for one thing only. Justice.

QUINTUS
[_His smile fades._]
Justice is a complicated machine, young man. It has many gears and levers, and most are operated by men of influence. Your truth is but one small cog. It can be easily removed, and the machine will run on as before. Or it can jam the works, and be crushed in the process.

LEOFRIC
Then let it be crushed. I will speak what I saw. I will honour Cassian’s name with the truth of his last moments. He was betrayed. I will not betray him again in the telling of it.

QUINTUS
You are a fool. A noble fool, perhaps, but a fool nonetheless. You choose a path of thorns when a path of roses is offered. Lord Valerius is not a man to be crossed. He offered you comfort. Do not force him to offer you pain instead.

LEOFRIC
Is that a threat?

QUINTUS
It is a statement of fact. A description of the world as it is, not as sentimental boys wish it to be. Reconsider. I will wait for your answer until the sun rises. If you are wise, you will take the gold and be gone from this city by noon. If you appear before the Magistrate… then you have chosen your fate. Good evening, Leofric. I hope you sleep well.
[_QUINTUS turns and departs, leaving the door open. LEOFRIC stands for a long moment, then slowly closes it and shoots the bolt. He walks back to the table and picks up the small wooden hawk, his hand trembling slightly._]

[_SCENE IV. The Magistrate’s chamber. It is an austere room, dominated by a large, dark wooden desk. MAGISTRATE CAIUS, a stern, intelligent man in his late fifties, sits behind it. LORD VALERIUS and HELENA stand to one side. LEOFRIC stands alone on the other. MARIUS is brought in by two guards, his arm in a sling. He looks pale but defiant. The CAPTAIN of the Watch is also present._]

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
This is a preliminary hearing, not a trial. The purpose is not to determine guilt or innocence, but to hear the charges and the testimony, and to decide if this matter warrants a full proceeding before the city’s tribunal. Lord Marius, son of Valerius, you stand accused of the murder of Cassian, son of Remus, and of Silas, a servant of your own house. How do you answer these grave charges?

MARIUS
I answer that they are a gross distortion of the truth, Magistrate. The man Cassian, known for his insolent tongue and his baseless pride, accosted me in the street. He hurled insults at me and at the honour of my family. He drew his sword first. I did but defend myself, as is the right of any nobleman. We fought a duel. It was a tragic outcome, but it was a matter of honour, not of murder.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
And the second death? That of your servant, Silas?

MARIUS
In the confusion of the fight, my servant, in a misguided show of loyalty, sought to intervene. His interference was a stain upon the duel’s honour. Cassian was already bested. In my rage at this dishonourable act, I struck the man. It was a fit of temper, a terrible mistake for which I feel the deepest remorse. But it was not murder. It was a swift and unfortunate consequence of a servant’s folly.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
A fit of temper that left a man dead. Lord Valerius, you wished to speak. Be brief. You are not a witness here.

VALERIUS
I wish only to speak to the character of the accuser. This man, Leofric. Who is he? A penniless friend of the deceased, his heart full of venom and grief. His testimony is tainted by his loyalty. Would you trust the word of a grieving whelp over the honour of a house that has served this city for five generations? My son is no butcher. He is a nobleman who defended his name. This is a private matter of honour that has been twisted into a public spectacle. I ask that you dismiss these baseless charges and release my son to my care.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Your house’s honour is noted, Lord Valerius. But the law must examine facts, not lineage. Leofric, you were witness to the entire event. Let the court hear your account. Speak freely, and without fear. You have the protection of this chamber.

LEOFRIC
[_His voice is clear and steady._]
Thank you, Magistrate. I was with my friend Cassian when Lord Marius and his servant approached. It was Lord Marius who began the quarrel. He mocked Cassian’s poverty and his family name. Cassian, who valued his honour above all else, was provoked by these cruel words. He drew his sword only after Lord Marius called him a liar and a coward’s son.

MARIUS
He lies!

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Silence! Continue, Leofric.

LEOFRIC
They fought. And it was a fierce duel. But Cassian was the better swordsman. He was quicker, more skilled. He wounded Lord Marius in the arm and disarmed him. The duel was won. But as Lord Marius stumbled back, his servant, Silas, who had been standing behind Cassian, drew a hidden dagger. He stabbed my friend in the back.

HELENA
[_Her voice cuts through the room, sharp as glass._]
A coward’s blow. How convenient for your tale. It paints your friend as a martyr.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Lady Helena, you will not speak again, or you will be removed.

LEOFRIC
It is the truth. Cassian fell, crying out at the treachery. He was dying. It was then that Lord Marius, clutching his wounded arm, turned not to his wounded opponent, but to his own servant. He called him a meddling fool and, in a fit of rage not at the dishonour, but at the interference, he ran Silas through with his sword. He did not kill Silas for honour’s sake. He killed him for pride’s. He killed the man who had just saved his life from a lost duel. Cassian died in my arms moments later. Those are the facts as I saw them.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Captain, does this account match what he told you at the scene?

CAPTAIN
It does, Magistrate. Word for word. His story has not changed.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Lord Marius, you claim you had already bested Cassian. Yet your wound and Leofric’s testimony suggest otherwise.

MARIUS
The wound is a trifle! A scratch! The boy is lying to avenge his friend and destroy my name!

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
I have heard enough. The testimony of an eyewitness, and an officer of the Watch, presents a narrative of treachery and a subsequent killing born of rage. This is no simple duel of honour. Two men are dead. One by a traitor’s stroke, and the traitor himself slain by his master. These are matters of the gravest import. I find that there is sufficient cause to proceed to a full and public trial.

VALERIUS
This is an outrage! An insult to my house!

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Your house is not on trial, my lord. Your son is. The charge is murder. Until the tribunal convenes, the prisoner shall be held in the Citadel. Bail is denied.

MARIUS
No! Father, do something!

VALERIUS
Caius, you forget yourself! You forget who your friends are!

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
In this chair, Lord Valerius, I have no friends. I have only the law. The law has spoken. Guards, remove the prisoner. This hearing is concluded.
[_The guards take MARIUS, who struggles and shouts, from the chamber. VALERIUS stares at the Magistrate with pure hatred, then turns his venomous gaze upon LEOFRIC. HELENA’s expression is unreadable, cold and calculating. She places a hand on her father’s arm._]

HELENA
Come, Father. The battle is not lost. It has merely begun.
[_VALERIUS allows his daughter to lead him from the room, but he does not break his stare from LEOFRIC until he is gone. LEOFRIC is left standing alone in the center of the room, facing the Magistrate. The weight of his choice, and the danger it has brought him, settles upon him like a shroud._]

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Stay a moment, Leofric.

[_LEOFRIC, who had started to turn, stops. The guards at the door close it, leaving the two of them alone with the Captain of the Watch. The room feels vast and empty now._]

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
What you have done today required courage. I will not pretend otherwise. You spoke the truth against a house that considers truth a commodity to be bought and sold.

LEOFRIC
I spoke for my friend. His memory is not for sale.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
A noble sentiment. But nobility is a poor shield against a rich man’s dagger. Lord Valerius will not let this rest. His pride is the most dangerous beast in this city, and you have wounded it deeply.

LEOFRIC
I am aware of the risk, Magistrate.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Are you? You have seen the father’s rage. You have not yet seen the daughter’s cunning. Helena is twice as dangerous. She does not shout. She plans. She does not threaten. She acts. They will not wait for the tribunal. They will seek to silence you.

LEOFRIC
I am a soldier. I can protect myself.

CAPTAIN
With respect, sir, you are one man. House Valerius commands a small army of servants, agents, and men who owe them debts of coin or fear. They will not challenge you to a duel in the street. They will find you in a dark alley. Or they will pay your landlord to forget he saw you. Or poison your wine at a tavern.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
The Captain speaks grimly, but he speaks the truth. You are no longer just a man grieving his friend. You are a witness for the state. The most important witness in the most volatile case this city has seen in a decade. Your life is now the property of the law, because without you, the law is blind.

LEOFRIC
What would you have me do? Flee the city? Let them win?

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
No. Nothing would please them more. You will stay. But not in your lodgings. You will be housed here, in the Citadel. In the officer’s barracks, under the direct protection of the City Watch.

LEOFRIC
You would make me a prisoner?

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
I would keep you alive. You will have your freedom within these walls, but you will not leave them. Not until you have given your testimony before the full tribunal. Captain, you will see to it personally. Assign your most trusted men. Men who do not have gambling debts or ambitious wives.

CAPTAIN
I have two such men. It will be done, Magistrate.

LEOFRIC
I do not want this. I need to arrange for Cassian’s funeral. I need to speak to his family.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Cassian’s family is already under the protection of the Watch. His father understands. He wants justice, and he knows you are the only key to it. As for the funeral, you may mourn him here. Justice will be his monument. Do you understand what is at stake? This is not about one dead boy in a garden. It is about whether the law rules this city, or if the gold and name of Valerius do. Today, you chose a side. You cannot now abandon the field.

[_LEOFRIC looks from the Magistrate to the Captain. The reality of his situation sinks in. He is no longer his own man. He nods slowly, a gesture of resignation._]

LEOFRIC
I understand.

MAGISTRATE CAIUS
Good. Captain, escort him. Find him a defensible room, away from the outer walls. And send a message to my clerk. Tell him to unseal the archives on House Valerius. All of them. I want to know every contract, every grievance, every rumour for the last fifty years. If they mean to fight a war, I will not be unarmed.

CAPTAIN
Yes, Magistrate. This way, Leofric.

[_The Captain opens the door. LEOFRIC takes one last look at the empty room, the place where his life was irrevocably changed, and follows him out. MAGISTRATE CAIUS remains, standing before his high backed chair. He runs a hand over his tired face, the weight of the city on his shoulders._]

***

[_The scene changes. A grand solar in the Valerius estate. The room is opulent, filled with gilded furniture and tapestries depicting the glorious history of their house. VALERIUS smashes a porcelain vase against a marble fireplace. It shatters with a pathetic crash._]

VALERIUS
Insupportable! An outrage! That jumped up clerk, that sanctimonious weasel Caius, dares to put my son, my heir, in a common cell! He will pay for this. I will ruin him! I will buy every magistrate on the tribunal! I will have his position, his home, his reputation!

[_HELENA sits in a high backed chair by the window, swirling a dark red wine in her goblet. She has not moved. She watches her father’s tantrum with a detached, clinical interest._]

HELENA
Breaking my mother’s favourite vase will not free Marius, Father. Nor will it sway Magistrate Caius. His kind do not have a price. That is what makes them so irritating.

VALERIUS
Everything has a price! You simply have not found it yet. Fear. Ambition. A loved one’s safety. Caius has a weakness. Everyone does.

HELENA
Perhaps. But attacking Caius is a frontal assault. It is messy, loud, and he will have anticipated it. It is what he expects you to do. You are thinking like a bull. You must think like a serpent.

VALERIUS
And you are the expert on serpents, are you not? What is your brilliant plan? Do you have one, or do you just enjoy watching me tear our house apart in my fury?

HELENA
The problem is not the magistrate. The problem is not the tribunal. They are merely the stage. The problem is the story they have been told. The foundation of this entire case rests on one man. One witness. The soldier. Leofric.

VALERIUS
The boy who lied! He should be in the cell next to Marius.

HELENA
He is not lying. Not in a way we can easily prove. He is telling what he saw, or what he believes he saw. To the court, his belief is fact. So we cannot break the story. We must break the storyteller.

VALERIUS
Kill him. It is the simplest way. A stray arrow in the barracks yard. A shiv in the night. It can be done. I have men.

HELENA
No. Killing him now makes him a martyr. It proves his story was true. It would confirm Marius’s guilt in the eyes of the entire city. It is a crude solution for a delicate problem. If you cut out the tongue, you do not silence the words it has already spoken.

VALERIUS
Then what? What is your serpent’s wisdom, daughter?

HELENA
First, we must understand him. Who is this Leofric? Where does he come from? Does he have family? Debts? Enemies? Does he have a lover? Does he favour boys? Is he fond of drink? Every man has a crack in his armour. We must find his. I have already sent agents to his former barracks and his lodgings. We will know everything about him by morning.

VALERIUS
And when you find this crack?

HELENA
We will insert a wedge. We will create a new story. A better story. Perhaps Leofric and Cassian were not friends. Perhaps they were rivals. Perhaps Leofric was in love with Cassian’s intended bride and saw an opportunity. Perhaps he owed Cassian money from a gambling debt and provoked the whole affair.

VALERIUS
Lies. Rumours. How does that help Marius?

HELENA
It muddies the water. If the witness’s character is questionable, his testimony becomes questionable. We will feed these stories to the gossips in the market, to the clerks in the scribes’ guilds, to the servants of other noble houses. By the time the tribunal convenes, Leofric will not be seen as an honourable soldier telling a hard truth. He will be a jealous, debt ridden liar with a grudge. We will give the judges a reason to doubt. Doubt is all we need.

[_VALERIUS stops pacing. He looks at his daughter, a grudging respect in his eyes._]

VALERIUS
That is a longer game. Marius is in the Citadel now.

HELENA
He will be comfortable enough. I have already sent a wagon of our own bedding, food, and wine for him. And a large purse for the warden. My brother will not suffer any true hardship. But before we begin this war of whispers, there is a cleaner path. A more direct one.

VALERIUS
I am listening.

HELENA
We make him an offer. People who cannot be bought by the state can often be bought by a house that offers more. Security. Wealth beyond his dreams. A new life somewhere far from here. Why destroy him if we can simply purchase him? It is much tidier.

VALERIUS
And who makes this offer? He will not listen to me.

HELENA
Of course not. You are the raging bull. This requires a gentler hand. I will send Corvus.

[_VALERIUS raises an eyebrow. Corvus is the family’s discreet agent, a man who handles their most sensitive and unpleasant affairs._]

VALERIUS
Corvus? He is not known for his gentle hand.

HELENA
He will be whatever I tell him to be. He will begin with honeyed words. If Leofric refuses, the honey will turn to poison. Corvus will make him understand the consequences of his stubbornness. He will explain, in precise detail, the other future that awaits him. The one where his name is filth and his life is forfeit. Give Leofric a choice between a golden road and a pit of vipers. Even a man of honour may be tempted to save his own skin.

VALERIUS
And if he still refuses?

HELENA
Then we will know he cannot be bought. And we will proceed to destroy him. In the meantime, you will do exactly as I say, Father. You will visit the temples. You will make a show of praying for the soul of young Cassian. You will speak to all who will listen of this terrible tragedy, this misunderstanding between noble sons. You will be the grieving patriarch, concerned only with the truth and the honour of your house. You will not threaten Caius. You will not try to bribe any more judges. You will be the picture of dignity. Leave the dirty work to me.

[_She takes a slow, deliberate sip of her wine. Valerius stares at her, a mixture of pride and unease on his face. He sees not just his daughter, but the future of his house, cold, ruthless, and far more clever than he._]

VALERIUS
Very well. Send Corvus. Let us see if this soldier’s honour has a price.

***

[_The scene shifts. A small, stone room in the Citadel barracks. It contains a simple cot, a wooden chest, and a single, high, barred window. LEOFRIC stands at the window, staring out at the practice yard below. He holds a small, crudely carved wooden hawk in his palm, turning it over and over. The door opens and the CAPTAIN enters._]

CAPTAIN
The Magistrate was right. You have been here less than a day, and already the vultures are circling.

LEOFRIC
What do you mean?

CAPTAIN
Word is spreading through the city. I have heard three different versions of your story in the last hour alone. One says you were Cassian’s debtor. Another that you planned the whole affair to ruin Lord Marius. My personal favourite is the one where you and Cassian fought over a barmaid at The Gilded Flagon the night before the duel.

LEOFRIC
None of that is true. We were there, but we were not fighting. We were toasting his future.

CAPTAIN
The truth does not matter to the storytellers. This has Helena’s signature all over it. A little poison in a thousand ears. By week’s end, you will be the villain of the piece.

LEOFRIC
[_He clenches his fist around the wooden hawk._]
Let them talk. I will speak the truth at the tribunal. That is all that matters.

CAPTAIN
Perhaps. But be careful. Words are not their only weapons. Do not eat or drink anything that is not brought to you by me or Sergeant Kaelen. Do not speak to any guards you do not recognize. Do not stand too close to any high ledges. Valerius has gold, and gold buys loyalty, or at least a moment’s carelessness.

LEOFRIC
You paint a cheerful picture, Captain. I am a prisoner in a fortress meant to protect me.

CAPTAIN
That is the nature of the beast. You poked the leviathan, son. Now you must live in the cage until we can kill it. There is someone here to see you.

LEOFRIC
I do not want to see anyone.

CAPTAIN
He says he is an agent of House Valerius. He calls himself Corvus. The Magistrate says you should hear what he has to say. Know your enemy’s heart, he said. It is your choice.

[_Leofric hesitates. His instinct is to refuse, to offer nothing but contempt. But Caius’s advice rings true. He needs to understand what he is facing._]

LEOFRIC
Send him in. But you will remain outside the door.

CAPTAIN
I will be right outside. And so will Kaelen. Shout if he so much as looks at you wrong.

[_The Captain exits. A moment later, a man enters. He is of middling age, dressed in the dark, immaculate robes of a wealthy secretary or scholar. He is neither imposing nor threatening in his appearance. He moves quietly, and his face is calm, almost serene. This is CORVUS. He gives a slight, respectful bow._]

CORVUS
Leofric. Thank you for agreeing to see me. I am Corvus. I serve the house of Lord Valerius. May I sit?

[_He gestures to the wooden chest. Leofric just stares at him, saying nothing. Corvus takes it as permission and sits, folding his hands neatly in his lap._]

CORVUS
First, allow me to offer the sincere condolences of my lord and his daughter, Helena. The death of your friend, Cassian, was a tragedy. A dreadful, unforeseen tragedy. Passions ran high. Mistakes were made. It is a cause for sorrow, not for vengeance.

LEOFRIC
Is that what you came here to tell me? That your masters are sorry?

CORVUS
They are. They are also practical. They recognize that a great wrong has been done to you. You have lost a friend. And now, you find yourself in this uncomfortable position, caught in the gears of the law. It is an ordeal no man should have to suffer.

LEOFRIC
The only thing I suffer is the knowledge that my friend’s murderer has not yet faced justice.

CORVUS
Justice is a complicated thing, Leofric. Is it justice to destroy a great house over a moment of youthful folly? To ruin a young man’s life for a mistake made in the heat of combat? Or is justice the restoration of balance? You have lost a friend. A hole has been torn in your life. Lord Valerius wishes to mend that hole.

LEOFRIC
He can’t bring Cassian back.

CORVUS
No. But he can secure your future. He can give you a life Cassian would have wanted for you. A life of purpose and prosperity, far from the troubles of this city.

LEOFRIC
Get to your point. What is your offer?

[_Corvus smiles, a thin, bloodless expression._]

CORVUS
Direct. I admire that. It saves time. The offer is this. Your memory of the events in the garden has become clouded. The shock of your friend’s death, the chaos of the moment. You are no longer certain of what you saw. You cannot say for sure who struck which blow, or in what order. Your testimony becomes confused, unreliable. You recant your original statement.

LEOFRIC
I will not.

CORVUS
Please, let me finish. In exchange for this... clarification... you will be given a captain’s commission in the Sixth Legion, currently stationed in the eastern provinces. A quiet post, with good pay and ample opportunity for advancement. You will also receive a chest of silver, five thousand pieces, delivered to you before you leave. Enough to buy an estate when your service is done. A new name. A new life. No one will ever trouble you again. You will be safe, wealthy, and respected. All for a simple lapse of memory.

[_Leofric stares at Corvus, his expression a mask of contempt. He says nothing for a long moment._]

LEOFRIC
This is what my friend’s life is worth to you? A commission and a chest of silver?

CORVUS
It is not a valuation. It is a recognition of your inconvenience. And it is an incredibly generous offer. I urge you to consider it. It is, I assure you, the best you will receive.

LEOFRIC
Tell Lord Valerius that my friend’s honour is not for sale. And neither is mine. Tell him I will see his son punished for what he did. That is my answer. Now get out.

[_Corvus does not move. His serene expression remains, but a coldness enters his eyes. The room seems to drop in temperature._]

CORVUS
I was afraid you might say that. Pride is a luxury, Leofric. A luxury you can no longer afford. I was sent to show you two doors. You have just closed the golden one. I am now obliged to show you what lies behind the other.

LEOFRIC
Are you threatening me?

CORVUS
Threatening is such a vulgar word. I am educating you. If you persist in this folly, your life will unravel. The whispers you may have heard? They will become a roar. We have learned much about you. We know about the money you borrowed two months ago from the moneylender on the Street of Tinsmiths. A gambling debt, was it not? We will make it seem as though you owed that money to Cassian.

LEOFRIC
It was for my sister’s dowry. And I have nearly paid it back.

CORVUS
The moneylender will say otherwise. His loyalty can be purchased for a pittance. We know your training master in the legion disliked you. He thought you were insubordinate. He will testify that you have a history of disrespect for your betters. We know you once came to blows with a man whose wife smiled at you. He will swear you have a violent, jealous temper.

LEOFRIC
These are lies. Twists.

CORVUS
They are threads. And we will weave them into a tapestry of your character. By the time I am done, every man and woman on that tribunal will see you as a violent, debt-ridden opportunist who envied his noble friend and engineered this tragedy for his own gain. Your truth will be buried under a mountain of our lies. Your name will be a curse in this city.

LEOFRIC
I do not care what people think. I care about what happened.

CORVUS
Then perhaps you care about your sister. The one with the dowry. She lives in a small village south of here, does she not? A quiet place. It would be a shame if bandits were to raid it. Or if a sudden sickness were to sweep through her husband’s livestock. Or if her husband were to simply decide he made a bad bargain and cast her out. Accidents happen. Fortunes change.

[_Leofric takes a step forward, his hands clenched into fists. His face is pale with fury._]

LEOFRIC
You leave her out of this.

CORVUS
I am leaving her out of nothing. I am merely showing you that your actions have consequences that extend far beyond these walls. You see yourself as a pillar of honour, standing alone. But you are not alone. You are connected. And we will pull on every one of those connections until you break. Think about it. Your reputation, ruined. Your family, endangered. Your own life, a constant, waking fear. The guards here can be bought. The cooks can be bought. Is this the life you choose? A life of looking over your shoulder, waiting for the knife that will surely come? All to avenge a man who is already dead?

LEOFRIC
Get out.

CORVUS
I will leave you with that thought. The golden door is not locked yet. But the handle grows hot. You have until the new moon. If we do not hear from you by then, we will assume you have made your choice. And we will make ours.

[_Corvus stands, smooths his robes, and gives the same, small, polite bow he did upon entering. He walks to the door and leaves without a backward glance. The door closes, the bolt sliding shut from the outside. Leofric stands alone in the center of the small room, trembling not with fear, but with a profound and chilling rage. He looks down at the wooden hawk in his hand, its smooth shape a stark contrast to the ugliness of the world he now inhabits._]

[The sounds of the city are muffled, distant. Leofric remains motionless for a long time, the wooden hawk held tight in his palm. The rage is a fire in his belly, but it burns with a cold, clear light. He begins to pace the small cell, a caged wolf. Three steps to the wall, turn, three steps back. His movements are precise, economical, the ingrained discipline of the legion. The fury is not wild, but focused. It is a weapon being sharpened.]

LEOFRIC
(To himself, a low whisper)
Caius. They will not do this to you. They will not make your memory a lie to cover their own filth. They will not.

[He stops pacing and looks at the heavy wooden door. He walks to it and places his ear against the wood, listening. He hears nothing but the faint, rhythmic tramp of a distant patrol. He is utterly alone. He goes to the small, hard cot and sits, placing the hawk beside him. He runs a hand over his face, the gesture of a man bone weary, not of the body, but of the soul. After a moment, a heavy bolt scrapes on the other side of the door. Leofric tenses, his hand instinctively going to where the hilt of his gladius would be. The door swings inward with a groan. A hulking, brutish guard stands there. His name is BASTO. He carries a wooden bowl with a lump of grey bread and a cup of water.]

BASTO
Food.

[He shoves the bowl and cup onto the small table near the door with a clatter, slopping some of the water.]

LEOFRIC
Who was he? The man in the fine robes.

BASTO
Not your concern. Eat or starve. Your choice.

LEOFRIC
He works for Senator Valerius. Caius’s father.

[Basto stops, his hand on the door. He turns his head slightly, a flicker of something, perhaps interest, perhaps annoyance, in his dull eyes.]

BASTO
Clever boy. Cleverness gets you a shorter rope, sometimes.

LEOFRIC
Does the Senator pay you well to keep me quiet? Does he pay you enough to look the other way when a different kind of visitor comes? One with a knife instead of words?

BASTO
You talk too much. The kind of talk that gets a man’s tongue cut out.

LEOFRIC
They threatened my sister.

[Basto pauses again. He gives a short, humourless laugh that is more like a bark.]

BASTO
Everyone has a sister. Or a mother. Or a dog. They always find something to pull on. Now shut your mouth. I have other cells to feed.

[Basto pulls the door shut. The bolt slides home with a final, metallic thud. Leofric does not touch the food. He picks up the wooden hawk again, turning it over and over. The memory of Corvus’s words about his sister coils in his gut like a serpent. He sees her face, her easy smile, the small home she has made for herself. The rage returns, hotter this time, blurring his vision. He wants to smash something, to howl, to fight until he can no longer stand. But he forces the feeling down, packing it tight, turning it into fuel. An hour passes. The light from the high, barred window begins to fade into a dusty grey. Again, the sound of the bolt. Leofric gets to his feet, his body coiled like a spring. The door opens, but it is not Basto. It is a man his own age, dressed in the worn but clean tunic of a retired legionary. He has a square jaw and steady, thoughtful eyes. This is GALLUS.]

GALLUS
Leofric. By all the gods, what have you gotten yourself into?

[Leofric’s tension breaks in a wave of disbelief and relief.]

LEOFRIC
Gallus? How did you get in here?

GALLUS
The same way we got into the fortress at Vercunium. A bit of silver and a lot of nerve. The day guard owes me from a dice game. I told him I was your cousin, come to deliver a final message. He seemed to like the idea of it being final.

[Gallus steps inside, and the door is pulled shut behind him by an unseen hand. The cell suddenly feels much smaller.]

LEOFRIC
You should not have come. It is not safe.

GALLUS
When have we ever cared about safe? I heard the whispers. ‘The commoner who killed the Senator’s son.’ I knew it was a lie the moment I heard it. You loved Caius like a brother. You were more of a brother to him than his own blood.

LEOFRIC
They are trying to bury him under that lie. And me with him. A man was just here. Corvus. A soft-spoken snake with poison in his heart.

GALLUS
I know of him. He is the Senator’s shadow. He does the work that requires no honour. What did he want?

LEOFRIC
He offered me a choice. Take the blame for a hunting accident, and they will give me a quiet life in some forgotten corner of the empire. Or I can fight them, and they will destroy me. They will twist my service record, invent debts, bribe witnesses. They will make me a monster.

GALLUS
Empty threats. The legion knows you. Your name is good.

LEOFRIC
He said they would go after my sister. Elara. He described her village. He knew about her husband’s farm. It was not an empty threat, Gallus. It was a promise.

[Gallus’s face hardens. He looks around the grim cell, at the untouched food.]

GALLUS
The Valerius name has always cast a long and dirty shadow. Caius was the only clean thing to come from that house. So what will you do?

LEOFRIC
I will not let them win. I will not let them dishonour him.

GALLUS
Be careful, my friend. Honour is a luxury for men who have nothing left to lose. You still have Elara. Sometimes the bravest thing a soldier can do is retreat. Live to fight another day. They offer you a life. Take it. Disappear. I can help you. We can get you on a grain ship to Egypt before the new moon.

LEOFRIC
And let them say I ran because I was guilty? Let his father spit on his grave and call it justice? No.

[Leofric holds up the wooden hawk.]

LEOFRIC
Do you remember this?

GALLUS
Of course. The winter we were pinned down on the Rhine. We were all half starved, half frozen, and starting to see ghosts in the trees. Caius sat by the fire for three nights, carving that from a piece of firewood. Said it was a hawk so it could fly high enough to see the way home.

LEOFRIC
He gave it to me. He said, ‘You see things as they are, Leofric. You see the mud and the blood. But a good leader must also see what could be. He needs the hawk’s eye.’ He believed that. He believed in the Republic. In justice. Not the Senator’s justice, bought with gold and threats, but real justice.

GALLUS
He was a dreamer. It is what we loved about him. It is also what got him killed.

LEOFRIC
It was not a dream that got him killed. It was what he found. I think he found something. Something rotten. The day before we went on that hunt, he was not himself. He was quiet, disturbed. He had spent the morning in the city archives. He told me he was looking into old legion supply manifests. I asked him why. He just shook his head and said, ‘Even the best fields can hide stones, Leo. You just have to know where to dig.’

GALLUS
Supply manifests? That is as dull as watching rust form. What could he possibly find there?

LEOFRIC
I do not know. But his father knew he was looking. The ‘accident’ was too perfect. A frayed bowstring. A spooked horse. I was a hundred paces away when it happened. By the time I reached him, he was gone. But there were two other men with us. The Senator’s men. They said they saw my horse bolt towards him. They said I was careless.

GALLUS
And they are the only witnesses. Apart from you.

LEOFRIC
Exactly. Corvus told me I have until the new moon. Then they move against my sister.

GALLUS
The new moon is four days from now. There is no time. Leofric, you are one man in a locked room. The Senator controls the city guard, the tribunals, the flow of coin. This is not a fair fight. It is a man standing against a tidal wave.

LEOFRIC
Then I had better learn to swim. You said the day guard owes you.

GALLUS
He does. But not enough to risk his neck against a Senator.

LEOFRIC
I do not need him to risk his neck. I just need him to carry a message. And to bring me something.

GALLUS
What? A sword? You will not get ten feet.

LEOFRIC
No. Not a sword. Something better. Papyrus and a charcoal stick.

[Gallus looks at his friend, at the hard set of his jaw and the cold fire in his eyes. He sees the man he followed into battle a dozen times. He nods slowly.]

GALLUS
I will do it. But what message can you possibly send that will change anything?

LEOFRIC
Caius was looking for stones. I am going to give him a mountain.

***

[The scene changes. A lavish study, walls lined with scrolls in neat cubbies. A large desk of polished cedar stands in the center of the room, upon which sits a single, elegant wine decanter and two silver cups. The air is rich with the scent of beeswax and old papyrus. SENATOR VALERIUS stands by a window, looking out over the city lights. He is a man in his late fifties, with a face that was once handsome but has been hardened by years of political maneuvering. He holds himself with an iron rigidity, but his eyes are haunted. CORVUS stands silently near the desk.]

VALERIUS
He is a stubborn fool. Just like his mother. Common born. They mistake obstinacy for principle.

CORVUS
He is a legionary, my lord. They are trained to hold the line, even when it is hopeless. It is their defining virtue. And their greatest weakness.

VALERIUS
Did he believe you? About his sister?

CORVUS
He understood the sincerity of the threat. It is the only part of our conversation that truly landed. Fear is a much more effective tool than reason. He will break. They all do, eventually.

VALERIUS
He has until the new moon. I want this business concluded. The grain allocations for the Eastern legions are up for debate in the Senate next month. I cannot have this… distraction. My family name must be impeccable.

CORVUS
It will be. By the time of the tribunal, if he forces our hand, Leofric will be remembered as a footnote. A cautionary tale about a commoner who flew too close to the sun.

[Valerius turns from the window and pours two cups of the dark red wine. He hands one to Corvus.]

VALERIUS
My son… Caius… he trusted this man. He spoke of his courage, his loyalty. He saw him as the embodiment of the old republican virtues.

CORVUS
Your son was an idealist, my lord. He saw the world as he wished it to be, not as it is. It is a flaw of the young. This Leofric was an opportunist who took advantage of that idealism. That is the story we will tell. It is a story the world will easily believe.

VALERIUS
The story we will tell. Yes. It is all stories, in the end. The one that is repeated the most becomes the truth. See to it that ours is shouted from the rooftops.

[The door to the study opens and a young woman enters. It is LIVIA, the Senator’s daughter and Caius’s sister. She is sharp, intelligent, and has none of her father’s political artifice. She looks at Corvus with open dislike.]

LIVIA
Father. I thought you would be alone.

VALERIUS
Corvus was just updating me on a matter of state.

LIVIA
Does your matter of state have a name? Does it have the name Leofric? The man you have locked away in the Mamertine?

VALERIUS
Livia, this is not your concern. It is a painful, legal matter.

LIVIA
It is my brother’s death. That makes it my concern. I have been speaking to the servants. To the stable boys. They say Leofric worshipped Caius. That he pulled him from a collapsed trench at the siege of Danubia. That he carried him for two miles on his own back. That is not the act of a jealous rival.

CORVUS
Grief can curdle loyalty into resentment, my lady. A hero one day can be a murderer the next. Human nature is… malleable.

LIVIA
My brother was not killed by a malleable man. He was killed by a secret. His own secret. What did he find out, father? What did my idealistic, honest brother uncover that made you so afraid?

[Valerius’s face becomes a mask of stone. His voice is dangerously quiet.]

VALERIUS
You will watch your tone. I am your father and a Senator of this Republic. I am grieving for my only son. This Leofric is a murderer who used my boy’s trust to get close to him. End of story.

LIVIA
No. That is the beginning of your story. Not the truth. Caius was looking into your grain contracts. He mentioned it to me. He said there were discrepancies. He said the numbers did not add up. He said the grain shipments to the Northern legions were lighter than the manifests claimed. Light enough for soldiers to go hungry. Light enough for a man to grow very, very rich.

[The silence in the room is heavy, suffocating. Corvus does not move a muscle. Valerius stares at his daughter, his grief momentarily eclipsed by a cold, calculating fury.]

VALERIUS
He was mistaken. A boy playing with ledgers he did not understand.

LIVIA
Was he? Or was he a good man who discovered his father was a thief? A man who stole bread from the mouths of the very soldiers who defend us? Did you have my brother killed to protect your fortune?

[Valerius takes a step toward her. His hand is raised as if to strike her, but he catches himself. He lowers it slowly, trembling with effort.]

VALERIUS
Get out. Go to your room. You are overwrought with grief. You are not to leave the house until this… business is concluded. Corvus will see to it.

LIVIA
You can lock me in my room, but you cannot lock the truth in a cell with an innocent man. It will get out. It always does.

[Livia turns and walks out, her back straight and proud. Valerius stands for a long moment, his breathing heavy. He drains his wine cup in one swallow.]

VALERIUS
She has her mother’s fire. And her mother’s foolishness.

CORVUS
A threat, my lord?

VALERIUS
A child. She will be dealt with. But this complicates things. It makes the Leofric problem more urgent. If he knows what Caius knew…

CORVUS
He does not. Your son was noble, but not careless. He would not have confided something so dangerous without proof. And if he had proof, we would have found it by now. Leofric knows only that his friend is dead and he is being framed. His motivations are simple. Honour. Revenge. We can manage that.

VALERIUS
See that you do. The new moon. Not a day longer. I want his answer, or I want his silence. Permanently.

CORVUS
It will be done, my lord.

[Corvus gives a slight bow and exits the study, leaving Valerius alone with the ghosts of his children: one dead, one a stranger.]

***

[Back in Leofric’s cell. It is the dead of night. The only light is a sliver of pale moonlight from the high window. Leofric is not sleeping. He is sitting on the edge of his cot, the piece of papyrus Gallus brought him on his lap. He has been writing, and now he stares at what he has written. The door bolt scrapes softly. It is Basto, making his nightly rounds.]

BASTO
Still awake, legionary? Plotting your escape?

[He holds a small oil lamp, which casts long, dancing shadows on the walls.]

LEOFRIC
I am composing a letter. To my sister.

BASTO
Touching. It will make a nice keepsake for her, after they hang you.

[Basto starts to move on, but Leofric’s voice stops him.]

LEOFRIC
They threatened her, you know. The Senator’s man. Corvus. He said accidents happen. Fortunes change. He said a man like you could be bought. Are you a man who can be bought, Basto?

BASTO
I am a man who gets paid. There is a difference. Now go to sleep.

LEOFRIC
What is your price? For a night’s work? A new pair of boots? A cask of wine? A week with a woman who does not smell of stale beer? The Senator can offer you that. But what can he offer you a year from now? When this is all forgotten. When you are just a guard who was on duty the night a troublesome prisoner had an ‘accident’.

[Basto turns fully now, his face grim in the lamplight.]

BASTO
What are you talking about?

LEOFRIC
Men like Corvus and the Senator do not like loose ends. They will pay you to look the other way. But later, when they look at you, they will only see the man who knows their secret. And secrets make them nervous. You will be a loose end, Basto. And they are very good at tying them up. A tavern brawl. A robbery gone wrong. A slip on a wet cobblestone.

BASTO
You are trying to scare me.

LEOFRIC
I am trying to save you. I am offering you something better than the Senator’s gold. I am offering you a future.

[Leofric stands and walks toward Basto. He holds out the folded piece of papyrus.]

LEOFRIC
This is not a letter to my sister. It is a letter to a man named Marcus Vipsanius. He is the Tribune of the Plebs. He is a rival of Senator Valerius. A man of ambition. And, they say, a man of some integrity.

BASTO
You want me to deliver a letter? You are insane. If I am caught, they will flay me alive.

LEOFRIC
You will not be caught. And you will not deliver it. Not yet. You will take it. You will hide it. And if anything happens to me, if I am found in my cell with a rope around my neck or a knife in my gut, you will make sure that letter finds its way to the Tribune.

[Basto looks at the papyrus as if it were a scorpion.]

BASTO
What is in it?

LEOFRIC
Everything. The grain contracts. The names of the Senator’s front companies. The shipping routes. My testimony about what Caius was investigating. It is the Senator’s political ruin, all on one sheet. It is your protection. As long as I am alive, you are just a guard. The moment I am dead, you become the man who holds the dagger at the Senator’s throat. They will not dare touch you.

BASTO
And if I just take this to the Senator now? He would pay me a fortune.

LEOFRIC
He would pay you. And then he would kill you before the coin was cold in your purse. You would have proven you cannot be trusted. My way, you live. You might even find yourself rewarded by a grateful Tribune Vipsanius, when he becomes the most powerful man in the city.

[Basto stares at Leofric, then at the papyrus. He is a simple man caught in a complex game. He sees the truth in Leofric’s words. The cold logic of survival.]

BASTO
And what do you get out of this?

LEOFRIC
I get to stay alive until the new moon.

[Slowly, Basto reaches out his hand. His rough fingers take the folded papyrus. He tucks it deep inside his leather tunic. He looks at Leofric, a new, grudging respect in his eyes.]

BASTO
Four days, legionary. You had better be right about this Tribune.

LEOFRIC
He wants power. Valerius is in his way. That is all I need to be right about.

[Basto nods once, a short, sharp gesture. He turns without another word and leaves, the bolt sliding shut with its familiar, final sound. Leofric stands in the center of the cell. The chilling rage is gone. In its place is a cold, patient certainty. He picks up the wooden hawk from the cot, its form solid and real in his hand. He is no longer waiting for the tide. He is building a wall.]

[The scene is Leofric’s cell. He remains standing for a long moment after Basto has gone. He walks the small space, three paces one way, three paces back. It is the caged walk of a wolf. He stops, runs a hand over his shorn head. He looks down at his hands, calloused and scarred from years with a gladius. He clenches them, unclenches them. He is a weapon, left to rust. But the rust is being scraped away. He lies down on the cot, but not to sleep. He stares at the stone ceiling, his mind a battlefield of strategies and contingencies. The wooden hawk is on his chest, rising and falling with his slow, steady breaths.]

[FADE TO BLACK]

[SCENE CHANGE]

[The light changes. It is now warm, cast from oil lamps of polished bronze. We are in the private study of SENATOR VALERIUS. The room is the antithesis of Leofric’s cell. Walls are covered in fine frescoes depicting mythological hunts. A large desk of dark, citrus wood is laden with scrolls. A man in a simple but immaculately clean grey tunic, QUINTUS, stands by the desk. He is in his forties, with a lean, intelligent face and eyes that miss nothing. He is a freedman, and the Senator’s most trusted agent. SENATOR VALERIUS sits in a large, high backed chair, swirling a cup of deep red wine. He is older, perhaps sixty, with a face that was once handsome but has been eroded by indulgence and cruelty into a mask of cold command.]

VALERIUS
The prefect of the annona sends his apologies. He says the dockworkers are threatening to strike. Again. He invents new excuses with every shipment.

QUINTUS
He is a nervous man, Senator. He fears rumors.

VALERIUS
He fears shadows. And I am paying him a small fortune to stand in the sun and pretend it is noon. See that he receives a gift. Something to bolster his courage. A reminder of who fills his plate.

QUINTUS
Of course. And the other matter? The prisoner?

[Valerius takes a slow sip of wine. He savors it, letting the question hang in the air. The prisoner is an annoyance, a piece of grit in a well oiled machine.]

VALERIUS
The legionary. Leofric. Yes. A stubborn piece of business. His friend Caius was a fool. A romantic. He thought he was uncovering some great crime against the Republic. He was merely uncovering the way the world works.

QUINTUS
Fools can be dangerous. Their beliefs make them unpredictable. Caius was found with notes. Names. Shipping manifests. Incomplete, but… suggestive.

VALERIUS
All of which were conveniently lost in the fire at his apartment. Along with the fool himself. A tragedy. I believe I sent a note of condolence to his mother. Did I, Quintus?

QUINTUS
You did, Senator. A very moving tribute to a promising young clerk.

VALERIUS
Good. Appearances are the foundation of power. As for his friend, this Leofric. He knows what Caius knew. Possibly more. We cannot have him speaking at a trial. Even a trial we control. Words have a way of echoing outside the courtroom.

QUINTUS
He has refused all offers. He has been… uncooperative with the interrogators. They say he has a soldier’s discipline. He can endure pain.

VALERIUS
Pain is a crude instrument. Any butcher can use it. I need to know precisely what he knows and who he might have told. I cannot simply have him killed in his cell. Not yet. It would look too convenient, coming so soon after the tragic death of his friend. It would invite questions. And our friend, the Tribune Vipsanius, loves to ask questions.

QUINTUS
What are your orders?

VALERIUS
Go and see him. You have a way of finding cracks in a man’s armor. Do not threaten him with the rack. He expects that. He is prepared for it. Find what he loves. A sister in the countryside, I believe? A childhood home? Remind him that the world is much larger than his cell. Remind him that fires can happen anywhere.

QUINTUS
I understand. And if he remains obstinate?

VALERIUS
Then we will have to arrange a more permanent solution before the new moon. But I want his knowledge first. It is a loose thread. I will not have it dangling. Go now. I wish to read my correspondence from Alexandria. I am told this year’s harvest is exceptional.

[Quintus gives a slight bow, a gesture of perfect subservience, and exits the room as silently as he entered. Valerius stares into his wine, a small, cruel smile touching his lips. He is not angry. He is merely solving a logistical problem.]

[FADE TO BLACK]

[SCENE CHANGE]

[The squalid, one room apartment of BASTO. A thin stew bubbles over a small brazier. His wife, ELARA, a worn but resilient woman in her late thirties, is mending a tunic. The air is thick with the smells of cooking and poverty. Basto enters, his face pale. He shuts the door and leans against it, his eyes darting around the small space as if seeing it for the first time.]

ELARA
You are late. The watch changed an hour ago.

BASTO
There was… a delay.

ELARA
Is it the prisoner? The legionary? The men talk at the market. They say he was friend to that clerk who died in the fire. They say he is dangerous.

[Basto does not answer. He walks to the table and splashes water from a clay pitcher into a cup. His hand is shaking slightly. He drinks it down in one gulp.]

BASTO
He is just a man.

ELARA
You look as if you have seen a ghost. Basto, what has happened? Every day you go to that pit, I pray to Vesta to keep you safe. You are a guard, not a torturer. Not an executioner. This is not what you were meant for.

BASTO
It is what feeds us.

[His voice is harsh. He runs a hand over his face. He can feel the stiff fold of the papyrus inside his tunic, a patch of ice against his skin.]

ELARA
It barely feeds us. And it is draining the life from you. What did he do? What did he say to you?

BASTO
Nothing. He said nothing. Leave it be, woman.

[He turns away from her, unable to meet her gaze. He pretends to check the latch on the window. Elara gets up and comes to him, placing a hand on his arm. Her touch is gentle.]

ELARA
I am your wife. I know when a lie is on your lips. Your fear is a stench in this room. It is him, isn't it? He has put some poison in your mind.

BASTO
Poison? Or the antidote. I do not know which.
[He pulls away from her. His eyes scan the room again. The loose floorstone near the hearth. The space behind the small household shrine. He needs to hide it. He needs to put it out of sight, but he knows he will never put it out of his mind.]

ELARA
What are you looking for? Basto, you are frightening me.

BASTO
Go and see if old Mara has any bread left to sell. I have a few extra coins. Get the good loaf.

ELARA
We cannot afford the good loaf.

BASTO
Do it! Just… go. Please. I need a moment.

[Elara stares at him, her face a mixture of hurt and fear. She sees a desperation in him she has not seen since their child was sick with the fever. She nods slowly, takes a thin shawl from a hook, and leaves, closing the door softly behind her. The moment she is gone, Basto moves with frantic energy. He pulls the papyrus from his tunic. He looks at it, his hand trembling. It feels heavy, impossibly heavy. He kneels by the hearth and uses the blade of his eating knife to pry up the loose floorstone. The space beneath is dark and smells of damp earth. He hesitates for a second, then shoves the papyrus into the hole. He pushes the stone back into place, scraping it until it sits flush. He brushes the dirt and dust over the cracks, trying to make it look untouched. He stands up, breathing heavily, his back covered in a cold sweat. He has buried the danger. But he knows, with a terrible certainty, that it is a seed. And he does not know what will grow from it.]

[FADE TO BLACK]

[SCENE CHANGE]

[The office of MARCUS VIPSANIUS, Tribune of the Plebs. It is a practical, working room, not a luxurious study. It is filled with maps of the city, stacks of legal scrolls, and wax tablets. VIPSANIUS is a man in his early forties, with the sharp, focused energy of a tireless politician. He is not a patrician by birth, and it shows in his lack of casual arrogance. His aide, FLAVIA, a woman of sharp intelligence and severe hairstyle, stands opposite him at a large table.]

FLAVIA
The motion to fund the aqueduct repairs failed. Valerius’s faction voted it down. They claim budgetary constraints.

VIPSANIUS
Of course they do. The same week he buys a new villa in Baiae and three prize horses from Hispania. He bleeds the city for his own coffers and calls it fiscal responsibility. What of the grain dole?

FLAVIA
That is the more interesting news. The shipments from Sicily and Africa are down. Officially, due to poor weather. But prices at the market have not yet spiked. Because three new ships, large freighters, arrived from Alexandria two days ago.

VIPSANIUS
Alexandria? The state contracts are with merchants in Carthage. The Egyptian grain is a private import. Expensive.

FLAVIA
It was imported by the ‘Oceanus Trading Company.’ A new name. Registered only six months ago. Their offices are a rented room above a tavern in the Subura. No one has ever seen the owners.

VIPSANIUS
A front. For who?

FLAVIA
The port master is a cousin of Senator Valerius’s wife. He waived the usual inspection fees. A gesture of goodwill, he called it, to encourage new trade.

[Vipsanius looks at Flavia. They have worked together for years. They can have a whole conversation in a single shared glance.]

VIPSANIUS
So. Valerius is suppressing the state subsidized grain to create an artificial scarcity. Then his own shadow company brings in a private supply, which he will sell back to the state at an inflated price to make up for the ‘shortfall.’ He profits from the problem he creates.

FLAVIA
It is elegant, in its own corrupt way. But it is all circumstantial. A cousin. A new company. We have no proof that links Valerius to Oceanus Trading. Nothing but smoke.

VIPSANIUS
Where there is this much smoke, Flavia, there is a fire burning somewhere. Valerius is becoming bolder. He thinks himself untouchable. He buys senators, he starves the people, he treats the Republic as his personal treasury. One day, he will make a mistake. He will leave a witness. He will drop a piece of paper. And I will be there to catch it.

FLAVIA
Let us hope it is soon. The people are growing restless. They blame the magistrates for the high prices. They do not see the hand pulling the strings.

VIPSANIUS
Then we must find a way to show them. Keep digging into this Oceanus Trading. Find me a name. A clerk. A ship’s captain. Someone will talk for the right price. Or the right threat.

[He turns to look at a map of Rome on the wall, his jaw set in determination. He is a man playing a long, dangerous game, waiting for his opponent to overextend.]

[FADE TO BLACK]

[SCENE CHANGE]

[Back in Leofric’s cell. Hours have passed. The light from the high grate has shifted. Leofric is sitting on the edge of his cot, carving a new detail into the wooden hawk with a small, sharp stone he has worked loose from the wall. He is completely absorbed in the task. The sound of the bolt being drawn is different this time. It is slower, more deliberate. Not the rough, hurried movement of Basto. Leofric does not look up, but his hands still. The door opens. QUINTUS enters. He is dressed as he was before, a simple grey figure who seems to absorb the light around him. He looks utterly out of place in the filth of the cell, yet perfectly comfortable. Two guards wait in the corridor behind him. Quintus dismisses them with a flick of his hand. The door is closed, but not bolted.]

QUINTUS
The legionary, Leofric. Formerly of the Sixth Victrix. Decorated for courage at the siege of Massilia. Twice. An impressive record.

[Leofric continues to look at the wooden hawk in his hands. He says nothing.]

QUINTUS
My master, Senator Valerius, takes an interest in men of talent. He believes your current situation is a waste. He has authorized me to make you an offer.

[Quintus waits. Leofric remains silent, turning the hawk over in his fingers.]

QUINTUS
A full pardon. A purse of gold large enough to buy a farm. A new life, far from Rome. All you have to do is sign a statement. A simple document, confirming that your friend Caius was distraught. That he had debts. That he spoke of taking his own life.

LEOFRIC
He had no debts. And he was not distraught. He was angry.

[Leofric’s voice is quiet, rough from disuse. He finally looks up at Quintus. His eyes are like chips of flint.]

QUINTUS
Anger can curdle into despair. It is a sad, but common, story. Your signature would simply confirm it. It would close an unfortunate chapter. You would be free.

LEOFRIC
I am already free. This is just a cage. My mind is not in it.

QUINTUS
A philosophical distinction that will be of little comfort when your bones are aching from the cold. The Senator is a generous man. But his generosity has a limit. He wishes to know what Caius found. And what he told you.

LEOFRIC
He told me he was serving the Republic.

QUINTUS
He was meddling in affairs that did not concern him. Like a child playing with his father’s sword. He cut himself. It is a pity. It need not happen to you. Tell me what you know, Leofric. Names. Companies. Shipments. Give me the details, and the farm is yours.

LEOFRIC
If I knew nothing, I would already be dead. If I told you everything, I would be dead an hour later. So it seems my silence is the only thing of value I have left.

[A flicker of something, perhaps admiration, perhaps annoyance, crosses Quintus’s face.]

QUINTUS
You are intelligent. I will grant you that. You believe your silence is a shield. But a shield can be broken. Or it can be bypassed. You have a sister, do you not? Octavia. She lives near Beneventum. She has two small children. A boy and a girl.

[Leofric’s hands stop moving. He grips the wooden hawk so tightly his knuckles are white.]

LEOFRIC
Leave her out of this.

QUINTUS
I cannot. You brought her into it when you chose this path. Farms can be prone to accidents. A stray spark from a lamp. A dry thatch roof. Bandits on the road. A family can disappear overnight. And who would ask any questions? A poor farming family, gone. It is a tragedy, like your friend Caius. But it is not news.

[The air in the cell grows thick, heavy. Leofric slowly places the wooden hawk on the cot beside him. He rises to his feet. He is taller than Quintus, broader. For a moment, the placid prisoner is gone, and the legionary stands in his place.]

LEOFRIC
If a single hair on their heads is harmed, you will not live to see another winter.

[Quintus does not flinch. He does not step back. He meets Leofric’s gaze with unnerving calm.]

QUINTUS
A threat from a man in a locked cell. How novel. You misunderstand your position completely. You are a fly in a jar. I can watch you buzz and beat yourself against the glass for as long as I like. And when I grow bored, I can put the lid on. Your life, your sister’s life, the lives of her children… they are all in my hand. Your information is the price of their safety. The Senator is growing impatient. You have until the new moon. Four days. After that, his generosity will expire. And so will his patience.

[Quintus turns to leave. He has his back to Leofric, an act of supreme confidence.]

LEOFRIC
Tell your master he is not the only man in Rome who has friends.

[Quintus stops, his hand on the door.]

QUINTUS
The friends you had are dead or hiding. You are alone, legionary. You would do well to remember that. Think of the children. Think of the fire.

[Quintus opens the door and is gone. The bolt slides home with a deafening finality. Leofric stands frozen in the middle of the cell. The cold certainty he felt before is gone, replaced by a churning sea of ice and fury. The threat is no longer to him. It is to them. His plan, his letter, his gamble with Basto… it was a shield for himself. But it is no protection for a farm hundreds of miles away. He walks back to the cot and picks up the wooden hawk. His hand is shaking now, not with fear, but with a rage so profound it threatens to consume him. He has built a wall, but his enemy has simply walked around it.]

[FADE TO BLACK]
