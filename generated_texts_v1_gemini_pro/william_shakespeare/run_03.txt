Enter Cassian and Leofric.

LEOFRIC
I pray thee, good Cassian, let us retire.
The day is hot, the Ironcloaks are abroad,
And if we meet we shall not scape a brawl.

CASSIAN
Thou art like one of those fellows that, when he enters the confines of a tavern, claps me his sword upon the table and says, God send me no need of thee; and by the operation of the second cup, draws it on the drawer when indeed there is no need.

LEOFRIC
Am I like such a fellow?

CASSIAN
Come, come, thou art as hot a jack in thy mood as any in Ilyria, and as soon moved to be moody, and as soon moody to be moved. Thou wouldst quarrel with a man for having a button undone on his coat. Thou wouldst quarrel with a man for drinking from a pewter cup, having no other reason but that thine is made of wood. What head but such a head would hatch such a quarrel? Thy wit is as full of quarrels as a fisherman’s net is full of fish, and yet thy wit hath been beaten as addled as a fish for quarrelling. Didst thou not fall out with a watchman for waking thee at noon, because the sun had disturbed thy cat that lay asleep on the wall? And yet thou wilt school me from quarrelling!

LEOFRIC
And I were so apt to quarrel as thou art, any man should buy the lease of my life for a single year and a day.

CASSIAN
The lease! O base!

Enter Valerius and his Followers.

LEOFRIC
By my heart, here come the men of iron.

CASSIAN
By my shoe-leather, I care not.

VALERIUS
Gentlemen, good morrow. A word with one of you.

CASSIAN
And but one word with one of us? Marry it to another; make it a word and a purpose.

VALERIUS
You shall find me apt enough to that, sir, if you give me the matter.

CASSIAN
Could you not find the matter without the giving?

VALERIUS
Cassian, thou keepest company with Orin.

CASSIAN
Company? What, dost thou make us merchants of cheap wares? And thou make merchants of us, look to find our accounts are sharp. Here is my ledger, here is that shall make you reckon. Zounds, company!

LEOFRIC
We speak here in the public market square.
Either withdraw unto some private court,
Or reason calmly of your discontent,
Or else depart; here all the world looks on.

CASSIAN
The world has eyes, and let it use them well.
I will not stir for any man’s command.

Enter Orin.

VALERIUS
Well, peace be with your council, sir, here comes my quarry.

CASSIAN
But I’ll be flayed, sir, if he answers to your falcon’s call. Go, seek your prey in another field; he’ll be your follower only to see you gone.

VALERIUS
Orin, the hatred I do bear for thee
Can find no better name than this: thou art a traitor.

ORIN
Valerius, the reason I have to wish thee well
Doth much excuse the fury of thy words.
A traitor am I none; therefore be gone,
I see thou knowest not the man I am.

VALERIUS
Wretch, this shall not excuse the slights and scorns
That thou hast done me, therefore turn and draw.

ORIN
I do protest I never slighted thee,
But hold thee in a regard thou canst not guess
Until thou knowest the reason for my peace.
And so good Valerius, which name I cherish
As dearly as mine own, be satisfied.

CASSIAN
O weak, dishonourable, vile surrender!
[_Draws._]
The forward thrust carries the day away.
Valerius, you brave tin soldier, will you walk?

VALERIUS
What wouldst thou have with me?

CASSIAN
Good Master of the Forge, nothing but one of your nine tempers, that I mean to cool withal, and, as you shall use me hereafter, hammer out the rest of the eight. Will you pluck your sword from his scabbard by the hilt? Make haste, lest mine be at your throat ere it be out.

VALERIUS
[_Drawing._]
I am for you.

ORIN
Gentle Cassian, put thy weapon up.

CASSIAN
Come, sir, your counter.
[_They fight._]

ORIN
Draw, Leofric, beat down their angry points.
Gentlemen, for pity, cease this strife.
Valerius, Cassian, the Duke expressly hath
Forbid this clashing in Ilyrian streets.
Hold, Valerius! Good Cassian!
[_Orin steps between them. Valerius, under Orin’s arm, stabs Cassian._]
[_Exit Valerius._]

CASSIAN
I am struck. A pox on both your banners! I am done for.
Is he gone, and carries no mark?

LEOFRIC
What, art thou hurt?

CASSIAN
Ay, ay, a prick, a prick. Marry, ‘tis enough.
Where is my boy? Go, villain, fetch a physician.
[_Exit Boy._]

ORIN
Courage, man; the hurt cannot be great.

CASSIAN
No, ‘tis not so wide as the harbour mouth, nor so deep as a mine shaft, but ‘tis enough, ‘twill serve. Ask for me tomorrow, and you shall find me a silent man. I am seasoned, I warrant, for this earth. A pox on both your banners! Zounds, a dog, a coward, a braggart, a fiend that fights by the numbers! Why in God’s name came you between us? I was struck from under your arm.

ORIN
I thought to keep the peace.

CASSIAN
Help me into some house, Leofric,
Or I shall fall. A pox on both your banners!
They have made dust of me. I have it, and soundly too. Your banners!
[_Exeunt Cassian and Leofric._]

ORIN
This gentleman, the Duke’s own distant kin,
My truest friend, hath got his mortal wound
In my quarrel; my honour smirch’d
With Valerius’s slander, Valerius, that an hour
Hath been my brother. O sweet Helena,
Thy virtue hath made me soft and womanish
And in my soul hath cool’d my valour’s fire.

Re-enter Leofric.

LEOFRIC
O Orin, Orin, witty Cassian’s dead.
That nimble spirit hath climbed the sunbeams now,
Which all too soon on earth did hold his scorn.

ORIN
This day’s dark business on more days depends;
This but begins the grief another ends.

Re-enter Valerius.

LEOFRIC
Here comes the furious Valerius back again.

ORIN
Alive in triumph, and Cassian butchered?
Let gentle patience fly to heaven’s gate,
And burning vengeance be my pilot now!
Now, Valerius, take the ‘traitor’ back again
That late thou gav’st me, for Cassian’s soul
Is but a little way above our heads,
Waiting for thine to bear him company.
Either thou or I, or both, must go with him.

VALERIUS
Thou wretched boy, that didst consort him here,
Shalt with him hence.

ORIN
This shall decide that now.
[_They fight; Valerius falls._]

LEOFRIC
Orin, away, be gone!
The citizens are roused, and Valerius slain.
Stand not confounded. The Duke will sentence thee
To death if thou art taken. Hence, be gone, away!

ORIN
O, I am fortune’s plaything!

LEOFRIC
Why dost thou stay?
[_Exit Orin._]

Enter Citizens.

FIRST CITIZEN
Which way ran he that kill’d Valerius?
Orin, that villain, which way did he run?

LEOFRIC
There lies that Valerius.

FIRST CITIZEN
Up, sir, go with me.
I charge thee in the Duke’s name to obey.

Enter Duke, attended; Lord Armitage, Lady Valeria, and others.

DUKE
Where are the foul beginners of this fray?

LEOFRIC
O noble Duke, I can explain it all,
The sad beginning of this fatal fight.
There lies the man, slain by young Orin’s hand,
That slew thy kinsman, clever Cassian.

LADY VALERIA
Valerius, my brother! O my father’s son!
O Duke! O husband! O, the blood is shed
Of my dear kinsman! Duke, as thou art just,
For blood of ours, shed blood of Armitage.
O brother, brother!

DUKE
Leofric, who began this bloody fray?

LEOFRIC
Valerius, here slain, whom Orin’s hand did slay.
Orin, that spoke him fair, bid him recall
How slight the quarrel was, and urg’d withal
Your high displeasure. All this, uttered
With sober breath, calm look, and humble spirit,
Could not appease the choler of Valerius,
Deaf to all peace, but that he rushes forth
With ready steel at honest Cassian’s breast,
Who, just as hot, turns deadly point to point,
And with a swordsman’s pride, with one hand beats
Grim death aside, and with the other sends
It back to Valerius, whose skill returns it.
Orin he cries aloud,
‘Hold, friends! Friends, part!’ and quicker than his tongue,
His nimble arm beats down their clashing swords,
And ‘twixt them steps; underneath whose arm
A hateful thrust from Valerius took the life
Of fiery Cassian, and then Valerius fled.
But soon comes back to Orin,
Who had but then embraced a burning rage,
And to’t they go like lightning; for, ere I
Could draw my sword to part them, was proud Valerius slain,
And as he fell did Orin turn and fly.
This is the truth, or let Leofric die.

LADY VALERIA
He is a friend to the house of Armitage.
His love for them makes him a liar here.
Some twenty of them fought in this foul strife,
And all those twenty could but kill one life.
I beg for justice, which thou, Duke, must give.
Orin slew Valerius, Orin must not live.

DUKE
Orin slew him, he slew Cassian.
Who now the price of Cassian’s blood doth own?

LORD ARMITAGE
Not Orin, Duke, he was Cassian’s friend.
His crime concludes but what the law should end,
The life of Valerius.

DUKE
And for that offence
Immediately we do banish him from hence.
I have a stake in your destructive hate,
My own sworn guard is bloodied by your strife.
But I’ll chastise you with so great a fine
That you shall rue the loss of what is mine.

Your sentence is a breath, a fleeting sound,
While Valerius lies silent in the ground.
This is not justice, Duke, it is a slight,
A darkened lamp to mock the rule of right.
If blood for blood be not the law you serve,
Then what from chaos does this state preserve?

DUKE
Peace, Lady Valeria, your grief is wild.

LADY VALERIA
My grief has reason, and is not a child
To be soothed with words. I am a sister, Duke,
And in that name my very soul is shook.
You have a sister. If some bloody hand
Should strike her down within this very land,
And you, the fount of justice, should but say,
‘Go, murderer, and see another day,’
Would not your heart, your honour, and your crown
Cry out for vengeance, and strike the villain down?
Banishment! ’Tis but a holiday
For him to boast how he made us his prey.
He will find comfort in some other place,
While we must live and look on this disgrace.

LORD ARMITAGE
The lady speaks from sorrow, not from sense.
Orin’s act was but a consequence
Of Valerius’s crime. He took the law
Into his hands, a fault, a fatal flaw,
But not a murder born of chilling hate.
He answered rage with rage, and sealed his fate.
To take his life for this would be to kill
The arm of justice for its hasty will.

LADY VALERIA
You plead for him, you house of Armitage,
And with your words you write a bloody page.
You call it justice? Justice wears a brand
Of cold resolve, not a hot, trembling hand.
O Duke, be warned. If you let this seed grow,
A crop of daggers in our streets will show.
The private sword will be the only law,
And every quarrel will to murder draw.
I ask for Orin’s head upon a block,
To still the tempest and reset the clock.

DUKE
No more! My judgment stands. It is decreed.
I will not sow more death to stop the bleed.
Orin is banished, on pain of his life
Should he return. I will not see this strife
Consume my city and my people’s peace.
From this sad hour, let all contention cease.
And for the houses, both of you shall pay
A heavy fine before the close of day.
For Armitage, because your son did strike
The final blow, your penalty shall like
A winter storm upon your coffers fall.
For you, my lady, though your loss be all,
Your brother’s hand shed first and noble blood,
And broke the dam that held back this dark flood.
Your house shall pay. Let gold where blood was spent
Remind you of this ruler’s discontent.
Now, clear this place. Take up your honoured dead.
Let sorrow be confined to its own bed,
And not lie screaming in the public square.
Attend to this. See all is done with care.

[_Exeunt Duke, attended. Soldiers bear the bodies of Cassian and Valerius offstage. Lord Armitage looks to Lady Valeria._]

LORD ARMITAGE
Lady, our grief is a most bitter cup.
Let us not add more poison, but sup
In sober sorrow. What the Duke commands,
Though harsh, may be a balm for both our lands.

LADY VALERIA
Balm? You speak of balm, whose house is whole?
My brother’s gone, they have torn out my soul.
Your Orin lives and breathes the common air,
He feels the sun, and is not filled with care
Beyond the simple want of his own home.
My brother lies where no light dares to roam.
Speak not of peace to me. There is no peace.
My heart is now a vessel of disease,
And it will spread until the source is dead.
Mark my words, Armitage. For the blood he shed.

[_Exit Lady Valeria._]

LORD ARMITAGE
Her threats are like the wind before a rain,
A promise of a greater, coming pain.
Leofric, stay.

LEOFRIC
My lord, I am your man.

LORD ARMITAGE
You are a friend, part of a loyal clan.
You saw my boy, you saw his hurried flight.
Where did he turn, into what waiting night?

LEOFRIC
He ran towards the southern gate, my lord,
As if by some unspoken, urgent word.
His mind was chaos, and his face a mask
Of one who’s failed at some eternal task.
He cried he was a plaything of his fate,
Then vanished, cursing his unhappy state.

LORD ARMITAGE
Then to the southern gate we send our aid.
A heavy purse must for his health be paid.
And word of where to go, a safer shore,
Where Valeria’s hate can reach no more.
This banishment is but a painted screen;
Her vengeance will be sharp, and swift, and keen.
She will not trust the Duke’s slow, tedious hand,
But send her own assassins through the land.
We must be quicker. Come, let us away.
There is much work to do before the day
Can claim a victory for our weary house.
We must be cunning as the cornered mouse.

[_Exeunt._]

SCENE II. A ROOM IN THE HOUSE OF VELIUS.

[_Enter Lady Valeria and Cassander._]

LADY VALERIA
Is the door barred? Are we secure from spies?

CASSANDER
As is a tomb, my lady. No prying eyes
Can pierce these walls, no ear can steal a word.
The very air is loyal. I have heard
The city’s pulse. It beats with discontent.
They say the Duke is soft, his judgment bent
By friendship for the house of Armitage.
They call for blood to turn this tragic page.

LADY VALERIA
The common tongue speaks truth. The Duke is weak.
He patches wounds that in a fortnight leak
A greater poison. Banishment, he says!
He sends the wolf to pasture for some days,
And thinks the sheep are safe. It is a lie.
Orin of Armitage was born to die
The moment that his sword found Valerius’ heart.
The law has failed, so I must play my part.
You are a man who understands ambition.

CASSANDER
It is the only faith and true religion
For those not born to power, but who would climb.
I understand the art of choosing time
And opportunity.

LADY VALERIA
The time is now.
I have a task for you, if you’ll allow
Your fortune to be wedded to my cause.
I’ll not be bound by faint, half-hearted laws.
Find me two men, no, three, whose hands are stained
Already, and whose souls are unconstrained
By pious fear or sentimental dread.
Men who would kill their brother for their bread,
And feel no pang.

CASSANDER
My lady, such men are
As common as the stones. They are not far.
But they demand a high and certain price.

LADY VALERIA
Their price is nothing. Name it in a trice.
I have the gold from my late brother’s chest.
It shall be used to give his spirit rest.
They are to hunt a man. A boy, in truth.
The fleeting, banished, miserable youth,
Young Orin.

CASSANDER
He is gone beyond the walls.

LADY VALERIA
A banish’d man is one who slowly crawls
From town to town, a beggar and a ghost.
He will be found along the southern coast,
Or hiding in the forests to the east.
I want his head brought to me for a feast.
No, not his head. That is too crude a thing.
I want a token that his death will bring.
A ring he wears, a locket from his neck,
Some proof that he is but a bloody wreck,
And that my brother’s soul can be at peace.
Tell them the gold is theirs when his life cease.
And for your service, Cassander, you will find
That I am not forgetful, nor unkind.
A place at court, a title, lands, and name,
These are the prizes in this deadly game.

CASSANDER
My lady, your resolve gives me a fire.
To serve your will is now my one desire.
I know the very men. One is a brute,
A soldier, but of ill and low repute,
Who’d kill a king for coin. The other two
Are brothers, silent as the evening dew,
And just as deadly. They can track a bird
By a bent feather, hear an unsaid word.
Orin will not escape them. By the moon,
I’ll have them on his trail, and very soon.

LADY VALERIA
Go then. Let every moment be a spur.
Do not delay, do not to chance defer.
For while he lives, my brother’s blood cries out
From the cold earth, and puts my soul in doubt.
It whispers to me in the dead of night,
Avenge me, sister, for I have no light.
Go, Cassander. Bring me my release.
In Orin’s death is my only peace.

CASSANDER
I am your instrument, my lady. I obey.

[_Exit Cassander._]

LADY VALERIA
Now, fortune, do not turn your face away.
I have cast all my hopes upon this die.
Let Orin fall, and let his memory die.
The Duke’s soft justice is a thing of air.
My own is forged of iron and despair.
It will not bend, it will not break, it will not fail.
A sister’s love will in the end prevail.

[_Exit._]

SCENE III. A FOREST PATH BEYOND THE CITY GATES. NIGHT.

[_Enter Orin, alone. His clothes are torn._]

ORIN
Banished. The word is easy on the tongue.
A simple sound from which my ruin is sprung.
Banished. Cast out from all I ever knew,
Beneath a sky of unfamiliar hue.
My home, my father, all the sunlit days
Are lost to me in this bewildering maze.
O, restless hand, what have you done to me?
You struck for justice, not for cruelty.
You answered murder when it called your name,
And for that honour, I am given shame.
Was it not right? When Cassian fell and bled,
And Valerius stood living in his stead,
Was I to sheath my sword and let him go?
My heart, my blood, my very spirit said no.
The Duke himself would have condemned the man,
So I but hastened what the law had planned.
And for this service I am made a thrall
To lonely roads, with no one I can call
A friend. O fortune, you are blind and mad.
You take the righteous and you help the bad.
I am your plaything, as I said before,
A broken toy cast out upon the shore
Of this dark world. What hope is left for me?
To starve, to freeze, or fall to robbery?
Perhaps this is my punishment, to feel
The slow decay, the world’s uncaring heel
Upon my neck. So be it. Let it come.
My heart is weary and my soul is numb.

[_Enter Leofric, cautiously._]

LEOFRIC
My lord? Orin? Is that your voice I hear?

ORIN
Leofric! My friend! What brings you to this fear,
This wretched darkness? Have you come to see
The final act of my life’s tragedy?

LEOFRIC
I come with hope, my lord, and with a purse.
Your father’s love has countered fortune’s curse.
He sent me on your path, to find you here
And give you comfort, and to make all clear.
You must not linger on these public ways.
Lady Valeria’s hate will dog your days.
Her spies will be abroad, her assassins hired.
She is a furnace, with a vengeance fired.

ORIN
Let her come. What more can she take now?
My life is forfeit, and I know not how
To prize it any more. Let her assassins try.
There are far worse and slower ways to die.

LEOFRIC
Do not speak so. This is a passing storm.
Your father’s plan will keep you safe and warm.
Here, take this gold. It is a heavy weight,
Enough to buy your passage through the state
And to another land. You must make for the sea,
To the port city of Amantia.
There, seek a merchant known as Balthazar.
Say you were sent by the north-guiding star,
A password that your father has arranged.
He will provide you shelter, unestranged,
Until a ship is ready for its flight
To distant shores, and out of Valeria’s sight.

[_He gives Orin a heavy purse and a letter._]

ORIN
My father. He does this for me? For one
Who brought such ruin and such shame upon
His noble house? I am not worth this care.

LEOFRIC
You are his son. What burden would he not bear?
He knows your heart was true, though your hand was swift.
He sees this not as shame, but as a gift
Of bitter circumstance. He bids you live,
And learn what lessons exile has to give.
This letter holds more counsel. Read it when
You are secure among more friendly men.
And now, I must be gone ere I am missed.
The watch is set, the city gates are kissed
By morning’s light already. Fare you well.
May heaven guard you from the hounds of hell.

ORIN
Leofric, my truest friend, how can I say
The thanks I feel? You have turned my night to day.
Tell my father… tell him I will be strong.
I will endure this undeserved wrong.
And I will live to see his face again,
And prove my worth.

LEOFRIC
He knows it. Now, my friend, the rain
Begins to fall. Take this cloak as well.
Go south. Be swift. And may your journey tell
A better tale than its beginning. Go!

[_Leofric embraces Orin, then exits quickly._]

ORIN
A single friend in this great world of woe
Is like a star in a black, endless sky.
It proves that light exists. I will not die
A beggar on the road. I have a path,
A chance to flee from that mad woman’s wrath.
Amantia. Balthazar. The northern star.
These words are now the pillars that will bar
The gates of my despair. O, Valerius,
Your death has made my life calamitous,
But I will not be buried in your grave.
I’ll take the chance my father’s love has gave.
I’ll live. I’ll fight. I will one day return
And let the fires of my honour burn
So bright they cleanse the shadows from my name.
This is my vow. Now, to begin the game.

[_Orin pulls the cloak around himself and exits._]

SCENE IV. A HALL IN THE DUKE’S PALACE.

[_Enter Duke and Lord Armitage._]

DUKE
Armitage, I have summoned you to speak
Of this great fine. I know the sum must wreak
A havoc on your wealth. It is severe,
I grant you that, but you must hold it dear
As proof of my resolve. This bloody feud
Must end.

LORD ARMITAGE
Your Grace, I have not misconstrued
Your purpose, nor will I contest your will.
The gold is ready. It is a bitter pill,
But one we swallow for the city’s health.
My house is poorer, but it has a wealth
That is not counted in a store of coin.
It has its honour, which I will not join
To base rebellion or complaint. The fine
Is paid.

DUKE
I am relieved. This is a sign
Of wisdom, lord. I would that all my subjects
Had such a temper. But there are objects
That will not bend. Lady Valeria came
Before me yesterday, to speak the name
Of her dead brother, and to ask that his
Estates be settled. All was courtesies,
And yet, beneath her calm and mourning black,
I sensed a will that would not turn its back
On vengeance. Her eyes held a winter’s chill.
I fear her heart is plotting murder still.
Have you had word of Orin? Is he safe?

LORD ARMITAGE
He is beyond the reach of any chafe
From her designs, Your Grace. He has gone far.
The world is wide. I trust his guiding star
Will keep him from her sight.

DUKE
I hope it may.
For if she finds him, on that very day
My sentence is undone, my justice mocked,
And the great gates of chaos are unlocked
Once more. A private murder, born of hate,
Would do more damage to this fragile state
Than any public brawl. It would proclaim
The Duke is powerless, a ruler but in name.
Watch her, Armitage. For your own son’s sake,
And for the peace I am trying to make,
Have men you trust observe her servants well.
We must know what dark stories they can tell.

LORD ARMITAGE
I have already taken such a course.
My fear of her is a relentless force.
I have men posted near her gates, to see
Who comes and goes from that dark company.
So far, they have seen nothing but the trade
Of common servants, unafraid
And open in their work. But I am not
Deceived. A cunning spider in a spot
Of darkness weaves its web unseen, unheard,
And only by the captured, dying bird
Is its existence known. We must be wise.

DUKE
Good. Let us be the state’s two watchful eyes.
Your house has paid its due. The house of Velius
Has paid its own. Their purses, plundered thus,
May cool their lust for conflict. It is my hope.
Now go, my lord. We walk a narrow rope
Across a deep abyss. Let’s not look down.

[_Exit Lord Armitage._]

DUKE
How hard it is to wear a ruler’s crown.
To be a judge, a father, and a god
To all these people, on this patch of sod.
I please no one. Valeria cries for death,
Armitage grieves his son with every breath.
The citizens see weakness in my hand.
And yet, if I had let my sword command
And taken Orin’s life, as she desired,
The other half the city would be fired
With rage for Armitage. To rule is not
To find the perfect, clean, and happy spot,
But to select the least of all the pains,
And pray to God that some small peace remains.

[_Exit._]

SCENE III. A chamber in the House of Velius. The room is dark, hung with heavy tapestries of black and grey. A single candelabrum provides a nervous light. LADY VALERIA stands by a high window, looking out into the night. Her gown is the colour of ash. LUCIA, her lady in waiting, sits nearby, her hands folded in her lap.

LADY VALERIA
The moon is thin tonight, a sliver of bone
Lost in the velvet darkness, all alone.
It gives no comfort, sheds no gentle ray,
It only mocks the light that’s passed away.
Just so my hope, a sliver in my soul,
Which vengeance, and not time, must render whole.
You’re quiet, Lucia. Does the silence press
Upon your heart? Or is it weariness?

LUCIA
My lady, it is sorrow for your state.
This endless vigil, this consuming hate,
It hollows out the spirit, dims the eye.
My grief for Lord Silvanus will not die,
But living grief must find a place to rest,
Not be a sharpened dagger in the breast.

LADY VALERIA
Rest? Shall my brother rest within his tomb,
Wrapped in the endless, suffocating gloom,
While his own murderer draws a joyful breath
And lives a life denied to him by death?
Rest is a word for after, when the debt
Is paid in blood. I have not paid it yet.
The Duke’s soft justice is a fool’s reply,
A coward’s balm that lets the guilty fly.
He speaks of peace, of healing for the state.
What peace can grow upon a bed of hate?

LUCIA
But Orin is banished. He is gone from here,
To live in exile, penury, and fear.
His father’s house is shamed, his name a blot.
Is that not suffering? Is that justice not?

LADY VALERIA
(Turning from the window, her voice cold and sharp.)
You speak like a merchant, balancing a scale,
Where gold and shame are goods put up for sale.
A life for a life. The law is old and grand.
It is the firmest rock in all this land.
The Duke has built his peace on shifting sand,
With compromise and fines held in his hand.
A life is not a coin to be replaced.
A noble line, by murder’s hand erased,
Cannot be mended by a bag of gold.
The story’s end has not yet been told.
My brother’s ghost still walks these halls at night.
He asks for one thing only: his just right.
And I, his sister, am the only one
To see that holy, bloody business done.

LUCIA
My lady, please. Such words are dangerous things.
A whispered treason soon will grow its wings
And fly directly to the Duke’s own ear.
Lord Armitage is filled with sleepless fear.
His spies are watching, so the servants say.
They mark who leaves and enters, night and day.

LADY VALERIA
Let them watch. Let them see the mourning clothes,
The baker, and the priest who comes and goes.
My true designs are not for common view.
They are a seed that grows where none but few
Can see the soil. A thought within the mind,
A purpose of a much more subtle kind.
The sharpest blade is that which is unseen
Until it strikes. My will is just as keen.
Now leave me, Lucia. I expect a guest.
Go to your chamber. Seek your own small rest.

LUCIA
A guest, my lady? At this hour of night?

LADY VALERIA
One who prefers the darkness to the light.
His work is of the shadows. Do not ask.
Your ignorance will be your safest mask
Should questions ever rise. Now go. Obey.
I have a game of consequence to play.

[_Lucia bows and exits, her face a mask of worry._]

LADY VALERIA
She has a gentle heart. A loyal friend.
But cannot see the path, nor guess the end.
Some tools must be kept bright and sharp and clean,
While others do the work that is unseen.
My brother’s blood still cries out from the floor.
It will not be content till it has more.

[_A soft knock at a side door. Valeria turns._]

LADY VALERIA
Enter. The night is late, the door is fast.
Your journey’s end is reached at long last.

[_The door opens and CASSIAN enters. He is a man of no distinct age, dressed in dark, practical clothes. His face is weathered, his eyes watchful. He moves with a quiet confidence._]

CASSIAN
My lady. You are Valeria of Velius.

LADY VALERIA
I am. And you are Cassian, they tell me. The best.
A man whose skill is equal to the test.

CASSIAN
I am a man who finishes his task.
My name and reputation are the mask
That I present. What lies beneath, you buy.
It is a service. I do not ask why.
My price was sent to you. You found it fair?

LADY VALERIA
The cost of justice is beyond compare.
Your price is high. But if your work is true,
It will seem but a pittance when you’re through.
I have the gold. But first, I have to know
If you possess the will to strike the blow.
This is not some poor merchant to be slain
For a fat purse in some dark, muddy lane.
The man you hunt is Orin Armitage,
The son of a great lord, who fears my rage.

CASSIAN
A man is but a man. A lord can bleed
As red as any beggar. It’s my creed
To make no difference. Flesh and bone and breath,
All are alike when they are met with death.
His name may guard him with a wall of fear,
But walls have gates. I am a patient ear
Pressed to the stone. I listen for the key.
His name and rank mean nothing, lady, to me.

LADY VALERIA
Good. That is the answer I had hoped to find.
A tool should not possess a judging mind.
Now, listen well. Here are your commands.
I do not want him killed by clumsy hands
In some far foreign land, a tavern brawl,
A dagger thrust that none can trace at all.
That is the Duke’s desire. A quiet end.
A fading memory. But I, my friend,
I want a message sent. A lesson taught.
A reckoning for all the pain he’s wrought.

CASSIAN
You wish his death to speak? What should it say?

LADY VALERIA
It must say ‘Velius’. On that final day,
When you have found him, isolated, weak,
Before you grant the silence that you seek,
You will present him with this.

[_She goes to a carved wooden box on a table, and from it takes a dagger. It is ornate, with a silver hilt in the shape of a coiled serpent, the sigil of House Velius._]

LADY VALERIA
This is the blade
My brother wore the very day he paid
With his own life for Orin’s drunken pride.
This dagger was upon Silvanus’ side.
I want it back. But I want it returned
Bearing the proof of the lesson learned.
It must be covered in the killer’s blood.
Do you now see? Is my design understood?

CASSIAN
(Taking the dagger, weighing it in his hand.)
A signature. The work is to be signed.
It adds a risk. It leaves a clue behind.

LADY VALERIA
The clue is the entire point. I want
Lord Armitage, in his proud, daily haunt,
To get this blade. I want the Duke to see.
I want them both to know it came from me.
They will not touch me. They will have no proof,
Just a cold knowledge, standing quite aloof
From law and courts. The terror in the heart
Is worse than any public, legal smart.
They will know justice has a longer reach
Than their decrees, a more effective speech.
Can you do this?

CASSIAN
The work is as you say.
The risk is mine. For that, you will pay
A quarter more. A message has its fee.

LADY VALERIA
It shall be paid. Just bring his blood to me
Upon this blade. And where will you begin
To hunt the author of this grievous sin?
His father thinks him hidden, far away.

CASSIAN
A father’s hope. But men must eat and play.
A young lord, used to wine and company,
Will not long suffer quiet poverty.
He will seek out the ports, the dice, the drink.
He has more pride than common sense, I think.
My sources tell me of a ship that sailed
For the Free Cities. In its hold, concealed,
A passenger whose fare was paid in haste.
I follow on the trail. No time to waste.
He calls himself ‘Corvin’. A foolish choice.
He cannot hide his manner or his voice.
I will have him within a turning moon.
His life of running will be over soon.

LADY VALERIA
Then go. And when the deed is truly done,
When the last race of Armitage is run,
Return to me. Your fortune will be made.
Your future safety perfectly arrayed.

CASSIAN
I ask for half the payment now, in gold.
The other half when your revenge is cold.

LADY VALERIA
It is prepared.

[_She gestures to a heavy purse on the table. Cassian takes it, feeling its weight without opening it. He conceals the dagger within his coat._]

CASSIAN
I am a passing shade.
I will be gone. The contract has been made.

[_He bows slightly and exits the way he came, as silent as a ghost._]

LADY VALERIA
(She picks up the empty wooden box, running her fingers over the indentation where the dagger lay.)
Now, brother, you may start to rest at ease.
Your restless spirit soon will find its peace.
The winds of justice have begun to blow.
A storm is coming. This, I surely know.

SCENE IV. A street near the Velius estate. The night is dark and starless. Two men, JORIS and REN, are huddled in the shadow of a doorway. They are wrapped in plain cloaks.

REN
My feet are stone. My fingers have no feeling.
And all for what? To watch the paint that’s peeling
From Lady Valeria’s gate. A noble task.
I’d rather wear a fool’s degrading mask
And juggle for my bread. At least I’d be
Beside a fire for all the world to see.

JORIS
Quiet. A watchman’s virtue is his ear,
Not his complaining tongue. We’re posted here
Because our lord has reason to be vexed.
He fears the words that will be spoken next
From that dark house. He fears a sudden plot.

REN
A plot? She does not leave that gloomy spot.
She sees the priest, her steward, and her maid.
Of what, I ask you, should we be afraid?
A woman’s tears? A well-recited prayer?
There is no danger breeding in that air.
Lord Armitage is frightened of a ghost.
We are the ones who suffer from it most.

JORIS
He is our lord. His fears are our commands.
And you forget, a woman’s gentle hands
Can sign a warrant, or can hold a purse
That pays a man to do something far worse
Than she could do herself. Her weakness is
A cloak for strength. Remember what was his,
Her brother’s fate. The Velius line is proud.
They do not weep, they purchase a new shroud
For their own enemies. Be still. I heard
A latch, a footstep. Not a single word.

[_They press themselves deeper into the shadows. The small side gate of the Velius house opens, and CASSIAN slips out. He pulls his collar up, glances once down the street, and then begins to walk away with a steady, unhurried pace._]

REN
(Whispering)
Who is that man? I do not know his face.
He does not have a servant’s hurried pace,
Nor a lord’s swagger.

JORIS
No. He walks as though
He owns the shadows where he means to go.
He came from her house. At this time of night.
This is not common. This is not right.
The lord must know. You stay and keep the watch.
I’ll follow him. I mean to lift the latch
Upon this mystery. He has the scent
Of something dangerous. My discontent
Has vanished. Now, I feel the thrill of chase.
Keep your eyes open. Guard this cursed place.

[_Joris pulls his own cloak tighter and slips out of the doorway, following Cassian at a safe distance. Ren shivers, pulling his cloak tighter for a different reason._]

REN
The thrill of chase. He’s welcome to it all.
I’d rather face a tankard than a squall.
But still… that man… he had a wolf’s cold eye.
I pray that Joris doesn’t have to die
To learn his name. The air has grown more chill.
Or is it just my heart that stands so still?

SCENE V. Lord Armitage’s study. It is late, but he is fully dressed, pacing before a fire that has burned low. A map is spread on his desk. He stops as JORIS is shown in by a servant.

LORD ARMITAGE
Report. You were not meant to leave your post.
What news compels you? Is it ghost on ghost,
Or something solid? Speak, and tell me true.

JORIS
My lord, a man. Someone we never knew.
He left the house of Velius by the gate
That opens on the lane. The hour was late.
He was not noble, nor a servant either.
He had the look of one who answers neither
To law nor lord, but only to his gold.
A story in his posture could be told
Of hidden knives and journeys in the dark.
He bore a predator’s distinctive mark.

LORD ARMITAGE
Valeria’s guest. And you, you followed him?

JORIS
I did, my lord, upon a fearful whim.
He moved directly to the sailors’ town,
To a bleak inn of ill repute, well down
Among the docks, a place called The Drowned Rat.
He met the master of a trading cat,
A grubby vessel sailing with the tide.
He paid for passage, with no need to hide
His haste. The ship is bound for Lysimar,
The first of the Free Cities. It is far.

LORD ARMITAGE
Lysimar. That is where the trail began.
You did well, Joris. A most perceptive man.
This visitor, this creature of the night,
He is a hunter. And my fading light,
My son, is what he hunts. She did not wait.
She has unleashed the bloodhounds of her hate.
She scorns the Duke. She writes her own decree.
And with my son’s own blood, she’ll mock at me.

JORIS
My lord, what can be done? We have no proof.
The man just took a room beneath a roof
And paid for passage. Nothing in the law
Can touch him for it.

LORD ARMITAGE
Law? The law is raw
And clumsy here. This is a game of speed.
I must plant my own counter-acting seed.
I need a messenger, a man I trust,
To ride faster than a sudden gust
Of winter wind. He must find Orin first.
He must be warned about this coming worst.
My son is hiding, under a false name.
But such a hunter will still find his game.
This assassin, did you learn what he
Is called?

JORIS
The innkeep, for a silver fee,
Gave me the name he used to book the room.
A single name to pierce the midnight gloom.
He calls himself Cassian.

LORD ARMITAGE
(His face pales)
Cassian.
The gods are cruel. So she has hired the man
Whose name is whispered, never said aloud.
The best and coldest killer in the crowd
Of those who deal in death. He has no peer.
My son’s last days are numbered now, I fear.
Go, Joris. Find me Marcus, the captain of my guard.
Tell him to choose the rider who is hard
And swift and loyal. Give him all the gold
He needs. A story must be quickly told
To Orin. He must move, and move again.
He must become a whisper in the rain,
A ghost himself, until this danger’s past.
His life depends on how well he can last.
Now go! Each moment that we waste in speech
Puts Orin further into Cassian’s reach.

[_Exit Joris. Lord Armitage goes to the map on his desk, his finger tracing a path across the sea to the Free Cities._]

LORD ARMITAGE
Oh, Orin, Orin. My foolish, prideful son.
Your one mistake can never be undone.
And now a shadow falls across your sun.
The race for your own life has just begun.

SCENE VI. A noisy tavern in the port city of Lysimar. The air is thick with smoke and the smell of spilled ale. Sailors of various nations laugh and shout. ORIN ARMITAGE sits alone at a corner table, a half-empty cup in his hand. He is dressed in rough, common clothes, and his face is leaner, marked by anxiety. He is watching a game of dice with eyes that see nothing.

BARMAID
Another cup, Corvin? You look as though
You’ve lost your ship and have nowhere to go.
A sadder face I have not seen all week.
What is the trouble that you will not speak?

ORIN
The trouble, girl, is one I can’t undo.
It follows me in everything I do.
Just fill the cup. The drink may help me hide
From the loud, angry ghost I keep inside.

BARMAID
A ghost, you say? Well, this place has a few.
Old Captain Gryce, who fell into the blue
And choppy sea, sometimes comes back to claim
The chair he sat in. It is all a game
Our minds will play. Forget your ghost, I say.
Live in the sun of this brand new day.
You are too young for such a heavy frown.
The prettiest man in this whole dusty town,
And you just sit and stare into your drink.
It is a waste of good looks, I think.

ORIN
Your flattery is kind, but it is lost
On one who knows the true and bitter cost
Of his own folly. Leave the drink, and go.
I am not company you’d want to know.

[_The Barmaid shrugs and moves away. Orin lifts the cup, but stops. His hand is shaking. He puts it down. He looks around the tavern, at the laughing, shouting men._]

ORIN
They live. They drink. Their hands are clean and free.
They have not felt the awful gravity
Of a man’s final breath, a closing eye
That fixes on you as you watch him die.
I did not mean it. It was rage and wine.
But his whole future ended, and now mine
Is this. This exile. This unending flight.
This lonely table in the endless night.
My father thinks that I am safe and far.
He does not see the wound, the open scar
Upon my soul. Banishment is not life.
It is a slower, but a sharper knife.

[_The tavern door opens, letting in a gust of sea air. CASSIAN enters. He does not look at Orin. He moves to the bar, his eyes scanning the room with a practiced, casual glance. He orders an ale. His gaze rests on Orin for a moment, an almost imperceptible pause, a flicker of recognition and confirmation. He shows no reaction. He takes his drink and moves to a different empty table, one that gives him a clear view of the corner where Orin sits. He sips his ale, a patient hunter who has found the forest, seen the tracks, and now can take his time. Orin, lost in his guilt and self-pity, does not notice the new arrival. He picks up his cup again, and this time, he drains it._]

ORIN
Another cup to drown the one before.
Perhaps the next will settle the old score
Between my conscience and my aching head.
I wish I were asleep, or maybe dead.

[_Cassian rises from his table, his own cup empty. He walks slowly, not directly toward Orin, but as if to get a refill from the barmaid, a path that happens to take him right past Orin’s table. He stops._]

CASSIAN
A heavy heart for such a pleasant night.
The ale is good, the fire is warm and bright.
And yet you sit as if the world has ended.
Has some great wrong been done that can’t be mended?

ORIN
The world I knew has ended, that is true.
But who, my friend, is it I’m speaking to?
I do not know your face, nor you know mine.
So leave me to my thoughts and to my wine.

CASSIAN
Forgive me. It is just the sailor’s way
To share a word at the closing of the day.
My name is Kael. I serve a merchant lord
Whose ships bring spice and silk from far abroad.
We seek new hands for a long voyage east,
Where a man’s past is happily released
Upon the waves. A place to start anew.
I saw your face and thought, perhaps, of you.
You have the look of one who needs the sea.
A change of wind. A different destiny.

[_Orin looks up, a flicker of something other than despair in his eyes. Hope, faint and fragile._]

ORIN
A voyage east? To where do you set sail?
To leave this port, to follow a new trail…
That is a thought I have nursed in the dark.
To find a home on some well-seasoned bark.

CASSIAN
To the Jade Isles, a place of sun and sand,
A truly distant and forgotten land.
A man can wash his past out in the sea,
And build a life of new prosperity.
We need strong men, but men of thought as well.
What is the trade that you know how to tell?
Are you a clerk? A fighter? A command?
What is the skill held in your weary hand?

ORIN
My hands… my hands have only known misuse.
A gentleman’s pursuits, a poor excuse
For honest work. I read the ancient maps,
And learned to spring my father’s hunting traps.
I know the stars, the currents, and the tide.
But from myself, I have no place to hide.

CASSIAN
A man who knows the stars is never lost.
Come, friend. Forget the pain, forget the cost.
My captain seeks a man to be his eyes,
To read the chart beneath the foreign skies.
The pay is good. The future is your own.
A seed of hope from which a life is grown.

ORIN
I… I have nothing. Not a coin to give.
Just the desire to find a way to live
That does not choke me with each breath I take.
What must I do for this new life’s sake?

CASSIAN
My captain is a man of privacy.
He does not like the tavern’s company.
Meet me upon the northern dock, you know
The one where the old fishing vessels go.
When the moon climbs to its highest, sharpest peak.
We will discuss the future that you seek.
Do not be late. The tide will not attend
The lazy man. Will you be there, my friend?

ORIN
I will be there. You give me cause to dream.
To float again upon life’s flowing stream.
Your name is Kael? I am… just call me Corvin.
A name I took when my old life was shorn.

CASSIAN
Then Corvin, I will see you by the moon.
Do not despair. Your chance is coming soon.

[_Cassian nods, a thin, unreadable smile on his lips. He turns and leaves the tavern without another look back. Orin watches him go, his posture straightening for the first time. He pushes the cup away from him, a look of resolve on his face. He feels the ghost inside him quiet for a moment, soothed by a liar’s lullaby._]

ORIN
A ship. A new life. Can it be so near?
A sudden cure for all my doubt and fear?
This Kael, he seems a true and honest man.
Perhaps this is a part of some great plan.
To suffer first, and then to be redeemed.
A better fate than I had ever dreamed.
I must prepare. I must not fail this test.
To put this aching, heavy soul to rest.

[_He stands, leaving a few small coins on the table, and exits the tavern. The Barmaid watches him leave, a thoughtful expression on her face._]

BARMAID
He looks alive again. That stranger’s word
Was like a song the lonely spirit heard.
I hope for him. I hope his path is bright.
But there was something in that stranger’s night.
A stillness. A cold shadow in his gaze.
That does not speak of clear and sunlit days.

[_The scene fades. When it rises again, it is the northern dock. The moon is high and full, casting long, distorted shadows from the posts and stacked crates. The only sounds are the gentle lapping of water against the wood and the distant cry of a gull. Orin stands, looking out at the dark water. He seems nervous, but also full of anticipation. He checks the sky. Footsteps echo on the wooden planks. Cassian emerges from the shadows._]

ORIN
Kael. You came. I feared I was mistaken.
That the new hope I felt would be forsaken.
Where is your captain? Is he close nearby?
Beneath this cold and silver-dusted sky?

CASSIAN
The captain is not here. There is no ship.
Just a loose word from a deceiving lip.
There is no voyage to the eastern sun.
Your journey, Orin Armitage, is done.

[_Orin freezes. The name, his true name, hangs in the air like a death sentence._]

ORIN
I do not know that name. You have me wrong.
My name is Corvin. I have used it long.
I am a simple man who’s lost his way.
I know not of this Armitage you say.

CASSIAN
Denial is a weak and flimsy shield.
The truth is a hard sword for you to wield.
I know your house. I know your father’s crest.
I know the guilt that burns within your breast.
I know of the duel, if one could call it that.
The drunken rage where young Lord Valerius sat,
Unarmed and laughing, till your sudden blade
A wicked, fatal injury had made.

[_Orin stumbles back, his face a mask of horror and recognition._]

ORIN
No. It was not like that. It was a fight.
It was the drink, a word, a foolish slight.
He drew on me. Or I thought that he drew.
It happened fast. I swear I never knew…
I never meant for him to fall and die.
It was his pride, no different than my eye,
That was offended. By the gods, I swear
I live each day within that dark despair.

CASSIAN
You speak of pride. What of his father’s grief?
His house’s honor, stolen by a thief
In the dark night? You fled before the dawn,
While his life’s blood was still wet on the lawn.
You ran. You hid. You changed your noble name.
And thought that you could flee the burning shame.
But justice has long arms and patient feet.
It finds the guilty on a lonely street.

ORIN
Who are you? A king’s man? A magistrate?
Sent here to seal my miserable fate?
If you are here for blood, then take it now.
I will not fight you. I will not allow
Another death to stain my wretched soul.
Let your sharp steel exact its final toll.

[_Orin closes his eyes, waiting for a blade. Cassian lets out a short, cold laugh._]

CASSIAN
I am not here for blood. I am the hand
That served the man whose life you have unplanned.
I was his guard, his sworn and loyal sword.
I served the man you murdered, noble lord.
My name is Cassian. And I made a vow
Upon his grave, that I would find you now.
But death for you? A quick and painless end?
That is a mercy I will not extend.

[_Cassian moves with sudden speed. He grabs Orin’s arms, twisting them behind his back. Orin cries out, struggling, but Cassian’s strength is immense, born of training and resolve. He binds Orin’s wrists with a leather cord he pulls from his belt._]

ORIN
What will you do? Where are you taking me?
If not to death, then what will my fate be?
Let me go. I have gold. My father’s rich.
He will pay you to leave me in some ditch
To live my life in quiet, poor disgrace.
Name any price. Just take me from this place.

CASSIAN
Your father’s gold cannot buy back a soul.
It cannot make a shattered family whole.
I will not kill you. Death is too serene.
You will return to stand before the queen.
You will face the father of the son you slew,
And let his justice decide what’s right for you.
To see his face, to hear his broken heart.
That is your sentence. That is where you start.

[_Cassian pulls Orin along the dock, toward the dark alleys of the port town. Orin struggles, but it is useless. His brief hope has been extinguished, replaced by a dread far colder than the sea._]

ORIN
No, not back there. Please, anything but that.
To face Lord Cyric… I would rather a rat
Gnawed on my bones. His eyes will burn me through.
There is no torment worse than what he’ll do.
Kill me now, Cassian. It is a kinder way.
Let this night be my final, dying day.

CASSIAN
You forfeited your right to choose your death
The moment that you stole Valerius’ breath.
Now walk. We have a room not far from here.
A place to wait until the ship is clear
To sail at dawn. You will not be alone.
You will have me, and the deeds you must atone.

[_The scene fades. It rises on a small, squalid room above the tavern. There is a single bed with a rough blanket, a rickety chair, and one small, grimy window looking out over the rooftops. Orin is tied to the chair. Cassian stands by the window, a silhouette against the pre-dawn grey light._]

CASSIAN
The city wakes. The bakers light their fires.
The world moves on with all its small desires.
They do not know or care about your plight.
Another soul lost to the endless night.
Did you think of him, I wonder, in your flight?
Valerius. Did you see him in the light
Of morning sun, or hear him in the rain?
Did his memory cause you any pain?

ORIN
It is a ghost that never sleeps or eats.
A constant whisper on the foreign streets.
I see his face each time I close my eyes.
I hear his final, shocked, and soft surprise.
You think that I feel nothing? You are wrong.
My punishment has lasted all along.
This guilt I carry is a heavier chain
Than any iron forged in fire and pain.

CASSIAN
Guilt is a comfort for a man like you.
A private thing that no one else sees through.
You weep alone, you pity your own soul.
You think your sorrow makes your spirit whole.
It does not. It is selfish. It is vain.
True penance is in sharing in the pain
Of those you harmed. You have not yet begun
To understand the damage you have done.
Valerius was to be married in the spring.
Did you know that? Did you hear the church bells ring
For other lovers, and think of the bride
Who waits for him, with hope long since denied?
Her name is Lyra. She was his whole world.
A flag of beauty that his heart unfurled.
She does not weep now. She is like a stone.
A statue in a garden, left alone.

[_Orin flinches, the words finding their mark more surely than any blade._]

ORIN
I did not know. I swear I did not know.
We were just boys, letting our anger show.
A rivalry of horses, cards, and wine.
His life was his, and my life was not mine
To take away. It was a moment’s heat.
A senseless, bitter, and profound defeat
For both of us.

[_A soft knock at the door. Cassian tenses. He draws a short knife from his belt and stands beside the door._]

CASSIAN
Who is it that is there?
Speak now, or you will find death in the air.

BARMAID (from outside)
It is just me. The girl from down below.
I brought some food. I saw the candle’s glow.
You paid for lodging and a morning meal.
I have some bread and cheese, if you should feel
The need to eat. Just open up the door.
I will not ask for any trouble more.

[_Cassian hesitates, then unlocks the door. He opens it only a crack. The Barmaid, whose name is Elara, stands there with a small tray._]

CASSIAN
Leave it right there upon the floor and go.
You have no business here that you should know.

ELARA
I will. But let me see the other man.
Corvin. Is he a part of your grim plan?
I saw you speak. I saw him come alive.
And now I see the hope did not survive.
What is he to you? A debtor? Or a thief?
His sadness seemed to be a genuine grief.

[_She pushes the door slightly, enough to see Orin tied to the chair. Her eyes widen in shock and pity._]

ELARA
By the gods above, what is the meaning here?
You have him bound like some poor, frightened deer
For slaughter. This is not the justice of the crown.
This is a darkness in this sleeping town.

CASSIAN
His fate is not for you or me to write.
It was decided on a different night.
Now leave the food and go back to your bar.
Do not involve yourself. Stay where you are.

[_Elara places the tray on the floor inside the door. Her eyes meet Orin’s for a moment. He looks at her with a desperate, pleading expression._]

ELARA
He has a gentle face. It is not right.
Whatever he has done, this cruel sight
Is wrong. Let him at least eat with free hands.
What beast are you to make such harsh demands?

CASSIAN
The kind of beast that sees a promise through.
Now go, before I find a use for you
In this affair. Your pity is misplaced.
The man he was has been long since effaced.

[_Elara gives Orin one last look of sympathy, then turns and hurries down the hallway. Cassian shuts and bolts the door. He picks up the tray and places it on the small table._]

CASSIAN
She has a soft heart. She does not yet know
The bitter seeds that selfish actions sow.
Here. You will eat. You must be strong to face
The coming trial in that other place.

[_He cuts the ropes on Orin’s wrists. Orin rubs them, the circulation returning with a tingling pain. He does not reach for the food._]

ORIN
Her pity… felt more real than your contempt.
From honest judgment, no one is exempt.
But she saw me. The man I am inside.
Not just the monster that you see with pride.
You speak of Lyra, of Valerius.
You make it sound so grand and glorious.
Your loyalty. Your righteous, noble quest.
But is there not some hatred in your breast?
This is not justice. This is your revenge.
On which your own dark feelings can impinge.

CASSIAN
Call it what name you wish. It makes no change.
It does not alter or re-arrange
The facts. A good man’s dead. A killer fled.
And now the killer will be homeward led.
Eat now. The ship will be here with the tide.
There is no longer any place to hide.

[_Orin slowly picks up a piece of bread. His hands are shaking. He looks at Cassian, who has turned back to the window to watch the growing light of dawn._]

ORIN
You watch the dawn. Do you ever feel fear?
That your own end might be terribly near?
You live a life of shadows and of steel.
Is there any real warmth that you can feel?
Or has your vow turned you to ice and stone?
A man who walks this bitter world alone.

CASSIAN
I feel the cold. I feel the coming rain.
I do not run from sacrifice or pain.
I made a choice. To serve a worthy lord.
My life was second to my given word.
You had that choice as well, a noble son,
But you chose folly before you had begun
To be a man. You chose the easy way.
And for that choice, you will most dearly pay.

[_Orin watches him. He sees Cassian’s focus on the port below. He sees the knife on the table, used to cut the cheese. It is a small blade, but it is a chance. He knows he cannot overpower Cassian, but the door is just a few feet away. Elara is downstairs. If he could just get out, scream for help… He takes a slow, deep breath, and prepares to gamble one last time._]

ORIN
(Muttering to himself)
One final choice. One final, desperate throw.
To see which way the winds of fortune blow.

[_His fingers twitch, inching toward the blade. Just as he braces himself to move, a sharp knock sounds at the door, making both men freeze. Cassian’s head snaps away from the window._]

ELARA
(From outside)
Sir? I forgot. The water in the jug.
I feared the dust from off the worn floor rug
Had settled in it. I have brought you more.
May I come in and leave it by the door?

[_Cassian turns, his eyes narrowed in irritation. He glances at Orin, who has frozen, his hand hovering just an inch from the knife._]

CASSIAN
Your timing, girl, is wanting. Go away.
We have no need of water till midday.

ELARA
(From outside, hesitant)
But sir, the journey’s long. You should be supplied.
It is no trouble. I will not abide…

[_Cassian moves toward the door, his back to Orin for a fatal second. It is the opening Orin needed. In a flash, he snatches the small knife from the table. He does not lunge for Cassian. He lunges for the door. With a speed born of pure terror, he slides the heavy bolt back just as Cassian turns._]

CASSIAN
Fool! What have you…

[_The door creaks open. Elara stands there, a fresh pitcher in her hands, her face a mask of confusion. Orin yanks her inside, spinning her around to face Cassian. Her body is a shield. The pitcher smashes on the floorboards, water and clay shards scattering. He presses the dull edge of the cheese knife to her throat. It is not much of a weapon, but the threat is clear._]

ORIN
Stay back! One step and she will feel the steel.
This is no game. This agony is real.

[_Elara lets out a small, terrified cry. Her eyes are wide, fixed on Cassian’s unmoving form._]

ELARA
Please… sir… I do not understand… please help…
I heard a noise… a sudden, fearful yelp…

CASSIAN
(His voice a low, dangerous calm)
Let the girl go. You make a grave mistake.
For this choice, too, a reckoning you’ll make.

ORIN
No more of reckonings! I’ve heard my fill!
Your justice waits. My own will serves me still.
I want a horse. And gold. A ten league start.
Do this, and she may leave with beating heart.
Refuse me, and her blood will stain the floor.
Then you can explain that at heaven’s door!

CASSIAN
You are the one who deals in blood and death.
Do not presume to bargain with her breath.
You think that I, who serve a murdered lord,
Would flinch at one more soul put to the sword?
Her life is forfeit by your coward’s hand.
A truth I’m sure that she can understand.

[_His words are ice, but his eyes do not leave the knife at Elara’s throat. He has not moved an inch, his body coiled like a waiting serpent._]

ELARA
(Tears streaming down her face)
No… please… He is just scared. I saw his face.
There is some good inside him, some small grace.
Orin, you looked at me. You are not this.
Don’t trade my life for some imagined bliss.

ORIN
(His voice cracking, the knife trembling)
Be silent, girl! Your pity cannot save.
It is a fast horse and my life I crave!
He does not care for you. You heard his word.
To him, you are a shield, and not a bird
Whose song is precious. You’re a tool, a price.
And he will not be asked to pay it twice.
(To Cassian)
You see? Your coldness serves my purpose now.
It proves the darkness of your hollow vow.
You speak of serving, but you only hate.
You’d let her die to seal my wretched fate!

CASSIAN
I see a rat caught in a closing snare.
Who bites the helping hand in its despair.
She showed you kindness. Offered you fresh bread.
And you repay her with this mortal dread.
The monster that she hoped was not within
Now shows its face and glories in its sin.

ORIN
I am no monster! I am just a man
Who runs from butchers with a bloody plan!
You call this justice? Dragging me in chains
To face a court where your own venom reigns?
Valerius’s sons will not see truth.
They’ll see a scapegoat for their vanished youth.
They’ll tear me limb from limb to soothe their rage.
And you, his dog, will rattle my last cage.

CASSIAN
You should have thought of that before the deed.
Before you planted that most wicked seed.
You took a life, a great man, noble, kind.
And left a legacy of pain behind.
You speak of sons, of rage, of what they’ll do.
Is their grief not a mirror held to you?
What of your father? Does he not now weep
To know the company his son does keep
With murderers and thieves? To know his name
Is now a brand of infamy and shame?

[_Orin flinches, the words a physical blow. His grip on Elara tightens, and she gasps._]

ORIN
You know nothing of my father. Not a thing.
You do not know the sorrow that you sing.
Valerius… he was not what you believe.
The perfect lord for whom you sit and grieve.
He wore a mask of honor, bright and bold,
But underneath, his heart was hard and cold.
His goodness was a well-rehearsed disguise.
A web of charm built on a throne of lies!

CASSIAN
Blasphemer. You defile a good man’s name
To try and mitigate your own black shame.
It will not work. Your lies are thin as air.
A desperate plea born of a fool’s despair.
I knew the man. I served him twenty years.
I saw his triumphs and I saw his tears.
He was the soul of honor. Firm, but just.
In his good name I place my sacred trust.

ORIN
Your trust was blind! Your service was a sham!
You do not know the creature that I am,
Nor what he was! Did you know of the lands
He stole from farmers with his own two hands?
The tithes he raised that left the poor to starve
While he would feast on what his cooks would carve?
Did you know of the girls he took to bed?
The promises he broke, the tears they shed?
You saw the lord. The rest of us saw the beast.
Upon our stolen happiness, he’d feast.

ELARA
(In a choked whisper)
Is this true? What you say? Can it be so?
The lord we praised… the one we did not know…

CASSIAN
(His voice sharp, directed at Elara)
Girl, hold your tongue. His words are poison darts,
Meant to corrupt the faith of simple hearts.
A killer’s lies to make his crime seem small.
He’d burn the world to justify his fall.
(To Orin)
So this is your defense? A slanderous tale?
That you were justice, weighing on a scale
The life of one you deemed was not worth life?
That you were judge and jury with your knife?
How arrogant. How pitifully grand.
To think that vengeance was yours to command.

ORIN
It was not vengeance! It was… an escape!
A final, frantic bid to change my shape!
He had my father ruined, stripped of pride.
His debts were called, his every plea denied.
Valerius owned him. Owned us all. Our home.
The very ground on which we used to roam.
I went to beg him. Not for gold or grace.
But for more time. To leave that cursed place
With something more than rags upon our back.
To leave his shadow, venomous and black.

[_Orin’s voice is raw with memory. The knife shakes more violently now. Elara watches his face, her fear mixed with a dawning, horrified understanding._]

ORIN
He laughed at me. He sat upon his chair,
And with a smile, he savored my despair.
He said my sister had a fairer price
To pay. One single, sordid sacrifice.
She could erase the debt with one night’s shame.
And in that moment, I spoke his dark name.
We fought. He was a giant, strong and cruel.
He saw my rage and called me a poor fool.
He drew his dagger. Said he’d cut my tongue
For all the foolish, frantic songs I’d sung.
I only sought to push him, make him fall…
I did not mean to kill the man at all.
His dagger… in the struggle… it went deep.
A bloody harvest he himself did reap.

[_Silence hangs in the room. Cassian’s face is granite, unreadable. Elara is crying silently, her body trembling in Orin’s grasp._]

CASSIAN
A pretty story. Woven with fine thread.
To rouse the pity of a serving maid.
You think this changes anything? This plea?
That you were wronged, so you should be set free?
It matters not. The law is stark and plain.
A nobleman was by your action slain.
Accident? Malice? It is all the same.
You lit the fire. You must accept the flame.

ORIN
You will not even hear it! Will not see!
The man inside who’s screaming to be free!
You are a statue, carved to look like man,
Who only follows one obsessive plan.
Valerius is dead. Your world is gone.
And still you serve him in the empty dawn.

CASSIAN
My world is not gone. My world is my word.
The sharpest, truest, most enduring sword.
I swore an oath to him upon my life.
To guard his family from harm and strife.
To see his justice done, though he may lie
Beneath the cold and all-forgiving sky.
This is not for him. Not anymore.
It is for the order I am fighting for.
If men like you can kill and run and hide,
Then chaos reigns, with justice cast aside.
Your tale of woe, if true, is sad indeed.
But from your crime a worse crime would succeed.

[_He takes one slow, deliberate step forward. The floorboard creaks under his boot._]

ORIN
Stay back! I swear it, I will cut her now!
I will not break! I will not bend or bow!

[_Orin’s eyes are wild. He is losing control, his desperation fraying the last of his reason. Elara sees it. She sees the madness in his eyes, the terror of a trapped animal. And in that moment, she acts. With a sudden, sharp cry, she brings her heel down hard on Orin’s instep._]

ELARA
Agh!

[_Orin shouts in pain, his grip loosening for a fraction of a second. It is all the time Cassian needs. He moves with a blur of speed that is terrifying to behold. He does not lunge at Orin, but at the arm holding the knife. He grabs Orin’s wrist, twisting it with brutal efficiency. The small knife clatters to the floor. Simultaneously, he shoves Elara away from the struggle. She stumbles back, falling into a heap near the wall, safe._]

[_Orin, disarmed and off-balance, tries to fight back, but he is no match for the trained warrior. Cassian slams him against the door, the wood groaning in protest. He pins Orin there with his forearm pressed against his throat._]

CASSIAN
(His voice a lethal whisper)
Your last chance, squandered. Your last gamble, lost.
You should have stayed and counted up the cost.

[_Orin struggles for breath, clawing uselessly at Cassian’s arm. His face turns a dark shade of red, his eyes bulging._]

ELARA
(Scrambling to her feet)
Stop! You will kill him! Please, sir, that’s enough!
His tale was true… his life has been too rough!

CASSIAN
(Without looking at her)
Stay out of this. His life is not your own
To spend with pity. What the man has sown,
He now must harvest. There is nothing more.

[_He eases the pressure slightly, allowing Orin to gasp for air. He then spins him around and forces him to his knees. He retrieves the ropes he had cut earlier and, with swift, practiced motions, binds Orin’s hands behind his back, tighter this time than before._]

ORIN
(Gasping, kneeling on the floor)
You heard me… You heard the truth… and you don’t care.
It is a weight that you will have to bear.

CASSIAN
I bear the weight of duty. It is all
I need to stand while other men may fall.
Your truth is yours. The law has its own claim.
And it cares little for your father’s name,
Or for your sister’s honor, or your tears.
It is the bulwark against all our fears.

[_He stands up, leaving Orin kneeling and defeated on the floorboards amidst the shattered pottery. He turns to Elara, his face grim. She flinches back._]

CASSIAN
You are unhurt?

ELARA
I am. No thanks to you.
You would have let him see his vile threat through.

CASSIAN
He would have faltered. Men like him always do.
They lack the will to make the cut anew.
His soul is weak. He talks of grand designs,
But at the precipice, he underlines
His own deep cowardice. I saw his eyes.
They were not killer’s eyes. Just full of lies.

ELARA
He told the truth. I felt it in his voice.
He did not have another, better choice.
And you… you serve a man who would have done
Such wicked things? Beneath the midday sun?
Lord Valerius… a thief? A lecher? Worse?
And you his sword, his shield, his trusted purse?

CASSIAN
The dead are owed their peace. It is not right
To slander them when they can no longer fight.
He was my lord. He gave me home and place.
He showed me purpose. He showed me his grace.
If he had flaws, they were a mortal’s due.
No man is perfect. Not me. Not even you.
But what this man describes, this monster… no.
That is a lie to help his treason grow.
Now, clean this mess. The ship will soon be here.
And take this memory of foolish fear
And lock it deep. Forget the words you heard.
This is the world of men. Vile and absurd.

[_He turns away from her and goes back to the window, his vigil resumed. Elara looks from Cassian’s rigid back to Orin’s slumped form. She slowly kneels and begins to pick up the larger pieces of the broken pitcher, her hands shaking._]

ORIN
(In a low, broken voice)
He will not listen. He will never hear.
He is a prisoner of his own fear.
The fear that everything he lived and fought for
Was just a lie that washed up on the shore
Of his own life. To question is to fall.
So he builds up his own defensive wall.

ELARA
(Quietly, not looking at him)
Be still. Your words will only make it worse.
You have unleashed a far more potent curse
Than his revenge. You have unleashed a doubt.
A little seed that he cannot cast out.

[_A heavy tramp of boots is heard on the stairs. Cassian turns from the window, his posture straightening. There is a loud, authoritative rap on the door._]

VOICE
(From outside, deep and gravelly)
Cassian! The tide is turning with the sun!
Our time is short! The waiting now is done!

CASSIAN
(Calling out)
The door is unbolted, Kael. You may come in.
Let our dark journey finally begin.

[_The door swings open and a large man enters. He is bearded, dressed in the heavy leather and wool of a sea captain. His eyes are shrewd and take in the scene at a glance: the bound prisoner, the shaken girl, the water and shards on the floor._]

KAEL
By the sea gods, what happened in this room?
It looks as though you fought the very doom.
I trust the package is secure and fast?
This is one journey I would have be our last.

CASSIAN
He is secured. He had a flight of fancy.
A brief and foolish act of necromancy,
Trying to raise a spirit of rebellion.
He is no more a threat than a chameleon.

[_Kael grunts and walks over to Orin, grabbing him by the hair and forcing his head up. He studies his face._]

KAEL
He looks half starved. And scared. This is the man
Who brought the house of great Valerius down?
He looks more like a boy who stole a bun
And fears the baker more than anyone.

ORIN
I am not what you think. Let me just speak…

KAEL
I am not paid to think, boy, or to hear.
I’m paid to sail, and keep my cargo clear
Of any trouble. And you smell like trouble.
So you’ll be kept in chains, and on the double.
Get him on his feet. The light is growing strong.
I do not wish to be here overlong.

[_Cassian hauls Orin to his feet. Orin stumbles, his legs weak. He looks at Elara one last time, his eyes pleading._]

ELARA
Wait.

[_All three men look at her. She stands, holding a piece of the broken pitcher in her hand. She has a newfound resolve in her eyes._]

ELARA
He has not eaten. He will not survive
The journey if he is not kept alive.
(To Cassian)
You said he must be strong to face the trial.
Let me at least give him this one last vial
Of human kindness. Let him eat the bread.
Or will you have him meet his judges dead?

[_Cassian stares at her, a flicker of something unreadable in his eyes. Annoyance? Respect? He gives a short, sharp nod._]

CASSIAN
Five minutes, Kael. No more. Let him eat.
Then we will drag him down into the street.

[_Kael shrugs, releasing Orin and leaning against the wall by the door, his arms crossed. Cassian stands by the window, watching, but not just the port now. He watches the room. Elara goes to the table, picks up the piece of bread Orin had dropped, and holds it out to him. Orin, with his hands bound, cannot take it._]

ORIN
I cannot.

[_Elara hesitates for a moment. Then, she breaks off a small piece of the bread and raises it to Orin’s lips. He eats it slowly, his eyes closed. It is an act of profound and dangerous sympathy. Kael watches with cynical amusement. Cassian watches with an expression of stone._]

ELARA
(Softly)
There. For the journey. May you find some peace.
May all our bitter sorrows find release.

CASSIAN
That is enough. The time is at an end.
On this girl’s pity you can not depend.
Let’s go.

[_Cassian moves forward, taking Orin by the arm. Orin does not resist. As he is led toward the door, he looks back at Elara._]

ORIN
Thank you.

[_It is all he says. Cassian and Kael lead him out of the room. The sound of their boots recedes down the stairs. The front door of the inn opens, then closes. Silence returns. Elara stands alone in the room, with the uneaten food, the spilled water, and the small knife still lying on the floor. She walks over and picks it up, turning the dull blade over in her fingers. She looks at the door through which they left, her face a mixture of fear, pity, and a new, unsettling doubt._]

ELARA
The world of men. Vile and absurd.
And I have heard its every single word.

[_She looks from the knife in her hand to the window, where the dawn is now breaking fully, casting long, uncertain shadows across the empty room. She is no longer just a girl with a soft heart. She is now a keeper of a dangerous secret._]

[END OF SCENE]
