Enter Lucian and Gideon.

GIDEON
I pray thee, Lucian, leave this biting wit.
The market square is hot, and tempers likewise.
These Ironhawks do strut with feathered pride;
A careless word may hatch a bloody chick.

LUCIAN
And thou art ever the mother hen, to cluck
And fret and gather all thy brood to safety.
Why, thy blood runs so cold, a winter’s frost
Hath more of passion in its icy breath.
Thou’dst make apology to a stone for tripping,
Or beg a raincloud’s pardon for getting wet.
A man whose heart beats with such timid caution
Should wear a shroud to save the tailor’s labour.

GIDEON
And a man whose tongue is sharper than his sword
Oft finds his wit hath dug his own sad grave.

Enter Seraphina and her followers.

LUCIAN
Speak of the devil, and she doth appear,
Clad in the feathers of a carrion crow.
Good morrow, Lady Hawk. What prey d’you seek?

SERAPHINA
Thee, sea-worm, that doth slime our city’s stones.
I hear thou’st made a ballad of my house,
A scurvy tune that mocks my father’s name.

LUCIAN
A ballad, madam? Nay, a tragedy,
For what is sadder than a hawk that chirps
And cannot fly? Your wit’s a blunted dagger,
Fit only to spread butter on your bread.
I did but sing the truth, which, I confess,
To ears like thine must be a discord foul.

SERAPHINA
My truth shall be writ sharp upon thy skin!
Draw, lest I think thee coward as thou’rt knave.

GIDEON
Good lady, hold! We are in public view.
The Magistrate’s decree forbids this strife.

SERAPHINA
The Magistrate may rule the city’s laws,
But not the honour of a noble house!
He consorts with a mongrel pack, and so
Shall feel the talons that he dares to mock.

Enter Orion.

LUCIAN
My talons, madam, are for better game.
But here’s a bird of your own flock. Ask him.

SERAPHINA
Orion! What, dost thou keep company
With these salt-water dogs? Art thou a traitor?
Or hast thou learned to bark?

ORION
Sweet cousin, peace.
The words you speak are poison in the air.
These gentlemen and I were but conversing.
I pray you, put your anger in its sheath.

SERAPHINA
A sheath? When honour’s drawn and stands all bare?
Thou art an Ironhawk in name alone!
Thy heart is lily-livered, white with fear.

ORION
There is no fear in me, but such a love
For peace as makes thy fury seem a child’s.
The cause you have for anger, I know not,
But I serve a bond you cannot yet perceive.
And so, good Seraphina, fare you well.

LUCIAN
O meek, unmanly, base capitulation!
[_Draws._]
Hath he unmanned thee with his gentle words?
Turn, Lady Hawk. Here is one who will dance.

SERAPHINA
This is the music I have longed to hear!

ORION
Hold, Lucian! Cousin, for the love of God!
[_Orion moves between them._]

SERAPHINA
[_Drawing._]
Then take thou this for thy meddling!

[_They fight. Orion attempts to beat down their weapons. Under Orion’s arm, Seraphina wounds Lucian._]

LUCIAN
I have it.
A scratch. A little scratch. But ’tis enough.
A plague on both your houses! They are built
With mortal mortar and with bloody stones.

GIDEON
What, Lucian, art thou hurt?

LUCIAN
Ay, past all surgery. Ask for me at the tavern,
And you shall find me quiet company.
Not so wide as a river’s mouth, nor deep
As a miner’s grave, but it will suffice.
I am seasoned for the worms. That she-devil,
That hawk-eyed fury, hath undone me.
Orion, why didst thou step betwixt our points?
I took the wound meant for thy peaceful heart.
A plague... your houses!

[_Lucian falls. Gideon rushes to him._]

ORION
My friend, my truest friend, slain for my sake!
My vow for peace hath murdered him I loved!
O foolish peace! O honour stain’d and lost!
Thy gentleness, sweet heaven, made me weak.

Re-enter Seraphina.

GIDEON
Orion, look, she comes again in triumph.

ORION
In triumph? And my Lucian in his shroud?
Then pity, fly to heaven with his soul,
And burning rage be now my only guide!
Now, Seraphina, take the ‘coward’ back
That your foul tongue did give me. Lucian’s ghost
Waits for your spirit to attend his flight.
One of us two this very hour must join him.

SERAPHINA
Thou wretched whelp, that chose a dog for friend,
Thou’lt kennel with him in the dirt of death!

[_They fight. Seraphina falls._]

GIDEON
Orion, fly! The watch is coming fast!
The blood is up, the citizens awake!
If thou art found, the Magistrate will doom thee!
Away, man, hence!

ORION
O, I am fate’s own jester!

[_Exit Orion._]

Enter Magistrate, with Guards; Lord Valerius, Lord Cassian, and followers of both houses.

LORD CASSIAN
Where is the villain that hath slain my niece?
Where is that butcher, that accursed Seawolf?

GIDEON
There lies the lady, by young Orion slain.
And he that lies there was by Seraphina slain.

LORD VALERIUS
My son! Where is my son? Is he alive?

MAGISTRATE
Who first began this foul and fatal business?

GIDEON
The lady Seraphina, fiery-tempered,
Did challenge Lucian for a song, he said.
Orion, speaking words as calm as prayer,
With gentle reason sought to quench the fire.
But Lucian, hot of blood, miscalled it fear,
And drew his sword to answer honour’s part.
Orion rushed between their angry points,
Begging them hold, for love of law and peace.
Beneath his arm, an ill-aimed thrust from her
Did find the life of noble Lucian out.
She fled, but then returned, as if in pride.
Orion, new-enraged by his friend’s death,
Fought with a fury that I could not stay,
And ere I drew my breath, she lay there dead.
Then, as he saw what sorrow he had wrought,
He turned and fled. This is the honest truth,
Or let my life be forfeit for the lie.

LORD CASSIAN
He is a Seawolf. By his very nature
He speaks with tides and currents, not with truth!
A dozen of them must have fought with her,
A flock of gulls against a single hawk!
I demand justice! As the law is just,
Orion’s life must pay for Seraphina’s.

LORD VALERIUS
He slew the slayer of his dearest friend!
His was the hand, but hers the fault that moved it.
The law itself would end her life for this!
His sword did but outpace your formal justice.

MAGISTRATE
Orion’s sword outpaced his wisdom, too.
He took the law into his own possession.
I have an interest in your foolish feud;
My own guard’s blood upon your cobbles dries.
For thy offence, Orion is exiled.
Let him be gone from this city by sunrise,
On pain of death if he be found again.
So learn this lesson, writ in blood and tears:
Your rage shall purchase nought but barren years.

MAGISTRATE
The matter is concluded. Clear this street!
Take up these bodies, monuments to folly,
And bear them to their houses. Let the bells
Not toll for them, but for the death of peace.
Lord Cassian, and you, Lord Valerius,
Return to your own roofs. And mark me well.
This is my city. Its laws are my breath.
If any man of yours, by night or day,
By word or weapon, lifts a hand to break
The fragile truce this sentence has imposed,
His life is forfeit. Not exile, but the block.
This feud of yours has drunk its fill tonight.
I’ll have no more of it. Go. Now.

[The Guards lift the bodies of SERAPHINA and LUCIAN. The crowd begins to disperse.]

LORD CASSIAN
Exile! A gentle word for butchery.
A prize for murder, not a punishment.
You give him the whole world to wander in,
While my sweet niece is given six feet of earth.
This is no justice, Magistrate. This is fear.
You fear the Seawolves and their blustering pride.

LORD VALERIUS
You’d call it pride? I call it suffering.
My son is banished, his good name destroyed,
His best friend slain before his very eyes.
And all because your niece, a fiery cloud
That rained down lightning with no cause or care,
Could not abide a moment’s gentle peace.
He pays for her intemperance, not his own.

LORD CASSIAN
He’ll pay in full. The world has roads and lanes,
But none so long it outruns memory.
And you, young sir, that told this tidy tale,
[He turns to GIDEON.]
Your tongue is nimble. See it does not trip
When walking in the shadows of my house.
We have not finished here.

[Exit LORD CASSIAN with his followers, who bear SERAPHINA’s body.]

MAGISTRATE
Lord Valerius, see your son obeys.
The dawn’s first light must find him past the gates.
There is no other way.

[Exit MAGISTRATE and his Guards.]

LORD VALERIUS
Gideon, my thanks. You spoke the truth for him,
When all the world was set to speak in lies.
I must go home, to help my wretched boy
Prepare a life he never thought to live.
A father’s heart is but a brittle thing.

[Exit LORD VALERIUS, his followers taking LUCIAN’s body.]

GIDEON
[Alone on the stage, now empty and dark.]
The truth I spoke? A piece of it, perhaps.
A version polished to protect a friend.
I saw a rage in him, a sea-born storm
That sought her life as thunder seeks the ground.
I did not speak of that. O, conscience, peace!
What is one coloured word against a life?
He is my friend. And now, the enemy
Of a great house that trades in blood and spite.
The night grows cold. The stones beneath my feet
Are hungry for more blood. And I, I think,
Am marked to be the next to feed their thirst.

[Exit GIDEON. The stage is empty.]

SCENE II. A chamber in Lord Valerius’s house.
Enter ORION, his clothes dishevelled, a small stain of blood on his sleeve. He paces wildly.

ORION
Gone. Banished. Driven from the light of home
To wander like a ghost in foreign lands.
And for what crime? For loving Lucian well?
For answering a fury with a sword?
O justice, thou art but a painted whore,
That sells her favours to the loudest voice,
And I am poor in all things but my grief.
Lucian, my friend, my brother, you are dust,
And I, who should have guarded you from harm,
Am now the wind that blows your memory
Across a barren world. How could I fail?
How could I let her venom find your heart?
My shield was slow, my purpose was too weak.
I begged for peace when I should have drawn steel.
I am the coward that she named me then.

[Enter ELARA, Orion’s younger sister.]

ELARA
Orion? Brother? Is it true? The things
The servants whisper in the shadowed halls?
That Lucian is… that you must go away?

ORION
Sweet sister, do not look upon me now.
There’s blood upon my hands, though you see none.
My soul is stained. I am no more the brother
That taught you names of stars and sang you songs.
I am a thing of darkness and of wrath.

ELARA
No, you are not. I know your heart, Orion.
It is as wide and open as the sea
You love so well. It cannot hold a monster.
They say you fought for Lucian. Was it so?
Then you fought well. For friendship is a kingdom,
And you its loyal king. But must you leave?
Where will you go? The world is vast and cruel.

ORION
Where the sun guides me. Where my father sends me.
Where Cassian’s hunters will not find my trail.
Do not you weep for me, my sweet Elara.
Weep for the friend I lost, the life I took.
Pray that my exile scours my spirit clean.

[Enter LORD VALERIUS.]

LORD VALERIUS
The time is short. The east begins to grey.
There is no space for sorrow’s long embrace.
Here is a purse. It holds a hundred crowns,
Enough to buy you passage, food, and shelter.
And here, a map that shows the northern roads.
Ride hard for Oakhaven; my cousin there,
Lord Emmerich, will give you secret refuge.
Tell him I sent you. Tell him all the tale.
He is a man of honour, and of sense.

ORION
And I am now a beggar at his door,
A fugitive who brings but shame and trouble.
Father, I cannot. Let me stay and face
The Magistrate, the law, Lord Cassian’s rage.
To die here, in my city, is a death.
To run and hide is but a living grave.

LORD VALERIUS
And you will choose the grave? You foolish boy!
Is this the courage that you boast of so?
To throw your life away like a spent coin?
Your life is mine! I gave it, I preserve it!
You will obey me. Live, Orion, live.
Live to see justice done, your name restored.
Live, so your father’s house does not go dark.
Elara, leave us now. Go to your room.

ELARA
Father, be gentle. See, his heart is broken.
Orion, I will light a candle for you,
Each night until you stand here once again.
I will. I promise.

[Exit ELARA.]

LORD VALERIUS
She has a woman’s heart, all faith and tears.
But you must have a man’s, all stone and steel.
Lord Cassian will not honour this decree.
He’ll send his men to hunt you on the roads.
So be not you, Orion. Be a shadow.
A whisper in the tavern, a vague shape
Upon the path. Change your name, your gait, your hair.
Let Orion Seawolf die this very night,
And let some other man be born at dawn.

ORION
To live a lie? To slink from town to town?
My name is all I have that’s truly mine.
It was my grandfather’s, and his before.

LORD VALERIUS
And they are dead. They do not need the name.
But you need life. Now listen to my plan.
Your horse is saddled at the western gate.
But you will not go west. You’ll slip on foot
Out through the garden, to the river’s edge.
Gideon awaits you there with a small boat.
He’ll row you downstream, past the city’s watch.
Then take the eastern road. They’ll look for you
Upon the northern track. This buys you time.
Now, change your clothes. This velvet is a banner
That waves for every cutthroat to attack.
Wear this plain wool. It speaks of humbler stock.
Hurry. The birds begin to stir and call.

ORION
Each word you speak is like a nail that seals
The coffin of the man I was. So be it.
I do this for you, Father. Not for me.
My life had its true purpose, and it ended
When Lucian fell. The rest is but an epilogue,
Writ by another hand, in fading ink.
Farewell.

LORD VALERIUS
I will not say farewell, my son.
For that’s a word of ending. I will say,
Until we meet. And we shall meet again.
When this black tide of hatred has receded,
I’ll send for you. Now go. God speed your journey.

[ORION clasps his father’s hand, then turns and exits quickly.]

LORD VALERIUS
[Alone.]
O, what a curse it is to be a father.
To forge a life, to shape it with your love,
To see it grow into a thing of pride,
And then to watch it break upon the rocks
Of this cruel world. To send your only son
Into the wilderness, a hunted deer,
While you must stay and smile and play the courtier,
Bowing to men who’d gladly see him dead.
May heaven grant me strength to wear this mask,
And may my boy have cunning, and have luck.
For he will need them more than gold or steel.

[Exit LORD VALERIUS.]

SCENE III. A great hall in Lord Cassian’s house. Torches burn low.
Enter LORD CASSIAN, followed by LADY HELENA, his wife.

LADY HELENA
My lord, my husband, come away to bed.
The night is spent. Your anger feeds on air
And will consume you. Nothing can be changed
By pacing on these stones till morning comes.
Seraphina is at peace. She does not feel
The fire of your rage. Let her sleep well.

LORD CASSIAN
She will not sleep while her own murderer
Rides free beneath the moon! You speak of peace?
Peace is a coward’s truce, a weakling’s prayer.
I am a Firehawk. My house is fire.
And we do not make peace with the cold sea.
We burn until the waves themselves retreat.
That boy, that Seawolf, breathes the air that should
Be hers. He sees the stars that are denied her.
While that is so, there is no peace for me.

LADY HELENA
What can you do? The Magistrate has ruled.
To move against his word is to court ruin.
You risk our house, our name, our very lives,
To chase a ghost of vengeance through the dark.
We have lost one child of our blood tonight.
Must your pride offer up another score?
Let the law hold. Let exile be his fate.
Perhaps in loneliness he’ll find remorse.

LORD CASSIAN
Remorse? A Seawolf feels no more remorse
Than does a shark that rips a swimmer’s flesh.
His heart is salt and ice. And as for law,
The law is what a strong man says it is.
The Magistrate is weak, his city plagued
By merchants’ squabbles and the price of grain.
He does not understand the blood of nobles.
Our laws are older. Honour is our judge,
And retribution is its only verdict.
Ho, Marcus! To me!

[Enter MARCUS, a hard-faced man in captain’s livery.]

MARCUS
My lord, you called.

LORD CASSIAN
You heard the sentence that the city gave?

MARCUS
I did, my lord. A coward’s compromise.

LORD CASSIAN
My thought exactly. Now, this banished boy,
Orion, will make for the northern road.
His father’s kin hold lands beyond the mountains.
He’ll ride for sanctuary there. I want you
To take ten men, the swiftest horses, best
At tracking prey. Ride north. But do not use
The main road. Use the forest paths, the hills.
Move faster than a rumour, quieter
Than a falling leaf. Find him.

MARCUS
And when we find him, shall we bring him back
To face the Magistrate’s displeasure, lord?

LORD CASSIAN
Bring him back? You jest. The boy is exiled.
And exile is a perilous affair.
The roads are thick with robbers, are they not?
A man alone, a boy with a full purse,
Might meet with misfortune. A tragic tale.
He fought most bravely, but was overcome.
You understand my meaning, Marcus?

MARCUS
Perfectly, my lord.
His journey ends where we encounter him.
His body will not speak of who we are.

LADY HELENA
Cassian, no! This is a villain’s work!
An ambush in the dark! It has no honour!
You stain your name with this, you stain our house!
Seraphina would not wish for this!

LORD CASSIAN
You do not know what Seraphina wished!
She had the spirit of a hawk, my lady.
She would demand a payment made in blood!
Marcus, you have your orders. Take the men
Whose loyalty is absolute. Go now.
Before the sun is high, I want you gone.

MARCUS
By noon, his shadow will not touch the earth.
[Exit MARCUS.]

LADY HELENA
You’ve sent a wolf to hunt a frightened child.
And you have done it in the name of love.
O, twisted, foul, and damnable revenge!
It makes a monster of the man I married.
I’ll go and pray. Not for my niece’s soul,
For she is past all harm. I’ll pray for yours.
I fear it is in far more mortal danger.

[Exit LADY HELENA.]

LORD CASSIAN
Let her pray. Let the priests and women talk
Of mercy and the soul’s eternal state.
I am a lord of this world, not the next.
And in this world, a debt of blood is paid
With blood alone. The scales must balance out.
Valerius thinks his boy has slipped the net.
The fool. I have not cast my net. I’ve launched
A flight of arrows, and they will not miss.
And then, the witness. Gideon, the friend.
His clever tongue must be persuaded, too.
A lie for a lie. An eye for an eye. A son for a niece.
The world is simple, if you are but strong enough
To make it so.

[Exit LORD CASSIAN.]

SCENE IV. The riverbank east of the city. It is just before dawn. A small boat is tied to a willow tree.
Enter GIDEON, rowing the boat to the bank. ORION, in plain traveller’s clothes, waits for him.

GIDEON
The current runs fast here. The city sleeps,
Or pretends to. Every window seems an eye,
And every shadow holds a waiting blade.
Are you prepared?

ORION
As well as any man can be prepared
To leave his life behind. My father’s plan
Was sound. No one will watch the river gate.
They’ll cluster at the roads that lead to north.

GIDEON
Still, you must hurry. Time is not your friend.
Once you are clear of the downstream patrols,
Land on the southern bank and make for Ashwood.
From there, the eastern road is yours to take.
Here, I have brought you this.

[GIDEON offers ORION a sword.]

ORION
I have a sword. My father gave me one.

GIDEON
This one is better. It knows you. And its loss
Is what has set you on this wretched path.
It is Lucian’s.

[ORION takes the sword, his hand trembling slightly.]

ORION
His sword. I cannot. It is not my right.
I failed to guard its master. How can I
Presume to carry this? Its very weight
Accuses me. Each line of polished steel
Is like a page where my disgrace is writ.

GIDEON
Then let it be the pen with which you write
A different story. Lucian loved you, Orion.
He would not have you go unarmed, defenceless.
He’d want his blade to be your shield and guardian.
Take it. For him. For the love you both did share.
Use it to live, so that his memory
Does not die out upon some lonely road.

ORION
[Buckling on the sword.]
I’ll take it, then. And I will keep it bright.
Not for revenge. That fire is burned to ash.
I’ll keep it in remembrance of a soul
That was all light and laughter, till the end.
Gideon, my friend, my only friend, I think.
What will you do, when I am gone from here?
Cassian’s eye is on you. He will not forget
The witness who denied him total vengeance.

GIDEON
I am a humble scholar’s son. I have
No lands, no wealth, no power he might crave.
I can be small and quiet, like a mouse,
And hope the hawk’s sharp eye will pass me over.
But you must promise me one thing. Your sister.
Elara. She will feel this loss the most.
Write to me, when you can, so I may tell her
You are alive and well. It will preserve her.

ORION
I will. And watch her for me, Gideon.
Be the good brother that I can no longer be.
Keep her from sorrow’s chill.
And now, I go. The sky turns pale with dawn.
A new day comes, for a new kind of man.
I do not know who I will be tomorrow.
I only know I am not who I was.
Farewell.

GIDEON
Not farewell. As your father said,
Until we meet. Now go. The tide is turning.

[ORION pushes the boat into the river and gets in. He takes up the oars. GIDEON unties the rope.]

ORION
May fortune favour you, my honest friend.

GIDEON
And may the sea-winds guide you home again.

[ORION begins to row away, downstream, into the growing light. GIDEON watches from the bank until the boat is a small speck and then gone.]

GIDEON
And he is gone. A ship without a harbour,
A star without a name, pushed to the dark.
And I remain, to face the coming day.
The sun now touches on the city’s spires,
And paints them gold, as if to mock our grief.
It shines alike upon the murderer,
The victim, and the friend who stood and watched.
It lights the path for the avenging hunter
As clearly as it lights the fleeing prey.
O, indifferent and glorious sun, what truths
Will your cold light reveal, before you set
Upon the ruins of this broken day?

[The tolling of a single, solemn bell is heard from the city. GIDEON turns towards the sound as the light grows brighter. Curtain.]

ACT II

SCENE I

[The great hall in the house of ORION’s father. The room is large and shadowed, the morning light struggling through high, narrow windows. The furniture is draped in black cloth. The air is heavy with the scent of funerary herbs. ELARA stands by a window, a pale, still figure in a dark gown. She is staring out at the city. The sound of a heavy door opening. GIDEON enters. He looks weary, his clothes travel-stained. He hesitates for a moment.]

ELARA
[Without turning.]
I heard the bell. One single, iron tongue
To tell the city what my heart already knew.
They will be burying him at noon. And still,
My brother is not here. Does he not know?
Does he not care his friend lies cold and dead?
Or does his own great grief become a cage
That holds him prisoner in some dark room?
You were with him. I saw you from this window,
Walking together in the pre-dawn grey.
Where is he, Gideon? Does he come? Is he
Preparing now to pay his last respects?
Does he return? Oh Gideon, speak it plain.
Tell me my brother has come home at last.

GIDEON
[Taking a slow step forward.]
Elara. Lady. There is no easy way.
No gentle phrase to blunt the coming blow.
Orion… is not here. He will not come.

ELARA
[Turning to face him, her eyes wide with disbelief.]
Not come? To see his brother to the earth?
To stand beside me in this endless dark?
What madness holds him? Is his mind undone?
Did Cassian’s victory shatter him so deep
He cannot face the world, or face his home?
I must go to him. I will bring him back.
His duty is to be here. Not to hide.

GIDEON
It is not madness, nor a want of love,
That keeps him from you. It is Cassian’s will.
And the Duke’s decree. The price of his survival.
He has been banished. Sent away from here.
To live, he had to leave all that he loves.

ELARA
[Her voice a whisper, then rising in strength.]
Banished? No. No, that cannot be the truth.
You lie. You must be lying. For what cause?
He fought a duel sanctioned by the law.
He lost. His life was forfeit, not his home.
Cassian showed him mercy, let him live.
Why would he add an exile to the gift?

GIDEON
It was no gift. It was a poisoned cup.
To let him live here, broken and disgraced,
A constant, living trophy of his victory…
That was the mercy Cassian first designed.
But Orion chose a different path. He chose
The open sea, the stranger’s distant shore,
Instead of gilded chains and public shame.
He left before the dawn. I saw him go.

ELARA
[She stumbles back, as if struck, and leans against a heavy table.]
He left. Without a word. Without a look.
He left me here alone. With all this… this.
He left our brother to an empty grave.
And he left me. Oh, gods. He left me, too.
How could he be so cruel? To save his pride,
He sacrifices me to solitude.
First Lucian, stolen by a villain’s sword.
And now Orion, stolen by his own.

GIDEON
He did it for his life, not for his pride.
And for your sake, he thought. He could not bear
To see you watch him waste away in sorrow,
A ghost within these walls that once were his.
He thought his absence was a cleaner wound
Than his sad presence would have been, a sore
That would not heal, but fester day by day.
And in his heart, he did not say farewell.
He carries you with him. Your memory
Is the one compass that will guide his ship.

ELARA
What comfort is a memory to me?
I cannot speak to it, or touch its hand.
It cannot hold me when the nightmares come.
He is gone. It is a simple, awful fact.
And you… you helped him. You stood on the shore
And watched him sail away into the mist.
You were his friend, and you abetted this.
You were my friend, and helped to break my heart.

GIDEON
He asked me to. He begged me, as a friend.
And he made me swear an oath. A solemn vow.
That I would watch you. That I would not let
The chill of sorrow settle in your soul.
He charged me to protect you in his stead,
To be the brother that he cannot be.
And he will write. He promised he will write.
So you will know he lives, and where he fares.

ELARA
[Looking up, a flicker of something in her eyes.]
He will write? Truly? That was not a lie
You tell a grieving woman for her peace?

GIDEON
It is the truth. The one thread you can hold.
He will send word to me, and I will bring it,
A secret message from a distant world,
To let you know the sun still shines for him.
And you, my lady, you must live for that.
And for yourself. You are the last bright flame
Of this great house. You must not let it fail.
You must burn on, for Lucian’s memory,
And for Orion’s hope of a return.

ELARA
[She straightens up, pulling a cloak of dignity around her grief.]
A hope. It is a thin and meagre thing.
But it is better than the void I felt.
I will endure. We are not a house of reeds,
To bend and break in the first passing storm.
I will go to the temple. I will see
My brother laid to rest. I will stand tall,
And Cassian will not see a single tear.
But you, Gideon. You have made a promise.
Do not forget it. Do not let me be
Forgotten, in this cold and silent hall.

GIDEON
I will not. On my life, I will be here.
A friend. A messenger. A constant guard.
Until he comes again.

ELARA
Until he comes.
[She turns and walks from the room, her back straight, her steps measured. GIDEON watches her go, the weight of his promise settling upon him like a shroud.]

SCENE II

[A private chamber in CASSIAN’s manor. The room is richly appointed, with maps on the walls and a large, polished desk. The morning sun streams in, illuminating motes of dust in the air. CASSIAN stands before a crackling fire, though the day is not cold. He holds a steel gauntlet in his hand, turning it over and over. VALERIUS, a man of middle years with a soldier’s bearing and a cynical eye, stands near the desk.]

VALERIUS
The city mourns for Lucian. As is right.
The Duke has sent his formal condolences.
Your family’s honour has been satisfied.
Your brother’s name is cleansed. Your enemy
Is broken, shamed, and banished from the realm.
By any measure, this is victory, my lord.
A perfect, clean, and absolute success.
Why do you wear a face that suits defeat?

CASSIAN
Because a rat has slipped the trap. A loose thread hangs
Upon the tapestry of this revenge.
It mars the whole design. He should be here.
I should be able to walk through the square
And see him, see his downcast eyes, his shame.
I wanted him to breathe the air of my success,
To choke upon it with each passing day.
This exile… this is his escape, not his punishment.

VALERIUS
My lord, with respect, the man is dispossessed.
He has no land, no title, and no coin
Beyond what little he could carry with him.
He is a beggar in a foreign land.
A man like that, born to such privilege,
Will not survive a year. He is a ghost already.
Forget him. He is less than nothing now.

CASSIAN
Nothing? A man with a famous name, a sword,
And a heart full of hatred for me is not nothing.
He is a seed. And if that seed should find
A fertile ground in some rival city’s court,
It could grow into a tree of poison thorns.
A symbol for my enemies to rally to.
No. He should have been killed. Or he should be here,
Where I can watch him, hold him on a leash.
This in-between state, this freedom in disgrace,
It does not sit well with me. It feels… unfinished.

VALERIUS
What would you have me do? Send men to hunt him?
The sea is wide. He could be anywhere.
To chase a ghost is a fool’s errand, my lord.
It costs much gold and brings but little gain.
The Duke was pleased to have the matter closed.
To reopen it would be to question his decree.

CASSIAN
The Duke. The Duke is old. He speaks of balance,
As if justice were a scale for weighing grain.
He sees two noble houses in a feud,
And thinks that one dead son, one banished son,
Makes the scales level. I see a chance, Valerius.
A chance to prove that my house, my authority,
Is the true weight that settles this city’s fate.
Orion’s family were old rivals. Their power
Was a slow check upon our own ambitions.
Now, they are gone. A girl remains, a name.
Their lands, their holdings, lie in the Duke’s trust.
Ripe for the picking, when the time is right.

VALERIUS
The girl, Elara. She is her brother’s heir.
To move against her would be seen as… cruel.
Unseemly, after all that has occurred.
The people have a fondness for a tragic maid.

CASSIAN
The people’s fondness is a summer breeze.
It blows where it will, and then it is gone.
I am concerned with things of more substance.
Power. Legacy. The shape of what’s to come.
And there is one small stone still in my shoe.
This scholar’s son. This Gideon.

VALERIUS
The witness? The boy who stood with Orion?
He is of no account. A bookish child.
He has no faction, no wealth, no sword arm.

CASSIAN
He has a tongue. And he has loyalty.
A dangerous combination. He was there.
He saw Orion leave. He knows the how, the when.
He denied me the satisfaction of the kill,
By urging mercy on his craven friend.
And now he hovers round the sister, Elara.
A would-be guardian, a whisper in her ear.
I do not like it. Such men are like moss.
They find a crack in the stone, and they grow,
And in a decade, they can split the wall.

VALERIUS
What is your will? I can have him watched.
Or I could have him meet with an accident.
A slip beside the docks, a heavy purse,
A moonless night. These things are easily done.

CASSIAN
No. Not yet. That would be too coarse a deed.
It would give truth to any lies he’s told.
Bring him to me. I wish to speak with him.
I want to look into his scholar’s eyes
And see if he is fool enough to be a threat,
Or wise enough to be a useful tool.
I want to know what promises he made
To his fled friend. I want to know what secrets
He holds within that quiet, clever head.
Send for him. After the funeral. Let him sit
A while with grief. It makes a man compliant.

VALERIUS
It shall be done, my lord.
[VALERIUS bows and exits. CASSIAN turns back to the fire, tossing the gauntlet onto the mantelpiece with a loud clang.]

CASSIAN
A different story, the boy said. Indeed.
The story is not over. I will write the end.
And every character will play the part I choose.
Or they will be written out. For good.

SCENE III

[The private council chambers of DUKE ALARIC. The room is old and austere, dominated by a large table of dark, ancient wood. Tapestries depicting historical victories hang on the stone walls. DUKE ALARIC, a man in his sixties, grey-haired but still powerful, sits at the head of the table. Across from him sits his wife, the DUCHESS HELENA, a woman of quiet grace and intelligence.]

DUKE ALARIC
And so the blood is washed away with tears,
And the affair is concluded. One is dead,
The other banished. The city can resume
Its peaceful commerce. The feud is at an end.
It is a harsh arithmetic, I know,
But it is the one that keeps the state in balance.

DUCHESS HELENA
You call this balance, husband? I call it a wound,
Poorly stitched up, that’s sure to fester on.
We have lost two good sons from noble houses.
Lucian, the bright and hopeful, in his grave.
Orion, proud and honest, cast adrift.
And for what cause? A point of petty pride,
Fanned into flame by Cassian’s ambition.
He has what he desires: a rival humbled,
And his own path to power cleared of stones.

DUKE ALARIC
Cassian is ambitious. It is the nature
Of men like him. A lion must be a lion.
It is my task not to unmake the beast,
For that is nature’s work, but to ensure
His hunting grounds are kept within their bounds.
This duel, this ancient law of trial by combat,
It served its purpose. It contained the rage
That might have spilled into the open streets
And drawn in other houses, other swords.
It was a spectacle, a public purge.

DUCHESS HELENA
It was a butchery we gave our blessing to.
I think of Elara. A girl, alone,
Her brother lost to death, her other lost
To the cold sea. What justice is there for her?
She is the true victim of this balancing.
And Cassian will not leave her house in peace.
You know his nature. He will circle it,
Like a patient wolf, until he finds a way
To claim its lands, its wealth, its very name.

DUKE ALARIC
I am her guardian by law. The lands are safe
Beneath my seal until she comes of age
Or takes a husband. Cassian knows this.
He will not dare to move against my word.
He plays a longer game. He seeks to be
My chosen heir, should heaven not have blessed
Our own house with a son. He builds his strength,
His popularity, his reputation
As a strong arm that brings decisive ends.

DUCHESS HELENA
And you allow it. You see the danger there,
Yet you do little to impede his rise.
Why, Alaric? Why do you indulge a man
Whose heart is iron and whose soul is ice?

DUKE ALARIC
Because the city needs an iron hand.
My time grows short. My flesh begins to fail.
I see the threats that gather at our gates.
The pirates on the coast, the desert tribes,
The shifting loyalties of our trade allies.
I need a man who is not afraid of blood.
I need a Cassian, much as I might wish
For an Orion, or a gentle Lucian.
The times we live in do not favour gentle men.
And yet… this business with the banished son…
It troubles me. Cassian’s rage should be sated.
That it is not, that he still broods on it,
Suggests a hunger that will not be filled.
An appetite that grows with every meal.

DUCHESS HELENA
Then starve it, husband. Before it turns on you.
Show favour to the girl. Make it be known
Her house is under your explicit shield.
Find her a strong and honourable match.
Build up another power to oppose him.
Do not let Cassian be the only heir
To all our city’s hopes. That is a path
That leads to tyranny. I see it plain.

DUKE ALARIC
You have a woman’s heart, and see the world
In shades of love and hate, of good and ill.
A ruler’s map has but one colour on it:
The grey of what is possible. And yet…
Your counsel has the ring of wisdom in it.
I will consider it. This Gideon.
The scholar’s son who helped Orion flee.
What do we know of him?

DUCHESS HELENA
Only that he is loyal to his friends.
A quality that seems in short supply.
Cassian will see that loyalty as a threat.
The boy may be in danger.

DUKE ALARIC
Then let us watch. Let us observe the game
That Cassian plays. And let us see how this
Small, bookish pawn moves on the playing board.
Sometimes the smallest pieces can decide
The outcome of the war. For now, we wait.
The city grieves. Let it believe its Duke
Is grieving with it. We shall send a gift,
A wreath of silver leaves, to Lucian’s tomb.
A public gesture. Politics is theatre.
And we must play our parts with solemn grace.

SCENE IV

[Several weeks later. The garden of ELARA’s house. The formal, mournful black has been replaced by more subdued colours. The garden, though well-tended, has a slightly melancholy air. Roses are in bloom. ELARA is carefully pruning a rose bush. GIDEON enters, carrying a leather-bound book.]

GIDEON
My lady. I hope I do not intrude.

ELARA
[She looks up and offers a small, sad smile.]
Gideon. You are welcome here. You know that.
This garden has become my only solace.
Here, things still grow. They do not know of death.
They only know the sun, the rain, the turning year.
These roses were his pride. Lucian’s. He said they grew
With such a fire because they were ashamed
Of their own thorns. A poet’s foolish thought.

GIDEON
A beautiful thought. And in their scent his memory
Will live each summer. A sweet remembrance.
Orion loved this garden too, I think.
He spoke of it. The wall he tried to climb
When you were children. The old apple tree.

ELARA
He did. He would sit there, on that stone bench,
And dream of battles, quests, and distant lands.
And now he has them. I wonder if he finds
They are as glorious as his boyhood dreams.
Is there still no word? Forgive me. I should not
Ask you each day. It is a cruel reminder
Of your own burden, and my impatience.

GIDEON
Ask every hour, if it brings you peace.
There is no word. Not yet. But do not fear.
The journey is a long one, and the posts
Are slow and often careless. A letter sent
Could take a season, or be lost entirely.
We must have patience. He will not forget.

ELARA
I know. It is my heart that is a child,
And wants its comfort now. My head knows better.
What have you brought me there? Another story?

GIDEON
[Handing her the book.]
The travels of a philosopher, who went
In search of the lost city of the sun.
He never found it. But the things he learned
About himself, and all the folk he met,
Became a greater treasure than the gold
He thought he sought. I thought it might… distract you.

ELARA
[Taking the book, her fingers tracing the cover.]
Thank you. You are kind. You think of me,
When you have your own studies, your own life.
You have not forsaken this sad house.
Many old friends have found their paths lead them
Away from these gates, since our star has fallen.

GIDEON
Their friendship was with your house’s fortune, then.
Not with its people. Let them go. They are
Not worth a moment of your thought. I stay
Because I made a promise. And because…
Because your company is no sad thing, Elara.
It is a quiet honour.

[A moment of shared understanding passes between them. It is broken by the sound of heavy footsteps. A GUARD in CASSIAN’s livery enters the garden. He is armed and has an arrogant bearing.]

GUARD
Gideon, son of Theron?

GIDEON
[Stepping slightly in front of ELARA.]
I am he. What business have you here?

GUARD
I bring a message from my lord, Lord Cassian.
He requests your presence at his manor.
Immediately. He does not wish to be kept waiting.

ELARA
[Her voice sharp with fear.]
Lord Cassian? What can he want with Gideon?
He has no part in this. Leave him alone.

GUARD
[Ignoring her, his eyes fixed on GIDEON.]
My lord’s requests are not debatable.
He has… questions for you. About the night
Lord Orion fled the city’s justice.
You will come with me now.

GIDEON
[His face is pale, but his voice is steady.]
And if I should refuse this kind request?

GUARD
[He rests a hand on the hilt of his sword.]
Then my request would take a harder form.
It is no choice. It is a summons, scholar.
Lord Cassian will have you. Willingly,
Or dragged in chains. The choice is yours to make.

GIDEON
[He looks at ELARA’s terrified face, then back to the guard. He makes a decision.]
There is no need for threats. I will attend him.
My lady, do not be afraid. It is
A simple matter. I will answer what he asks
And be returned before the evening falls.

ELARA
Gideon, no. It is a trap. He means you harm.
Do not go with him. I will send a plea
Unto the Duke himself! He will protect you.

GIDEON
There is no time. And to refuse would show
A guilt I do not have. Courage, Elara.
I will be safe. I am a humble man,
No threat to one so powerful as him.
[To the GUARD.]
Lead on. I follow you.

[The GUARD grins, a cold, predatory expression. He turns and strides out of the garden. GIDEON gives ELARA one last look, a look of reassurance he clearly does not feel, and then he follows. ELARA is left alone, clutching the book to her chest, her eyes wide with a new and specific terror.]

[Curtain.]

ACT TWO

SCENE ONE

[The lights come up on a dusty, shadowed study. Books are piled high, not with the neat care of a scholar’s collection, but with the air of long neglect. A large chair sits near a cold hearth, and in it sits LORD VALERIUS, ELARA’s father. He is a man who was once powerful, and the memory of it clings to him like his expensive but faded robe. He is wrapped in a heavy blanket, a gnarled walking stick resting against the arm of his chair. ELARA enters in a rush, the book Gideon gave her still in her hand. Her face is a mask of panic.]

ELARA
Father! You must hear me. There is danger.

LORD VALERIUS
[Without turning, his voice is dry as autumn leaves.]
There is always danger, child. It is the air
We have been forced to breathe these bitter years.
It seeps beneath the doors and cracks the stones.
Has some new creditor grown bold? Or does
The roof protest the rain with further leaks?
Speak plainly. I have little taste for riddles.

ELARA
It is not the house. It is Gideon.
Lord Cassian’s men have taken him away.

[LORD VALERIUS turns his head slowly. His eyes, though tired, are sharp and intelligent. He assesses his daughter’s fear.]

LORD VALERIUS
Cassian. So, the wolf grows tired of circling
And comes at last to test the weakened fence.
Tell me precisely what occurred. Leave out
No word, no gesture. In such things, the seeds
Of motive can be found.

ELARA
A guard arrived. He said Lord Cassian wished
To ask him questions, at his manor. Now.
He spoke of the night Orion… the night he left.
Gideon refused at first, for my sake, but
The man laid hand upon his sword. He said
It was a summons, not a kind request.
He said he would be dragged in chains if he
Did not comply. And so he went, to spare
Us further shame. He told me not to fear.
But oh, my father, his own face was white
With it. Cassian means to harm him. I
Can feel it. He uses Gideon to strike
At us.

LORD VALERIUS
[He looks into the cold hearth as if seeing a vision there.]
He does. The boy is but a pawn, a tool
To pry the last few secrets from our house.
Cassian believes the scholar knows the path
Your brother took. He seeks to find Orion
And drag him back to face a traitor’s death,
To see our name extinguished in the mud.
He will not rest until the lion’s skull
Is bleached and broken, and the jackal rules
The empty plains.

ELARA
Then we must act. I will send word. A plea
Sent to the Duke himself! He is a man
Of justice. He was your friend, in better days.
He will remember you. He will not let
An innocent be tormented for a feud
That is not his.

LORD VALERIUS
[A bitter, hollow laugh escapes him.]
The Duke? The Duke is a fine statue, child.
He stands upon his pedestal and lets
The pigeons of the court attend him. Cold,
And deaf, and made of stone. His power now
Is but a shadow of his title. Men
Like Cassian hold the coin, they hold the steel.
They lend the Duke his grandeur, and in turn
He grants them leave to do his dirtier work.
A plea from this house would be intercepted
Long before it reached his gilded desk.
And if it did? He would but pass it on
To his most trusted man, his new right hand…
Lord Cassian.

[ELARA’s face falls. The hope drains from her.]

ELARA
Then there is no one. No one to appeal to.
We are alone.

LORD VALERIUS
We have been so since the day your brother chose
His honour over his obedience.
This is the price of that brave, foolish choice.
Cassian will not kill the scholar. Not yet.
A dead man tells no tales. He will apply
A pressure. Questions first, then threats. And then…
Well, Cassian’s cellars have a fearsome name.
They say his torturers are artists in their craft.
They can unmake a man and leave him breathing,
A vessel only, waiting to be filled
With any words his master wishes.

ELARA
[She shudders, clutching the book tighter.]
No. Gideon is strong. His mind is keen.
He will not break.

LORD VALERIUS
Every man breaks, Elara. It is just
A matter of finding the proper lever.
A scholar’s hands, so soft and used to paper…
They are not made for iron, or for fire.
They will threaten him. They will promise him
A life of comfort if he speaks the words
They wish to hear. They will tell him you are next.
That is the lever Cassian will choose.
Your safety, offered for your brother’s life.

ELARA
He would not betray Orion. He would not
Betray me. I know it.

LORD VALERIUS
You know the man. You do not know the beast
That cowers in us all, when the cage door
Is rattled. Do not place your faith in what
A man might do when standing in the sun.
Wait ‘til you see him shivering in the dark.
But your belief… it does you credit. It
Is the last coin of our old fortune. Spend it
With care.

ELARA
Then what are we to do? Sit here and wait
Until they send him back, a broken thing?
Or do not send him back at all? I will not.
I cannot.

LORD VALERIUS
[He grips the head of his walking stick, his knuckles white.]
There is nothing you can do. You are a girl,
The daughter of a fallen house. To go
To Cassian’s gate would be to walk directly
Into the spider’s web. He would be glad
To have you. Another piece to use against
The ones you love. Stay here. Be safe. Be silent.
That is the only shield we have remaining.

ELARA
A shield of silence? Father, that is not
A shield. It is a tomb. I will not be
Buried alive while my friends suffer for me.
If the Duke is a statue, and the lords
Are wolves, then I must find a different path.
There must be someone in this city still
Who values truth. Who still remembers you
As you once were.

LORD VALERIUS
[His voice softens, filled with a deep and weary sadness.]
The men who valued me are old like me,
Or gone. Or they have learned the bitter art
Of turning with the wind. Do not mistake
The world that is for the world that ought to be.
Be careful, Elara. Your heart is good.
It is a fragile thing to carry through
A wilderness of stone. Do not expose it.

ELARA
I will not. I will cloak it well. But I
Will not let fear dictate my every move.
Gideon walked into that monster’s hall
For me. I must do something now for him.

[She turns, her back straight with a new, desperate resolve. She exits the study, leaving LORD VALERIUS alone in the growing shadows, his eyes fixed on the empty doorway.]

[Curtain.]

SCENE TWO

[The scene is a spacious, richly appointed chamber in Lord Cassian’s manor. The walls are dark, polished wood, hung with severe-looking portraits of ancestors. A large fire burns in a stone fireplace, casting flickering shadows. The room is dominated by a heavy desk. GIDEON stands in the center of the room, flanked by two impassive GUARDS. He has clearly been kept waiting for some time. LORD CASSIAN enters. He is an impeccably dressed man of middle years, with a calm, almost gentle demeanor that seems at odds with his reputation. He moves with a predator’s unhurried grace.]

CASSIAN
Gideon, son of Theron. Forgive me.
An unexpected matter with the port
Required my full attention. I do hope
My men were not… discourteous to you.
They are soldiers. Subtlety is not
A virtue that they cultivate. Please.

[He gestures to a chair near the fire. GIDEON does not move. The guards remain at his side.]

GIDEON
I am not tired, my lord. I prefer to stand.

CASSIAN
As you wish. A stoic choice. Admirable.
[He walks to a small table, pouring two glasses of deep red wine from a decanter.]
It is a scholar’s name you bear, is it not?
I confess, I have a certain fondness
For men of letters. Such a clean pursuit,
The chase for knowledge. It is orderly.
Unlike the grubby business of the state,
Which is a constant war of wills and wants.
Wine? It is a fine vintage from the south.

GIDEON
My throat is not dry, my lord. Thank you.

CASSIAN
[He smiles, taking a slow sip from his own glass.]
No, I imagine not. The cool night air…
Though fear, I’m told, can parch a man’s tongue quite
Effectively. Let us not fence with words.
We are both men who value clarity.
You are a friend to the House of Valerius.
A tutor to the girl, a companion
To the disgraced and fugitive Lord Orion.
Is this a fair assessment of your role?

GIDEON
I serve Lady Elara, yes. I try
To offer what small knowledge I possess.
Lord Orion was my friend.

CASSIAN
Was. An interesting choice of tense. As if
Your friendship is a thing of history.
Perhaps it is. Loyalty is a current,
It shifts its course depending on the tides
Of fortune. And the tide has long since fled
From Valerius’s shore, has it not?
Leaving nothing but the cracked mud of old debts
And the dry rot of a forgotten name.

GIDEON
Friendship, my lord, is not a weather vane
To turn with every passing political
Breeze. It is a root. It holds its ground.

CASSIAN
A charming sentiment. And a foolish one.
Roots can be severed. Ground can be dug up.
I am interested in the night Orion
Fled from this city. He defied the Duke.
He struck a captain of the city watch.
He aided a known spy from the West Reach.
These are the facts. He is a traitor. And
Those who give aid and comfort to a traitor
Share in that treason. You understand the law?

GIDEON
I am a scholar of history, not law.
But I understand the principle of justice.

CASSIAN
[He sets his glass down, his voice losing its silken edge, becoming as hard as iron.]
Good. Then you understand the situation.
I have a witness. A stable boy who saw
You meet with Orion near the northern gate
An hour before he vanished. Saw you press
A purse of coins into his hand. Saw you
Point out the old, unguarded drover’s path
That leads into the Whisperwood.

GIDEON
[His face remains impassive, but a flicker of alarm shows in his eyes.]
Your witness is mistaken. Or he lies.
I was in the library that night.
All night. Lady Elara can attest.

CASSIAN
Ah, yes. The girl. Her word against a boy’s
Who has no cause to lie. And whose will be
Believed? The desperate daughter of a traitor,
Or a simple servant of the city?
The scales of justice are so easily tipped,
My friend. A little pressure here, a word
Of promise there. You see my point, I trust.
I do not need your confession. I have it.
I can present my proof before the Duke
Tomorrow morning. You will be condemned.
Treason by fellowship. The sentence is
Unpleasant. And it is slow.

GIDEON
If you have what you need, then why am I
Here in your study, and not in a cell?
Why offer me your wine and conversation?

CASSIAN
[A genuine, cold smile touches his lips.]
Because you are more useful to me living.
Because I am a reasonable man.
I do not care about a minor scholar.
Your life or death is but a footnote in
The city’s ledger. But Orion… he
Is a loose thread in the tapestry of state.
A symbol for the discontented fools
Who dream of yesterday. I want him.
I want to know where he has gone. I want
To know who shelters him. And you will tell me.

GIDEON
I cannot tell you what I do not know.

CASSIAN
Can you not? I think you can. I think you are
The keeper of their secrets. You are the one
They trusted. The quiet man no one would watch.
So I will make this simple for you, Gideon.
I offer you a choice. A crossroad.
Down one path lies a cell. Pain. Obscurity.
And an ugly end in a public square.
Down the other… lies a future.
Tell me what I want to know. Swear your service,
Your loyalty, to me. I can be a
Generous patron. A place within the court.
Access to the Duke’s own private archive.
Gold, status, safety. All the things you lack.
A warm fire. Good wine. The respect of men.
All for a few small words. The location of
A man who is already politically dead.
What is his life compared to yours?

GIDEON
You speak of loyalty as if it were
A cloak, to be discarded when the sun
Shines bright, and put on when it suits the need.
You do not understand. A promise made
Is not a trivial thing. My word is all
A humble man like me can truly own.
And it is not for sale. Not for a place
At court, not for all the archives in the world.
And not for my own safety.

CASSIAN
[His eyes narrow. He studies Gideon for a long, silent moment.]
You disappoint me, scholar. I had thought
You were a man of intellect. A man
Who calculates the odds. You choose to burn
Yourself upon a pyre of sentiment
For a lost cause. For a frightened girl and her
Pathetic, crippled father. There is no
Honour in that. There is only foolishness.

GIDEON
There is a difference, my lord, between
The price of a thing and its value. You
Know the price of everything. A man’s life,
A promise, a loyalty. But you do not
Seem to understand the value of
A single one. So I will make my choice.
I will not tell you lies, and I will not
Betray my friends. I will tell you nothing.

CASSIAN
[The calm mask finally shatters. His voice is a low, dangerous growl.]
Nothing. You think you have that choice to make.
You think your silence is a shield. It is not.
It is a vacuum. And I will fill it
With screams. You love your books? Your hands that turn
The delicate pages? We will start with them.
We will see how well you hold to your high thoughts
When you can no longer hold a quill to write them.
Guards.

[The GUARDS step forward, each taking one of Gideon’s arms in a hard grip.]

CASSIAN
Take the scholar to the cells below.
The ones beneath the east wing. Let him hear
The river running. It will help him think
About the choice he’s made. And tell the master
Of the cells to wait for my instructions.
I want our guest to have some time to weigh
The value of his silence.

[The GUARDS begin to drag the unresisting GIDEON from the room. He does not look at CASSIAN, but keeps his eyes fixed ahead, his face pale but set.]

GIDEON
You will not break me.

CASSIAN
[To Gideon’s departing back.]
Oh, I know I will. Everyone breaks.
It is simply a matter of finding
The proper lever.

[GIDEON is pulled from the room. CASSIAN stands alone for a moment, then walks back to the table and picks up the untouched glass of wine meant for Gideon. He stares into its dark red depths.]

CASSIAN
Everyone.

[Curtain.]

SCENE THREE

[The scene shifts to a small, humble room in a tenement in the lower city. The space is clean and sparse. A single lantern provides a warm but meager light. ANYA, a woman of Elara’s age, is mending a man’s tunic. Her face shows a premature weariness. There is a soft, urgent knock at the door. Anya freezes, her needle poised in midair. The knock comes again, more insistent.]

ANYA
[In a hushed, nervous voice.]
Who is it? Who comes at this hour?

ELARA
[From outside the door, her voice muffled.]
Anya? It is Elara. Let me in.
Please. I am alone.

[Anya’s eyes widen. She quickly puts down her sewing, hesitates for a second, then unbolts the door. ELARA, wrapped in a simple dark cloak with the hood pulled up, slips inside. Anya quickly re-bolts the door behind her.]

ANYA
My lady! What are you doing here? In this
Part of the city? It is not safe for you.
If anyone should see you…

ELARA
I was careful. No one followed me.
Anya, I am sorry to impose.
To bring my trouble to your door. I had
Nowhere else I could turn.

ANYA
[She leads Elara further into the room, her voice a mixture of affection and fear.]
Trouble? Of course you can come here. Always.
But my husband, Marcus… he is on the watch.
If his commander knew that you were here,
That we had spoken… Times are different, Elara.
Friendships from the old days are a risk now.
Lord Cassian has eyes in every street.
And ears in every tavern.

ELARA
It is because of him I am here.
He has taken Gideon. The scholar.
His guards came to the garden this afternoon.
They took him to Cassian’s manor for questioning.
He has not returned. I fear the worst.

ANYA
[She makes a sign against evil, her hand trembling slightly.]
The scholar? But he is a gentle soul.
He has no part in politics or plots.
Why would a man like Cassian want with him?

ELARA
He thinks Gideon knows where my brother is.
He wants to use him to find Orion.
My father says… my father says that Cassian
Will hurt him. That he will break him to make him speak.
Anya, you hear things. Marcus walks the streets.
He talks to other watchmen. Have you heard
Any whispers? Any word of what goes on
Inside that viper’s nest?

ANYA
[She wrings her hands, looking toward the door as if expecting a patrol at any moment.]
We try not to listen to such whispers.
To know things now is to be in danger.
Marcus says the watch is not the same.
Men he has known for years now look away
When he approaches. There are new captains,
Men loyal only to Lord Cassian.
They offer silver for small bits of news.
A name overheard, a face seen in a crowd.
People are disappearing, my lady. Not
Just lords. Tailors, masons, dockhands. Anyone
Who ever raised a cup and toasted to
The health of your brave brother. It is a purge.
A quiet one, but it is happening.

ELARA
A purge. Then Gideon is just the latest.
Oh, I was a fool to let him go.
I am so helpless. My father is confined
To his chair, lost in his bitter memories.
The Duke will not listen. Our friends have all
Abandoned us. I am alone.

ANYA
You are not alone. You have me. It is not much.
A seamstress and her husband on the watch.
But what we have is yours.
[She thinks for a moment, her brow furrowed in concentration.]
Marcus did speak of something. A strange thing.
He said the taverns near the docks are full
Of angry men who drink and mutter treason
In the dark corners. Most are just hot air.
But there is one… The Drowned Man, it is called.
A foul place. He says that men go in,
But fewer men come out than should. And those
Who do, they walk with a new purpose.
He thinks it is a meeting place for those
Who have no love for the new order.
Soldiers who were dismissed. Merchants who were
Ruined by Cassian’s taxes. Men with nothing
Left to lose.

ELARA
The Drowned Man. A meeting place.
Perhaps they could help. Perhaps they would listen.

ANYA
[Grabbing Elara’s arm, her voice urgent.]
No, my lady, you must not! It is a trap,
More like. Or if it is not, it is a nest
Of desperate cutthroats. They would sell you to
Lord Cassian for the price of a new coat.
You cannot go to such a place. Promise me.

ELARA
What other choice do I have, Anya?
To sit and wait for news of Gideon’s death?
To watch as Cassian strangles my whole house
And everyone connected to it? No.
You said these men have nothing left to lose.
Then they are the only ones who might be brave
Enough to stand against him. I must try.
I must find them.

ANYA
It is madness. You are Lord Valerius’s
Daughter. Your face is known.

ELARA
[She pulls the hood of her cloak lower.]
In the dark of a tavern, I am just a shadow.
Tell me where to find this place. I will not
Bring you or Marcus into any harm.
Just point me on the path. That is all I ask.

[ANYA looks at ELARA’s determined face, the fear in her own eyes warring with her old loyalty. She sees the girl she grew up with, but also a woman she does not recognize, forged in grief and desperation. With a sigh of resignation, she nods.]

ANYA
It is by the old fish market, near the wharf
Where the foreign ships no longer dock.
It has a sign above the door, a ship
Sinking beneath the waves. You will smell it
Before you see it. Elara… be careful.
The sea is not the only thing that drowns
A person in that part of the city.

ELARA
Thank you, Anya. You are a truer friend
Than all the lords who dined at my father’s table.
I will not forget this.

[ELARA gives ANYA’s hand a quick, grateful squeeze. She turns and, without another word, unbolts the door and slips out into the night. ANYA rushes to the door, watching her friend’s cloaked figure disappear into the dark, winding alleyways.]

ANYA
[Whispering to the empty night.]
May the old gods watch over you. You will
Surely need them now.

[She closes and bolts the door, leaning against it, her small room suddenly feeling like the last safe place in a city sinking into darkness.]

[Curtain.]
