LEON
I pray thee, cousin, cool this fever in thy blood. The sun is high, and tempers rise with it.

VALERIUS
My temper, Leon, is mine own to govern. But his! That Cassian's pride doth strut about the town as if he bought the cobbles with his father's coin. He gave me such a look as one might give a dog that fouls his path.

LEON
A look is but a puff of empty air. Let it pass by and trouble you no more. To catch at every shadow of a slight is to make a monster of your own unease.

VALERIUS
A shadow? Nay, it had substance, Leon. It had the weight of all his house's scorn. A house built up on usury and lies, whilst ours... but see, the braggart comes himself. And drags his sister, fair Elara, with him, to perfume the air he pollutes.

Enter Cassian and Elara.

CASSIAN
Look, sister, where the city's malcontents do gather. What sad conspiracy is hatch'd today? To whine the sun from out its very sphere?

VALERIUS
We hatch no plots, but speak of honest truth. A subject that your tongue is stranger to.

CASSIAN
My tongue speaks what my honour bids it speak. Thine clatters like a pot without a lid, full of hot steam and signifying naught.

ELARA
Brother, forbear. Good Master Valerius, we seek no quarrel here.

VALERIUS
Then bid your brother hold his saucy tongue, or I shall find a way to quiet it. He wears his wit as a dull blade, lady; it hacks and bruises but has got no point.

CASSIAN
No point? Thou art a judge of points, I hear. The point of shame, when last your father's lands were forfeit for a gambling debt? Or the point of flight, when last we met at tourney?

LEON
Sirs, in the name of peace! This is a public place. Let not the idle gossips of the town make sport of your discord.

CASSIAN
I care not for the gaze of common folk. I speak to him, this hollow man of straw, this echo of a noble name. Draw, if thou hast the courage of thy boasts.

VALERIUS
Now by the heavens, thou hast said the word that I did thirst to hear. [_Draws his sword._]

ELARA
No, Cassian! Valerius! Hold your hands!

LEON
Put up your swords! This madness will be death!

CASSIAN
[_Drawing his sword._] One of us shall find it so. Come, sir, let's see if your steel is sharper than your wit.

[_They fight._]

ELARA
Help! In the city's name, part them! O, God!

LEON
Valerius, cousin, stop! He is too rash!

[_Valerius parries a thrust and wounds Cassian. Cassian stumbles back, clutching his side._]

CASSIAN
I am hurt. A pox upon your sudden steel. My breath... it runs away from me. Sister...

[_Cassian falls._]

ELARA
Brother! O, no, no, no! Sweet Cassian! Speak to me! His eyes are glass. O, you have murdered him! Murderer!

VALERius
I did not mean... he gave the challenge first. His tongue did cut me deeper than my sword cut him.

LEON
The reason will not save you from the law! Valerius, fly! Before the watch is raised! The city gates are nigh. Away, be gone!

VALERIUS
And leave this bloody ruin at my feet? Leave her to weep upon my hasty work? I cannot.

LEON
Then you are lost. Your life is forfeit now.

Enter Lord Arden with Guards.

FIRST GUARD
Make way for Lord Arden! What foulness is here?

LORD ARDEN
A body on my city's stones? Whose sword hath made this grim account? Speak, who began this fray?

ELARA
[_Weeping over Cassian's body._]
My lord, that man. That Valerius. He set upon my brother with a viper's spite, for but a trifling word. He is the serpent in our garden here, and with his venom hath my brother slain.

LEON
My lord, she speaks from grief, and not from truth. It was a quarrel, hot on either side. Young Cassian gave the challenge, and Valerius but answered it in kind.

LORD ARDEN
In kind? With death? Doth honour live in wounds? Did I not make decree against this strife, this petty war of pride that spills the blood which should be saved for worthier enterprise? Valerius, your sword is red with Cassian's life. What say you in your own defence?

VALERIUS
He slander'd me, my lord. He call'd my courage into question, and my house's name. There is no man of noble blood who could abide such insult and not draw.

LORD ARDEN
More noble to have walked away and lived. Because your blood is hot, must mine run cold to see such waste? I'll make an end of it. For this offence, though Cassian's tongue was sharp, your hand was sharper. Therefore, from our walls, we banish thee.

ELARA
Banishment? My lord, it is too kind! His life should pay for my dear brother's life!

LORD ARDEN
His life is spared, for he was challen'd first. But if he's found within this city's bounds when next the sun doth rise, that life is mine. Away with him. Let this sad lesson be the last your houses teach this wretched town. Your hate has cost enough.

VALERIUS
My lord, I do accept your judgment. A life in exile is a life still, which is more than I have left to Cassian. Lead on. I would not look upon her face again, lest her tears burn my very soul to ash.

[Exit Guards with VALERIUS.]

LORD ARDEN
See that he is taken past the gates and given provision for a day. No more. The sentence stands. If he returns, he dies. Now, good Leon, see to the lady. Her grief is a tempest that needs a gentle harbour. And you, sirs, bear this poor gentleman to his father's house. The city mourns with them.

[The Guards lift CASSIAN's body.]

ELARA
Do not touch him! Do not handle him with your coarse soldier's hands! He was a jewel, too precious for this world of mud and iron. O, Cassian, your blood cries out from these unfeeling stones. It cries for justice, not for this weak compromise, this holiday that Arden calls a doom.

LEON
Elara, come away. The night grows cold. There's nothing here but sorrow's bitter dregs.

ELARA
Cold? I shall never feel the cold again. My heart is a furnace, and vengeance is the fuel. Lord Arden!

LORD ARDEN
Lady, my decree is made. Do not test my patience, which is worn as thin as a miser's coat.

ELARA
You speak of patience? I have a brother slain! His breath, which was but moments since a song, is now a silence that will scream forever in my ears! And you send his butcher on a pleasant walk, to see new lands and breathe a freer air? You call this justice?

LORD ARDEN
I call it law. It is a sterner thing than justice, and a kinder thing than vengeance. It serves the city, not the private heart. I am sorry for your loss, more than my words can frame. But I will not see another body fall to glut your sorrow. Go home. And pray.

[Exit LORD ARDEN and his remaining Guards.]

ELARA
Pray? I'll pray to darker gods than yours, my lord. I'll pray for storms to vex the sea he travels, for thorns to choke the path he walks. I'll pray his bread turns into dust, his water into brine. He is banished from these walls, but not from my remembrance.

LEON
These are but words, Elara. They are the wind that fans a fire, but cannot quench the burning in your breast. Let me help you to your father's house. Lord Tyrell will have need of you, and you of him.

ELARA
My father. O, Leon, what news is this to bring a father's ear? That his bright son, his hope, his morning star, is now a clod of earth? That his swift sword, which should have guarded us, lies broken on the ground?

[The Guards have fashioned a makeshift litter and place CASSIAN's body upon it.]

FIRST GUARD
My lady, we must. The city watch will clear the square.

ELARA
Then go. Take him. Take the ruin of my world and lay it at my father's feet. I shall follow. I must learn to walk in this new world you've made for me. A world without a sun.

[The Guards bear the body of CASSIAN offstage. LEON remains with ELARA.]

LEON
Let me attend you. The way is dark.

ELARA
It is. And I must learn to see in it. You are his cousin, Leon. And Valerius is yours. Your blood is split between two warring streams. How can I trust a man who is half my foe?

LEON
I am no one's foe, and friend to peace. I tried to part them. By all the saints, you saw I tried.

ELARA
You tried. And you failed. As all good men do, when faced with wolves. Leave me. Your presence is a comfort I disdain. My grief is mine alone. It is my sword and shield, and I will wear it till my task is done. Go to your kinsman's house, and tell them that Elara of the House of Tyrell has made a vow tonight.

LEON
What vow? O, do not speak it. Let it die unborn.

ELARA
I vow that since Lord Arden's law is lame, my justice shall be swift. Valerius may have fled the city walls, but he has not fled the earth. And there is no place on it, no cave, no peak, no distant shore, where he can hide from me. Now go.

[ELARA turns and walks slowly in the direction the body was taken. LEON watches her, his face a mask of sorrow and fear. He exits in the opposite direction. The stage is empty and silent for a moment. A bell tolls in the distance. The scene fades.]

[SCENE II]
[A great hall in the house of Lord Marius. The architecture is severe, the tapestries depicting battles and hunts. LADY ANYA, Valerius's mother, stands by a high window, looking out into the night. She is a woman of formidable composure.]

Enter LEON.

LADY ANYA
Leon. Your face is a letter I have long feared to read. Do not delay the message. Is my son alive?

LEON
He is, my lady. He breathes. He is unhurt.

LADY ANYA
Then the other is not. It is Cassian. I knew the boy's temper. It was a fire seeking straw.

LEON
He is dead. They fought in the main square. Valerius was provoked past bearing.

LADY ANYA
Provocation is the fool's excuse for folly. What of the law? Lord Arden was there, I hear.

LEON
He was. And he has passed his sentence. My lady... Valerius is banished from the city.

[LADY ANYA flinches, a barely perceptible tremor. She turns from the window.]

LADY ANYA
Banishment. So. He is alive, but he is lost to us. A ghost to haunt some foreign road. It is a kinder sentence than Lord Tyrell would have given, and a crueller one than death. For death is final, but this is a wound that will not close. Where is my husband?

LEON
I have not seen Lord Marius. I came directly here, to bring you word myself. To soften it, if such a blow can be.

LADY ANYA
You are a good man caught in a foul tide, Leon. I thank you for it. But there is no softening this. A son is banished. A blood feud, which had been sleeping, is now a raging beast again. Lord Tyrell will not let this rest. Arden's law is but a paper shield against that old man's hate.

Enter LORD MARIUS from another room. He is a grim, powerful man, older than his wife. He holds a letter.

LORD MARIUS
I have already heard. The city's tongue doth wag faster than a frightened dog's. A messenger from Arden's court brought me the news. My son, a killer and an exile. All for a puff of air, a foolish word.

LEON
My lord, Cassian's insults were as sharp as knives. He gave Valerius no choice.

LORD MARIUS
No choice? There is always choice. To walk away. To laugh. To be the bigger man and let the smaller cur go yapping home. But no. Our pride, this cursed pride of ours, it is a god that asks for blood. And our sons, they are the willing sacrifice.

LADY ANYA
What is done, is done, my lord. We cannot weep him back again. What must we do? Valerius is alone, cast out. He will need aid. He will need gold, and friends beyond the walls.

LORD MARIUS
He will have them. I will send riders forth tonight. They will find him, guard him, see him to the coast. He can take ship to the southern isles. He has an uncle there who will receive him. He will be safe.

LADY ANYA
Safe from the law, perhaps. But not from the House of Tyrell. Their memory is long, their patience is a hunter's trick. They will wait for years to have their vengeance. Elara... I know that girl. She has her father's iron in her soul.

LEON
She swore a vow tonight. To hunt him to the corners of the earth. Her grief has made her mad.

LORD MARIUS
Then let her rave. She is a girl. Her threats are smoke. It is her father I must watch. He will petition Arden, stir the council, use his wealth to turn the tide of favour. He will work within the law, until he finds a way to break it. I must go to the court. I must shore up my defences. Leon, you are a witness. You must speak what you saw. That Cassian gave the challenge. That my son fought in his defence.

LEON
I have already spoken it, my lord. It is the truth.

LORD MARIUS
The truth is often a poor weapon in these wars. It bends too easily. Go home, cousin. You have done enough for one night. Pray for our house. And for your own. For you are kin to both, and the lightning, when it strikes, cares not what tree it fells.

[Exit LEON.]

LADY ANYA
You are afraid. I have not seen you so since the great border wars.

LORD MARIUS
I am not afraid. I am weary. I have spent a lifetime building this house, this name. I have buried brothers, uncles, friends, all to feed that same foul god of pride. I thought we had seen the end of it. I thought this new generation, our son, their son... I thought they could be merchants, scholars, builders. But the old blood is too strong. It sings the same old, stupid, deadly song.

LADY ANYA
Then we must be strong as well. We are his parents. We will not let our son be hunted. Send your riders. Give them the swiftest horses. Tell them to ride as if the devil himself were at their heels. For I believe he is. And he wears Elara's face.

[LORD MARIUS nods, his face set like stone. LADY ANYA returns to the window, staring out into the darkness as if she could pierce it and see the road her son now walks. The scene fades.]

[SCENE III]
[The great hall in the house of LORD TYRELL. It is more opulent than the other, but now it is disordered. A litter bearing CASSIAN's shrouded body sits in the center of the room. LORD TYRELL, an old but fierce man, stands beside it, one hand on the shroud. ELARA is kneeling on the floor.]

LORD TYRELL
They bring him home to me upon a plank of wood. My son. My heir. The branch that should have grown when I was in the earth. They bring him home, and tell me that his killer takes a morning stroll to banishment. O, for the strength I had when I was young! I would have torn that palace down, stone by stone, and made Lord Arden eat the dust!

ELARA
Father, your grief is mine. My heart is broken with the selfsame blow.

LORD TYRELL
Grief? I am past grief. I am in a rage so pure it burns my tears away. This is not justice. It is an insult. It is a message from Lord Marius, sent through Arden's mouth. It says, "Our sons can kill your sons, and pay no price." It says, "Your house is fallen, Tyrell. Your power is a memory."

ELARA
Then we must send a message back. One written not in ink, but blood. I have already sworn it. Valerius will not live to see another year.

LORD TYRELL
An oath is easy. The deed is hard. The world is wide. And Lord Marius is cunning. His spies are everywhere. His gold can buy a thousand swords, a thousand lying tongues. While Valerius is alive, he is a living mockery of our name. He must be silenced. Not just for vengeance' sake. But for our house's honour.

ELARA
Give me the task. I will not fail. My mind is sharp, and my resolve is absolute. I will not sleep, I will not eat, I will not smile again until I know his corpse is cold.

LORD TYRELL
You are a woman. This is the work of men. Hard men.

ELARA
And are there any left? My brother, who was brave and bold, is dead. The men of your council wring their hands and speak of law. I have seen the fire in their eyes go out. But mine, father, mine burns brighter than the sun. My being a woman is a cloak. They will not suspect me. Lady Anya, Valerius's mother, she will watch you. She will expect you to send men with swords. She will not expect a daughter's quiet, patient, deadly hate.

LORD TYRELL
[Looks from his daughter to the body of his son.]
He loved you well. He said your mind was quicker than his own. He said you had a lion's heart inside a woman's form. Perhaps he saw the truth of you. What is your plan?

ELARA
Marius will send his men to guard his precious son. They will be looking for pursuers on the road, for soldiers in his livery. They will not look for a simple man, a merchant or a pilgrim, who joins his party for a league or two, and shares a cup of wine beside a fire.

LORD TYRELL
Poison. A coward's tool.

ELARA
It is a wise tool, when the enemy is stronger. Why risk a loyal soldier in a hopeless fight? A drop of cantarella in his cup, and he will sleep a sleep from which he never wakes. It is clean. It is quiet. It leaves no trace but death. We can say the grief and hardship of his exile broke his heart. Who would gainsay it?

LORD TYRELL
And who would be this merchant? This pilgrim with a poison'd cup?

ELARA
I know a man. Marcus. His father served you in the wars. You saved his life. He owes our house a debt. He is not a man of noble birth, and so will not be marked. He has a common face. He can be a dozen different men and none of them himself. He is loyal. And he is poor. He will do it for the right amount of gold.

LORD TYRELL
Marcus. I remember him. A quiet man. He sees everything and says but little. A useful quality. Yes. He might do it. But the risk... if he is caught, he will confess our names upon the rack.

ELARA
He will not be caught. I will instruct him. I will give him the means. I will pay him half the gold now, and half when I have proof the deed is done. Trust me in this, father. Let my hate be the weapon that you wield.

LORD TYRELL
[After a long silence, he looks at CASSIAN's body.]
All my hopes were in this silent clay. My future. The continuation of my name. Now all that's left is the past. And the settling of old scores. Very well. Send for this Marcus. Bring him to me tonight. We will give him his instructions, and his payment. And his poison. Let us see if a daughter's hate can do what a father's army cannot.

ELARA
It can. It will. Rest now, sweet brother. Your sleep will not be troubled long by your killer's waking breath.

[ELARA rises and kisses the shroud covering her brother's face. Her own face is calm, her eyes burning with a cold, clear light. The scene fades.]

[SCENE IV]
[A desolate road outside the city walls. It is just before dawn. VALERIUS is alone, walking. His clothes are plain, his sword is at his side. He looks back towards the unseen city.]

VALERIUS
There. The sky grows grey. The sun will rise, and I must not be found. Banished. The word is like a stone in the mouth. It has no taste, no meaning yet. I am the same man I was yesterday. My blood is still as warm, my limbs as strong. And yet I am a different creature. A thing to be cast out. A name to be reviled. And all for what? For honour. A word. A breath of air. Cassian, you fool. You hot-headed, glorious fool. You died for a word. And I am exiled for it. Did we win? Did either of us win? The worms have won your flesh. The road has won my future. I see you now, Elara. I see your eyes. Not with the fire of our dance, but with the ice of hate. I earned that hate. I must carry it. It is a heavier burden than this traveler's pack.

[He hears hoofbeats. He draws his sword and steps behind a tree. Two riders appear. They are armored and bear the crest of his family's house.]

FIRST RIDER
My lord Valerius!

[VALERIUS steps out, wary.]

VALERIUS
Who are you? How do you know my name?

FIRST RIDER
We are sent by your father, Lord Marius. I am Galen. This is Titus. He has commanded us to find you, and to be your guard.

VALERIUS
My guard? Or my gaolers?

SECOND RIDER (TITUS)
We are your servants, my lord. We are to see you safely to the port at Silver Creek. There is a ship prepared to take you to the southern isles. Your father's orders.

VALERIUS
My father works quickly. He has planned my future before I have even understood my present. And what if I do not wish to go to the southern isles? What if I wish to make my own way?

GALEN
My lord, the roads are not safe. Lord Tyrell's reach is long. His anger will not cool with the morning dew. It is your father's fear that he will send men after you. Not men of the law, but... others.

VALERIUS
Assassins. Let us use the proper word. He thinks Tyrell will have me murdered. And he is right. I saw Elara's face. That is a promise she will keep. Very well. I will not refuse your company. It seems my pride has cost me my freedom in more ways than one. Lead on to Silver Creek. The sooner I am gone from this cursed land, the better.

[GALEN offers VALERIUS his horse, but he refuses.]

VALERIUS
No. I was banished to walk. So I will walk. It is a fitting penance. Ride slowly. Be my eyes and ears. I will be the humble pilgrim, and you my watchful angels. A strange parade we make. The killer and his guards, fleeing from the ghost of the man he killed. On. Let us meet the rising sun.

[The two riders nod. They begin to move slowly down the road, and VALERius follows on foot, his head bowed, his shoulders slumped not with weariness, but with a weight that is invisible. The scene fades as they disappear down the long road.]

SCENE II

A FOREST ROAD. LATER THAT DAY.

The sun is lower, casting long, distorted shadows through the dense canopy of the ancient woods. The air is thick with the scent of damp earth and decay. The road has narrowed to little more than a dirt track, flanked by gnarled oaks and thick undergrowth. The slow, rhythmic clopping of the horses is the only sound, besides the rustle of unseen things in the leaves. VALERIUS walks between the two riders, his gaze fixed on the ground ahead. GALEN rides with a straight back, his eyes constantly scanning the trees. TITUS shifts in his saddle, his impatience a palpable thing.

TITUS
My lord, you must be weary. We have walked since dawn. There is no shame in taking a horse now. The penance has been paid for one day, surely.

VALERIUS
(Without looking up)
Weariness is a small price. I have traded my home for a long walk. It seems a bargain to me.

GALEN
Titus has a point, my lord. Not of shame, but of safety. We make slow progress. The light will fail us in another hour. We should make camp soon, or press on with more speed. The woods are not kind after dark.

VALERIUS
Then we will make camp. I have no wish to see the lights of Silver Creek tonight. Let it wait. Let it all wait.

TITUS
As you wish, my lord. We will share our rations. I trust the taste of salted beef and hard bread is not too far beneath your station.

GALEN
Titus.

The word is a quiet command. Titus falls silent, but his resentment hangs in the air. Valerius stops walking. The horses halt behind him. He finally looks up, first at Galen, then at Titus. His eyes are hollowed out by fatigue and something deeper.

VALERIUS
Your bitterness is earned, Titus. Speak your mind. Do not choke on it for my sake. I am not your lord in truth. I am a condemned man. A piece of cargo your master has paid you to deliver. So speak.

Titus looks at Galen, who gives a slight, almost imperceptible shake of his head. Titus ignores it.

TITUS
My brother served Lord Heston. He was a household guard. He died two years ago. Not in a great battle. Not defending the walls from invaders. He died in the courtyard, with a lord's rapier in his gut. Because one nobleman accused another of cheating at cards. An argument over a hand of kings and queens. My brother died for it. For their pride. He was a better man than both of them combined. He had a wife. A daughter.

VALERIUS
I see.

TITUS
Do you, my lord? Do you see what it is to be a tool in another man’s story? To have your life extinguished for a moment of their vanity? You and Lord Cassian, you fought for a woman’s honour. That is what they say. A noble cause. But Cassian is dead. And you are here. And the woman weeps. And men like me, and Galen, we are sent to clean up the mess. To guard the victor from the consequences of his glorious victory. So yes. Forgive me if I find your penance walk a little… self indulgent. Some men walk because they have no horse. Others because they choose a punishment that the rest of us must share.

A long silence stretches. Galen has his hand resting on the pommel of his sword, watching Valerius closely. Valerius does not look angry. He looks tired. Older.

VALERIUS
Your brother’s name?

TITUS
(Taken aback)
What?

VALERIUS
What was his name?

TITUS
Lucius. His name was Lucius.

VALERIUS
I am sorry for Lucius. And for you. You are right. This is self indulgent. It changes nothing. It brings no one back. It is the action of a boy who does not know what to do with the ruin he has made. But it is all I have. Tomorrow, I will ride. This last day of walking was for me. From now on, our speed is for you. For our safety.

Before Titus can respond, Galen stiffens in his saddle.

GALEN
Quiet.

He draws his sword. The sound of it leaving the scabbard is unnaturally loud in the sudden stillness.

VALERIUS
What is it?

GALEN
Listen.

Valerius and Titus fall silent. There is nothing. No birdsong. No rustle of leaves. The woods have gone dead quiet. An unnatural silence.

TITUS
I hear nothing.

GALEN
Exactly.
(To Valerius)
My lord. Your sword. Now.

Valerius draws his own blade. The feel of it in his hand is a sickness in his stomach. It feels like the same moment, the same duel, starting all over again.

GALEN
Titus, stay on the horse. Your height is an advantage. My lord, to my left. Back to back.

Suddenly, an arrow whistles out of the trees. It strikes Titus’s horse in the neck. The animal screams, a terrible, high pitched sound, and rears violently. Titus is thrown from the saddle, landing hard on the ground with a grunt of pain. The horse collapses, thrashing.

Out of the undergrowth, men emerge. Five of them. They are not soldiers. They are hard faced men in mismatched leather and steel, armed with crude axes and swords. They move with a predatory confidence. One, a large man with a livid scar across his face, points a notched blade at them.

SCARRED MAN
The lordling. Tyrell pays well for his head. The rest of you can die for free.

GALEN
Lord Tyrell’s dogs. I should have known. Stand firm!

Two of the brigands charge at Galen. He meets them with cool professionalism, his sword a blur of silver, parrying and turning their clumsy blows. Another two go for Titus, who has scrambled to his feet, his arm hanging at an odd angle. He holds his sword in his good hand, his face pale but determined.

Valerius is frozen. The clash of steel, the grunts of effort, the scent of blood from the dying horse. It all rushes back to him. He sees Cassian’s face, surprised, betrayed, as the point of his sword went in. His hand trembles.

The Scarred Man sees his hesitation. He grins, a cruel twisting of his lips, and advances on Valerius.

SCARRED MAN
What’s this? The great lord who killed Cassian Tyrell? You don’t look so grand now. You look like a frightened rabbit.

Valerius takes a step back. His feet feel like lead. His mind is a storm of guilt and horror. He is not a warrior. He is a duelist. An artist with a blade, they called him. This is not art. This is butchery.

SCARRED MAN
Your father’s gold can’t save you here.

He lunges. The move is fast but crude. Instinct, honed by years of training, takes over Valerius’s body even as his mind screams. He parries the blow, the force of it jarring his arm.

Meanwhile, Titus is in trouble. He manages to run one of his attackers through, but the other swings an axe, catching him on the shoulder. Titus cries out and stumbles back, his sword arm now useless. The brigand raises his axe for a final, killing blow.

GALEN
Titus!

Galen is occupied with his own two opponents, unable to help. He dispatches one with a swift thrust to the throat, but the other keeps him engaged.

Seeing Titus about to die snaps something in Valerius. The face of the brigand over Titus is not Cassian’s. It is a stranger’s face, twisted with bloodlust. And Titus is not an enemy. He is a man under his protection. A man who lost a brother to a lord’s pride.

A fire ignites in Valerius's chest, but it is not the hot fire of honour. It is a cold, desperate rage.

VALERIUS
No.

He shoves the Scarred Man back with a powerful kick, not waiting for an opening, but creating one. He doesn't fence. He fights. He ducks under a wild swing and drives his sword not into the man's chest, but deep into his thigh. The Scarred Man screams and drops to one knee.

Valerius pulls his blade free and spins, not towards his own attacker, but towards Titus's. He moves with a speed that surprises everyone. The brigand with the axe is still raising it, his back to Valerius. Valerius doesn't hesitate. He doesn't call a challenge. He plunges his sword into the man's back, right through the leather jerkin. The man gasps, a wet, final sound, and crumples on top of Titus.

Valerius stands over them, panting, the blood of a second man on his sword. He feels sick.

The remaining two brigands see this. Galen has just finished his opponent. The Scarred Man is on the ground, bleeding heavily. The odds have turned. With a shared look of panic, the last two turn and flee back into the forest.

The Scarred Man tries to crawl away, dragging his wounded leg. Galen walks over to him and places the point of his sword at the man’s throat.

GALEN
Who sent you? Was it Lord Tyrell himself?

SCARRED MAN
(Gasping)
A steward. A man in Tyrell’s colours. Paid us in gold. Said to make it look like a robbery.

GALEN
He will be disappointed.

Galen presses his sword down. The man convulses and then lies still. Galen cleans his blade on the dead man's tunic, his face grim and impassive.

Valerius stumbles over to Titus and helps pull the dead brigand off him. Titus is bleeding from his shoulder, his face slick with sweat.

TITUS
My arm... I think it’s broken.

VALERIUS
Hold still.

Valerius looks at his own hands. They are steady now. Covered in flecks of blood. He killed a man from behind. There was no honour in it. There was no glory. There was only survival. He feels a profound, chilling emptiness.

GALEN
(Approaching them)
We must get off the road. They may have others waiting. There’s an old watchtower marked on my map, a league from here. We can take shelter there for the night.
(He looks at Valerius, his expression unreadable)
You fought like a demon, my lord. You saved him.

VALERIUS
(His voice is a hollow whisper)
I am a demon. That is what this world has made of me. Let us go. The sooner we are in this tower, the better. I am tired of the trees watching me.

He tears a strip of cloth from his own tunic and begins to tightly bind Titus’s wounded shoulder, his movements efficient and detached, as if he is tending to a stranger. The last of the sunlight fades, and the forest plunges into a deep, unforgiving twilight.

SCENE III

A RUINED WATCHTOWER. NIGHT.

The interior of the tower is a circular stone room, open to the sky where the roof has long since collapsed. A small, carefully built fire crackles in the center, casting flickering shadows on the damp, moss-covered walls. The wind howls over the broken battlements, a lonely, mournful sound. GALEN is tending to TITUS’s injuries. He has set the broken arm in a crude splint and is now stitching the axe wound on his shoulder with a needle and thread from his kit. Titus sits rigidly, his jaw clenched, letting out a sharp hiss of pain with every stitch.

VALERIUS sits apart from them, near the crumbling doorway, staring out into the oppressive darkness. He has been meticulously cleaning his sword with an oiled rag for the better part of an hour. The blade is already spotless, but he continues the repetitive motion, back and forth, back and forth. His hands are no longer shaking. They are unnervingly still.

GALEN
There. It is not pretty, but it will hold. You were lucky. The blade was dull. It tore the flesh more than it cut it.

TITUS
(Through gritted teeth)
Some luck.

GALEN
You are alive. That is the only luck that matters on a day like this. Drink this. It will dull the pain and help you sleep.

He hands Titus a small leather flask. Titus takes it with his good hand and drinks deeply, shuddering as the raw spirit hits his throat. His eyes find Valerius in the shadows.

TITUS
My lord.

Valerius does not turn. He continues his work on the sword.

VALERIUS
I am not your lord. We settled that this afternoon.

TITUS
Valerius, then. I... I spoke out of turn. Before. My anger at the world, I aimed it at you. It was misplaced. You fought for us. You saved my life. A lesser man would have run.

Valerius finally stops his polishing. He sets the sword down carefully beside him, as if it were a venomous snake.

VALERIUS
A lesser man would not have killed Cassian Tyrell for a misplaced word at a banquet. A lesser man would not have turned his family’s name to ash for the sake of his own vanity. Do not mistake desperation for courage, Titus. I did not fight for you. I fought because I do not wish to die. Not like that. Not in the dirt, gutted by a hired thug. My death must mean something more. Or perhaps, it must mean nothing at all. But it will be my own.

GALEN
It was courage all the same. I have seen men freeze for less. You were tested, and you did not break. Your father would be proud.

VALERIUS
(He gives a short, bitter laugh)
My father. He would be proud of the clean kill, perhaps. The efficiency of it. He taught me how to fight. How to kill a man with the least amount of effort. But he would not be proud of the reason. He sent you to protect me from Tyrell’s assassins. He did not send you to watch me become one.

He stands up and walks to the fire, stretching his hands towards the warmth.

VALERIUS
When I fought Cassian, it was a dance. Every move was precise. There was a rhythm to it. A terrible beauty. I hated him in that moment, but I respected him. He was my equal. When he fell... I felt like a part of myself had been cut away. Today... there was nothing. Just grunting and sweating and the raw, ugly business of staying alive. I stabbed a man in the back. I felt his ribs grate against my blade. I heard the air leave his lungs. And I felt nothing but relief. That is what frightens me. Not the killing. The numbness.

GALEN
That numbness is what keeps a soldier alive, my lord. If you felt the weight of every life you took, you would be crushed by it. You did what was necessary. That is the first and last law of survival.

VALERIUS
And what happens when survival is all you have left? When you have stripped away honour, and home, and love, and all that remains is this ugly, necessary thing? What kind of man are you then?

Titus, his face pale in the firelight, speaks up. His voice is quiet, humbled.

TITUS
You are the kind of man who helps another who spoke ill of him. The kind of man who binds his wounds. My brother Lucius... he died for a lord's pride. But today, I lived for one's... necessity. I do not know what that makes you, my lord. But it makes me grateful.

Valerius looks at Titus, and for the first time since the duel, a flicker of something other than pain or guilt crosses his face. It is a weary, fragile understanding.

VALERIUS
We are all just men trying to see the sunrise, Titus. Lordship means little when a blade is at your throat. Get some rest. Both of you. I will take the first watch.

GALEN
You are wounded in spirit, my lord, and tired in body. Let me take the watch.

VALERius
The sleep I find tonight will be filled with ghosts. I would rather face the darkness with my eyes open. Rest. That is an order. Your lord's final command before he becomes mere cargo.

Galen studies him for a moment, then nods slowly. He finds a place near the wall, wraps his cloak around him, and is still. Titus, aided by the potent drink, is already slumping against his saddle, his breathing evening out.

Valerius picks up his sword again, but does not clean it. He holds it across his lap, its polished surface reflecting the dancing flames. He sits by the crumbling doorway of the broken tower, a solitary guardian watching over his wounded guards, waiting for a sun he is no longer sure he deserves to see.

SCENE IV

THE DOCKS AT SILVER CREEK. THE NEXT MORNING.

A thick, grey fog rolls in from the sea, blanketing the port of Silver Creek. It muffles all sound, turning the cries of gulls and the shouting of dockworkers into distant, ghostly echoes. The air is heavy with the smell of salt, wet wood, and fish. Wooden piers, slick with sea-slime, jut out into the murky water where the silhouettes of ships rock gently on the tide.

Valerius, Galen, and a heavily bandaged Titus make their way to the end of the longest pier. A grim looking merchant cog, the *Sea Serpent*, is moored there. It is a workhorse of a ship, not a vessel of leisure. Its timbers are dark and stained, its single mast stark against the grey sky. A handful of sailors move about the deck, their faces weathered and hard.

A broad, barrel chested man with a braided, grey-streaked beard stands at the gangplank. He wears boiled leather and a heavy wool cloak. This is CAPTAIN VORLAG. He watches their approach with narrowed, calculating eyes.

GALEN
Captain Vorlag?

CAPTAIN VORLAG
(His voice is a low rumble, like stones grinding together)
I am. And you would be Galen, the man with Lord Marius’s coin.

GALEN
I am. The final payment, as agreed.

He hands a heavy purse to the Captain, who hefts it, judging its weight with an expert’s assessment. He loosens the drawstring and peers inside, then nods, satisfied.

CAPTAIN VORLAG
The gold is right.
(His eyes move to Valerius)
So this is the passenger. He looks a bit green for the southern seas. The Serpent is a strong ship, but she has a rough temperament.

VALERIUS
I have a strong stomach, Captain.

CAPTAIN VORLAG
We’ll see. On my ship, titles don’t mean much. The sea doesn’t care who your father is. You’ll have a private cabin, as was paid for. You’ll eat at my table, as was paid for. But you’ll stay out of my crew’s way. They’re not servants. They’re sailors. Is that understood?

VALERIUS
Perfectly. I am seeking passage, not command.

CAPTAIN VORLAG
Good. We sail with the morning tide, as soon as this cursed fog lifts enough for me to see the headland. Get your goodbyes said. The tide waits for no man, not even an exiled lord.

He turns and stomps up the gangplank, disappearing onto the deck. The three men are left alone on the pier. The moment of departure has arrived.

GALEN
Our duty is done, my lord. We are to return to your father and tell him you are safely away.

VALERIUS
You will tell him what happened in the woods?

GALEN
I will tell him his fears were justified. And that his son is a capable fighter, who defended his men. I will tell him the truth.

VALERIUS
Thank you, Galen. For your service. For your counsel.

GALEN
It was my honour.
(He hesitates, then produces a much smaller purse from his tunic)
Your father gave me this for you. He said it was not much, lest it attract the wrong sort of attention. But enough to start a new life, perhaps. Or at least to buy a decent meal when you land.

Valerius takes the purse. It feels small and insignificant in his hand.

GALEN
He also gave me a message. He said… he said to remember the man you were meant to be, not the man one moment made you.

Valerius closes his eyes for a second, clutching the purse tightly. He nods, unable to speak. He turns to Titus.

VALERIUS
Titus. Your arm will heal. Will you return to service?

TITUS
Aye, my lord. It’s the only life I know.

VALERIUS
Then serve well. And try to find a master worthy of the life of men like your brother. They are rare.

TITUS
(He meets Valerius’s gaze directly, his earlier resentment gone completely, replaced by a quiet respect)
Safe travels, my lord. May you find… peace. Or whatever it is you’re looking for across the water.

VALERIUS
I am not sure what I am looking for. But I will know it if I find it. Farewell.

He clasps Galen’s forearm firmly, then gives a nod to Titus. Without another word, he turns and walks up the gangplank. He does not look back. He knows if he does, the sight of the last two men from his own land will break the fragile composure he has built.

He steps onto the deck of the *Sea Serpent*. The ship groans beneath his feet. He walks to the railing and looks not back at the pier, but out towards the sea, where the fog conceals the horizon. It is a vast, grey nothingness. An empty page.

VALERIUS
(To himself, a quiet whisper against the sound of the sea)
The road has won my future, I said. But the sea has no roads. It has only currents and tides. Perhaps there is a difference. Farewell, Galen. Farewell, Titus. Tell my father… tell him I will try.

A sailor shouts an order from the rigging. Another begins to cast off the thick mooring ropes. The ship gives a lurch as it begins to separate from the land. Valerius stands alone at the rail, a solitary figure pointed towards an unknown future, as the shores of his homeland dissolve into the all consuming fog behind him.

[The scene fades as the ship is slowly swallowed by the mist, leaving only the empty pier and the mournful cry of a distant gull.]

ACT II

SCENE 1.

The main deck of the *Sea Serpent*. Several days later. The thick fog has given way to a vast, monotonous expanse of grey water under a grey sky. The only sounds are the rhythmic creak of the ship’s timbers, the snap of the large sail in the wind, and the distant cries of seabirds following their wake. The air is cold and damp with salt spray.

Valerius stands near the stern, leaning against the railing, much as he did when the ship departed. His expression is unreadable, his gaze fixed on the endless horizon. He has barely spoken to anyone since they set sail, taking his meals alone and keeping to the shadows of the deck.

A man, DEMETRIOS, approaches him. He is older than Valerius, perhaps in his late forties, with a merchant’s shrewd eyes and a well fed frame wrapped in practical but expensive wool. He carries an air of someone who is comfortable anywhere in the world, as long as there is coin to be made.

DEMETRIOS
A bleak view, is it not? One would think the gods ran out of colours when they painted the sea. Grey on grey. It is enough to dull the spirit.

Valerius does not turn, his eyes still on the water.

VALERIUS
The spirit is what you make of it. The sea is merely water.

DEMETRIOS
(He chuckles softly and leans on the railing beside Valerius)
Ah, a philosopher. Or a man running from something. They often sound the same. Forgive my intrusion. I am Demetrios. My cargo is wine and olive oil, my destination is every port that will pay for it. And you are?

VALERIUS
A passenger.

DEMETRIOS
(Smiling, undeterred)
Of course. We are all passengers on this vessel. But a passenger with a name, surely? Unless you paid for anonymity. That costs extra, I am told. Captain Lysias is not a man to miss an opportunity for profit.

VALERius finally turns to look at him. His face is impassive.

VALERIUS
My name is of no consequence.

DEMETRIOS
Every name has consequence. A name can open doors or close them. It can call in debts or create them. Mine, for instance, is known in the markets of the south. It speaks of fair prices and timely delivery. It is my most valuable asset. What does your name speak of, I wonder? It has the sound of the north. The bearing of a soldier, perhaps? Or a nobleman’s son who found court life… distasteful.

VALERIUS
You have a vivid imagination.

DEMETRIOS
An imagination is necessary for a merchant. I must imagine the thirst of a city to sell them wine. I must imagine the hunger of a garrison to sell them grain. And I must imagine the story of a man who stands alone on a ship for three days without speaking a word to anyone. It is a puzzle. And I enjoy puzzles.

VALERIUS
Then you will find this one a disappointment. There is no story here.

DEMETRIOS
Every man is a story. Some are just written in a language few can read. You wear yours like a shield. Heavy. Well crafted. But every shield has a weakness.

Valerius turns away again, looking back out at the churning water. The wind begins to pick up, whining through the rigging. A young sailor, LEO, barefoot and nimble, scrambles up the mast to adjust a rope.

VALERIUS
I am not looking for company, merchant.

DEMETRIOS
No. You are looking for an ending. Or a beginning. They also look the same, from a distance. Tell me this, passenger with no name. What is it you hope to find across this great grey expanse? Riches? Forgiveness? A woman with warmer eyes than the ones you left behind?

VALERIUS
Peace.

The word hangs in the air, simple and heavy. Demetrios’s wry smile falters for a moment. He studies Valerius’s profile.

DEMETRIOS
Ah. The most expensive commodity of all. You will not find it in any market. And no man can sell it to you. I wish you luck in your search. Truly. But I will give you a piece of advice, free of charge. Peace is not found by looking backwards at the wake the ship leaves. It is found by looking forward, towards the port you have not yet seen.

The ship lurches as a larger wave hits its side. Demetrios stumbles slightly, catching himself on the rail. Valerius remains perfectly still, his balance undisturbed.

The captain, LYSIAS, emerges from his small cabin near the helm. He is a hard man, his face a roadmap of sun and sea and storms. He walks with a purpose, his eyes constantly scanning the sky, the sails, the water. He gives Valerius and Demetrios a passing glance.

LYSIAS
(To a nearby sailor)
Lash down the barrels. All of them. And check the ties on the lifeboat. The sky has a sour look to it.

SAILOR
Aye, Captain.

The sailor hurries off. The atmosphere on the deck begins to change. The lazy rhythm of the voyage is replaced by a quiet, focused urgency. Men move about, securing loose items, their voices low and serious. The wind is a constant, rising howl now.

DEMETRIOS
(Frowning at the darkening clouds)
It seems our conversation is to be cut short. I do not enjoy the sea when it is angry. It is bad for digestion. And for profits. A lost cargo can ruin a man.

VALERIUS
Better a lost cargo than a lost life.

DEMETRIOS
(He raises an eyebrow)
That depends entirely on the cargo. And the life. I will retire to my cabin. It is small, but it does not have this… dramatic view. A pleasure speaking with you, whoever you are.

Demetrios nods and makes his way carefully across the tilting deck, disappearing below. Valerius remains where he is, feeling the spray on his face. The waves are higher now, great rolling hills of dark water. The ship climbs each one and then slams down into the trough with a shuddering crash.

Lysias stands at the helm, his hands gripping the wheel, his knuckles white. He shouts an order, his voice cutting through the roar of the wind.

LYSIAS
Leo! Get down from there! Now!

The young sailor, LEO, is still high in the rigging, trying to secure a flapping section of the sail. The mast is whipping back and forth violently.

LEO
(Shouting back, his voice thin)
Almost got it, Captain! The rope is… stuck!

VALERIUS
(To himself)
Foolish boy. Pride will kill you faster than the sea.

As he says it, a tremendous gust of wind hits the ship. The main sail billows with a sound like thunder, and a thick rope, a stay line, snaps from its mooring with a crack like a whip. The free end, heavy with a block of wood, swings wildly across the deck. Sailors dive out of its path.

SAILOR 2
Stay line’s loose! Watch yourselves!

The rope snakes through the air. Leo, trying to descend, loses his footing. He cries out, managing to grab onto a smaller rope, but his leg becomes entangled in the flailing stay line. He is jerked sideways, dangling precariously over the churning water below, held only by his grip and the tangled rope around his ankle.

LEO
Help! I’m caught! My leg!

Lysias curses, his eyes darting from the waves to the boy to the helm. He cannot leave his post.

LYSIAS
Someone cut him loose! Get a knife up there!

Two sailors start to move towards the mast, but the deck is awash with water, and the swinging block makes it a treacherous path. They are hesitant, moving slowly. The ship groans, tilting at a severe angle. Leo screams as his grip begins to slip.

Without a second thought, Valerius moves.

He is a blur of motion. He vaults over a coil of rope, his soldier’s instincts taking over. The world narrows to a single objective: the boy. He ignores the crashing waves and the wind that tears at his clothes. He sees the arc of the swinging block, times it, and ducks under it as it whistles past his head.

He reaches the base of the mast. The other sailors stare at him, surprised by his sudden, decisive action.

VALERIUS
(To the sailors)
Your knives! Give me two!

One of the sailors, stunned, fumbles at his belt and hands him a short, heavy blade. Another does the same. Valerius puts one between his teeth and holds the other in his hand. He begins to climb.

He moves with a speed and agility that surprises everyone, including Lysias, who watches with a grim, focused intensity. Valerius is not a sailor, but he is a warrior, and his body is a tool honed for balance and strength. He finds handholds and footholds on the wet, swaying mast as if he were born to it.

He reaches the terrified boy in moments. Leo is white faced, his fingers slipping.

LEO
I can’t… I can’t hold on!

VALERIUS
(His voice is calm, authoritative, cutting through the boy’s panic)
Look at me. Look at me! I have you. Stop struggling.

Leo freezes, his frightened eyes locking onto Valerius. Valerius braces himself against the mast, wrapping one arm around a support rope. He leans out, knife in hand, and begins to saw at the thick stay line that has trapped Leo’s leg. The ship lurches again, threatening to throw them both into the sea. Valerius holds firm, his muscles straining.

The rope is thick and wet. The knife is slow. Below, the sailors watch in silence, the storm forgotten for a moment.

LYSIAS
(Shouting into the wind)
A wave! Hold fast!

A mountain of water rises up, its crest foaming, and crashes over the deck. It slams into Valerius and Leo, drenching them, trying to tear them from the mast. Valerius shields the boy with his own body, taking the brunt of the impact. The water recedes, leaving them gasping and soaked. Leo is sobbing now, from fear and cold.

VALERius takes the second knife from his teeth.

VALERIUS
Your other hand. Hold onto my belt. Do it now!

Leo obeys, his small hand grabbing a fistful of Valerius’s tunic. Valerius now has two hands free. He works with a desperate efficiency, one knife then the other, sawing at the remaining strands of the rope. The fibres part. With a final cut, the rope springs free. Leo’s leg is released, but he is now held only by Valerius and his own weakening grip.

VALERIUS
Down. We are going down. Foot by foot. Follow my lead. Do not look at the water.

He begins the slow, treacherous descent, guiding the boy, practically carrying him. He moves with the same unnerving calm as before. When they finally reach the deck, he pushes Leo towards the other sailors.

VALERIUS
Get him below. See to his leg.

The sailors rush to help the boy, half carrying him towards a hatchway. Leo looks back at Valerius, his eyes wide with awe and gratitude. He tries to speak, but can only manage a choked sound.

Valerius stands alone for a moment, breathing heavily, his hair plastered to his forehead. He looks down at his hands, which are raw and bleeding from the rope. He sees the sailor’s knife still clutched in one of them. He looks at it, and for a fleeting second, his expression darkens, as if remembering another knife, another time. He closes his hand around it tightly, then deliberately opens his fingers and lets it clatter to the deck.

The worst of the storm is beginning to pass. The wind is still strong, but its shrieking has lessened to a mournful moan. The waves are less violent. Lysias lashes the helm and walks over to Valerius. He stops in front of him, his expression unreadable. He looks at Valerius’s bleeding hands, then up at his face.

LYSIAS
You climb like a man who has scaled fortress walls.

VALERIUS
I have had practice with heights.

LYSIAS
And with knives. You risked your life for my sailor. A boy whose name you do not know. Why?

VALERIUS
It needed to be done. No one else was moving fast enough.

LYSIAS
(A small, grim smile touches his lips)
No. They were not. Men hesitate. They weigh the risk. They think of their wives, their children, their own skin. You did not. You saw a life in peril and you acted. That is the mark of a leader. Or a fool with nothing to lose. Which are you?

Valerius looks away, towards the horizon, where a thin sliver of lighter grey is appearing.

VALERIUS
I have been both. I am no longer a leader of men.

LYSIAS
Perhaps not. But you saved one of mine. The *Sea Serpent* is in your debt. My name is Lysias. And I would know the name of the man who saved my crewman.

Valerius is silent for a long moment. He looks at the breaking clouds, then back at the captain. He seems to come to a decision.

VALERIUS
My name is Valerius.

LYSIAS
Valerius. A good name. A strong name. Well, Valerius. The sea has tested you. It does that. It strips away titles and coin and pride. It shows you what a man is truly made of. Some are made of fear. Some of greed. Some are made of nothing but brittle bone. You... you are made of something else. Something I have seen before. In legion commanders. And in kings.

VALERIUS
Those days are over.

LYSIAS
(He shakes his head slowly)
A man cannot run from what he is. You can cross a hundred seas, change your name a dozen times. But what is inside you... that is your travelling companion for life. The question is whether you let it command you, or you command it.

Lysias bends down and picks up the knife Valerius dropped. He wipes the blade on his trousers and offers it back to Valerius, handle first.

LYSIAS
A good tool. You should not be so quick to discard it. You never know when you will need it again.

Valerius looks at the knife. He remembers his father’s words. *Remember the man you were meant to be, not the man one moment made you.* He slowly reaches out and takes the knife from Lysias. His grip is steady.

VALERIUS
Thank you, Captain.

LYSIAS
Get your hands seen to. There is a man below who knows something of medicine. And get some dry clothes. We have a long way to go yet. But the worst of it is behind us.

The captain nods once, then turns and walks back to the helm. The sun breaks through the clouds, casting a weak but welcome light across the deck. The sea is still rough, but it no longer feels like an enemy.

Valerius stands there, the sailor’s knife in his hand. He is no longer just a passenger. He is a man who acted. He looks out at the water, which is now turning from grey to a deep, promising blue. The empty page is no longer empty. A single word has been written. And for the first time since leaving his homeland, Valerius feels not the weight of his past, but the faint, unfamiliar possibility of a future.

[The scene fades as the sun grows brighter, the ship sailing steadily on towards the newly visible horizon.]

SCENE TWO

[The lower deck of the *Sea Serpent*. The space is cramped and dimly lit by a few swinging oil lamps. The air is thick with the smell of damp wood, tar, and sweat. The rhythmic creaking of the ship’s timbers is a constant, groaning sound. Several crewmen are tending to minor injuries or slumped in exhaustion on crates. In a small, slightly sectioned off area, THERON, a man with a face like weathered leather and hands stained with old poultices, is stitching a gash on a sailor’s arm. He works with a grim, practiced efficiency.]

[Valerius descends the ladder from the main deck, moving stiffly. He pauses, his eyes adjusting to the gloom. He holds his hands in front of him. The rope burns are raw and red. He approaches Theron’s makeshift infirmary.]

VALERIUS
The captain sent me.

[Theron does not look up. He pulls a stitch tight, and the sailor on the crate flinches, hissing through his teeth.]

THERON
The captain sends many things. Most of them broken. Wait your turn.

VALERIUS
It can wait.

[Valerius stands patiently, observing the space. It is a world away from the open sky and sea. Here, everything is confined, functional, and worn. He sees another sailor carefully re-coiling a length of rope, his movements slow and deliberate, a small act of order in the post storm chaos. Valerius’s gaze lingers on the rope, and for a moment, he seems to feel its phantom burn again.]

[Theron finishes his work. He ties off the thread with a deft twist and slaps a greasy salve over the stitches.]

THERON
There. Try not to fall on it again. Or if you do, fall on the other one. Gives me variety. You.

[He gestures with his chin towards Valerius, not bothering to look at him directly. The patched up sailor grunts his thanks and shuffles away, cradling his arm.]

THERON
Let me see.

[Valerius steps forward and holds out his hands. Theron takes them, his grip surprisingly strong and calloused. He turns them over, examining the raw flesh with a dispassionate, clinical eye.]

THERON
A fool’s hands.

VALERIUS
They did the work required.

THERON
So does a hammer. It does not feel the nail. You gripped the line like a drowning man, not a sailor. A sailor knows the rope is not his friend. It is a tool. You treat it with respect, not passion. Passion gets you hands like these. Or a watery grave. Sit.

[Valerius sits on the crate the other sailor just vacated. Theron rummages in a wooden chest, pulling out a small clay pot and some strips of clean linen.]

THERON
The captain seems to think you are some kind of hero. He is shouting your praises from the helm. The passenger who saved Gallus.

VALERIUS
I did what anyone would have.

THERON
No. You did what you did. Anyone else would have done something different. Most would have watched him drown. A few might have prayed. One or two might have tried and drowned with him. Heroism is just a name for a specific kind of foolishness that happens to succeed.

[He begins to clean the burns with a wet cloth. Valerius does not wince, though the touch must be agonizing.]

THERON
You are not a sailor. That much is clear. Nor a merchant. Your posture is too straight. Your eyes are too quiet. A soldier?

VALERIUS
I am a passenger.

THERON
(He lets out a dry chuckle, a sound like stones rattling in a tin cup)
Everyone on this ship is a passenger, my friend. We are all just passing from one place to the next. The only difference is some of us have to pull the ropes to get there. I was a soldier once. A long time ago. In the eastern legions. Learned how to do this. More practice than any man should ever want. You learn quickly what a man is made of when you are scraping it off the sand.

[He stops cleaning and looks up at Valerius for the first time. His eyes are sharp, missing nothing.]

THERON
You have that look. The look of a man who has given orders that other men died following. I know it well. I have seen it in the mirror.

[Valerius remains silent, his expression unreadable. Theron seems to take the silence as confirmation. He dabs a cool, sharp smelling salve onto the burns.]

VALERIUS
(His voice is low)
Those orders are a heavy burden.

THERON
They are not a burden. They are ghosts. And they are lighter than air. They do not weigh you down. They follow you. They whisper in the quiet watches of the night. They remind you that every choice has a price, and it is usually paid by someone else. The captain, he sees a leader in you. A commander. I see a man with a great many ghosts. A man trying to find a ship that can sail faster than they can fly.

[He begins to wrap Valerius’s hands in the linen strips, his movements precise and final.]

THERON
There. Keep them clean. Keep them dry. In a few days, the skin will harden. The memory will not. You will find no such salve for that in my chest.

VALERIUS
I did not ask for one.

THERON
Good. You would be disappointed. Now. There is a stack of dry clothes over there. Spare stores. They will smell of moth dust and mildew, but it is better than salt water. Find something that fits. The captain does not like his heroes catching a chill. Bad for morale.

[Theron turns his back, already looking for his next patient, his part in the drama concluded. Valerius stands. He looks at his bandaged hands. They feel strange, foreign. He walks over to a pile of folded tunics and trousers. As he picks through them, a young man approaches him hesitantly. It is GALLUS, the sailor he saved. He is pale and shivering slightly, but his eyes are wide with a fierce, burning gratitude.]

GALLUS
Sir.

[Valerius turns, a plain wool tunic in his hand.]

VALERIUS
You should be resting.

GALLUS
I could not rest. Not until I… I had to thank you. My name is Gallus.

VALERIUS
There is no need for thanks.

GALLUS
No need? I was gone. The sea had me. I felt the cold in my bones. I saw my home, my mother’s face. It was the end. And then… then there was your hand. You pulled me out of my own grave.

[Valerius looks uncomfortable. He is used to the weight of command, the calculus of battle, the finality of death. He is not used to the raw, unvarnished gratitude of a life he has personally salvaged from the brink.]

VALERIUS
The rope did most of the work.

GALLUS
The rope was held by you. Everyone else was just trying to save the ship. You were trying to save me. Why? You do not even know me.

VALERIUS
It was the right thing to do.

GALLUS
(Shakes his head, a small, sad smile on his lips)
I have been a sailor since I was a boy. I have seen many things done on the sea. Necessary things. Profitable things. Cruel things. I have not seen many men risk themselves for the “right” thing. Especially not for a lowly deckhand. I have nothing. No coin to offer you. My family are poor farmers a hundred leagues from the coast. But my life… my life is yours. If you ever need anything. A cup of water. A man to stand watch for you. A knife in the back of an enemy. You have but to ask.

[Gallus’s sincerity is absolute. It is a vow, sworn in the damp belly of the ship, more binding than any contract.]

VALERIUS
I need nothing. Just live your life, Gallus. That is payment enough.

GALLUS
I will. And I will remember the man who gave it back to me. Valerius. The captain said your name is Valerius. I will not forget it.

[Gallus gives a short, formal bow, a gesture of profound respect that seems out of place in the cramped hold. He then backs away, leaving Valerius alone with the spare tunic in his hand. Valerius looks down at the rough fabric. A simple garment for a simple man. But Theron’s words and Gallus’s vow have complicated everything. He is not a simple man. He is Valerius. A name that now means ‘savior’ to one man, and ‘commander’ to another. He slowly pulls off his wet, torn tunic and puts on the dry one. It feels like a borrowed skin. He is no longer just running from his past. He is now being actively pulled towards a future he did not seek.]

[He climbs the ladder back to the main deck. The scene that greets him is one of organized urgency.]

[The sun is higher now, a brilliant disk in a sky of pale, washed blue. The sea has calmed to a deep swell, the waves rolling with a steady, hypnotic rhythm. The crew of the *Sea Serpent* moves across the deck with purpose. The immediate danger is gone, but the evidence of the storm’s fury is everywhere. A section of the railing is splintered and gone. The deck is littered with frayed rope ends and a canvas sail that has been torn to shreds. But the crew’s attention is focused on the main mast.]

[A massive crack, a pale, jagged wound, runs a third of the way up the thick timber. It groans with every roll of the ship. Lysias stands at its base, his hand resting on the wood as if feeling for a pulse. His first mate, a burly, bearded man named PHRIXUS, is beside him, his face grim.]

PHRIXUS
It is deep, Captain. Deeper than it looks. The core is compromised. If we get another heavy blow, or have to put full sail on it… it will snap.

LYSIAS
And leave us crippled. A floating target for pirates or the next squall. What are our options?

PHRIXUS
We can try to splint it. Lash timbers around the fracture. But it will not hold for long. It is a bandage on a broken leg. The best we can do is limp to the nearest port for a proper repair. That would be three, four days off our course. And the ports on that stretch of coast are… expensive.

LYSIAS
They prey on desperation. I know. We do not have the coin for that kind of robbery. Nor the time.

[Lysias sees Valerius emerge onto the deck. He watches him for a moment. Valerius is not looking at him; he is looking at the mast, his head tilted, his eyes narrowed. He is not seeing a piece of wood. He is seeing a tactical problem. An engineering challenge.]

LYSIAS
Valerius.

[Valerius turns. Lysias beckons him over.]

LYSIAS
Your hands are mended?

VALERIUS
They are wrapped.

LYSIAS
Good. Theron is a miserable old goat, but he knows his trade. Come. Lend me your eyes. You are a passenger no longer. You have paid your passage in full. Now you are a member of my council. Phrixus here thinks we are destined to limp into port and be fleeced by the shipwrights of Melos. What do you see?

[Phrixus gives Valerius a skeptical look, sizing up the newcomer who has suddenly been elevated by the captain. Valerius ignores him. He walks to the mast, his newly bandaged hands held carefully at his sides. He runs his gaze up and down the length of the crack. He walks around the mast, examining it from all sides. He presses a thumb against the wood near the edge of the fracture, feeling its give.]

VALERIUS
The splint idea is sound. For a temporary fix. But he is right. It is a surface solution for a deep wound. The stress comes from the core.

PHRIXUS
See, Captain? Even the landsman agrees.

VALERIUS
But a port is not your only option. You have a spare yardarm, yes? In the hold?

LYSIAS
We do. A heavy one. For the mainsail.

VALERIUS
And tools? A saw? An adze? Augers?

PHRIXUS
Of course we have tools. This is a ship, not a pleasure barge. What are you thinking? We cannot carve a new mast from a yardarm.

VALERIUS
No. We are going to perform surgery. We relieve the pressure on the mast now. Lower the mainsail completely. We use the auxiliary sails to keep way. Then, we do not just splint the crack on the outside. We reinforce it from the inside.

LYSIAS
How?

VALERIUS
We find the heart of the fracture. Drill through it. From one side clear to the other. We take that spare yardarm and we shape a pin from it. A massive dowel of solid hardwood. Long enough to pass through the entire diameter of the mast. We drive it through the hole, locking the two sides of the crack together. A wooden rivet. Then, we drill smaller holes above and below it, and drive smaller pins. And then, we lash the external splints. We will turn the weakness into the strongest part of the mast. It will not be pretty. But it will be strong. Stronger, perhaps, than before.

[There is a silence. Phrixus stares at Valerius with a new expression, the skepticism replaced by a grudging respect. He is a sailor, and he understands the solid, practical genius of the plan. Lysias is smiling faintly.]

LYSIAS
A wooden rivet. You have done this before.

VALERIUS
On siege towers. The principle is the same. Wood under strain is wood under strain. Whether from the wind or a catapult’s torsion.

LYSIAS
(He claps a hand on Phrixus’s shoulder)
Well, First Mate? It seems our passenger knows a thing or two about how timber splinters. What do you think?

PHRIXUS
(He rubs his beard, still looking at the mast, but seeing it differently now)
It is a bold plan. A great deal of work. But… by the gods, I think it will hold. It will hold all the way to Alexandria.

LYSIAS
Then that is the plan. Phrixus, get a team. Clear the hold and get that yardarm on deck. Find our best sawmen. Gallus!

[The young sailor, who had been watching from a distance, hurries over.]

GALLUS
Yes, Captain!

LYSIAS
You will work with Valerius. You will be his hands, since his are… temporarily out of service. Whatever he needs, you get it. Understood?

GALLUS
(His face lights up)
Yes, Captain. Understood.

[Lysias turns to Valerius. The pretense of him being a mere passenger is completely gone. He is speaking to him as an equal, a fellow commander.]

LYSIAS
The work is yours to oversee. I will keep the ship on course. You will keep my mast standing. Are you up to the task?

[Valerius looks from the broken mast to the eager face of Gallus, to the calculating eyes of Phrixus, and finally to the steady, knowing gaze of Lysias. He feels the old weight settling on his shoulders. The burden of responsibility. The mantle of command. For weeks, he has sought emptiness, anonymity. He has tried to be no one. But the sea and the storm have conspired against him. They have stripped him bare and shown him the man he still is.]

[He remembers his father’s words again. *Remember the man you were meant to be.* He clenches his bandaged fists. The pain is a dull, steady throb. It reminds him he is alive.]

VALERIUS
I am.

[He turns to Gallus, his voice quiet but carrying a new note of authority that was absent before.]

VALERIUS
Gallus. I need charcoal and a flat piece of board. And I need the precise measurements of the mast’s diameter at the fracture point. We will draw this out before we cut a single piece of wood. There is no room for error.

GALLUS
At once, sir.

[Gallus practically runs to do his bidding. Valerius looks up at the cracked mast, the sun behind it. He is no longer seeing a symbol of his broken past. He is seeing a problem to be solved. A command to be executed. A ship to be saved. The page of his life is no longer empty. The single word written there during the storm has been joined by others. Duty. Purpose. Command.]

[Lysias watches him for another moment, a satisfied nod his only comment. He then turns and walks back to the helm, confident that the heart of his ship is in good hands. Valerius, the man who wanted to be no one, stands at the center of the deck, planning, measuring, and quietly beginning to lead once more.]

[FADE TO BLACK]
