FLORIAN
Why dost thou wear the ocean’s melancholy? Thou hast a face so long, a fisherman might use it for a sounding line. Hath some stray mermaid stolen thy heart and pledged it to the fishes?

CASSIAN
Peace, Florian. My mood is mine own, and it troubles none but me.

FLORIAN
None but thee? Why, it troubles the very air! The gulls fly crooked to see thy sorrow. Thou wilt sigh with a man whose boat hath a leak, for no other reason but because thy soul is taking on water. Thou wilt frown at the sun for shining, because it reminds thee of some lady’s golden hair. What soul but such a soul would find such cause for grief? Thy heart is as full of sighs as a ship’s sail is full of wind, and yet thy purpose hath been becalmed as a ship in the doldrums for this lovesickness. Didst thou not pick a quarrel with a cartwright for whistling a merry tune? with another for selling ribbons of a colour thy lady doth not favour? And yet thou wouldst have me believe thou art merely thoughtful!

CASSIAN
And I were so apt to jest as thou art, any man might buy my sorrows for a copper piece.

FLORIAN
A copper piece! O, poor currency!

Enter Lord Valerius with two Servants.

CASSIAN
By my honour, here comes Lord Valerius.

FLORIAN
By my boots, I do not stir.

VALERIUS
Keep back, for I would have a word with him. Sir, a moment of your leisure.

FLORIAN
And but a moment with the man? Add something to it; make it a moment and a meaning.

VALERIUS
You shall find I have meaning enough, sir, if you give me leave to speak.

FLORIAN
Could you not take some leave without the giving? Your presence takes much already.

VALERIUS
Cassian, thou hast been seen near my estate.

FLORIAN
Estate? What, dost thou make him a trespasser? If thou make a trespasser of him, look to find your fences broken. Here’s my argument, here’s that shall mend your borders. Zounds, estate!

CASSIAN
We speak here in the public eye, my lord. If you have some grievance, let us find a place where reason, not rumour, may attend our speech.

VALERIUS
Men’s eyes were made to witness honour’s due. I will not hide my quarrel for no man’s ease, I.

Enter Helena.

HELENA
Brother! I pray you, hold. What means this fury?

VALERIUS
Peace, sister, this is not your concern. He hath dishonoured our name.

FLORIAN
But I’ll be flayed, sir, if his honour be less than yours. Marry, go find a looking glass, you’ll see the greater blemish on your own account; your worship in that sense may call him peer.

VALERIUS
The patience that I have for thee, Florian, can find no better word than this: Thou art a knave.

FLORIAN
Knave? Tybalt, the reason that I have to scorn thee doth much excuse the appertaining rage to such a greeting. Knave am I none; therefore stand corrected; I see thou know'st me not.

VALERIUS
Boy, this shall not excuse the insolence that thou hast shown me, therefore turn and draw.

FLORIAN
I do protest I never was thy boy, but am a man that scorns thee more than thou canst devise till thou shalt know the reason of my scorn. And so, good Lord of Pomp, whose name I hold as cheaply as mine own, be satisfied.

VALERIUS
O calm, dishonourable, vile submission! [_Draws._] Alla stoccata carries it away. Florian, you wharf rat, will you walk?

FLORIAN
What wouldst thou have with me?

VALERIUS
Good King of Braggarts, nothing but the air from your lungs; that I mean to make bold withal, and as you shall gasp hereafter, silence the rest of your jests. Will you pluck your sword out of its scabbard by the hilt? Make haste, lest mine be about your throat ere it be out.

FLORIAN
[_Drawing._] I am for you.

CASSIAN
Gentle Florian, put thy rapier up.

FLORIAN
Come, sir, your passado. [_They fight._]

CASSIAN
Draw, friends; beat down their weapons. Gentlemen, for shame, forbear this outrage! Valerius, Florian, the Magistrate expressly hath forbid this brawling on the city docks. Hold, Valerius! Good Florian!

[_Helena cries out. Florian is distracted. Valerius wounds him._]

FLORIAN
I am hurt. A plague o’ both your houses. I am sped. Is he content, and hath paid nothing?

CASSIAN
What, art thou hurt?

FLORIAN
Ay, ay, a scratch, a scratch. Marry, ’tis enough. Where is my hat? Go villain, fetch a priest.

HELENA
Courage, man; the hurt cannot be much.

FLORIAN
No, ’tis not so deep as the harbour, nor so wide as a sail, but ’tis enough, ’twill serve. Ask for me tomorrow, and you shall find me a quiet man. I am salted, I warrant, for this world. A plague o’ both your houses. Zounds, a lord, a fop, a popinjay, a coward, that fights when a lady cries out! Why the devil came she between us? I was hurt by her tears.

CASSIAN
I thought all for the best.

FLORIAN
Help me to some bench, Cassian, or I shall fall. A plague o’ both your houses. They have made fish bait of me. I have it, and soundly too. Your houses! [_Exeunt Florian, supported by Cassian, who lays him on a bench where he dies._]

HELENA
O, Florian, slain! My brother’s hand hath done this wicked deed.

CASSIAN
This gentleman, my truest, dearest friend, hath got his mortal hurt for my small cause; my reputation stained with Valerius’ slander, Valerius, that I tried to call my friend. O sweet Helena, thy brother’s pride hath made me desperate and in my temper turned patience to steel.

Re-enter Valerius.

HELENA
Brother, be gone! O, fly from this dark place!

CASSIAN
Again in triumph, and brave Florian slain? Away to heaven respective lenity, and fire-eyed fury be my conduct now! Now, Valerius, take the ‘knave’ back again that late thou gav’st me, for Florian’s soul is but a little way above our heads, staying for thine to keep him company. Either thou or I, or both, must go with him.

VALERIUS
Thou wretched fool, that didst consort him here, shalt with him hence.

CASSIAN
This shall determine that. [_They fight; Valerius falls._]

HELENA
O, I am sorrow’s fool!

CASSIAN
Stay not amazed. The watch will be upon us. Hence, be gone, away!

HELENA
Where shall I go? My brother lieth dead, and he that slew him holds my heart in thrall.

Enter Magistrate and Citizens.

FIRST CITIZEN
Which way ran he that made this bloody scene? Valerius, that murderer, which way ran he?

CASSIAN
There lies that Valerius.

FIRST CITIZEN
Up, sir, go with me. I charge thee in the Magistrate’s name obey.

MAGISTRATE
Where are the vile beginners of this fray?

CASSIAN
O noble Magistrate, I can discover all the unlucky manage of this fatal brawl. There lies the man, slain by my own right hand, that slew my friend, brave Florian.

HELENA
Valerius, my brother! O my father’s child! O Magistrate! O, the blood is spilled of my dear kinsman! Magistrate, as thou art true, for blood of ours shed blood of this sad man. O brother, brother!

MAGISTRATE
Cassian, who began this bloody fray?

CASSIAN
Valerius, here slain, whom my hand did slay; I, that spoke him fair, bid him bethink how small the quarrel was, and urged withal your high displeasure. All this uttered with gentle breath, calm look, could not appease the unruly spleen of proud Valerius, deaf to peace, but that he tilts with piercing steel at bold Florian’s breast, who, all as hot, turns deadly point to point, and with a courtly scorn, with one hand beats cold death aside. But then my lady Helena appeared, and her cry did steal my friend’s defence, and ‘twixt their blades he rushed; underneath whose guard an envious thrust from Valerius hit the life of stout Florian, and then Valerius fled. But by and by comes back to me, who had but newly entertained revenge, and to’t we go like lightning; for, ere I could think to part them was proud Valerius slain. This is the truth, or let Cassian die.

HELENA
He speaks the truth. Affection makes me true. My brother’s rage did author this black strife, and with his pride he cost two noble lives. I beg for mercy, which thou, Magistrate, must give; Valerius is slain, let Cassian live.

MAGISTRATE
Cassian slew him, he slew Florian. Who now the price of his dear blood doth owe?

A NOBLEMAN
Not Cassian, sir, he was Florian’s friend; his fault concludes but what the law should end, the life of Valerius.

MAGISTRATE
And for that offence immediately we do exile him hence. I have an interest in your pride’s proceeding, my city for your rude brawls doth lie a-bleeding. But I’ll amerce you with so strong a fine that you shall all repent this loss of mine.

MAGISTRATE
I will be deaf to pleading. Pity sleeps.
Let him be gone ere the next sun doth rise,
Or forfeit his own life for this his crime.
The law is spoken. Bear the bodies hence.
My heart is heavy for this grievous sight.
Attend me, sirs. Justice hath wept today.

[_Exeunt Magistrate and his Attendants. Citizens begin to disperse, murmuring._]

CASSIAN
Then banishment is mercy’s softest name
For this day’s work. A world without these walls
Is but a wilderness, a silent tomb,
If Helena breathes not the selfsame air.
One word, dear lady, ere my fortune falls
Into the endless night that is my doom.

HELENA
O Cassian, what word can my tongue find?
My brother’s blood still stains this cursèd ground,
My father’s wrath, a storm about to break,
And my poor heart, a vessel torn between
The shore of duty and the sea of love.
Go, save thyself. To think of me is vain.

CASSIAN
To not think of thee were a truer death
Than any headsman’s axe could ever grant.
Thy memory shall be my only sun
In the cold lands where I am forced to roam.
Farewell. May heaven’s guard be ever thine.

[_He turns to leave. Enter LORD PETRONIUS, an old, stern nobleman, with two Servants._]

PETRONIUS
Hold. Let no man stir. My ears have heard
A tale of blood, a discord in the streets
That sings a hateful song of my son’s name.
Where is Valerius? Why stand ye all
Like frozen statues at a funeral?
Helena? Daughter? What means this tableau?
This blood? O, speak!

[_He sees the body of Valerius as it is being lifted by two citizens._]

No, no. It cannot be. My son! My heir!
The pillar of my age, my house’s pride!
Who hath done this? What devil’s hand hath quenched
The brightest fire that did warm my soul?
Speak, girl! Thy brother’s ghost cries from the earth,
And I, his father, must give it a voice!

HELENA
O father, fortune hath conspired this day
To make our house the argument of woe.

PETRONIUS
Fortune? Do not name that idle jade.
Men’s hands spill blood, not fortune’s careless whim.
Who is the author of this misery?
His name! That I may carve it on his tomb
When my own sword hath emptied his black heart.

FIRST CITIZEN
My lord, the man you seek stands yonder. See.
Young Cassian. The Magistrate hath judged
The matter, and for this deed he is exiled.

PETRONIUS
Exiled? Exiled! For the precious blood
Of my Valerius? For a prince’s ransom
Held in one vein of his? Banishment?
Why, ’tis a holiday, a brief sojourn
From which a man returns refreshed and bold.
It is no punishment! It is a prize!
And you, my daughter, you who should have torn
Your hair and called down curses from the sky,
You stand and speak with him, the very fiend
Who made you sister to a lifeless shade?

HELENA
Father, your grief mistakes the course of things.
Valerius, in heat, did strike the first.
He slew the noble Florian, Cassian’s friend.

PETRONIUS
He what? He defended his good name,
As any son of mine is taught to do!
And for this Florian, this upstart weed,
My mighty oak must fall? Is this the law?
And you defend the woodman with his axe?
O, viper! I have nourished in my breast
A serpent that doth turn its fangs on me.
You plead his cause?

CASSIAN
My lord, she speaks the truth.
The fault was with your son. His hasty pride
Lit this sad fire, and he himself was burned.
I did but what his bloody challenge bid;
I gave him what he sought, a warrior’s end.

PETRONIUS
Thou dost confess it, brazen murderer!
Thou speak’st of his end, but thine is yet to come.
The Magistrate is but a man of forms,
Of ink and paper. I am a man of flesh,
Of blood and steel, and by my sacred name,
The breath thou draw’st to boast of this thy deed
Shall be thy last.

[_Petronius draws his sword. Cassian draws his own. Enter LORD AURELIAN, Florian’s father, old and sorrowful, but with a noble bearing._]

AURELIAN
Put up your swords. Is there not grief enough
To drown this city, that you seek to add
Another river of it to the flood?
My lord Petronius, I have lost a son today.
I know the winter that doth grip your heart,
For that same frost hath settled in my own.

PETRONIUS
Aurelian! Your son was but the cause,
The paltry spark that set my house aflame.
This man, this Cassian, is the true disease.
Stand back. This is a private agony.

AURELIAN
No, it is mine as well. My Florian’s life
Was not a thing of paltry worth to me.
He was my joy, the comfort of my years.
And yet, I hear that this young man, his friend,
Avenged his death, as honor’s law requires.
Valerius took a life, and paid with his.
It is a brutal balance, but ’tis struck.
Cassian, though my heart doth break to say it,
I owe thee thanks. Thou didst what I could not.
Thou wert a better friend to him in death
Than I, his father, in this slow despair.

PETRONIUS
Thanks? You give him thanks? For what? For making
Two fathers childless on a single day?
Your wits are addled by your sorrow, man.
This is not honor, this is butchery!
And you, my child, look on your father’s foe
With eyes that swim not with deserved tears,
But with a kind of foul, abhorrent love.
I see it now. You are his accomplice.
Your heart is traitor to your own bloodline.

HELENA
Father, I love my brother. But I love
The truth as well. To curse this man is wrong
When rage, not reason, did condemn them all.
Must I be false to what mine own eyes saw
To prove my love for what is dead and gone?

PETRONIUS
Aye, that you must! Or be no child of mine.
Get from my sight. Go to your chamber. There
You shall remain, a prisoner of your shame,
Until I find a way to purge this stain.
Perhaps a convent’s walls may teach you prayers
To cleanse the sin of such a wicked love.
Away with you!

[_He shoves Helena towards his Servants._]

CASSIAN
Touch her not with violence!

PETRONIUS
Wilt thou command me, boy? Thou who art naught
But fleeting dust upon the city’s roads?
The guards will see you to the gates. Be gone.
And if by chance you meet with bandits there,
I pray they show you the same courtesy
My son received from you.

AURELIAN
Petronius, cease.
Your words are venom. Let the law suffice.
The young man is exiled. Let that be the end.
Do not court further sorrow.

PETRONIUS
The end? It is the start. My memory
Is long, Aurelian. And my house’s honor
Will not be mended with a word like ‘end’.
Come, daughter. You will learn what duty means.

[_Exeunt Petronius, dragging Helena, and his Servants. The Citizens who remain look on in silence._]

CASSIAN
And so the sun of my content is set,
And I am left in darkness absolute.
Exiled from her, which is from all the world.

AURELIAN
Young man, the world is wider than these walls.
Grief makes a prison of a paradise.
My son is gone. His laughter will no more
Make music in my halls. That is my doom.
Yours is but journeying. There is a hope,
However faint, that flickers in your path.
Mine is extinguished. Here, take this purse.

[_He offers a heavy purse to Cassian._]

It is but gold. It cannot buy back joy,
But it can purchase bread and buy you time.
Time may do much. It may not heal a wound,
But it can teach a man to live with scars.
Use it in Florian’s name. He would have wished
His friend to live, and live with some small ease.

CASSIAN
My lord, your goodness in this bitter hour
Doth shame your rival’s rage. I thank you for it,
And for your son, whose friendship was a gift
I valued more than all the gold on earth.
I will remember him. Farewell, my lord.

AURELIAN
Farewell, Cassian. May your path be safe.
Now leave me here, to keep a lonely watch
With what remains.

[_Cassian bows and exits. Aurelian kneels beside the spot where Florian fell, his shoulders slumped in grief. The scene fades._]

---

ACT I, SCENE III

[_A chamber in Lord Petronius’s house. The room is opulent but shadowed, the heavy curtains drawn against the day. HELENA stands by a window, trying to peer through a crack in the velvet. LYRA, her waiting-woman, stands nearby, wringing her hands._]

LYRA
My lady, you will wear away the floor
With your incessant pacing. And your eyes,
So red with weeping. Pray, sit down and rest.
What’s done is done. No sorrow can rewind
The thread of fate to yesterday’s bright morn.

HELENA
O, Lyra, rest is but a stranger to me now.
My mind’s a battleground where armies clash.
My brother’s ghost on one side, pale and stern,
And Cassian’s dear image on the other.
My heart is torn, the trophy of their war.
And over all, my father’s thunderous rage,
A sky of iron that permits no light.
He called me serpent, viper. Said my love
Was wicked, foul. Is love a crime, Lyra?
Is truth a treason to a father’s house?

LYRA
Love is a madness, mistress, so they say.
It makes the wise seem foolish, and the loyal
Forget their station. But a crime? No. Never.
Your heart is good. Your only fault is that
You gave it where your father would not have it go.

HELENA
He means to lock me in a convent. There
To waste my youth in cold and sunless prayer,
And count my beads until my fingers bleed,
And all for what? For seeing what I saw?
For loving one who, though his hand is stained,
Did act from honor, not from villainy?
My father speaks of honor, but his own
Is but another name for stubborn pride.

[_The door opens and LORD PETRONIUS enters. He is cold and resolved. Lyra curtsies and retreats to a corner._]

PETRONIUS
So, you have wept your fill? Or are there still
Some traitorous tears to shed for your family’s foe?
I come to tell you of my firm resolve.
The convent was a thought born of my rage,
Too simple a solution for your crime.
A life of prayer would sanctify your sin,
And I would have you feel its earthly weight.

HELENA
Father, what mean you? What new punishment
Have you devised? My spirit is so bruised,
It cannot bear another heavy blow.

PETRONIUS
You speak of love. You claim a woman’s right
To choose the vessel for her fond affections.
So be it. I have made a choice for you.
A noble choice. A match to cleanse our name
And graft our wounded house to one so great
Its power will eclipse this dark day’s shame.
You are to marry Duke Octavius.

HELENA
The Duke? That old and cruel, ambitious man?
Whose first two wives were sent to early graves
By his cold heart and his possessive rage?
Father, you cannot mean it. That is not
A marriage. That is a living burial.
I will not do it.

PETRONIUS
You will not? You will not? Who are you, girl,
To speak that phrase to me? You are my child.
Your will is but the echo of my own.
The Duke is powerful. His alliance
Will silence all the whispers in the court
That say my daughter loved her brother’s slayer.
He knows the tale. He takes you as you are,
A damaged prize, and for his courtesy,
Our houses shall be joined. The contracts are
Already drawn. The wedding is in a month.

HELENA
A month? O, mercy! Father, I am on my knees.
[_She kneels before him._]
Do anything but this. Send me away.
Imprison me within this very room
For all my days. Let me not see the sun.
But do not give me to that man. His touch
Would be a profanation, and his bed
A colder place than my poor brother’s tomb.

PETRONIUS
Your brother’s tomb is cold because of you!
Because your cry distracted him, your heart
Was fixed upon his enemy. Do you forget
The part you played? This marriage is your penance.
Octavius will teach you discipline.
He’ll curb this willfulness that I have failed
To tame. He is a master of the art.
Now rise. A daughter of my house does not
Grovel upon the floor. Prepare yourself.
You have a month to make your peace with it.
Your tears are wasted. My decree is stone.

[_Petronius turns and exits, locking the door from the outside. The sound of the key turning in the lock is loud and final._]

HELENA
He’s locked it. I am now in truth a prisoner.
Lyra, my friend, my world has shrunk to this.
This room, these walls, and at the end of it,
A monstrous marriage that my soul abhors.

LYRA
O, lady. This is tyranny indeed.
What can be done? The Duke’s a man of might,
And your good father’s will is absolute.

HELENA
There is no hope for me. There’s none. Unless...
Unless I could get word to Cassian.
But he is banished. Whither has he gone?
Into what wilderness? What foreign land?
He does not know this new and dreadful fate
That rushes toward me like a winter flood.

LYRA
My lady, think. If you could write a letter,
I have a cousin in the city watch.
A good and honest boy. For my sake, he
Might find a way. Perhaps he knows the road
That Cassian took. Perhaps a messenger
Could overtake him, ere he goes too far.

HELENA
A letter? Yes! O, Lyra, you are hope
Made manifest! Bring me some ink and paper.
Quickly, before my father’s spies return.
I’ll write to him. I’ll tell him of the Duke.
I’ll tell him that my heart is his alone,
In this world and the next. I do not know
What he can do, an exile, poor and friendless.
But he must know. My soul must speak to his
One final time, before this silence falls.
Go now, good Lyra. Speed must be our shield.

[_Lyra hurries out through a small servant’s door. Helena goes to the window, her expression shifting from despair to a desperate, fragile resolve._]

He shall not have me. That old, withered Duke.
My father’s pride will not become my cage.
I’ll find a way. A dagger or a drug
Can cut a knot a king cannot untie.
O, Cassian, I would rather be thy bride
In death’s cold kingdom, than a queen on earth
In Duke Octavius’s hateful arms.
My choice is made. My love, or else my grave.

ACT I, SCENE IV

[_The outskirts of the city. A windswept hill overlooking the main road leading into the wilderness. The sun is setting. CASSIAN stands alone, a small pack at his feet. He looks back towards the city walls, now dark against the fading light._]

CASSIAN
There lies my life. My past, my hope, my love.
And here before me lies... what? Emptiness.
A nameless road, a future like a page
On which no story has been written yet,
Nor is there any hand to write it. Mine
Is stained with blood, and trembles with the loss
Of all I held most dear. O, Florian!
Your friendship cost you life, and me, a world.
And Helena... her name is like a prayer
I am no longer worthy to pronounce.
To be an exile is to be a ghost,
To haunt the memory of what one was,
Invisible to all who still can live and breathe
Within the warmth of that beloved place.

[_Enter MARCELLUS, a young man of Cassian’s age, climbing the hill hastily._]

MARCELLUS
Cassian! I thought I’d miss you. The guards
Close the main gate at sunset. I came round
The northern path. I brought you these.

[_He hands Cassian a sword belt and a small, heavy satchel._]

A proper sword, not just that dueling blade.
And coin enough to see you to the coast.
From there, you must decide your own new world.

CASSIAN
Marcellus. Ever true. I knew you’d come.
My thanks are poor, but they are all I have.
How did you leave the city? Is there talk?

MARCELLUS
Talk? The whole city buzzes with the news.
The houses of Petronius and Aurelian
Are now two warring camps. They say your name
In whispers. Half do call you murderer,
The other half, a hero of revenge.
Lord Aurelian mourns, but with a quiet pride.
But Lord Petronius... he is like a fire,
Consuming all. He breathes but threats and rage.
His house is barred. He holds his daughter close.

CASSIAN
Poor Helena. A dove in a hawk’s nest.
I fear for her. Her father’s iron will
May seek to bend or break her gentle heart
For what he deems her part in this sad day.
I am the cause of all her present grief.

MARCELLUS
You are the cause of her undying love.
Which is a thing that may yet be her shield.
But listen, for my news is not yet done.
A little bird did sing a song to me,
A waiting-woman’s cousin, it is said.
The song was this: Petronius has decreed
His daughter is to wed the old, dark Duke.
Octavius.

CASSIAN
What? The Duke? That lecherous old goat?
That spider who devours all he traps?
No. Heaven would not suffer such a thing.
You jest to lighten my despairing mood.

MARCELLUS
My mood is grave, my friend. The news is true.
The wedding is to be within the month.
Her father seeks to bury your love deep
Beneath the weight of Duke Octavius’s gold
And cruel authority.

CASSIAN
A month. Then this is not my banishment,
It is a race. A race against the time
That seeks to steal the jewel of my soul.
O, Marcellus, what power has a man
With nothing but a sword and empty hands
Against a Duke? Against a father’s law?

[_From the shadows of a nearby copse of trees, LYRA emerges, cloaked and breathless._]

LYRA
The power of a love that will not yield.
Sir. I am Lyra. I serve Lady Helena.

[_She kneels and offers a sealed letter to Cassian._]

She sends you this. And with it, sends her heart.
She bid me tell you that her hand may be
Promised to another, but her soul is sworn
To you. She says you are her only hope.
Read it. I must be gone ere I am missed.

[_Lyra vanishes back into the trees._]

CASSIAN
Her only hope. That word has given me
The strength of ten. My hands no longer shake.
[_He breaks the seal and reads the letter by the dying light._]
Her words are fire. They burn away my grief
And forge it into purpose. She speaks of death
Before dishonor. She would take her life
Before she takes the Duke. She calls on me
To save her not from him, but from herself.

MARCELLUS
What can you do? To re-enter the city
Is certain death. The Magistrate’s decree
Is absolute. Petronius would have you
Hanged from the walls before the sun could set.

CASSIAN
Then I will come by darkness. I will be
A thief of love, and steal what is my own
From those who guard it with unjust designs.
My exile ends tonight. My journey’s goal
Is not some distant shore, but that same wall
From which they cast me out. Marcellus, you
Must be my eyes and ears within that cage.

MARCELLUS
You know I will. What is your plan?

CASSIAN
I have none yet. Only the raw resolve.
I’ll find a place to hide, in the wild woods
That skirt the city. I will live and watch,
And learn the Duke’s routines, Petronius’s guard.
There must be some flaw, some unguarded hour,
Some moment when a desperate man might strike.
You must find means to get my messages
To Helena. Let her know I am near.
Tell her to feign compliance with her fate,
To lull her father’s vigilance to sleep.
And when the moment comes, she must be ready.

MARCELLUS
This is a desperate gamble, Cassian.
The odds are stacked against you like a mountain.

CASSIAN
Then I will learn to climb. For at the peak
Is Helena. There is no other prize.
Go now, my friend. Return before the gate
Is truly sealed. Our work begins tonight.
Farewell.

MARCELLUS
Farewell. May fortune favor the bold.

[_Marcellus clasps Cassian’s arm, then hurries back down the hill. Cassian stands alone, clutching the letter. He looks not at the road ahead, but back at the city, his face a mask of grim determination as the last light of day disappears._]

CASSIAN
The world may call me exile, rogue, or fool.
But ere a month is passed, it shall add one
More name to that poor list. Her saviour, or
Her fellow martyr in the cause of love.
The night is come. And I must learn its ways.

ACT II

SCENE I

[_A lavishly appointed chamber in the Magistrate’s palace. Heavy tapestries depict historical victories. DUKE ALARIC stands by a large window, looking down upon the city square. PETRONIUS enters._]

PETRONIUS
My Lord Duke. I trust the city’s air
Agrees with you. We have done all we can
To make your stay as comfortable as may be.

DUKE ALARIC
The air is fine, Petronius. The wine
Is finer still. But comfort is a thing
For old men and for invalids. I seek
A different prize. I seek a union,
A binding of our houses, strong as steel.
Yet your fair daughter, she remains unseen.
She keeps her chambers like a cloistered nun.
Does she still grieve for that young fool you banished?

PETRONIUS
She grieves for a delusion, nothing more.
A girl’s romantic fancy, like a fever
That burns intensely, but must soon break.
She is my daughter. She knows well her duty.
The blood of statesmen runs within her veins,
Not that of flighty poets. Give her time.
A week, no more. The shock is still too fresh.

DUKE ALARIC
A week? Time is a luxury, Magistrate.
My own affairs of state call me away.
My provinces grow restless in my absence.
Delay breeds rumour, and rumour breeds dissent.
They’ll say the Duke was spurned, his offer scorned
By a mere Magistrate’s daughter. They will laugh.
I do not suffer being laughed at, sir.

PETRONIUS
No one would dare. Your name is feared and honoured.

DUKE ALARIC
Then let us give them cause for honour, not
For whispers in the dark. The wedding day.
It must be soon. I will not be kept waiting
Like some green boy begging for a first kiss.
We spoke of a month. I find that I am now
Less patient. Let it be a fortnight hence.
No more.

PETRONIUS
A fortnight. It is very sudden.
The preparations, the announcements...

DUKE ALARIC
Details.
Such things are for your clerks and for my stewards.
Our task is to decide. And I have done so.
Tell her. Tell Helena the date is set.
And tell her this. My patience, though considerable,
Is not a boundless ocean. It has shores.
Let her not test their limit. Is that clear?

PETRONIUS
It is, my Lord. Crystal. She will be ready.
She will be a wife worthy of your name.
I will go to her now and quell this lingering
And childish fantasy. I’ll make her see
That duty is the rock on which our world
Is built. Her love is but a passing stream.

DUKE ALARIC
See that you do. I wish to see my bride.
Perhaps tomorrow, at the evening meal.
Let her appear. Composed. Content. A vision
Of the bright future our two houses share.
Leave me now. I have letters to attend to.

[_Petronius bows low and exits. Alaric turns back to the window, a small, cruel smile on his face._]

DUKE ALARIC
The cause of love. The fools. They do not see
That love is but the currency of power,
Spent to acquire what cannot be bought
With gold or threats. It is a useful tool.
And once the tool has served its turn, it breaks.

SCENE II

[_A shadowed corridor in the servants’ quarters of the palace. Stone walls weep with damp. MARCELLUS moves cautiously, keeping to the darkest corners. He sees LYRA carrying a basket of linens and steps out._]

MARCELLUS
Lyra. A word with you.

[_Lyra starts, dropping the basket. Linens spill across the floor._]

LYRA
By the gods!
You move like a ghost, Marcellus. You near stopped
My heart from beating. What is it you want?
This is no place for you. If you are seen...

MARCELLUS
The danger’s known to me. As it is to you.
I have not slept a full night since he left.
Have you had word? Have you seen her today?
How does she fare?

LYRA
She fares as would a caged bird
That once knew the whole sky. She sits and stares.
She does not eat. Her father’s words are walls
She cannot seem to breach. She speaks of death
With a calm voice that terrifies my soul.
I fear for her. I truly do.

MARCELLUS
Then I
Bring medicine for that fear. A dose of hope.
He is safe. Cassian is safe and near.
He waits within the woods beyond the wall.
He saw you give the letter. He has read it.
And it has turned his sorrow into a sword.

[_Lyra’s eyes widen. She gathers the fallen linens with trembling hands._]

LYRA
He is near? You are sure? It is not just
A story to appease a frightened girl?

MARCELLUS
My hand on it. My life on it. He is
A changed man, Lyra. The fire in his eyes
Could burn this city to the ground. He has a task
For us. For you. He says that Helena
Must seem to bend. She must not fight her father,
But feign a slow acceptance of her fate.
A daughter’s sorrow, giving way to duty.
It is our only way to make them blind.
Their vigilance must sleep, or we are lost.
Can you tell her this? Can you make her see
The wisdom in it? She must play a part.

LYRA
To ask a burning heart to feign the coldness
Of ash and embers? It is a hard role.
But she is strong. Yes. I will tell her.
She must know he is close. That news alone
Will be the sun to thaw her winter grief.
But how will we have contact? It is death
To be caught passing letters.

MARCELLUS
We will find a way.
Leave a white flower on her balcony
When you have spoken to her and she agrees.
I’ll see it from the gardens. Then Cassian
Will know his message has been understood.
Be careful, Lyra. We walk on a spider’s thread.

LYRA
The thread is spun of loyalty and love.
It will hold fast. Now go. Before you are discovered.
I will see to the lady.

[_Marcellus gives a curt nod and melts back into the shadows. Lyra finishes gathering the linens, her movements now filled with a new and nervous energy._]

SCENE III

[_Helena’s chambers. The room is beautiful but feels like a prison. HELENA sits by a window, not looking out, but staring at her own reflection in the glass. She is pale and still. PETRONIUS enters. She does not turn._]

PETRONIUS
Helena.

HELENA
Father. You have come again
To lecture me on honour, I presume.
Or is it duty’s turn to be my tutor?
I find I have forgotten which is which.
They sound so much alike.

PETRONIUS
They are the same.
And you will learn the lesson, though it pains you.
I have had word this day from Duke Alaric.
He grows impatient with your maiden’s grief.
He is a man of action, not of sighs.

HELENA
Then let his actions take him far from here.
I am no prize for him to claim. I am
A person, not a province to be won.

PETRONIUS
You are a daughter of this house. And you
Are blind to what this union signifies.
It is not about you. Or your fleeting passions.
It is about survival. It is about
A legacy that stretches back through time
And will push forward, through your future children,
If you have sense enough to see it through.
Cassian was a dream. A pleasant one, perhaps.
But morning comes. You must awake.

HELENA
My morning
Is blacker than the deepest pit of night.
The man I love is banished, called a traitor.
The man I loathe is hailed and called my lord.
You call this waking? I call this a nightmare
From which I pray that death will be my rescue.

PETRONIUS
Enough of this. This is your final warning.
The Duke has set the date. A fortnight hence.
You will be wed within this city’s temple.
You will smile. You will speak the holy vows.
And you will give this house an heir. You will
Forget the boy who scribbled poetry
And chose his heart above his own good name.
There is no other path. The choice is made.

[_Helena is silent for a long moment. She slowly turns from the window to face him. Her expression is unnervingly calm._]

HELENA
A fortnight. Twelve days hence. And then I am
To be his bride.

PETRONIUS
Fourteen days. Yes. It is decided.

HELENA
And there is nothing I can say or do
To change this course you’ve set for me? No plea?
No argument of reason or of soul?

PETRONIUS
None. Absolutely none. My mind is fixed.
As is the Duke’s.

HELENA
Then you have broken me.
I have no strength to fight you any more.
The walls are too high. My spirit is too weak.
I see the truth in what you say, though it
Is bitter as a poison on my tongue.
A daughter’s duty. Yes. I understand.
You will have your dutiful daughter, Father.
Leave me to make my peace with this new life.

[_Petronius stares at her, surprised by this sudden capitulation._]

PETRONIUS
This is wisdom speaking. I am pleased, Helena.
Truly. You will see. In time, you will
Come to respect the Duke. And you will thank me
For guiding you to safety and to power.
The Duke has asked to see you at the supper.
You will attend?

HELENA
As you command. I will attend.

PETRONIUS
Good. It is for the best.

[_He exits, a look of profound relief on his face. Helena waits until the sound of his footsteps has faded. Her calm expression crumbles, and she buries her face in her hands, a single, silent sob shaking her body._]

SCENE IV

[_The woods bordering the city. Night. The moon is a sliver. CASSIAN is crouched behind a thicket of thorns, observing the city wall. He is thinner, his courtly clothes are torn and stained. A rough beard shadows his jaw._]

CASSIAN
You sleep, you city of my birth and ruin.
You draw your walls around you like a blanket
And dream your little dreams of law and order.
But I am here, awake. A waking nightmare
That you yourself have made. You cast me out
To live among the wolves. You did not know
I would learn how to hunt. I’ve watched for days.
I know the guards. I know their names, their gaits.
I know that Marcus drinks upon his watch.
That Paulus sings sad songs to keep him warm.
I know that every dawn, the eastern gate
Groans on its hinge like some great beast in pain.
And I have found your weakness. Your dark secret.
A hidden history you have forgotten.
The old aqueduct. Its mouth choked with weeds,
A stone maw gaping, thirsty for a rain
That fell three hundred years ago. The maps
I studied in my youth, they showed its path.
It runs beneath the wall, a secret scar,
And spills into the cisterns that lie deep
Below the Magistrate’s own palace floors.
A filthy path. A rat’s road to my love.
But I will walk it. I will brave the dark,
The stagnant water and the things that dwell
Within it, if it leads me back to her.
The courtier is dead. The man of books
And gentle words is buried in the past.
The thing that remains is grim and sharp and cold.
It has one purpose. One consuming thought.
To steal the sun from a sky full of thieves.

SCENE V

[_Helena’s chambers. Later that night. Helena stands on her balcony, staring up at the moon. LYRA enters quietly from the room._]

LYRA
My lady? The hour is late. You will take a chill.

HELENA
The cold is welcome, Lyra. It feels honest.
Unlike the warmth within these palace walls.
There is a flower, Lyra. On the rail.
A white rose. Did you place it there?

LYRA
I did.
It is a signal. To a friend.

HELENA
I had
A terrible dream. That I agreed to wed the Duke.
That I told my father I would be his pawn.
And the most dreadful part of this dark dream
Was that I felt a kind of hope within it.
How could that be?

LYRA
Some dreams are sent as guides.
My lady, you have played your part so well.
Your father is convinced. He told the steward
You had returned to reason. But the hope
You felt was not a dream. It is as real
As this small note I bring you.

[_Lyra produces a tiny, tightly folded piece of paper from her sleeve._]

LYRA
From Marcellus. From him. Read it.

[_Helena takes the note with a trembling hand. She unfolds it and reads by the moonlight. Her breath catches._]

HELENA
“My heart. The night is long, but dawn is near.
Be brave. Play the dove, but keep the eagle’s soul.
I am coming. I am close. I am yours. C.”
He is alive. He has not given up.
Oh, Lyra. This small paper in my hand
Weighs more than all my father’s gold. It holds
The world.

LYRA
Then you must hold that world tight.
You must be the actor he requires.
Can you do it? Can you face the Duke at supper?

HELENA
I can. Yes. I can do more than that.
I can look him in the eye and let him see
A future bride. I can let my father think
He has at last defeated my poor heart.
They wish to see a dove? Then they shall have one.
But they will never know its wings are strong
And waiting for the signal to take flight.

SCENE VI

[_The woods. By the overgrown entrance to the old aqueduct. A few nights later. The air is thick with the smell of damp earth and decay. CASSIAN is clearing away vines from the stone archway when MARCELLUS arrives, moving silently._]

CASSIAN
Marcellus. Your news. I saw the flower.
She knows? She has agreed to play the part?

MARCELLUS
She plays it like a master of the stage.
She sat at supper with the Duke himself.
So pale, so quiet. Yet so full of grace.
He is entirely fooled. Her father beams
As if he forged the sun with his own hands.
They are complacent. Swollen with their victory.
Which is the news I bring you, good and bad.

CASSIAN
Then give me the bad first. My stomach’s strong.

MARCELLUS
The wedding. They have moved the date. It is
No longer in a fortnight. It is now
But five days hence. The feast of the new moon.
They mean to waste no time. Alaric grows
More arrogant and impatient by the hour.

[_Cassian stops his work, his face hardening in the gloom._]

CASSIAN
Five days. They give me five days to tear down
The walls it took a hundred years to build.
So be it. It only sharpens my resolve.
The good news, then. Let’s have it.

MARCELLUS
Their haste has made them careless. The pre wedding feast
Is on the night before. The palace guard
Will be distracted. Half of them will drink
More than they should. Security will be
Focused upon the hall, the honoured guests.
The lower levels will be all but empty.
The cellars. The cisterns. The place where this...
This foul old channel surfaces.

CASSIAN
The cistern room. I know it. There is a spiral stair
That leads up to the servant’s corridors
Behind the kitchens.

MARCELLUS
Yes. And I have done
What you have asked. I bribed the quartermaster,
A man whose thirst for coin exceeds his loyalty.
He thinks I wish to smuggle wine, not men.
For a heavy purse, he has supplied a key.
This key.

[_Marcellus hands a large, rusted iron key to Cassian._]

MARCELLUS
It opens the service door at the top
Of that same spiral stair. The door is old,
And rarely used. It will not be observed.
On the night of the feast, I will ensure
The corridor is clear. I will be waiting.

CASSIAN
Then the plan holds. That night, while they are feasting,
I will make my own journey through the dark.
I will ascend from the city’s forgotten gut
And find my way to her. You must get word
To Lyra. Helena must feign a sickness,
A sudden faintness from the wine and heat.
She must retire to her chambers early,
Before the feast has ended. She must be alone.

MARCELLUS
Lyra will see to it. Her wits are sharp.
But Cassian, what then? You reach her room.
You are one man, inside a guarded palace.
How do you mean to then escape the city?

CASSIAN
Escape? My friend, you still mistake my goal.
I am not here to flee into the night
With Helena in tow, to live in hiding.
I am here to reclaim what is my own.
My life. My name. And my beloved bride.
The Duke will fall. But not by my own hand.
His pride will be the weapon I shall use.

MARCELLUS
I do not understand.

CASSIAN
You do not need to.
You need only to open that one door.
Leave the rest to me. Now go. We have but
Four nights to wait until the die is cast.

[_Marcellus clasps Cassian’s shoulder, then vanishes back into the trees. Cassian holds the iron key, turning it over in his hand as he stares into the black mouth of the aqueduct._]

SCENE VII

[_The great hall of the palace. The night of the pre wedding feast. The hall is ablaze with candlelight, filled with music and the chatter of richly dressed LORDS and LADIES. At the head table sit DUKE ALARIC, PETRONIUS, and HELENA._]

DUKE ALARIC
A toast! A toast to this fair city’s jewel!
My perfect bride, the Lady Helena!
Her beauty is surpassed only by her
Humility and grace. A quiet spirit,
Which is a quality most prized in wives.
Tomorrow, we are joined. And with our union,
A new age of prosperity begins!
To the future!

LORDS AND LADIES
To the future!

[_They all drink. Helena barely touches the goblet to her lips. She is dressed in white and silver, looking ethereal and untouchable. Her eyes are fixed on a point in the distance._]

PETRONIUS
(Leaning towards Helena)
My daughter, you are quiet this evening.
The Duke has paid you tribute. You should smile.

HELENA
Forgive me, Father. The music and the heat...
I feel a little faint. It is overwhelming.

DUKE ALARIC
Nonsense. It is the joy of the occasion.
It can be quite a dizzying affair.
Here, drink some more wine. It will steady you.

[_He gestures for a servant to refill her goblet. As he does, HELENA catches LYRA’s eye. Lyra stands near a pillar, watching. She gives an almost imperceptible nod. Helena sways slightly in her seat, placing a hand to her forehead._]

HELENA
Your grace is kind. But I fear I must
Retire. I would not wish to swoon and make
A scene upon this perfect night. If you’ll
Excuse me.

PETRONIUS
Helena, you cannot leave.

DUKE ALARIC
Let her go, Petronius. It is better
She rests tonight, so she is fresh and radiant
For the main ceremony on the morrow.
Go, my dear. Your handmaiden will attend you.
Dream of our future.

[_Helena rises, curtsies deeply to the Duke, and then, without looking at her father, walks gracefully from the hall. LYRA follows a few steps behind. The music swells again. Alaric and Petronius resume their conversation, laughing. The feast continues, loud and oblivious, as the real drama prepares to unfold in the silent corridors above and the dark tunnels below._]

SCENE VIII

[_Helena’s chambers. The room is lavishly appointed, but feels like a cage. Moonlight streams through a large, barred window. LYRA is bolting the heavy oak door from the inside._]

HELENA
He is secure. The bolt is thrown and strong?

LYRA
As strong as any lock in this cold place.
But bolts will not deter a fearful mind.
Your father may yet come and beat it down.
We must be swift. The night does not stand still.

HELENA
My heart beats like a trapped and frantic bird.
To stand before them all, to lie, to feign…
I thought my legs would fail me on the stone.
The Duke’s eyes, Lyra. They devour the light.
He looks at me as if I were a prize,
A thing of polished gold he has acquired.

LYRA
And that is why your legs did not give way.
You are no spoil of war, no golden cup.
You are your mother’s daughter, fierce and true.
Now, cast this wedding gown upon the floor.
It is a shroud. You will not wear it more.

[_Helena pulls at the silver threads of her gown, her movements frantic at first, then deliberate. Lyra helps her out of it, and it pools on the floor like melted silver. Lyra produces a bundle of dark, simple clothing: a woolen tunic, breeches, and a cloak._]

HELENA
How did you find these clothes?

LYRA
The stable boys
Have many fears, but loyalty is one.
A coin, a promise, and a memory
Of your good mother’s kindness to his kin
Was all it took to purchase these dark weeds
And his convenient blindness for one hour.
Now dress. The moon is climbing to its peak.
That is the signal Cassian awaits.

[_Helena dresses quickly. The simple clothes seem to give her a strength the fine gown did not._]

HELENA
And the guards outside? The Duke’s own men?
They patrol this hall at every bell.

LYRA
They do. And they expect a silent night.
A sleeping bride who dreams of her new lord.
They will not think to look for shadows flitting
Where no shadows ought to be. But still,
We must be careful. Sound will be our foe.

[_There is a sharp, heavy rap at the door._]

VOICE (O.S.)
My lady? The Duke commands me to inquire
About your health. Are you recovered now?

[_Helena freezes, her eyes wide with terror. Lyra puts a finger to her lips, her expression calm and sharp._]

LYRA
(Whispering)
It is Valerius. The Sergeant. Cunning.
Let me speak. Stay back, away from the door.

[_Lyra approaches the door._]

LYRA
(Calling through the door)
Who is it that disturbs my lady’s rest?
She has retired, as the Duke allowed.

VALERIUS (O.S.)
Sergeant Valerius. His Grace is concerned.
He asks if you require the court physician.
He would not have his bride unwell tomorrow.

LYRA
The lady sleeps. A fever from the heat,
No more. I have prepared a calming draft
Of poppy milk and lavender. She will
Be well by morning. You may tell the Duke
His fears are needless.

VALERIUS (O.S.)
Open up the door.
I must see for myself that all is well.
These are my orders. Do not disobey.

HELENA
(Whispering)
Lyra, what can we do? We are undone!

LYRA
(Whispering)
Patience. A man like this one trusts his eyes,
But not his wits. He sees what he expects.

[_Lyra unbolts the door, but opens it only a sliver, blocking the entrance with her own body. SERGEANT VALERIUS, a stern man in ducal livery, stands outside, flanked by two guards._]

LYRA
Sergeant, you overstep. My lady is
Indisposed. It is not fit for men
To enter now. Would you defy all custom?

VALERIUS
I would defy a hangman for the Duke.
Step aside, woman. Let me see the bride.

LYRA
She is abed. The sleeping draft was strong.
To wake her now would spoil its potent charm
And leave her pale and trembling for the priest.
Would you wish to explain that to the Duke?
That his new Duchess looks a haggard ghost
Because you chose to burst into her room?

[_Valerius hesitates, frowning. He tries to peer past Lyra into the darkened room. He can just make out the shape of the bed, with a form beneath the covers, and the white and silver gown discarded on the floor._]

VALERIUS
Hmm. You say she sleeps? I hear no sound.

LYRA
It is the poppy milk. She will not stir
Until the morning bells. It is a balm
Her mother used. A secret of our house.
Now, if you are satisfied, my lord,
My lady’s health requires peace and quiet.

[_Valerius stares at her for a long moment, his eyes narrowed in suspicion. Lyra meets his gaze without flinching._]

VALERIUS
Very well. But I will post a guard
Directly here, outside this very door.
No one enters, and no one is to leave
Until the Duke himself shall send for her.
Is that understood?

LYRA
Completely, Sergeant.
We would not dream of leaving. You have my word.

[_Valerius gives one last, searching look, then nods curtly. He steps back. Lyra closes the door and throws the bolt again, her back pressed against the wood, her eyes closed in relief._]

HELENA
My blood ran cold as ice. I thought for sure…

LYRA
His pride is just as thick as his master’s is.
He could not bear the shame of being wrong,
Of making such a fuss for just a girl
Who has the vapours. Now he has his story.
He came, he saw, he was assured. Come now.
His new precaution is our new problem.

[_Lyra moves to a large tapestry on the far wall, one depicting a forest hunt._]

HELENA
What are you doing?

LYRA
The guard is at the door.
Which means he is not watching from the wall.
This palace is a honeycomb of paths.
The new Duke builds on top, all gilt and stone,
But he forgets the passages below,
The ways the servants used in older days
When masters were more trusted by their staff.

[_She pulls at the edge of the tapestry, revealing not stone, but a slender, dark wooden door, almost invisible against the wall’s timber frame._]

HELENA
A secret door? Here, all this time?

LYRA
Your mother showed it to me long ago.
She said a woman, even in a palace,
Always needs a path that is her own.
She little knew how right she was to be.

[_Lyra produces a small, ornate key from a pouch at her belt. She inserts it into a hidden lock._]

The lock was rusted. I have spent a week
With oil and patience making it comply.
It leads down to the pantry service stairs,
And from there, to the cellars. And beyond.
Are you prepared?

[_Helena looks from the bolted main door, where a guard now stands, to the dark, narrow opening Lyra has revealed. She takes a deep breath, her fear replaced by a cold resolve._]

HELENA
I am. Let us descend.
My future is not here. It waits below.

[_Lyra swings the door open into blackness. She takes a small oil lamp, lights it, and hands it to Helena. They slip through the opening, and Lyra pulls the door silently closed behind them. The tapestry falls back into place, hiding all trace of their escape. The lavish room is empty, save for a silver gown shimmering in the moonlight._]

SCENE IX

[_The main tunnel of the old aqueduct. The air is thick with the smell of damp earth and decay. Water drips from the vaulted stone ceiling, the sound echoing in the oppressive darkness. CASSIAN stands alone, holding a single hooded lantern. The small pool of light illuminates his tense face and the iron key in his hand._]

CASSIAN
Four nights. Four years. A lifetime in the dark.
Each second stretches, breaks upon the wheel
Of this long waiting. Did she get my word?
Does Lyra’s courage hold? Or does the plan,
So finely wrought, now fray upon the loom
Of some unseen and cruel mischance of fate?
Come, Marcellus. Come, my only friend.
Bring me some news, or I shall turn to stone
Like these cold walls that weep for what is lost.

[_Footsteps echo from down the tunnel. Cassian raises the lantern, his other hand going to the hilt of a short sword tucked in his belt. MARCELLUS emerges from the gloom._]

MARCELLUS
Put up your steel. It is no enemy.
Though I confess, this place would make a ghost
Of any living man. The air is foul.

CASSIAN
It is the air of freedom, my good friend.
It smells far sweeter than a palace cell.
What is the word? The feast is at its height?

MARCELLUS
The feast is done. The Duke is deep in wine,
Toasting a future that will never dawn.
The Lady Helena has left the hall,
Pleading a sudden illness of the nerves.
Her handmaid Lyra followed. All is set.
They should be on their way.

CASSIAN
She played her part.
My brave Helena. And what of the rest?
The men you spoke to? Will they stand with us?

MARCELLUS
They will not fight. They are not soldiers, Cassian.
They are the city’s heart. The merchants, smiths,
The men whose fortunes Alaric has crushed
With his new taxes and his cruel decrees.
They loved your father. They remember you.
They will be in the chapel on the morn,
Dressed as wedding guests. When you appear,
They will not draw a sword, but they will shout.
Their voices will protect you, give you time
To speak the truth before the Duke’s guard cuts
You down. It is the best that I could do.

CASSIAN
It is enough. A thousand angry shouts
Are stronger than a shield. The Duke’s great fear
Is not a blade, but his own people’s rage.
He rules by terror, not by their consent.
Once that is broken, he is but a man.
A thief who wears a crown. Did you acquire it?

[_Marcellus reaches into his tunic and pulls out a heavy, tarnished object. He hands it to Cassian._]

MARCELLUS
The old curator of the city vault,
His wife was cousin to my mother’s maid.
He owed my family a long forgotten debt.
He thought this lost, melted for ducal coin,
But it was merely tossed into a chest
With other relics of your father’s reign.

[_Cassian takes the object. He holds it up to the lantern light. It is a large signet ring, bearing the crest of his house: a falcon in flight._]

CASSIAN
The ring of my father. And his father’s, too.
They told the world I drowned in the great storm,
That this was pulled from my own lifeless hand
Before the sea could claim my corpse. Another lie
To seal my tomb and build his throne upon.
When I hold this aloft tomorrow, all
Will know the truth. A ghost has come for justice.

[_A faint scraping sound is heard from further down a side passage._]

MARCELLUS
What was that?

CASSIAN
It is the door. They have arrived.

[_He moves quickly towards the sound, raising his lantern high. A low, iron-bound door set in the curved wall groans open. HELENA steps through, holding her small lamp, followed by LYRA, who closes the door behind them._]

HELENA
Cassian!

CASSIAN
Helena. My love. At last.

[_He crosses the space in three strides and takes her in his arms. The lanterns they hold cast a warm, dancing light on their faces. It is a desperate, heartfelt embrace._]

HELENA
I thought this moment was a foolish dream.
I feared I would not see your face again
Except in memory. They said you were gone.

CASSIAN
I am not gone. I am returned. For you.
Forgive me for the years of silence, love.
To send a word was to endanger you,
To place your life within his grasping hands.
I had to wait. To let him think me dead,
To let his arrogance become his shield,
And now, his weakness.

HELENA
(Pulling back to look at him)
You are changed. The sun
And hardship have rewritten you. But your eyes…
They are the same.

CASSIAN
And yours still hold the stars.
There will be time for us. I swear it now.
But this one night, we serve a harsher cause.
You were magnificent, from what Marcellus said.
You played the timid bride without a flaw.

HELENA
My fear was not an act. It chills me still.
He plans to post a guard outside my door
Until the morning. We escaped just moments
Before the passage would have been discovered.

MARCELLUS
Then we must move. This tunnel has two ends.
One leads to freedom in the outer woods.
The other, deeper in, beneath the palace,
Emerges in the crypt below the chapel.

HELENA
The crypt? You mean to go back in? I thought…
I thought we were to flee. To find a life
Somewhere beyond his reach.

CASSIAN
That is a life
Of hiding, Helena. Of looking back
Over our shoulders every waking day.
I will not live like that. I will not have
You live like that. I am no traitor. He is.
And all the world will know it by midday.

LYRA
My lord, it is a risk beyond belief.
To walk back into the lion’s maw…

CASSIAN
The lion is distracted. He prepares
A wedding. He expects a weeping bride,
Not an accuser rising from the grave.
Helena, this plan cannot succeed
Without your strength. I need you now to be
Much more than just the woman that I love.
I need you to be brave for one more hour.

HELENA
What must I do?

CASSIAN
When you are at the altar,
And the High Priest speaks the holy words,
He will ask if any man has cause
Why you and Alaric should not be joined.
At that moment, all of heaven and earth
Will hold its breath. And in that silence,
You must not answer him. You must look past
The Duke. Towards the chapel’s great main doors.
Your eyes will be my signal and my shield.
Can you do that for me? For us?

[_Helena looks at his earnest face, at the stolen ring he clutches in his hand. Her fear is still present, but a new fire is kindled in her eyes._]

HELENA
I can. And I will.
Let him not have my voice, my vow, my name.
I will look to the doors and wait for you.

CASSIAN
Good. Marcellus, Lyra, you will take her now.
The passage to the crypt is safe, but long.
I will wait here, for some men who are late,
And follow you. Be watchful. Go.

[_Marcellus nods. Lyra takes Helena’s arm gently._]

LYRA
Come, my lady. The final act begins.

[_Helena gives Cassian one last look, a universe of hope and fear passing between them. Then she turns and follows Marcellus and Lyra into the deeper darkness of the tunnel. Cassian watches them go, then turns his gaze back towards the aqueduct’s entrance, his face set like flint._]

SCENE X

[_The Duke’s private study. Later that night. DUKE ALARIC is pouring himself a goblet of deep red wine from a crystal decanter. PETRONIUS paces nervously before the hearth, where a low fire burns._]

DUKE ALARIC
Do stop your fretting, Petronius. You wear
A groove into my finest marble floor.
The night is perfect. The city is quiet.
The nobles are all drunk on my good wine.
And soon, your daughter will be my good wife.
All is as it should be. Sit. Drink with me.

PETRONIUS
Forgive me, Your Grace. I cannot be still.
Helena’s sudden illness… it feels like
A poor omen on the eve of such joy.

DUKE ALARIC
An omen? Nonsense. It is the way of women.
They are such fragile, finely crafted things.
The scale of the event, the weight of her
Good fortune, it simply overwhelmed her.
It is a sign of her delicate nature,
A quality I find most appealing.
She will be radiant tomorrow. My sergeant
Assured me she was sleeping like a babe.

PETRONIUS
But to refuse you, to leave the feast…
It shows a willfulness I did not know
She had. It troubles me.

DUKE ALARIC
(He laughs, a short, barking sound)
Willfulness? I call it spirit. And it
Is a spirit I look forward to breaking.
Like a fine horse, she must be taught the rein.
And you, my friend, should be celebrating!
Our long campaign is finally complete.
The last great house that could have challenged me
Is now allied. Your lands and mine are one.
The city is secure. Cassian is dust.
His name a half forgotten traitor’s curse.
We have won everything.

PETRONIUS
We have. And yet…
To win by such deceit. To slander a boy
Who was your ward. To steal his name, his lands,
And now, the woman that he was to wed.
Sometimes at night, I see his father’s face,
Your brother, staring at me from the dark.
He trusted me. And I betrayed his son.

DUKE ALARIC
My brother was a fool. A gentle soul
Who thought that kindness was a way to rule.
He would have lost this city to our rivals
Within a year. And Cassian was his son,
Cut from the same soft, sentimental cloth.
I did what was required. I forged a letter,
Implicating him in a foreign plot.
I paid two witnesses to swear they saw him
Meeting with our enemies. It was
A simple, elegant, and neat design.
The storm that took his ship was just a gift
From the kind gods of sheer coincidence.

PETRONIUS
You speak of it so lightly. It was treason.

DUKE ALARIC
(His voice drops, becoming cold and hard)
I am the Duke. What I decree is law.
What I declare as treason, becomes treason.
Do not forget your place in this, Petronius.
Your hands are just as deep in this as mine.
You stood beside me in the council hall
And gave your voice to his condemnation.
Your fear, your greed, they made you my accomplice.
So do not trouble me with your late pangs
Of useless conscience. You have what you wished.
Your house is safe. Your daughter is a Duchess.
Be content with that.

[_A knock comes at the door. Sergeant Valerius enters._]

VALERIUS
Your Grace. Forgive the intrusion.

DUKE ALARIC
What is it, man? Can you not see I am
At leisure?

VALERIUS
A report from the western watch.
The guards near the old aqueduct have heard
Strange noises from within the tunnels. Echoes.
Like voices, and the scrape of stone on stone.

PETRONIUS
The aqueduct? But that has been sealed up
For twenty years!

DUKE ALARIC
(Waving a dismissive hand)
It is the river rats.
They grow as large as hounds in those old pipes.
Or it is vagrants, seeking shelter from the cold.
Send a patrol at dawn to clear them out.
I will not have my wedding day disturbed
By squeaking vermin, be they men or beasts.
Is that all, Sergeant?

VALERIUS
Yes, Your Grace. That is all.

[_Valerius bows and exits._]

DUKE ALARIC
You see? The world is full of trivial things.
Rats in a pipe. A girl with trembling nerves.
A councilman who has mislaid his spine.
These are the matters that command my state.
It is almost laughable. Come now, old friend.
One final toast. To my magnificent bride.
And to the long, untroubled, peaceful sleep
Of all our enemies.

[_He raises his goblet, his face a mask of supreme, unshakeable confidence. Petronius, his face pale with dread, reluctantly raises his own._]

SCENE XI

[_The crypt beneath the palace chapel. The air is cold and still. The only light comes from a single lantern held by MARCELLUS, casting long, dancing shadows across ancient stone tombs. The faint, muffled sound of an organ playing a prelude can be heard from the chapel above. HELENA, LYRA, and a small group of MARCELLUS’S MEN are gathered. CASSIAN stands before them. He is no longer dressed as a fugitive, but in the dark, noble attire of his house, the falcon crest embroidered over his heart. The signet ring is on his finger._]

CASSIAN
The music has begun. The guests arrive.
Soon, he will come to claim what is not his,
To stand upon that holy ground and weave
The final thread of his great tapestry of lies.
But we are here to pull that thread, and watch
It all unravel.

MARCELLUS
My men are in their places in the crowd.
They know the signal. They will block the guards,
But they will not draw first. This must be won
With truth, not swords.

CASSIAN
(To Lyra)
Lyra, your loyalty has been a light
In the darkest years. I owe you more
Than I can say.

LYRA
You owe me nothing, my lord.
I serve the daughter of the woman who
Was sister to my soul. I do this for her.
And for a memory of better days.
I will be near the side door. When the moment
Is right, I will unbar it, to provide
A path, should we have need of one.

CASSIAN
Thank you.

[_He turns to Helena. She is pale but composed, dressed in a simple dark gown. She looks more like a queen than she ever did in silks and silver._]

CASSIAN
Are you afraid?

HELENA
My heart is a stone.
I do not know if it is courage or
That I have passed beyond all fear. I see
The path ahead. I will not turn from it.

CASSIAN
Look only at me when I enter. Do not
Look at him. His rage will be a storm.
Find my eyes and anchor your soul there.
I will not let you fall.

HELENA
I know you will not.
Go. Rise up from this place of death and silence.
And bring the truth with you into the light.

[_From above, the sound of trumpets blares, announcing the Duke’s arrival in the chapel. The organ music swells in power and majesty. The sound vibrates through the stone floor of the crypt._]

MARCELLUS
It is time. This passage leads to a small
Antechamber behind the altar. From there,
A single curtain separates you from
The chapel proper. Wait for the High Priest.
His words will be your cue.

CASSIAN
The die is cast.
Whatever comes, we face it now together.
Not as a traitor, fugitive, and pawn,
But as the rightful lord of this domain,
His loyal friends, and his beloved bride.
Let us go forth and wake them from their dream.

[_Cassian leads the way to a narrow stone staircase spiraling up into darkness. Marcellus follows, then his men. Helena and Lyra exchange a final, determined glance. The triumphant music from the world above rains down upon them, a sound no longer of a wedding, but of a coming reckoning._]
