What wounds are these, good friend? A heavy blow,
But not unto the heart, I pray?
(He speaks to a young soldier clutching his side.)

SOLDIER A
A cruel thrust, my Prince, from Grakon blade.
It bites deep, yet my breath still fills my lungs.
I saw you, Prince, against their chieftain fierce!
A lion's roar within a Prince's frame!
You saved the Blackwood, and you slew the beast!
For such a sight, my pain doth seem as naught!

PRINCE JULIAN
Your courage shames my sorrow, worthy man.
Hold fast, the healers come. You fought with skill,
And live to tell the tale. We owe you much.
(He moves on, his gaze falling upon another group. He sees a veteran soldier, his arm badly broken, biting back a cry of pain as a makeshift splint is applied.)
Old Bran! Is that you, friend? By all the gods,
Your arm hangs twisted. Does it pain you sore?

SOLDIER BRAN
Aye, Prince, a mighty ache, but I have known
Worse bites than this from winter's icy tooth.
'Twill heal, I warrant, with a good strong ale
And time to mend. We broke them, Prince! We broke them!
I saw their faces, pale with fear, as you
Did strike down Mangor. That was a sight to see!
A deed for bards to sing in halls of kings!

PRINCE JULIAN
Your loyalty warms me, Bran. Rest well, old friend.
Your deeds this day shall not be soon forgot.
(Julian continues his rounds, sharing a word, a look, a gesture of comfort with each man he encounters. His presence seems to lend a strange solace, a balm to the weary and the hurt.)

VALERIUS
(To The Blackwood, watching Julian)
He learns the trade of kingship, does he not?
To balance mercy with necessity,
A delicate and fraught affair. His heart
Is sound, his will, though young, begins to steel.
He'll make a worthy monarch, come his time.
This Mangor's death has marked him, Blackwood.
The innocence is gone, replaced by grim
Experience. 'Tis the crucible of war
That forges princes into rightful kings.

THE BLACKWOOD
He feels the weight of life and death, Valerius.
That is well. A king who does not feel
The burden of his people, is no king,
But merely a figurehead, a painted doll.
Yet feeling must not paralyse the hand,
Nor dim the eye that sees the wider field.
He hath the spirit. Now must he acquire
The cold resolve that separates the just
From those who falter. There is much to learn.
The Grakons, though they flee, are not dissolved.
They breed like vermin, multiply in gloom.
This Mangor was but one tooth in their maw.
Their fangs remain. And deeper in the east,
The whispers grow of older, fouler things,
That stir from ancient slumber. This one fight
Is but a skirmish in a greater war.
We must prepare, not merely for their return,
But for what stirs beyond the Sunken Marches.

VALERIUS
You speak of shadows, Blackwood, known to few.
What ancient evils do you portend?
Your words are ever shrouded in a mist
Of dread and mystery. Speak plainly, friend.
What deeper threats now gather in the dark?

THE BLACKWOOD
The Grakons are the hounds, Valerius.
But who doth loose their leash? And what dark hand
Doth point their way towards our peaceful lands?
Mangor was strong, a chieftain fierce and proud,
But lacked the cunning of a master mind.
These raids, these sudden surges from the east,
They bear a pattern, not of savage whim,
But of design. For years, the Grakons fought
Among themselves, a fractured, squabbling horde.
Now, they unite beneath a single banner,
Their purpose honed, their fury sharper still.
Someone has stirred them, given them a cause,
Beyond their primal hunger. I have seen
Strange sigils carved on stones near border camps,
Not Grakon marks, but older, darker script.
And tales of shadows, moving through the woods,
Of beasts that walk like men, and men who howl
Like beasts, with eyes like coals from deepest hell.
The folk beyond the marches whisper tales
Of a Necromancer, one who weaves with death,
And draws his power from the ancient tombs
Of forgotten kings, awakening their hosts.
This Mangor may have been his puppet, sir,
A bloody pawn to test our strength and will.

PRINCE JULIAN
(Returning from attending the wounded, his face now more resolute, though still pensive)
A Necromancer? Such fell magic, Blackwood,
Is but the stuff of ancient, fearful legends.
Do you truly hold such monstrous claims as truth?
That Mangor was a tool? What purpose then,
Beyond mere conquest, drives this hidden foe?
And where doth such a sorcerer reside?

THE BLACKWOOD
Truth, Prince, is often stranger than the myths
That cling to childhood fancies. I have trod
The wilder paths, where shadows stretch and cling,
And seen enough to know that evil walks
In many forms. As for his purpose, Prince,
Power is its own dark end. To gather strength,
To dominate, to sow despair and fear.
His lair? It lies beyond the Whisperwood,
Where mountains rise like giants to the sky,
And ancient fortresses, long lost to man,
Now stir with fell enchantment. There, they say,
The Veil between the living and the dead
Is thin, a gossamer that evil tears.
This bridge, though won, is but a single stone
In a wall that must be built against such night.
We have but bruised the serpent, not cut off
Its wicked head.

VALERIUS
If what you say is true,
Then our victory here, though sweet, is brief.
A Necromancer? This is grave news indeed.
Such dark arts could corrupt the very land.
This changes all. Our strategy must shift
From merely holding passes to confronting
A power far more subtle, more malign.
We must inform the King, and swiftly too.
This war is not against a simple horde
Of Grakons anymore, but something vast,
Unnatural. My Prince, your first great trial
Was but a prelude. The true storm now brews.

PRINCE JULIAN
Then let it brew! For if these tales are true,
And darkness seeks to swallow up our world,
Then we must stand against it, come what may!
My sorrow for the fallen will remain,
A constant reminder of the price of strife,
But it shall not impede my will to fight,
Nor dim the fire of my resolve. If death
Doth walk by magic, raising up the slain
To serve a wicked master, then our cause
Is all the holier, our purpose pure.
To fight for life against unholy death,
To guard the living from the cold embrace
Of animated corpses, that is a war
Worth waging, with every drop of blood we own.
We will send word to father, urgently.
And for the Grakons that now flee the field,
Let no man think their terror is complete.
A beaten beast may turn to savage bite.
We will pursue them, yes, but cautiously,
To learn their refuge, and to gauge their strength.
Let Captain Thorne take twenty mounted men,
The swiftest riders, skilled in tracking game.
Bid him ride east, but keep a cautious distance,
And bring us word of where the Grakon main
Doth seek to gather. He must be back by dawn,
Or send a swift dispatch.

(A sturdy, watchful captain, who had been overseeing the care of the horses, steps forward.)

CAPTAIN THORNE
My Prince, I stand ready to ride.
My men are swift, our horses fresh and strong.
We'll bring you tidings, or we shall not return.
The Grakons' trail is clear, a bloody path.
We'll follow it, though shadows fill the woods.

PRINCE JULIAN
Go swiftly, Thorne, and with a wary eye.
The Blackwood speaks of dangers more profound
Than Grakon swords. Watch for strange signs,
For carvings not of savage, crude design,
But symbols dark and ancient. And if you
Should see a flicker of unnatural light,
Or hear a sound that chills the very bone,
Come back at once, and bring us every detail.
Your lives are precious, captain, do not risk
Them needlessly. Reconnaissance, not battle,
Is your true task. May fortune guide your way.

CAPTAIN THORNE
I hear and obey, my Prince. To horse, my lads!

(Captain Thorne rallies his men, who quickly mount their horses, stirrups jingling, and ride off into the gathering gloom of the eastern forest. The last rays of the setting sun paint the western sky in hues of orange and purple, casting long, eerie shadows across the battle-scarred bridge.)

VALERIUS
He commands with clarity and foresight.
The shadow of the crown sits well upon
His youthful brow. This Necromancer threat,
If true, requires an urgent council, Prince.
The King must know, and all his loyal Lords.
Our forces here are strong, yet not enough
To combat ancient magic and the dead.
We need more levies, more seasoned swords,
And perhaps the wisdom of our learned scribes,
Who delve in lore of old and forgotten things.
This bridge must be a fortress, for a time,
A bastion against the growing night.
I shall arrange for watchtowers to be raised,
For palisades to bar the eastern approach.
And messengers must ride to every corner
Of our kingdom, to warn of what approaches.
We stand upon a precipice, my Prince.
The world as we have known it, is at stake.

PRINCE JULIAN
Then let us stand as one, and face the threat
With courage tempered by our love for home.
The men are weary, Lord, and much in need
Of rest and succour. Let them eat and drink,
And bind their wounds. We hold the bridge tonight.
Tomorrow's dawn will bring us further plans,
And greater strength. But for this night, Valerius,
Let vigilance be our shield, and hope our star.
I would inspect the fallen once again,
And say a prayer for each forgotten soul.
Their names may fade from memory, but their deeds
Are writ in heavens high, and in my heart.
Let torches be lit across the bridge,
To banish shadows, and to guide the hands
That tend the injured and prepare the dead.
No Grakon carrion shall lie beside
A Valian hero, even in this gloom.
Separate them, Sergeant, with all respect.

(Sergeant Alaric, having returned, nods grimly and relays the Prince's commands. Torches are soon lit, casting a flickering, unsteady light upon the bridge, highlighting the gruesome scene with dramatic shadows. The work continues, a silent, macabre dance of the living among the dead.)

THE BLACKWOOD
The Prince grows swiftly, Valerius.
His heart is strong, his mind begins to grasp
The deeper currents of this brutal world.
He does not shy from duty, nor from pain.
A good sign, for the times that lie ahead
Will test the strongest spirit. I shall take
The eastern flank myself tonight. No Grakon,
Nor creature born of darkness, shall pass through.
My axe is sharp, my watch is ever keen.

VALERIUS
And I, the western, where our weary men
Will rest more soundly, knowing you are near.
A quiet night, I pray, for all our sakes.
But fear not, Blackwood, for my own old bones
Are not so brittle that they cannot wield
A sword against the dark. This bridge, my friend,
Is more than stone and wood. It is a symbol
Of our resolve, our kingdom's frontier bold.
And we shall hold it, come what may, till dawn.

(Valerius claps The Blackwood on the shoulder, a rare gesture of familiarity from the usually reserved general. The Blackwood merely nods, his gaze already fixed on the dark expanse of the eastern woods, his powerful form silhouetted by the flickering torchlight. Prince Julian stands near the edge of the bridge, watching the waters flow beneath, his youthful face now bearing the heavy lines of a man who has witnessed too much, too soon. The sounds of the aftermath of battle continue: the groans of the wounded, the hushed commands of the officers, the rhythmic splash of Grakon bodies being cast into the river, and the distant, mournful cries of night birds in the forest. A chill wind sweeps across the bridge, carrying the scent of blood and dust, a grim herald of the trials to come.)

PRINCE JULIAN
(To himself, barely audible above the sounds of the night)
The cost of victory, a heavy toll.
Each life a story, now abruptly closed.
And for what end? To merely hold a span
Of stone? If Blackwood's words are truly cast
From truth, then this small triumph is a whisper
Against a shout of horrors yet to rise.
A Necromancer! Magic foul and dark,
To twist the very fabric of our world.
My father's kingdom, built on honour, peace,
Now threatened by a power so malign,
It beggars faith. What strength have we to face
Such ancient evils? My young hands, though stained
With Mangor's blood, feel weak against the weight
Of such a burden. Yet, I am a Prince.
And royalty is not a gilded cage
Of comfort, but a vow, a sacred trust.
To shield my people, with my very life,
From every threat, be it of steel or spell.
The laughter of the children, the gentle touch
Of lovers, the wisdom of the aged folk,
The quiet peace of hearths, the fertile fields,
All these depend upon this bridge, this stand.
This night, I choose my path. No longer boy
Who dreams of glory, but a man who bears
The crown's harsh burden. Let the shadows fall.
Let ancient evils stir from their deep sleep.
The Valians shall not yield. We shall not break.
For honour, for our people, for the light,
We stand. And if we fall, then we shall fall
As titans, grappling with the very night.
My heart is heavy, but my spirit keen.
The war has just begun.

(Prince Julian turns from the bridge, his youthful frame now imbued with a newfound resolution. He walks towards where Valerius and The Blackwood stand, their figures like pillars in the torchlight, ready to face the long, grim watch of the night. The moon begins to rise, a pale, watchful eye in the star-dusted sky, casting an ethereal glow over the scene of conflict and sorrow, promising neither peace nor rest, but only the relentless march of time and the endless demands of war.)

(FADE TO BLACK)
