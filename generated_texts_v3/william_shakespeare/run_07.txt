The folk who dwell on the plains must be recalled,
Or find safe harbour in the nearest towns,
Or gather here beneath our castle's shield.
And what we cannot move, we must render useless.
Wells poisoned? No, that is a cruel extreme,
That might boomerang upon our own retreat.
But granaries emptied, and their timbers burned,
Water sources diverted, or choked with stone.
This is a bitter task, but one that saves
Our people from the sword, and starves the foe.
Kaelan, you are the man to lead this grim harvest.
Take half your fighting force, those swift of foot,
And strong of back. Work with the local reeves.
Bring in all you can, or spoil what remains.
But do not engage the enemy's vanguard.
Avoid all contact. Your mission is logistics,
Not battle. Return before the morrow's dawn.

KAELAN
To play the farmer, not the warrior?
My King, my heart desires to draw my sword!
But I obey. To feed our people, starve the foe,
This too is battle. I shall act with speed.
My men shall sweep the plains like winter winds,
Leaving no sustenance for Fenris's maw.
(Kaelan bows, his face still reflecting a warrior's impatience, but his resolve to obey clear.)

VALERIUS
A wise decree, my King, though harsh its sting.
To deny the foe sustenance is to wage
A war of attrition, slow but sure.
Whilst Kaelan gathers in the harvest grim,
I shall inspect the outer bulwarks, sire,
And rally all our engineers and masons.
We must shore up our weakest points, prepare
Our murder-holes and boiling oil supplies.
The arrow slits must be made clear and wide,
And every parapet secured and strong.
And then, my King, the serpent in our breast.
What of Caelanth, whilst this storm descends?

KING
(His gaze hardens once more)
He cannot be confronted now. To do so
Would risk exposing all, and drawing factions
Into open enmity, at a time
When unity is our only hope of life.
But neither can he be allowed to act.
Valerius, you must watch him. Closely, subtly.
Let none suspect he is under the Crown's eye.
His actions, his movements, his very breath,
Must be reported to you, then to me.
If he attempts to pass intelligence,
Or sabotage our efforts from within,
You must act swiftly, Valerius. Silence him.
No public trial, no clamour, no display.
A quiet apprehension. A secluded cell.
We cannot afford to waste a single man,
Or stir a single whisper of dissent,
While Fenris batters at our gates. This task,
More delicate than any other, I entrust
To your discretion, Valerius. Can you bear it?

VALERIUS
(A deep breath, his face pale)
My King, to act as judge and executioner,
Against a man of high renown, the Queen's own kin,
And do so cloaked in shadow, is a weight
That might crush lesser men. But for Eldoria,
For you, my King, and for the Queen I serve,
I shall perform this grim and silent duty.
My network, honed for years, shall be his eyes
And ears, and if he stirs to do us harm,
I shall descend upon him like the night,
And hide him from the light of mortal men.
I pray to all the gods that it comes not to pass.

KING
So do I, Valerius. So do I.
But hope is a poor shield against a blade.
Go now, both. Let swiftness be your guide.
The sun declines. We have but hours left
Before the darkness brings the foe still closer.
(Valerius and Kaelan bow deeply and exit once more. The King stands alone, his head bowed, then slowly raises it, his eyes fixed on the distant horizon.)

KING
(To himself)
Oh, Eldoria! My kingdom, my life's blood!
To stand between the darkness and the dawn,
To bear the burden of a thousand souls,
To see the shadows gather, deep and dread,
And feel the serpent's coil, the wolf's cold breath.
My father's father, and his father's sire,
Each bore this Crown with honour, and with might.
Now 'tis my turn to face the coming night.
(He walks to a chest, opens it, and removes a heavy, ornate battle-axe, its head gleaming with ancient runes. He tests its weight, a grim determination hardening his features. A sense of impending conflict hangs heavy in the air.)

(The scene shifts to a less formal chamber within the castle, perhaps a private study or an antechamber, later that evening. Lord Caelanth is seen, hunched over a desk, poring over ledgers. A small, nervous-looking clerk, MASTER FINN, stands nearby.)

MASTER FINN
My Lord Caelanth, the figures here do vex me.
The intake of silver from the western mines
Shows a decrease, yet the coin we've struck
Seems to maintain its former output. How
Can lesser raw material yield the same
Quantity of finished coin? My sums
Are clear, yet they defy this strange accounting.

CAELANTH
(Without looking up, his voice smooth and slightly dismissive)
Finn, your mind is too accustomed to the simple,
The straight lines of a merchant's easy ledger.
The Mint is a complex beast, with many mouths
To feed, and many hands that draw the wealth.
There are allowances for wastage, Finn,
For wear and tear upon the dies, for spills,
For natural loss in smelting. Did you not
Read the Royal Ordinance of forty years past,
Which grants the Master of the Mint a fraction,
A small percentage, for such common losses?
It is within the letter of the law.
And I, dear Finn, am but a humble servant
Of the law, and of the King.

MASTER FINN
But Lord, the percentage. It seems high.
And the reports from Master Perin's office,
The Treasurer, they speak of discrepancies
That far exceed such natural wastage, sir.
He has been asking questions, questions keen,
Regarding the purity of recent strikes.

CAELANTH
(He finally looks up, his eyes cold and sharp, though his smile is placid)
Master Finn, you tread on dangerous ground.
To question the integrity of the Mint,
Or worse, the honesty of those who serve it,
Is to invite the Crown's displeasure, and
To risk the accusations of ill will.
Master Perin is an old man, Finn,
His eyes perhaps not as sharp as they once were,
His memory clouded by the mists of age.
I have explained these matters to him oft.
He understands. Or claims he does. For you,
My counsel is to simply do your duty,
Record the figures as they are presented,
And leave the grander schemes to those in power.
Is that quite clear, my good and diligent Finn?

MASTER FINN
(Swallowing hard, his gaze dropping from Caelanth's)
Aye, my Lord. Crystal clear. I shall comply.
I merely sought to understand the figures.
My apologies if I have caused offence.

CAELANTH
(Smiling again, a chilling warmth in his tone)
No offence taken, Finn. Your earnestness
Is quite endearing, in its own quaint way.
Now, return to your duties. Let me not
Disturb your precious calculations further.
(Finn bows hastily and scurries out. Caelanth watches him go, his smile fading, replaced by a calculating sneer. He then pulls a hidden drawer from his desk, revealing a small, intricately carved wooden box. He opens it to reveal a folded parchment.)

CAELANTH
(To himself)
The fool. So easily swayed, so simple-minded.
They cling to honesty as to a shroud,
Unknowing that the world is ruled by wit,
By subtle craft, and by the power of coin.
Perin suspects. The King suspects. I feel
Their wary eyes upon me. But they move
With caution, lest they wound themselves in striking.
The King is bound by justice, by the law,
By foolish honour. These are but the chains
That bind a lion, whilst a fox goes free.
(He unfolds the parchment. It is a crude map, with markings for secret paths and rendezvous points. He traces a finger along one path.)

CAELANTH
Tonight. The Western Gate, an hour past midnight.
The courier from Fenris. Swift and silent.
The message: Eldoria's strength, her dwindling stores,
Her internal rifts, the King's own doubts.
And the payment, the promise of the north,
When this old kingdom crumbles at my touch.
Fenris will break their walls. I'll break their will.
And from the ashes, a new order rise,
With Caelanth at its heart. The Queen, my sister,
Will weep, perhaps, but later understand
The necessity of power, the iron grip
That makes a king, or unmaking, a new realm.
(He refolds the map, places it back in the box, and locks the drawer. He then rises and walks to a window, looking out into the night. A figure, barely discernible in the deepening gloom outside, moves swiftly past the window, then melts into the shadows of the castle courtyard. It is one of Kaelan's chosen men, beginning his watch.)

(The scene returns to the King's council chamber, hours later. The King is there, with Valerius. Kaelan enters, dust and fatigue marking his countenance.)

KING
Kaelan! You are returned. What news? What progress
In this grim harvest from the plains?

KAELAN
(Bowing, though still catching his breath)
My King, we worked as devils in the fields.
My men, with local reeves and loyal folk,
Have swept the plains of every living thing
That might sustain the foe. The cattle driven,
The sheep impounded, grain-stores emptied, then
The timber fired to leave naught but charred earth.
The wells, diverted, or choked with heavy stone.
The villages deserted, folk brought in
To safe havens, or within our castle walls.
We moved like shadows, sire, and finished all
Before the vanguard of the Northmen stirred
From their encampment. No engagement sought,
No blood was shed, save for the beasts we took.
The plains are bare, my King, a desolate waste,
From here to Fenris's camp. He shall find naught
But dust and ashes, wind and empty air.

KING
(A nod of approval, grim but satisfied)
Well done, good Kaelan. A bitter victory,
But one that buys us time, and starves the foe.
You bear the mark of toil. Go, rest now.
Prepare your men for watch upon the walls.
For soon, the Northmen will arrive in full.
And battle's grim music shall begin to play.

KAELAN
I go, my King. My sword is sharp, my men
Are ready. Eldoria shall not fall this day.
(Kaelan bows and exits. The King turns to Valerius.)

KING
And you, Valerius? Have Caelanth's movements
Given cause for further dark suspicion?

VALERIUS
My King, I have placed my most trusted eyes,
A network of observant, silent folk,
Within the castle, and around his dwelling.
One, a chambermaid, who serves him daily fare,
Reported that he paced for hours alone
This afternoon, then hunched o'er papers late,
Dismissing even his accustomed page.
Another, a kitchen boy who fetches water,
Observed a light still burning in his study
Long past the hour when honest folk retire.
No overt act, no treason stark revealed,
But such a heightened secrecy of late
Is not Caelanth's usual placid custom.
His nature is precise, but not so covert.
He hides a thing, my King, that much is clear.

KING
He prepares, Valerius. He knows I watch.
He feels the serpent of suspicion coil
Around his neck, and plans to strike ere dawn.
But we are ready. My guard captains are alert.
No man shall leave the castle after midnight
Without my express writ. All gates are sealed.
Unless his courier is a ghost, or bird,
He cannot pass our vigilance tonight.
Valerius, you must remain here with me.
We shall await his treachery. And then,
When proof is certain, act.

VALERIUS
My King, I am with you, heart and soul.
(Suddenly, a frantic banging comes from the council chamber door.)

KING
(Drawing his sword)
Who stands without? Speak now, or feel my steel!

(SIR GARETH's voice, strained and urgent, from outside)
My King, it is Sir Gareth! News most dire!
The Northmen's main host! They come upon us now!
Their numbers vast, their fury like a storm!
The vanguard was but a skirmish, a mere bait!
They bypassed the Greywood Pass by stealth,
Through ancient, forgotten paths only known
To mountain folk, or traitorous intent!
They are upon us, King! Their host surrounds
The castle! And their catapults are set!
The first stone flies even as I speak!

KING
(His face set in grim lines, he sheathes his sword with a clang)
Bypass the Pass? Unheard of! Damnation!
Traitors indeed! This speed, this cunning route,
Speaks of a guide, a map, a knowing mind
That serves the foe. Caelanth! He must have known!
Valerius, this is the proof we need!
He planned this very moment, to distract
Us with the vanguard, while the main host slipped
Through unseen! This is his treason, clear as day!
Gareth, barricade the doors! Let no one pass!
Valerius, find Caelanth! Now! Before
He can send word, or act to aid the foe!
But do not harm him! Bring him here alive!
I would look into his serpent's eyes and know
Why he would sell his king, his realm, his kin!
Go! And may the gods speed your swift steps!

VALERIUS
(Drawing his own short blade, his face stern)
Aye, my King! He shall not escape my grasp!
(Valerius exits quickly, a furious determination in his stride. The King turns to the door as Sir Gareth enters, looking distraught.)

SIR GARETH
My King, the castle walls already shake!
The northern engines hurl their crushing stones!
And from the battlements, our archers see
Lord Caelanth, my King! He stands upon
The highest tower, the Dragon's Watchtower, sire,
A lantern held aloft, a beacon bright,
Directing Fenris's bombardments to
Our weakest points! He signals to the foe!

KING
(His voice a roar of pure fury and betrayal)
Caelanth! A signal? The Dragon's Watchtower?
The very point that guides our defence!
The villain! The traitor! He dares to light
The path for ruin from my very keep?
Gareth! Gather a guard! Follow me!
To the Watchtower! We shall seize the traitor!
Let no man hold him, or defend his life!
He shall face justice now, beneath the eyes
Of the very foe he sought to aid!
To arms! To arms, all loyal men of Eldoria!
(The King strides purposefully towards the door, his hand once again on his sword. Sir Gareth and a small contingent of guards fall in behind him, swords drawn. The sound of shattering stone and the distant roar of a massive army fills the air. The castle is under siege. The internal and external threats have converged.)

(The scene shifts rapidly to the base of the Dragon's Watchtower, a winding stair leading up. Valerius and a handful of guards, led by SERGEANT BRONN, confront Caelanth, who stands at the entrance to the tower's summit, still holding a lantern high.)

VALERIUS
Caelanth! Drop the lantern! Lower your arm!
You stand accused of treason to the Crown!

CAELANTH
(His face illuminated by the lantern, a defiant smirk playing on his lips)
Treason, Valerius? Or foresight, rather?
This kingdom is a dying beast, you fools!
Its King too weak, its people too compliant,
Its future dimmed by ancient, dusty ways.
I simply pave the way for a new dawn,
A stronger hand, a mightier dominion!
Fenris will sweep away this rotting edifice,
And from its dust, a grander empire rise!
I am no traitor! I am Eldoria's prophet!
(He gestures wildly with the lantern, sending a flash of light towards the besieging army. A particularly loud crash is heard as a section of the outer wall nearby crumbles.)

SERGEANT BRONN
He signals them, my Lord! He guides their aim!
Let me strike him down!

VALERIUS
(Holding Bronn back)
No! The King commanded he be taken alive!
