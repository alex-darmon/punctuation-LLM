LORD ALARIC
Seated at a heavy oak table, perusing a scroll.

SIR KAELEN
Enters, bows low.
My Lord Alaric, a moment of thy grace,
If time permits to hear a troubled soul.

LORD ALARIC
Rise, Kaelen. Thou art ever welcome here,
Though thy brow seems furrowed like an ancient field
Ploughed by the storm. What grievance bringeth thee?

SIR KAELEN
A grievance, aye, a burden on my heart,
That long hath lain unuttered, like a stone
Beneath the moss of years. 'Tis of a birthright,
A parcel of this earth, which by true blood
Should be mine own, yet is withheld by might
And custom's rust, that choketh ancient truths.

LORD ALARIC
Birthrights and blood; these words have oft bred strife,
And set the kingdom's teeth on edge, young knight.
What house dost thou accuse, what claim dost make,
That might unseat the peace our Duke doth hold?
Speak plainly, Kaelen. Let no veiled speech
Obscure the root of thy deep discontent.

SIR KAELEN
My Lord, the lands of Stoneglen, that fair tract
Of fertile earth, now held by old Lord Malcom,
Were once the demesne of my father's sire,
Ere foul deceit and guile did steal them hence.
My grandsire, by a spurious, forged deed,
Was dispossessed, and cast from his own halls.
The scroll they showed, a cunning web of lies,
Did damn his name and leave his line bereft.
I have a document, a true testament,
Discovered 'mongst my mother's keepsakes hid,
Which proves the forgery, and makes my claim
As clear as noonday sun on a calm lake.
He produces a parchment, carefully wrapped.

LORD ALARIC
Takes the parchment, examines it without opening.
A dangerous fire thou dost seek to fan,
To question titles of fourscore years past.
Lord Malcom is a pillar of the realm,
His roots deep set, his branches wide and strong.
To shake him thus, were to disturb the earth,
And loose a hundred dormant, hungry worms.
What proof is this, that can outweigh such risk?

SIR KAELEN
The proof of truth, my Lord, the rightful cause!
Hath justice not a higher seat than peace,
When peace is built upon a falsehood's grave?
My grandsire died a broken, poor man's death,
His spirit vexed by wrongs he could not mend.
Shall I, his grandson, know of this foul act,
And let it sleep, 'neath comfort's silken shroud?
Nay, by my honour, and by heaven's light,
I must pursue this path, though it be fraught
With dragons' teeth, and serpents in the grass.

LORD ALARIC
Sighs, runs a hand over his brow.
Honour is a sharp sword, but cuts both ways.
Truth, too, may bleed the realm, if ill-applied.
I know Lord Malcom's father was a man
Of stern repute, but ne'er a known deceiver.
This document, if it be what thou say'st,
May bring down more than Malcom's house alone.
The Duke himself, whose grandsire sealed the deed,
May find his wisdom called into account.
Think well, young Kaelen, of the consequences.
A storm once raised, doth not obey the hand
That first unleashed its fury from the clouds.

SIR KAELEN
And shall I then ignore my father's pleas,
Whispered in dreams, a ghostly, sad lament?
Shall I deny my blood, and let this wrong
Endure forever, for a hollow peace?
I beg thee, Lord Alaric, lend thine ear,
Thy wisdom, and thy influence, to right
This ancient wrong, and cleanse my family's name.

LORD ALARIC
Looks at the parchment, then at Kaelen's earnest face.
Thy passion moves me, Kaelen, I confess.
But passion blinds as often as it guides.
Leave me this scroll. I shall peruse its lines
With weary, cautious eyes. No hasty word
Shall cross my lips, nor swift decree be made.
Return tomorrow, at the vesper bell,
And I shall give thee then what counsel I,
With all my years and burdens, can bestow.
Go now, and let thy fiery spirit cool;
A sober mind must weigh so grave a charge.

SIR KAELEN
I thank thee, noble Lord, from deepest heart.
My hope doth spring anew, like morning flowers.
Bows again, deeply, and exits.

LORD ALARIC
Unrolls the parchment slowly, his face grave.
Old roots, when stirred, yield bitter fruit indeed.
God grant this hand can pluck it without harm.
He begins to read.

(LORD ALARIC reads for a long while, his expression shifting from careful scrutiny to one of profound shock and sorrow. He sighs deeply, then re-reads a passage, tracing the lines with a trembling finger.)
Alas, poor Kaelen. And alas, poor realm.
The truth, when long concealed, bursts forth at last,
A fiery torrent from a dam long built.
This scroll doth speak with such convincing tongue,
No courtier's art, nor lawyer's subtle twist,
Could well gainsay the witness here inscribed.
The hand of Master Eldridge, scrivener true,
A man of probity, beyond all doubt,
Hath set his seal to every damning word.
The very ink, though faded by the years,
Doth seem to burn with righteous indignation.
Here 'tis, the fraud, laid bare in starkest light.
Lord Malcom's father, called the Steadfast then,
A name now mocked by this most vile deceit,
Did bribe this Eldridge, not to forge a deed,
But to conceal a truth, to swap a scroll,
To hide the true testament of the sire
Of Kaelen's line, which gave to them these lands,
And planted Malcom's father on the soil
Of another's heritage, by cunning means.
A codicil, a later will, they say,
Was brought forth by Malcom's cunning sire,
A false device, to disinherit those
Whose birthright it was. This document

doth prove it so. The very parchment whispers infamy.
This Eldridge, in his dotage, or perhaps
Tormented by the burden of his soul,
Did here confess the monstrous, secret crime,
And set it down, with all its foul particulars,
Then sealed it with his dying, trembling hand,
And swore an oath upon his children's lives
That this alone contained the righteous truth,
And placed it where it might one day be found
By one whose heart was just, and mind astute.
A brave confession, coming far too late
To mend the broken lives it tore asunder,
Yet timely now to wake a sleeping wrong.

(LORD ALARIC paces the chamber, the parchment clutched in his hand. His brow is furrowed with deep thought.)

Alas, the sins of fathers, how they haunt
The generations that come after them.
Lord Malcom, current bearer of that name,
A man of influence, with friends at court,
Esteemed for wealth, and deemed of noble stock,
Hath built his house upon this very lie.
His father, Malcom called the Steadfast then,
Now stands exposed, a villain in disguise,
Whose steadfastness was but a cloak for greed.
This Kaelen, young, impetuous, and bold,
Hath sought the truth, and found a viper's nest.
What counsel now can ease this bitter truth?
To bare this scandal to the light of day
Would shake the very pillars of the realm.
The Duke himself, whose grandsire, trusting, blind,
Confirmed the false inheritance long past,
Would find his royal judgment much impugned.
The common folk, ever swift to judge,
Would rail against the lords, and question all
The ancient charters, and the sealed decrees.
A tremor now, might grow into an earthquake.

And yet, how can I hide what I have seen?
My conscience doth revolt against the thought.
Justice, though slow, must ever find its way.
To let this falsehood stand, would be to make
Myself a party to the long deceit,
And poison my own soul with knowing silence.
Poor Kaelen, he doth seek a father's peace,
A family's honour, soiled and cast aside.
His youthful zeal, though dangerous in its haste,
Is yet a flame of pure, untarnished right.
He trusts in me, an old and weary man,
To guide his footsteps through this thorny maze.
I swore to him to give my best advice,
With all my years and burdens. Now the burden
Weighs heavier than any crown of state.
To disinherit Malcom, now so strong,
Would stir a tempest, fierce and uncontrolled.
His loyal retainers, swift to draw the sword,
Would not sit idly by, and watch him fall.
The land itself, so long held by his line,
Hath seen its harvests gathered by his hand.
His tenants know no other lord but him.
To pluck him from his seat, would be to wrench
A sturdy oak from ancient, rooted soil.

But Kaelen's claim is undeniable,
Authored by one who sealed it with his death.
The very language of this Eldridge's script
Doth breathe of guilt and desperate atonement.
No subtle turn, no twist of legal phrase,
Could here undo the clear intent and proof.
The dates, the names, the property described,
All fit the tale that Kaelen did unfold.
My soul doth grieve for all the wasted years,
The generations born to unjust want,
Whilst Malcom's line grew fat on stolen earth.
This wrong must be addressed, but how to do it
Without the fabric of the realm unravelling?
The Duke is just, but loves a quiet reign.
He values order above all dispute.
This revelation strikes at order's heart.
I must tread softly, like a mountain cat,
Lest I awake a sleeping dragon's wrath.

(He gazes out the window as the sun begins to set, casting long shadows across the room.)

Tomorrow, Kaelen comes, at vesper bell.
His eyes will hold the light of rising hope.
I must not crush that hope, but guide its flame
To burn with purpose, not with reckless fire.
He needs more than my blessing, he needs wisdom,
And strength to face the formidable foe.
Malcom is cunning, proud, and quick to anger.
He will not yield his lands without a fight.
His father's ghost, now tainted by this scroll,
Will be a specter haunting all the court.
This is no simple plea for common debt.
This is a wound that cleaves the body politic.
I must consider every consequence,
Each stone I turn, each word I choose to speak.
The Duke will question me, my judgment too.
Did I not serve his grandsire as a boy?
Did I not vouch for peace these many years?
Why now, this ancient wrong, brought to the fore?
It falls to me, to bear this heavy cross.
God grant me strength, and clarity of mind,
To steer this ship through such a treacherous tide.

(LORD ALARIC carefully re-rolls the parchment and places it within a hidden drawer in his desk, locking it with a small key he wears about his neck. He extinguishes the lamp, leaving the chamber in deepening twilight.)

***

(The following day, as the vesper bell tolls, SIR KAELEN enters LORD ALARIC's study. Kaelen's face is alight with anxious hope, but he tries to compose himself.)

SIR KAELEN
My Lord, I come as bid, at vesper's call.
Hath thy deep wisdom found a path for me?
Doth truth indeed still hold dominion here,
Or are the scales of justice weighted ever
By gold and power, and by ancient name?

LORD ALARIC
(Rises slowly from his chair, his expression grave, yet kind.)
Come Kaelen, sit. The hour is ripe for truth,
Though truth itself be often bitter fare.
I have perused thy scroll, with care profound,
And traced its lines, and weighed its every word.
The scrivener, Eldridge, spoke with honest pen,
Though long concealed, his witness stands unbowed.

SIR KAELEN
(Leaning forward, his breath held.)
Then it is true? My father's plaintive cry,
The whispered wrongs, the shadows in my dreams,
Were not the phantoms of a fevered mind?
My family's lands, they were indeed our own?
And Malcom's sire, a thief, a base deceiver?

LORD ALARIC
The document doth speak of clear deceit.
Thy father's line was wronged, beyond all doubt.
The lands, thou claim'st, were rightfully thy kin's,
Before a false device, a swapped decree,
Did disinherit them, by cunning means,
And planted Malcom's father on thy soil.
The evidence is stark, unyielding, plain.
There is no room for doubt, nor soft excuse.

SIR KAELEN
(Leaps to his feet, a cry of exultation escaping him.)
Oh, glorious day! The sun at last doth shine!
My father's name, redeemed from ignominy!
My ancestors, now freed from baseless shame!
Then let us hasten, noble Lord Alaric!
Let us to court, and lay this bare before
The Duke himself, and cast this Malcom down!
Let him face justice for his father's crime,
And yield the lands that he unjustly holds!
The time for reckoning is surely come!

LORD ALARIC
(Raises a hand, a look of solemn warning on his face.)
Hold, Kaelen, hold! Thy passion doth become thee,
And righteous anger warms thy youthful blood.
But swift revenge, however justly earned,
Doth oft precede a greater, darker woe.
The truth, though clear, is yet a fragile thing,
When it doth challenge power, wealth, and might.
Lord Malcom is no mere common rogue,
Whose villainy can be exposed and punished
Without a ripple in the placid pond.
He holds great sway, his coffers deep with gold,
His friends are many, potent in the court.
His house hath stood for generations now,
Esteemed and rooted deep within the realm.
To strike it down, though just, will be to shake
The very foundations that uphold our peace.

SIR KAELEN
And shall we then let wrong endure forever?
Shall justice falter, lest it stir a storm?
My Lord, is this the counsel thou dost give?
To turn a blind eye to a monstrous fraud?
To suffer Malcom's line to flourish still
Upon the soil they stole from mine by guile?
My father's ghost would surely walk unquiet,
And I, a craven son, unmanly, weak,
If I should shrink from truth, however harsh.

LORD ALARIC
Nay, Kaelen, nay. I speak not of surrender,
But of the path that wisdom doth dictate.
To rush headlong, with fury in thy heart,
Would be to throw away thy rightful cause
Upon the jagged rocks of public rage.
Malcom would rally all his strength against thee,
And paint thee as a madman, rash and wild,
A schemer seeking to usurp his place.
The court, ever wary of new claimants rising,
Might lend an ear to his well-practised lies.
The Duke, though just, doth weigh the realm's repose.
He would not lightly see a noble house
Dismantled, and the public peace disturbed.

SIR KAELEN
Then what remains? To whisper in the dark?
To plot in shadows, lest we wake the powerful?
I seek no shadows, Lord, but open day,
Where truth may stand, revealed to all men's eyes.

LORD ALARIC
And open day it shall be, Kaelen, I assure thee.
But with a careful hand, and guarded tongue.
This scroll alone, though potent, may not suffice
To cleave through years of deep-entrenched repute.
Malcom will claim it false, a cunning forgery,
Concocted by thy hand to seize his lands.
He will accuse thee of conspiracy,
Of seeking to subvert the ancient order.
Thou must be armed not only with the truth,
But with a strategy as sharp as steel,
And patience, Kaelen, such as old men learn
When wrestling with the giants of the world.

SIR KAELEN
Then tell me, Lord, what strategy is this?
What patient steps must I, in truth, pursue?
My heart is ready, but my mind is young,
Untutored in the serpent's winding ways.

LORD ALARIC
First, this parchment must be held most safe,
Away from prying eyes, or grasping hands.
Its value is beyond all earthly price.
Next, we must consider how to bring it
Before the Duke, without alarm or haste.
To burst into his presence, crying 'Fraud!',
Would only serve to make him close his mind.
He must be gently led to comprehend
The depth and gravity of this dark wrong.
I, as his trusted counsellor, may pave
The path for thy petition, and attest
To the true nature of this damning proof.

SIR KAELEN
Wouldst thou, my Lord, indeed so lend thy name,
Thy honour, and thy age's grave repute,
To champion my cause, so long forgot?

LORD ALARIC
I would. My conscience bids me do no less.
For silence now would be a deeper sin
Than any storm that truth might stir in waking.
But understand, young Kaelen, what this means.
My standing, too, will suffer Malcom's wrath.
He will accuse me of old age's folly,
Or worse, of seeking to displace his power.
Yet for the sake of justice, and the realm,
I cannot shrink from what my duty calls.
We must present this matter to the Duke
With sober mien, and calm, unruffled voice,
Laying the facts before him, one by one,
And letting proof speak louder than our words.
We must anticipate Malcom's every move,
His counter-claims, his venomous attacks.
We must have witnesses, if any yet remain,
Who knew of Eldridge, or the circumstances.
Though years have passed, and memories grow dim,
We must search well for any corroboration.

SIR KAELEN
(His fiery eagerness now tempered by Alaric's sober counsel, yet his resolve undiminished.)
My Lord, thy wisdom shines a guiding light
Through what was darkness to my youthful eyes.
I shall obey thy counsel, every word.
My hastiness I'll curb, my temper bind,
And learn the patience that thy years bestow.
But tell me, when, and how, shall we proceed?
For every day that Malcom holds these lands
Is one more insult to my father's grave.

LORD ALARIC
Tomorrow morn, I shall petition audience
With Duke Roderick, under pretext slight,
To speak of matters of the realm's defence,
Or tax collection, trivial, mundane affairs.
But in that private chamber, I shall hint
At weighty matters, touching ancient wrongs,
And ask his grace to grant thee audience,
That thou mayst lay thy grievance at his feet,
Supported by this proof, and by my word.
It must be done with utmost secrecy.
No rumour must escape these very walls
Until the Duke himself hath heard the case.
For if Lord Malcom catches wind of this,
He will employ all means, however foul,
To snatch this scroll, or silence thee forever.

SIR KAELEN
(A shiver runs through him at the thought.)
I understand. His power is indeed vast.
And he is not a man to brook defiance.
But I will guard this secret with my life.
My life, my Lord, is pledged to this high cause.

LORD ALARIC
Good Kaelen, for thy safety, I must warn thee,
Be circumspect in all thy comings and goings.
Speak not a word of this to any soul.
Even thy closest kin, if they should know,
Might inadvertently imperil all.
Assume that Malcom hath a thousand eyes,
And ears that listen to the rustling leaves.
Move with the caution of a hunted stag,
Until this matter is before the Duke,
And under his protection, thou art safe.
For Malcom, when cornered, is a beast most fierce.
He fights with venom, and with cruelest guile.

SIR KAELEN
Thy warnings strike a chill into my heart,
Yet strengthen my resolve. I shall be wary.
I shall be silent as the midnight grave.
What more, my Lord, must I prepare myself for?
What questions will the Duke himself demand?
And what defence can Malcom truly mount
Against the hand of Eldridge, scrivener true?

LORD ALARIC
The Duke will question thy motives, and thy claim.
He will inquire of thy family's standing,
And why this truth hath lain so long concealed.
Thou must explain thy father's fearful silence,
His quiet suffering, his humble means,
Which kept this secret buried in his heart,
Until his deathbed revelation spurred
Thy righteous quest. Speak of thy father's honour,
His steadfast nature, though bereft of lands.
Emphasize his reluctance to incite
Discord in the realm, preferring quiet shame
To public strife, until his dying breath
Implored thee to seek justice for his line.
This paints a picture of a loyal subject,
Not a disruptive rebel seeking gain.
As for Malcom's defence, he will deny it all.
He will decry the scroll as a foul trick,
Accuse old Eldridge of a madman's ravings,
Or claim he was suborned by Kaelen's kin
To slander his respected, noble sire.
He will perhaps produce his own old documents,
Sealed and attested, though by fraud obtained,
And argue that long tenure grants his right.
He may even seek to prove thy lineage
Is somehow flawed, or that thy claim's absurd.
No villain ever lacked a clever lie.
But we have Eldridge's confession, Kaelen,
Witnessed by his own hand, upon his death,
And placed in trust, for truth to be unveiled.
That is a bulwark strong enough to stand
Against Malcom's cunning and his practiced scorn.

SIR KAELEN
I shall remember all thou hast advised.
My Lord Alaric, my heart doth swell with thanks.
Thou art indeed the noblest friend that fate
Hath granted to my long-afflicted house.
I pray that God may grant thee length of days,
To see this wrong made right, and peace restored.

LORD ALARIC
Go now, young Kaelen, and prepare thy soul.
The path ahead is arduous and long,
But justice, like a river, finds the sea.
Return to me tomorrow, just before
The midday meal. I shall have secured our plea.
Wear simple garments, not thy courtly best,
Lest any notice thy unusual visit.
And bring no weapons, Kaelen, for the court
Is where the tongue doth battle, not the blade.

SIR KAELEN
I hear and shall obey, my noble Lord.
Farewell till midday then. My spirit's eased,
Yet sharpened, ready for the coming fray.

(SIR KAELEN bows deeply, his face reflecting a newfound determination, tempered with caution, and exits LORD ALARIC's study.)

LORD ALARIC
(Sits heavily in his chair, a sigh escaping him.)
And so the die is cast. No turning back.
The ancient wrong, now brought into the light,
Will either cleanse the realm, or rend it sore.
Malcom will fight with all the strength he has.
His pride, his wealth, his very name depend
Upon the perpetuation of this lie.
He will not yield without a bloodied struggle.
And Kaelen, though a brave and honest soul,
Is but a sapling 'gainst an ancient oak.
I must protect him, guide his every step.
My honour, and the realm's serenity,
Are bound upon this venture, now begun.
The Duke, God grant him wisdom for this choice.
To disinherit Malcom is a blow
That echoes through the land, from tower to cot.
But to permit such fraud to go unpunished,
Would undermine the very rule of law,
And make a mockery of all our charters.
This is the choice, between a perilous justice,
And a false peace, built on a festering wound.
A serpent coils beneath the throne itself.
It must be brought to light, and truth prevail,
Lest greater evils sprout from its dark seed.

(LORD ALARIC rises and walks to a window, looking out into the fading light. He clasps his hands behind his back, his brow still furrowed in deep contemplation of the difficult road ahead.)

The weight of years, they say, doth bring much wisdom.
But sometimes, Kaelen, it doth bring but dread,
When foresight shows the dangers yet unseen,
That youthful ardour cannot yet perceive.
This Malcom, he is subtle, shrewd, and cruel.
He keeps his spies in every public house,
His informers whisper in the market square.
He'll know of Kaelen's coming to my house.
No, not directly, but a restless spirit
About the court, a subtle change of mood,
Will reach his calculating, watchful ears.
I must be even more discreet than planned.
My visit to the Duke must seem most casual,
A mere exchange of pleasantries, a brief report
On trivial matters, lest suspicion wakes.
And Kaelen's entry to the Duke's own chambers
Must be arranged with utmost secrecy,
Perhaps through a forgotten, hidden door,
Or late at night, when all the palace sleeps.
Aye, that is it. The cover of the night.
The Duke's most private cabinet, at midnight.
A time when shadows guard the whispered words.
And I shall tell the Duke that Kaelen hath
A matter of such personal, grave import,
Affecting honour, family, and his peace,
That it must only be unveiled to him,
And under deepest oath of strict discretion.
This will appeal unto his royal dignity,
And to his kingly duty to hear out
The pleas of every subject, high or low.
If I present it as a private shame,
Not a public scandal from the very start,
He may be more inclined to lend an ear.

(He nods slowly, firming his resolve.)

Yes, this course holds the best chance of success.
To rouse the Duke's attention, not his ire.
To make him see the fraud, before the storm.
And Kaelen must present his tale with grace,
With humble bearing, not with burning wrath,
Though righteous anger fuels his very soul.
The Duke will judge the man, as well as cause.
A clamorous demand, a fiery accusation,
Will only harden royal ears against him.
He must present himself as one who seeks
To right a wrong, not to exact revenge.
This Kaelen is intelligent, and quick to learn.
He grasps the gravity of this affair.
His youthful zeal can be a potent weapon,
If guided by experience and calm.

But then, Lord Malcom. How will he react?
When summoned to account, what will he say?
Will he confront the Duke with open challenge?
Or will he try to weave a web of lies?
He has the power to stir rebellion,
To rally those whose fortunes are entwined
With his own house. A subtle threat, perhaps,
Of discontent among the northern lords.
This is a game of high and dangerous stakes,
Where every move may tip the balance either
To ruin, or to justice's sweet triumph.

I must consider Malcom's character anew.
He is a man who treasures his good name
Above all else, or so it doth appear.
But this foul scroll doth prove his lineage built
On falsehoods, avarice, and base deceit.
His very honour is a painted mask.
When it is torn away, what will remain?
A furious beast, stripped naked, shamed, enraged.
His dignity, his family's proud repute,
His lands, his wealth, all balanced on this thread.
He will not let it break without a fight.
He will not willingly descend from grace.
And so, precautions must be doubly taken.
Kaelen must be shielded from his malice.
Perhaps I shall invite the lad to stay
Within my own safe house, until this matter
Is brought before the Duke, and thus resolved.
A temporary lodging, for his health,
For learning, for some minor service here.
A plausible excuse, to keep him safe
From Malcom's vengeful, and swift-striking hand.

(He ponders this idea, then nods decisively.)

Yes, that is sound. His life is far too precious
To leave exposed to danger's cruel caprice.
A gentle summons, then, to Kaelen's dwelling,
To bid him come and dwell beneath my roof,
For safety's sake, and for the greater good
Of this momentous, justice-seeking quest.
He cannot then be waylaid, or waylaid.
His voice will not be silenced by a knave.
The scroll will be secure within my keeping,
And Kaelen, too, will be beneath my gaze.
This is the path of prudence and foresight.
God grant that all may follow as I plan,
And bring the light of truth to shadowed lands.
The morrow holds the fate of many men.
Let it be ushered in with grace and strength.

(LORD ALARIC walks to his desk once more, and taking a fresh piece of parchment and a quill, begins to write a short note, sealing it with his personal crest. He then rings a small bell on his desk.)

A PAGE
(Entering quickly.)
My Lord, thou didst call?

LORD ALARIC
Aye, boy. Take this message to Sir Kaelen's dwelling.
Deliver it with haste, and see it placed
Directly in his hand. Insist no other
May read its contents, for it is private.
And tarry not, for time is of the essence.
Return anon, and tell me of its safe delivery.

PAGE
(Bows.)
As you command, my Lord. I fly on wings.

(The PAGE exits swiftly with the note. LORD ALARIC watches him go, his gaze fixed on the door for a moment before returning to his thoughts.)

So, let the grand design unfold itself.
A mighty oak, though rooted for a century,
May yet be felled, if cankered at its core.
And from its ruin, new, more righteous growth
May spring, to flourish in the sun's warm gaze.
This Kaelen's courage is a hopeful sign.
A new beginning for a worthy name.
And for the realm, a cleansing fire, perchance,
To burn away the rot of old deceits.
My life, my service, now are pledged to this.
Let come what may, the truth must have its day.
The Duke will understand, when all is shown.
He values justice, though he cherishes peace.
And a peace built on lies, is no true peace.
It is a slumber, pregnant with revolt.
Better to face the storm, and steer it right,
Than let the tempest gather in the dark,
And break in fury, unpredicted, wild.

Good morrow, Lord Kaelen. I bear a message
From His Grace, the Duke. He doth request
Your presence, and that of young Alarcus here,
Without delay, within his private study.
He bids you come, and quickly, as the matter
Is one of some pressing weight, it would appear.

ALARCUS
(To Kaelen, his voice low)
The very fates conspire to pull us forth.
The time is now.

KAELEN
(To Messenger)
We shall attend His Grace without delay.
Our thanks for carrying so swift a word.
(The Messenger bows and exits.)
(Kaelen turns back to Alarcus, his face pale but determined.)
It seems our private audience is granted,
Though by the Duke's own will, not by our asking.
Perhaps the fates indeed have paved the way.
Are you prepared? Your heart, your mind, your soul?
To face the tempest that will surely break?

ALARCUS
As ready as a man can be who stands
Upon the precipice of destiny.
My heart beats firm, not with a coward's dread,
But with the drum of generations past.
My mind is clear, resolved to speak the truth.
My soul, though weary from its long delay,
Now quickens with the hope of righteous peace.
Let us go, Kaelen. Let us meet our fate.

KAELEN
(Nodding solemnly)
Then let us walk this path together, friend.
The path to justice, though it wind through thorns.
(They exit the room. The scene shifts to a grand corridor within the Duke's castle. Alarcus and Kaelen walk in silence, their footsteps echoing softly on the stone floor. Alarcus looks around, observing the tapestries, the portraits of past Dukes, his expression a mix of awe and grim resolve.)

ALARCUS
These halls, so grand, so steeped in ancient power,
Have oft seen schemers, plotters, and false friends.
How many lies have whispered through these stones,
How many truths been buried neath their weight?
And now, to bring a truth that shakes their very
Foundations, makes my spirit strangely light,
Yet heavy with the burden of its telling.
This Duke, my kinsman, though he knows it not,
Will face a choice no ruler seeks to make:
Between the long-held peace, and bitter justice.

KAELEN
He is a man of conscience, Alarcus,
Though often swayed by prudent, cautious thought.
Remember that. Appeal unto his honour,
His innate sense of right and common good.
He seeks stability, and we must show
That true stability can only spring
From honest ground, not from a field of lies.
The truth, though disruptive in its first reveal,
Will in the end secure the realm more firmly.
(They approach a large, oaken door, guarded by two silent, armoured sentinels. Kaelen gives a slight nod to one, who then raps gently on the door. A voice from within bids them enter. Kaelen pushes the door open, revealing a spacious study, filled with books and maps. The DUKE, a man of venerable age and dignified bearing, sits at a large desk, his brow furrowed in thought. Standing respectfully a few paces from him is MALCOM, younger, impeccably dressed, with a subtle smirk playing upon his lips that vanishes instantly as he sees Alarcus.)

THE DUKE
(Looking up, his gaze keen)
Kaelen. And Alarcus. You are prompt.
I trust no ill news drives your hurried steps?
Come closer, sirs. I have a matter pressing
Upon my mind, which touches on you both,
Though Alarcus knows not yet its full extent.
(The Duke gestures for them to approach the desk. Alarcus feels Malcom's eyes upon him, a cold, piercing stare that attempts to strip him bare. He meets it with calm defiance.)

MALCOM
(With a smooth, almost pitying tone)
Good morrow, Kaelen. Young Alarcus too.
What weighty matters bring you to the Duke
At such an early hour? I trust all's well
Within your manor walls, and that no slight
Affront has ruffled your calm country peace?

ALARCUS
(His voice even)
Good morrow, Malcom. Our concerns today
Are not of manors, nor of country peace,
But of the peace and justice of this realm,
And matters that pertain to truth itself.

THE DUKE
(Cutting across them, a hint of impatience in his voice)
Enough. Let us dispense with pleasantries.
Malcom, your presence here, by chance, is timely,
For this indeed concerns your place within
Our household, and the broader governance.
Lord Kaelen, you sent word to Lord Beaumont,
Indicating urgence, though the cause
You kept concealed. Speak now, and clearly state
The purpose of your coming. My ears are open.

KAELEN
(Bowing deeply)
Your Grace, forgive my earlier reticence.
The matter is so grave, so fraught with peril
For all the quiet order of your reign,
That I dared not commit it to a message,
Lest careless ears should catch a whispered word
Before the full and proper truth was known.
What I present to you this day, Your Grace,
Is not a rumour, nor a baseless charge,
But solid, undeniable, true proof,
That touches on the very line of power
Within this realm, and on your noble house.
(He pauses, taking a deep breath. Malcom's smirk has entirely vanished, replaced by a subtle tension around his jaw. He glances sharply from Kaelen to Alarcus.)

THE DUKE
(His gaze narrowing)
Speak plainly, Kaelen. Drop this measured prose.
My mind abhors a riddle, or a speech
That skirts about the edges of a truth.
If there is danger, let its shape be known.
What proof? What truth? What peril do you speak of?
And how doth it concern my household's line?

KAELEN
Your Grace, for twenty years, this kingdom hath
Believed a truth that is, in fact, a lie.
For twenty years, a rightful heir was robbed,
His birthright stolen, his true name erased,
While one who had no claim, no drop of blood
From your ancestral line, usurped his place.

(A sharp gasp escapes Malcom's lips. The Duke's eyes snap to him, then back to Kaelen, his face now a mask of stern disbelief.)

THE DUKE
Kaelen! What mad, outrageous accusation
Is this you bring before me? Do you speak
Of Malcom? Of my adopted son, my heir
By virtue of my solemn, public declaration?
Are you gone mad? Or have some jealous rivals
Put these dark whispers in your once-wise ear?

MALCOM
(Stepping forward, his voice low and menacing, but carefully controlled)
Your Grace, I beg you, hear this scurrilous
And baseless slander not. Lord Kaelen, known
For wisdom, must be fooled by some vile trick,
Some forged device, concocted by those who
Would envy my good fortune, and my place
Within your heart, and in this kingdom's trust.
What proofs could stand against a ruler's word?
My lineage is known, declared by you.
This is a treasonous and wicked lie!

ALARCUS
(Stepping forward, his voice firm, meeting Malcom's glare)
It is no lie, Malcom. It is the truth
You have so long suppressed, and hoped to bury.
My birthright you did steal, my father's name
You tarnished, and my very life you sought
To cast into the shadows of oblivion.

THE DUKE
(Banging a fist lightly on his desk)
Silence! Both of you! This is my court!
Lord Kaelen, you have made a monstrous claim.
Now prove it. Or by all the saints above,
Your venerable years shall not protect you
From justice for so foul a calumny!
Let's see these proofs that speak such weighty words.

KAELEN
(Withdrawing a heavy, leather-bound satchel from beneath his cloak)
Your Grace, I hold within this humble bag
The very echoes of the past, preserved
By ancient scribes, by hands that honoured truth.
Herein lies the lineage, the testament
Of birth, the seal of state, the very words
Of your own brother, Duke Valerius,
Who perished on the field of Eldoria.
These documents, kept safe by loyal hands
Through twenty years of fear and hidden sorrow,
Proclaim the rightful heir to be Alarcus.
(He lays the satchel on the desk. Its weight seems to press down on the polished wood. He opens it, and carefully extracts several scrolls, yellowed with age, and a small, ornately carved wooden box.)
Here, Your Grace, behold the scroll of birth.
It bears the royal cipher of Valerius,
And the sworn mark of the royal physician,
Doctor Elms, who passed away in peace
But five years hence. His hand, so oft I've seen
Affix his mark to royal documents.
This scroll declares the birth of a male child,
To Duke Valerius and his Duchess true,
A full two days before the tragic news
Of Valerius's death at Eldoria's siege.
And that child's name, as written by the Duke
Himself, in his own hand, upon this vellum,
Is Alarcus.

(The Duke's hand trembles slightly as he reaches for the scroll. He unrolls it, his eyes scanning the ancient script. Malcom stands frozen, his face now a mask of controlled fury, his eyes darting between the scroll, Kaelen, and Alarcus.)

THE DUKE
(His voice hoarse)
Valerius's hand... I know it well.
The flourishes... the firm and noble strokes...
This is his writing. And the seal...
(He examines the wax seal, tracing its intricate design with a finger.)
The royal seal of our house. Unbroken.

MALCOM
(Finding his voice, a strained, almost hysterical note in it)
Forged! It is a forgery! A clever trick!
These old, decaying papers, how can they
Contest the living breath of royal law?
My birth, my lineage, declared by you,
Your Grace, by solemn rite and public vow!
This man, this vagrant boy, he seeks to steal
My honour, and to plunge the realm in chaos!
These are the works of dark conspirators,
Who envy my position, and would seek
To tear apart the fabric of your rule!

KAELEN
(Calmly)
Conspirators, Malcom? Or honest men
Who guarded truth when truth was under threat?
This is no vagrant boy, but the true son
Of Duke Valerius, your predecessor.
And his identity was changed, his name
Concealed, his very life put in great danger,
By those who served your wicked sire, Malcom,
The treacherous Lord Varen, who sought to place
His own son on the ducal throne.

THE DUKE
(Looking up from the scroll, his face pale with dawning horror)
Varen? That traitor, long since dead and buried?
He was Malcom's father, yes, I know it.
But that he sought to place his son...
To weave so foul a plot? What motive? Why?
Valerius was my brother, sound and strong.
His death was a great loss, a blow to all.

KAELEN
His death, Your Grace, was opportune indeed
For Lord Varen, who, by cunning means,
Had already laid his vile foundations.
Duke Valerius was indeed strong, but Varen,
Ambitions burning, sought to bind the throne
To his own blood. He knew Valerius's wife,
The Duchess Eleanor, was with child.
Should it be male, Varen's hopes were dashed.
So, with a dark physician, Doctor Corvus,
A man of ill repute, though skilled in potions,
And a midwife bribed, his plan unfolded.
When the true Duke, Alarcus, was born,
His life was threatened. The midwife, terrified,
But loyal to the Duchess, took the child,
And smuggled him away, replacing him
With Malcom, Varen's son, delivered near
The selfsame hour, though sickly, small, and weak.
The Duchess, weakened by her labour's strain,
And then plunged deep in grief for her slain lord,
Was told her son, born frail, had passed away.
A double blow that broke her noble heart,
And left her vulnerable to Varen's lies.
The true child, Alarcus, was hidden then,
Raised by a loyal retainer, one who kept
These documents, these proofs of his true birth,
Concealed for twenty years, awaiting justice.
That retainer, Your Grace, was my own father,
Whose dying wish was that I bring this forth
When the time was ripe, and truth could safely bloom.
He tasked me with this sacred, solemn trust.

MALCOM
(His voice now a snarl, barely contained)
Lies! All lies! A monstrous, vile invention!
My father, Varen, was a loyal man!
A brave commander! He would never stoop
To such a vile deception! Kaelen, you,
You venerable fool, you are deceived!
This boy, this pauper, trained to weave a tale
To steal my birthright! Where's the witness? Where's
The proof beyond these faded, ink-stained rags?
Doctor Corvus? He died years ago!
The midwife? Long departed to her grave!
You have no living witness to this plot!

ALARCUS
(Stepping forward, his hand resting on the wooden box Kaelen placed on the desk)
There is a witness, Malcom. One you know.
The very instrument of your dark fraud.
(He opens the wooden box, and carefully lifts out a small, intricately carved silver locket on a delicate chain.)
This locket, Malcom, was worn by the midwife.
It was given to her by your true father,
Lord Varen, as a pledge for her dark service.
It bears his crest upon one side, concealed
Beneath a false inscription. On the other,
A tiny serpent, coiled about a crown,
Symbol of his ambition, and his craft.
And see, Your Grace, within, a hidden spring,
That opens to reveal a lock of hair,
Cut from a babe. The hair of Malcom, Varen's son,
Before he was exchanged for the true heir.
The midwife, wracked with guilt, and fearing Varen's wrath,
Confessed her part, not to my father, but to
A trusted priest, who placed this locket in
My father's care, with a full, written confession,
Sworn on the altar, sealed with holy wax.
This priest, Father Anselm, though aged and frail,
Still lives in quiet contemplation, miles
From here. His words, his testament, would seal
The truth of Kaelen's telling, and these proofs.
He knows the secret, held it for the soul
Of that poor midwife, who sought final grace.

(Malcom reels back, his face drained of colour. The Duke looks at the locket, then at Malcom, his expression a mixture of profound shock and cold fury. He picks up the locket, his fingers fumbling slightly, and with practiced ease, he finds the hidden spring. The locket clicks open, revealing a tiny, almost invisible strand of dark hair. He then looks at the other side, revealing the serpent and crown. His face hardens.)

THE DUKE
(His voice low, dangerous)
This locket... I have seen this locket once before.
Or one precisely like it. Varen wore
A similar device, a trinket he
Did prize above all others, so he said.
He claimed it was a gift from some forgotten
Lover. But this... the serpent and the crown...
This is his private mark, his secret crest!
He would not show this to the court. Oh, Varen,
My trusted captain, my false, treacherous friend!
To plot such evil, to betray my brother...
And you, Malcom? You stand here, silent now?
Does this not speak to your accuser's truth?
Do you deny this locket? Do you deny
This hidden hair, so dark, so fine, a child's?

MALCOM
(Stuttering, his composure finally breaking)
Your Grace... I... I know nothing of this!
This is a vile contrivance! A dark spell
To poison your clear mind! My hair? How could
Such a thing be proven? This Anselm, this priest,
He is a senile dotard, full of dreams!
His mind is clouded! He would swear to tales
Of demons if you asked him! It is a trick!
A clever, evil, and well-rehearsed deception!

KAELEN
The priest's confession, Your Grace, is here,
(He pulls another parchment from the satchel)
Sealed and witnessed by his bishop, a man
Of unassailable faith and solid mind,
Who holds the original, if further proof
Is needed. Father Anselm, though aged,
His mind is sharp concerning matters of
The soul and solemn vows. He remembers all.
He knew the midwife, heard her anguished plea.
He saw the locket. He kept the confession,
A burden to his spirit, till my father
Approached him, seeking truth, led by a fragment
Of whispered rumour, a dying mother's hint.
My father, Your Grace, served Valerius
With an unwavering and loyal heart.
He would not, could not, rest until he knew
The truth of his dead Duke's departed child.
He sought it out, not for ambition's sake,
But for pure justice, and for loyalty.

THE DUKE
(His face ashen, his gaze fixed on Malcom)
Malcom. My adopted son. My trusted heir.
Is this true? Have you lived a lie?
Have you usurped the name, the blood, the throne
Of one whose very innocence was stolen?
Speak now, boy! For if this tale be true,
Then all your years of favour, all your trust,
All that I gave you, turns to dust and ashes!
And your punishment shall be swift and stern!

MALCOM
(Falling to his knees, his voice now a desperate plea, though laced with a venomous undertone)
Your Grace! I swear upon my very soul,
I knew nothing of this! If my father, Varen,
Did such a thing, then it was his dark deed,
Not mine! I was an infant, helpless, small!
How can I bear the burden of his crimes?
I have served you with loyalty, with love!
I have protected this fair realm, Your Grace!
My heart is yours, my sword, my very life!
These men, these Kaelen and this nameless boy,
They seek to ruin me, to cast me out,
To blacken all my years of honest service!
This Alarcus, he is an impostor,
A tool of envious, grasping men!
He has no claim! His words are empty wind!
My father, Varen, was a loyal man,
Misunderstood, perhaps, by envious eyes!
This locket, this priest, these ancient scrolls,
They are a web of cunning, intricate lies!
Do not believe them, Your Grace! Do not let
These vipers poison your wise, noble heart!

ALARCUS
(His voice unwavering)
Your tears are false, Malcom. Your pleas are hollow.
You knew. You knew your father's dark design.
For did you not, when I was but a child,
Attempt to silence me, to end my life,
When word of my existence reached your ears?
The ambush on the road, the silent blade,
The men-at-arms who sought to spill my blood?
Who sent them, Malcom? Who but you, who feared
The truth might one day surface?

MALCOM
(Springing to his feet, his desperation turning to rage)
Liar! Traitor! You speak of ambushes
From childhood memory? A fever dream!
You weave a tapestry of foul deceit!
I never sought your life! I knew you not!
You are a fraud! A base and wicked fraud!
I will not stand by and let this lie
Destroy my honour and my rightful place!
(He draws a small dagger from his belt, its blade glinting in the morning sun streaming through the window. He lunges towards Alarcus. Kaelen, with surprising speed for his age, steps between them, pushing Alarcus back. The Duke, with a shout, rises from his desk.)

THE DUKE
(Roaring)
Guards! Seize him! Seize him, Malcom! What madness
Possesses you? To draw a blade within
My very study? This confirms your guilt!
(The two sentinels burst into the room, their swords drawn. They quickly disarm Malcom, wrestling him to the floor. Malcom struggles, spitting curses, his rage uncontrollable.)

MALCOM
(Screaming)
No! You cannot do this! I am your son!
Your rightful heir! This imposter, this lie!
He will destroy you all! He will bring ruin!
(The guards drag Malcom from the room, his shouts fading down the corridor.)

THE DUKE
(Standing, leaning heavily on his desk, his face a mask of grief and betrayal)
Alarcus. Kaelen. Are you unharmed?
(He looks at Alarcus, then back at the locket and the scrolls on his desk. His gaze is distant, troubled.)
Oh, Varen. My friend, my brother's trusted man.
To harbour such a viper in my heart.
And Malcom... The son I raised, the boy
I loved, I nurtured, trained to take my place.
To think that all these years, his gentle smiles,
His loyal service, were a painted mask
To hide a thief, a liar, and a plotter.
My heart is torn. My spirit deeply wounded.
This truth... It is a bitter, burning draught.

ALARCUS
Your Grace, my heart grieves for your noble pain.
It was never my intent to cause you sorrow,
But only to restore the honour lost,
And right the ancient wrong that festers still.
The choice was not of my own making, Duke.
The truth, like spring, will ever find its way
To burst forth from the winter's icy grasp.

THE DUKE
(Sinking back into his chair, covering his face with a trembling hand)
And what now, Kaelen? What must now be done?
The realm. The people. How will they receive
This monstrous revelation? Malcom holds
Their trust, their loyalty, by long habit.
To strip him of it, to reveal his fraud,
Will shake the very pillars of my reign.
My peace, my quiet governance, all shattered!

KAELEN
Your Grace, the people are not blind to justice.
They will, at first, be shocked, perhaps dismayed,
But in the end, they will embrace the truth.
A realm built on a lie cannot long stand
Against the judgment of a righteous God.
To keep this secret now, to let this fraud
Continue, would be to compound the wrong,
And sow the seeds of future, darker strife.
The truth, though turbulent at first, will bring
A deeper, more enduring peace, founded
On rightful claim, and honour, justly served.
Alarcus, Your Grace, is of your blood,
The true descendant of Valerius.
