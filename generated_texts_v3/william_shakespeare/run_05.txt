SCENE I. A council chamber in Duke Octavius's castle.

Enter DUKE OCTAVIUS, LORD VALERIUS, and LADY ISOLDE.

DUKE OCTAVIUS
Lord Valerius, your bold tongue doth once more sting
The quiet ear of our long-suffering realm.
What new complaint, what fresh-forged grievance brings
Thy restless spirit to this solemn helm?

LORD VALERIUS
No fresh-forged grievance, good my gracious Duke,
But ancient truths, by your long reign concealed,
Now rise like spectres from a silent tomb,
Demanding audience, their wrongs revealed.

LADY ISOLDE
My lord, I pray you, let not hasty words
Like poisoned arrows pierce the troubled air.
This hour demands a balm, not sharper swords,
To mend the breach, and banish all despair.

LORD VALERIUS
Whence comes this quiet, save from slumbering eyes,
That wink at foes, and sleep whilst dangers grow?
The northern marches, Duke, hear nightly cries
Of ravaged hamlets, where thy harvests flow
To feed the raider's maw. Thy coffers swell,
But not with means to guard thy trusting folk.
Whilst we debate, a thousand torments fell
Do bind thy subjects 'neath a foreign yoke.

DUKE OCTAVIUS
Thou speak'st of dangers, Valerius, as if
They were not faced with many a watchful eye.
My loyal captains, true beyond belief,
Do stem the tide, though oft 'neath bitter sky.
Hast thou forgotten how, these score of years,
I held the wolf from many a tender lamb?
My youth was spent in toil, in blood, and tears,
To forge this peace, which now thou dost condemn.

LORD VALERIUS
A peace of rust, a peace of slow decay,
Where ancient honours fade to dusty scrolls.
The sinews of this state, once strong and gay,
Now droop and languish, losing all their souls.
I ask thee, Duke, what purpose hath a crown
If it doth rest upon a sleeping head?
The common voice throughout the weary town
Doth murmur of a change, lest all be dead.

LADY ISOLDE
My lord, such words do wound my father's heart,
And mine as well. Canst thou not see the lines
Of care that mark his brow? No subtle art
Could hide the labour that his spirit pines
To bear for all his people. Is it wise
To stir up strife where harmony should dwell?
The realm hath need of unity, not lies
To fan the flames of discontent and hell.

LORD VALERIUS
Sweet lady, thine is ever a gentle plea,
A tender note in discord's jarring sound.
But gentle words, alas, will never free
The captive soul that lies in shackles bound.
Thy father's age, though venerable and grey,
Doth dim the vision that the helm requires.
A younger hand, more vigorous and gay,
Must guide the vessel through these stormy fires.

DUKE OCTAVIUS
Valerius, thou dost tread on dangerous ground.
Thy youth may lend thee strength, thy ambition fire,
But wisdom, born of years, cannot be found
Within the breast that burns with mad desire
To snatch the reins of power. Know this well:
A sovereign's burden is a heavy chain.
And those who seek it, often find a hell,
Where once they dreamt of glory and of gain.
I am thy Duke. My word remains my law.
And I command thee, cease this bitter strife.
Or else, by heaven's grace, thou soon shalt draw
The full measure of my wrath, upon thy life.

LORD VALERIUS
(Drawing a dagger, but not pointing it immediately, more a gesture of defiance)
My life, old Duke? A life not lived in shame,
But forged in truth, and dedicated solely
To what is right. If truth be but a game
That threatens power, then let me die most holy.
I fear no wrath that springs from aged fear,
But rather scorn the hand that shrinks from fight.
The people's will, my lord, doth draw me near
To challenge darkness with the rising light.

LADY ISOLDE
(Alarmed, stepping between them)
Father, Valerius, put up thy blade!
This madness must not seize us in its grasp!
What sudden fury hath your souls arrayed
In such dread guise? Ere bloodshed bids us gasp,
Consider well the cost of such a war.
Let reason guide your hearts, not blinded rage!
The kingdom trembles at your warring roar,
A bitter chapter in our history's page!

DUKE OCTAVIUS
(To Valerius, holding himself with composure despite the blade)
Put up thy weapon, boy, lest thou repent
The folly of this moment, rash and proud.
My patience, though it seemeth gently bent,
May break in fury, like a thunder cloud.
Think not thy youth can shield thee from my hand.
I have seen younger, stronger men than thee
Fall swift to dust, by my just wrath unmanned.
Put up thy blade! I say it, Valerius, see!

LORD VALERIUS
(Stares at Octavius, then at Isolde, a moment of hesitation)
My lord, I mean thee no dishonour grave.
But for the realm, my conscience bids me speak.
This blade, I draw it not a life to save,
But truth to sever from the false and weak.
(He slowly sheathes the dagger, but his gaze remains firm)
Yet for thy daughter's plea, and for the peace
She seeks to mend, I shall restrain my hand.
But know, old Duke, this grievance shall not cease,
Till justice wakes, and frees this weary land.

DUKE OCTAVIUS
(A long, stern look at Valerius)
Then let it be. But know, young, restless Lord,
Thy path is fraught with peril and with pain.
The seeds of discord, once they are outpoured,
Will yield a harvest reaped in bitter rain.
Go, ponder well thy words, and weigh their might.
We shall discourse anon, at morrow's dawn.
Until that time, depart, and seek the light
Of wiser counsel, ere thy hopes are gone.

LORD VALERIUS
(Bowing stiffly to Octavius, then a more respectful, lingering glance at Isolde)
I shall, my Lord. Till morrow, then.
(Exits, leaving a charged silence)

LADY ISOLDE
(To Octavius, with concern)
Oh, father, what a storm doth gather round!
His heart is bold, his mind doth burn with zeal.
I fear the outcome, should no common ground
Be found to bind him, and the realm to heal.

DUKE OCTAVIUS
(Wearily, but with resolve)
Fear not, my child. The ancient oak stands fast,
Though young saplings may bend before the gale.
His fire is bright, but it may prove too fast
To burn long true. Time will unveil the tale.
But for now, Isolde, we must prepare
For what the morrow's troubled light may bring.
A Duke's old age doth carry heavy care,
And sometimes, dearest, such a weight will sting.

(Turning back to Isolde, his voice softens, but with a firmness underlying.)
My daughter, understand. My heart doth ache To cause thee sorrow, or to quell thy hope. But in this moment, for this kingdom's sake, I cannot yield to sentiment, nor grope In darkness, when the path is stark and clear. This Valerius, this populist's foul spawn, Must be confronted. Else, our kingdom here Will vanish with the breaking of the dawn.
He plays upon the suffering of the poor, A suffering I know, and feel myself. But not with chaos can we open doors To brighter futures. Not by greed and pelf Can justice truly blossom. Order first, Then gentle reform. Else, anarchy will reign, And all we've built, by centuries immersed In sweat and blood, will wash away like rain.
I see thy face, so troubled and so pale. Thou dost not wish for violence, nor for strife. Nor I, my child. But when the bitter gale Of treason blows, to save a single life, A thousand may be lost. This is the price Of governance, the monarch's heavy cross. To guard the many, at the sacrifice Of few, to shun the gain, and count the loss.
Go to thy chamber now, sweet Isolde. And pray for wisdom, and for peaceful days. Let not thy spirit be by doubts made cold, But trust thy father, through these winding ways. This threat is grave, and calls for sternest hand. No time for weakness, nor for softest plea. The safety of this realm, and all the land, Rests on my will, and my resolve, you see.
(Isolde hesitates, her eyes still filled with conflict, then curtseys slowly and exits.)

KING
(Alone, he walks to the window, looking out over his city. He draws a deep, weary breath.)
So, Valerius. The serpent shows its fangs. He thought me blind, perhaps, or merely old. He played his game, with subtle, sweet harangs, And watched his power, like a river, rolled Into a torrent. Fool! Did he not think I saw his machinations, day by day? Did he believe my ancient spirit shrink From challenge? Nay!
My father fought the Saxons, fierce and bold. My grandsire crushed rebellion's hydra head. This crown, by generations, hath been held Against the envious, and against the dread Of civil strife. I shall not be the king Who lets it fall, by weakness or by fear.
He thinks to use the people, and to bring A new age forth? A bloody, bitter year He'll bring instead, if he doth not desist. He stirs the embers of a sleeping fire, And thinks to guide the flame, by his own fist. He knows not what he sows, nor his desire.
These common folk, they crave a simple ease, A full-stocked larder, and a quiet hearth. They listen to the wind among the trees, And find discomfort in its sudden mirth. They seek a leader, one to ease their pain, And Valerius, with cunning, fills that role. But when he promises to break the chain, He seeks to bind them to his own control.
He speaks of burdens? He will add to them! He speaks of rights? He will deny them all! When power's grasped, by cunning stratagem, The people's voice becomes a hollow call.
And Isolde, my sweet, trusting child. Her heart, so pure, so quick to sympathize, Is like a garden, innocent and mild, Where bitter seeds may sprout 'neath watchful skies. She sees the suffering, but not the cause. She hears the plea, but not the hidden threat. She values mercy, and disdains the laws That guard the kingdom, lest they make us fret.
To rule is not to please, but to protect. To govern is to bear a lonely weight. To choose the path that reason doth elect, Though popular opinion rail at fate.
This cannot fester. I must act, and fast. Before his whispers turn to open roar. Before this rebel's influence holds fast, And treason knocks upon my kingdom's door.
(He walks back to his chair, sits, and gazes at the royal seal upon his desk.)
A king's dilemma: mercy, or the sword? To hold the balance, when the scales are swayed. To answer challenge, by the spoken word, Or by the might of armies, unafraid. He forces my hand, this Valerius. He brings a darkness to my latter years. But I will stand, as doth the ancient yew, And guard my people, through their hopes and fears.
My father's spirit watches, I am sure. My ancestors demand that I be strong. This sacred trust, I ever shall secure, And right the wrong that doth to us belong.
But how to quell this fire, ere it spreads? Without the brutal hand that fuels the rage? To cut the head, but leave no bloody shreds, To turn anew this dark and fearful page?
He holds the city, in his verbal sway. My loyal guards, they may be forced to act. If blood is spilled, upon this fateful day, Then all his lies will seem an honest fact. The people then will mourn a martyr's fall, And turn their wrath upon their lawful king.
No, not yet. A subtler path, a call To reason, ere the bloody bells do ring. I must expose his falsehood, make them see The hollow promise, and the hidden blade. Let them discern the truth, and then be free To judge the choice that he hath falsely made.
But how, when every whisper builds his name? When every grievance doth his cause enflame?
Perhaps Fitzwilliam, with his cunning wit, Can find a chink in Valerius's armour bright. Or Rhys, whose wisdom doth so firmly sit, May shed a guiding, diplomatic light.
This night will prove a crucible of doubt, A turning point for kingdom and for crown. I pray to heaven, as the stars hang out Their silent vigil, that I cast not down This realm to ruin, by a single choice. But oh, the burden, heavy on my soul, To heed the truth, and scorn the clamour's voice, And make this fractured kingdom once more whole.
(He rises, walks to a small altar in the chamber, and kneels, his head bowed in silent prayer as the light outside dims.)
The weight of kingship is a lonely task. God grant me strength to face this coming storm. And guide my hand, behind this kingly mask, To keep my people safe, and whole, and warm.
And for a moment, sorrow's heavy cloak is lifted from their brow. They see a dawn where long hath been but night. A promise whispered, 'tis no idle vow To those who hunger for a fairer light. Thou say'st he seeks ambition's gilded prize, And 'tis a truth that power doth corrupt. But can we fault the thirst that in them lies For waters pure, from wells long interrupt? My heart doth ache to see their weary gaze, Their stoic patience in the face of wrong. What solace can I offer in these days? What comfort to the weak, where burdens throng? Thou speak'st of order, father, and of peace, A peace that cloaks a quiet, slow decay. A silence bought by suffering's release, A future mortgaged for a tranquil day.
I walk the market square, in humble guise, And hear their murmurs, see their sunken eyes. The mother weeping as her infant cries For bread ungotten, 'neath indifferent skies. The craftsman's hand, grown gnarled from endless toil, His artistry now bartered for a crust. The farmer watching barren, stony soil Yield but despair, and turn his hopes to dust. They speak of Valerius, not as a king, But as a voice that echoes their deep pain. A silver tongue, a song their spirits sing, A promise that their prayers be not in vain. They crave no revolution, blood, nor strife, But simple justice, freedom from their plight. A chance to build a better, fuller life, Beneath the sun, unshadowed by the night.
Is this sedition, father, to desire A common good, a balance in our state? Must all who question feed ambition's fire, Or tempt the fearful hand of cruelest fate? I pray thee, pause, and weigh their honest plea. For if we scorn their trust, or mock their hope, Then truly, father, doth my spirit see A path where desperate men in darkness grope. I know thy wisdom, deep and firmly set, And honour well the crown thou dost uphold. But in the balance, let us not forget The cries of those whose stories are untold. Their loyalty is like a fragile glass, So easily shattered by a careless hand. Let not their silent anguish come to pass Unheeded, lest it break across the land.

KING
Daughter, enough! Thy tender heart doth blind Thine eyes to treachery, thy soul to guile. Thou seest but shadows, where a cunning mind Doth weave a web, to catch thee for a while. 'Tis ever thus, that when the mob doth stir, Incited by a voice that promises all, A viper hidden 'neath a cloak of fur Doth wait to strike, and watch the mighty fall.
Thou speak'st of justice, child, as if it were A simple harvest, gathered in the sun. But justice, Isolde, is a costly care, A fortress built, when battles must be won Against the chaos that doth ever lurk Beyond the ramparts of a reasoned law. Thou seest the labour, but not all the work That keeps this kingdom safe from tooth and claw.
Did I not raise these walls, and stand my ground Against the envy of a thousand foes? Did I not bring this peace, where strife was found, And make the weary land again to close Its wounds of war? These hands, though now grown old, Have fought to guard the very peace you crave. This crown, a burden heavier than gold, Was placed upon a brow that sought to save This realm from ruin, ere thou drew'st thy breath.
Thou art too young to know the bitter sting Of false ambition, masked by honeyed word. Too pure to gauge the malice men do bring To power's feast, when 'tis by hunger spurred. Valerius, forsooth! A silver tongue, a voice That stirs the rabble, paints a gilded dream. He bids them question, bids them to rejoice In fancied wrongs, a dangerous, shallow stream.
He preaches burdens shared, and common good? These are but phrases, hollow and untrue. He seeks to plant disquiet where he stood, And watch the seeds of discontent accrue. He speaks of rights? What rights are there to claim Beyond the duties that bind all to all? To serve the state, and honour kingly name, And rise above the lure of freedom's call When freedom means but license to destroy The very fabric that sustains our lives?
Think'st thou these common folk, whose simple needs Thou dost lament, have wisdom to discern The subtle poison in his artful deeds? Or do they blindly follow, nor discern The precipice towards which he doth lead? They seek a saviour, child, a god to praise, And he, a mortal, plants a wicked seed, To reap a harvest in these troubled days.
I love them, Isolde, with a father's heart, This kingdom is my child, my flesh, my blood. And every vein, and every vital part, Is woven in this land where I have stood For fifty years, a monarch and a guide. I know their weakness, and their strength also. I know the ebb and flow of fortune's tide, And where the seeds of true contentment grow.
But Valerius, this upstart, doth profane The sacred trust betwixt a king and folk. He seeks to shatter, for his selfish gain, The ancient bonds, the very spirit's yoke Of governance. He'd tear the crown from me, Not for their good, but for his ravenous maw. He'd cast this kingdom to the storm-tossed sea, And drown all order 'neath his lawless law.
This is no plea for justice, Isolde, nay! 'Tis but a cloak for treason, dark and deep. A gilded snare, to lead my realm astray, And plunge it into chaos, while I sleep. Beware, my daughter, lest thy noble heart Be made a pawn in his ambitious game. Lest thou, by innocence, play out a part In forging ruin in thy father's name.
Go now, and meditate upon my word. Let reason guide thy passion, and thy youth. Think not of whispers by the people heard, But of the ancient, consecrated truth.
(A knock is heard at the chamber door.)
Who waits without? What urgent summons now Doth interrupt a king's paternal care?

LORD BERTRAND (entering, bowing low)
My liege, forgive my haste, and pardon now My rude intrusion. News of weight I bear, From market square, where Valerius doth hold A multitude in thrall, with fiery speech. His words grow bolder, as his tale is told, Beyond the customary, cautious reach.

KING
What new impertinence hath he now dared? What fresh offense against our royal peace? Hath he our patience utterly ensnared? Doth his presumption ever know release? Speak, Bertrand, speak! And hold no word in thrall. What venom from his lips hath now been flung?

LORD BERTRAND
My Lord, he spoke of taxes, heavy, tall, And ancient wrongs that on the people clung. He spoke of charters broken, rights ignored, Of granaries full, while common folk do starve. He painted pictures of a land devoured By greedy lords, who from the poor do carve Their sustenance. But then, my Lord, he turned His fiery gaze upon thy sacred crown. He spoke of kings who have their lessons learned To hold their power, lest it tumble down.
He mentioned not thy name, by cunning art, But spoke of "tyrants, cloaked in royal purple." And how the people, playing well their part, Might cast aside their chains, their painful scruple. He spoke of new beginnings, of a choice, To follow freedom's banner, bright and bold. And in his words, I heard a rising voice, A murmur, then a shout, so clear and cold: "Valerius! Valerius! Our righteous guide!"

KING
(His face darkens with anger, a vein throbbing in his temple.)
"Our righteous guide!" The very words he used In ancient Rome, to stir the plebeian mob! A thousand times hath history been abused By ambitious men, who seek to rob The lawful monarch of his rightful sway. He treads a path oft trodden, steeped in gore. And thinks, forsooth, that he can hold the day, By echoing the cries of ages o'er!
This is outright sedition, bold and stark! A naked challenge to my royal might! He seeks to quench the flame, and leave a mark Of chaos, plunging all into the night.
Isolde, didst thou hear? This is the "hope" Thou didst commend, this "justice" he doth preach! A rebel's cry, to cast aside the rope Of order, and to tear beyond all reach The bonds of fealty, the sacred oath That ties the subject to his sovereign lord! He seeks to feed the dragon's ravenous growth, And bring a civil war by his own sword!

LADY ISOLDE
(Her voice trembling, her face pale.)
But father, surely there are other ways Than swift reprisal, and the iron hand. Can we not reason, in these troubled days, And seek to heal the wounds throughout the land? If what he speaks, though framed with sharpest sting, Holds but a grain of truth, a valid plea, Then to dismiss it, father, would but bring A deeper sorrow, and a misery
That festers underneath a forceful peace. To crush the voice, doth not subdue the heart. If burdens truly press, if pains increase, Then let us listen, and play well our part.
Could we not send a herald, noble, wise, To hear their woes, to parley with their chief? To mend the fractures 'neath our anxious skies, And offer solace, giving some relief?
A gentle touch, a word of understanding, Might quell the rising tempest, hold it fast. For harsh decrees, however well commanding, Oft sow the seeds of greater storms to blast. Remember, father, when the people rise In anger, 'tis because they feel unseen. A moment's grace, a lifting of their eyes To brighter prospects, might yet make serene This troubled hour.

KING
(He stops pacing, turning sharply to Isolde.)
Grace? Prospects? Child, thou dost not comprehend The nature of the beast we now confront. To parley with a traitor, is to lend Authority to him, and bear the brunt Of his defiance, making him more bold. To show him mercy now, would be perceived As weakness, by the hungry and the cold, Who deem our kingdom's strength already cleaved.
He seeks no parley, Isolde, no gentle grace. He seeks my throne, my power, and my name. He covets all, and would usurp my place,

My sacred crown, my sceptre, and my name!
He builds his treason on a base of lies,
And thinks to wear a kingly diadem,
Wrought from the sighs and sorrows of my realm!
No child, no grace! He seeks but my destruction,
And by the heavens, he shall not have his way!
To treat with him were to legitimise
His vile ambition, and to seal my doom.
He offers nothing but a bloody prize,
A throne defiled, a kingdom clad in gloom.
His words are honeyed poison, sweet to taste,
But in their core, they carry death and blight.
He speaks of freedom, yet he brings but waste,
And casts our ancient loyalties to night.
No. My command shall be both swift and stern.
He chose defiance, he shall reap the sword.
The lessons of the past we must discern,
And crush this serpent with a righteous word
Of royal wrath, ere it infest the land
And turn our faithful subjects to a mob.
My power is from God, not from his hand,
Nor from the fickle hearts he seeks to rob
Of reason, with his false and painted plea.
He stirs the pot of malice, greed, and scorn,
And dreams, forsooth, that he can rise above
The throne anointed, since creation born.
My father's father, and his father's sire,
Did hold this sceptre, by God's own decree.
And shall I yield it to a rebel's fire?
No! Death itself were sweeter far to me!

LADY ISOLDE
(Her voice still trembles, but gains a touch of pleading resolve.)
But father, think of all the blood that flows,
If war's dark banner flutter in the breeze!
A thousand homes may weep, a thousand woes
Afflict our folk, and bring them to their knees.
Is not a kingdom's strength its people's love?
And if that love be lost in cruel strife,
What gain hath victory, when from above
The heavens frown upon a wasted life?
To quell a tempest with a greater storm,
Doth oft but drive the tempest to despair,
And leave a shattered wreck, no longer warm
With life's true spirit, but a vacant stare.
Though Valerius doth speak with words of fire,
And seems to challenge all that you hold dear,
Perchance his anger springs from common dire
Conditions, that provoke both doubt and fear
Amongst the lowliest, those who bear the weight
Of heavy burdens, and of meagre fare.
To hear their voices, ere it be too late,
Might save us from a sorrow beyond compare.
A king's true power lies not in his might
Alone, but in the mercy he can show.
To temper justice with a guiding light,
Doth make the seeds of lasting peace to grow.
Oh, father, listen to your daughter's plea!
Consider well the costs of war's dark game.
For what is triumph, if the victory
Be purchased with our kingdom's bitter shame,
And soaked in tears, and shadowed by the dead?
Let gentle wisdom guide your royal hand,
Lest from this conflict, misery be bred,
And ruin walk unchecked throughout the land.

KING
(He clenches his fists, his voice low and dangerous.)
Misery is bred by treason, and by those
Who sow sedition, undermining trust.
You speak of mercy, child, yet Valerius throws
His gauntlet down, and grinds my crown to dust!
He seeks not parley, but surrender vile.
He seeks not justice, but my very head.
And in his treason, he doth reconcile
All discontented spirits, justly dread.
My power is absolute, from heaven given.
To question it, is to question God's own will.
And those who dare to make my kingdomriven,
Shall find their rebellion meet its bitter fill.
(A knock sounds at the chamber door. The King turns, his anger barely masked.)
Enter! What news doth interrupt our plea
For peace, when war's grim shadow starts to loom?

(LORD GAIUS, a man of stern countenance and martial bearing, enters quickly. He wears a simple tunic but carries the aura of one accustomed to command.)

LORD GAIUS
My Liege, forgive this urgent, swift intrusion,
But news hath flown on wings of fearful haste.
Valerius hath thrown off all illusion,
And openly declares his rebel taste!
From southern marches, where the river flows,
To northern holds, where granite mountains rise,
His agents whisper, sowing bitter woes,
And turning common folk with artful lies.
He hath assembled forces, men-at-arms,
Disgruntled farmers, labourers in chains
(Now freed by him), who heed his false alarms,
And lend their ragged strength to lawless gains.
He holds the town of Veridia, where he seized
The armouries, and swayed the city guard.
He claims their loyalty, their hearts appeased
By promises of change, however hard
To truly grasp, given his rebel cause.
He styles himself "Protector of the Realm",
And openly defies your royal laws.
He rides upon a dangerous, storm-tossed helm.
The word "Valerius" is on every tongue.
A thousand rumours, half-truths, and dire fears,
Are from his dark machinations sprung,
And fill the common folk with anxious tears.

KING
(His face hardens, all trace of indecision gone. He glances at Isolde with a triumphant, albeit grim, expression.)
Thou seest, Isolde? This is the "grain of truth"
Thou didst commend, this "justice" he doth preach!
A swift, sharp sword to cleave our kingdom's youth,
A rebel's banner, tearing beyond reach
The bonds of fealty, the sacred oath!
Valerius, Protector! By my crown,
He seeks to feed the dragon's ravenous growth,
And bring a civil war to cast me down!
Gaius, what forces have we ready now?
What loyal legions guard our royal seat?
This treachery must meet a crushing blow,
And swift retribution seal his foul defeat!
No moment now for counsel or for pause.
The time for reason hath entirely fled.
Our righteous vengeance must enforce our laws,
And spill the blood of traitors, swiftly shed.

LORD GAIUS
My Liege, our garrisons are standing fast.
The loyal legions, ever true and keen,
Await your word, prepared to hold the blast
Of rebel fury, with their steel unseen
(Until the call to arms). Lord Marcus holds
The western pass, with two score thousand men.
Sir Kaelen guards the northern mountain folds
With thrice ten thousand, ready even then
To march at speed. Our cavalry is poised,
Their horses snorting, eager for the fray.
And in the city, ten full cohorts voiced
Their deep allegiance, only yesterday.
But Veridia's fall, though not a mortal wound,
Doth grant him stature, and a base of power.
We must prevent his influence to be found
In other towns, lest we lose hour by hour.
I have despatched swift riders to all lords,
Commanding them to gather all their might,
And bring their levies, with their spears and swords,
To meet us here, and join the coming fight.
I would advise, my Liege, that we make haste.
To strike before his strength can truly grow,
And all his newfound rebel gains be waste,
Before his evil seed can firmly sow
Its roots of discord deep within the land.

KING
Thou speakest wisely, Gaius. Haste we shall.
This sun shall set on our decisive hand.
I shall myself lead forth the martial call!
Prepare the Royal Guard! Sound the trumpets loud!
Let every loyal heart throughout the realm
Know that their King doth stand before the crowd
Of rebel traitors, grasping at the helm
Of state, with bloody, sacrilegious grip!
My sword shall taste the rebel's bitter gall!
Valerius shall know the crushing whip
Of royal justice, ere this season fall!
(He strides purposefully towards a map spread upon a nearby table.)
Where is Valerius said to be encamped?
In Veridia still, or hath he ventured forth?

LORD GAIUS
Reports confirm he holds the city clamped
Within his grasp, controlling south and north
Of the great river, with his growing host.
He fortifies the walls, and gathers grain.
He means to make that city his strong boast,
A thorn embedded in your royal reign.
He levies taxes, seizes noble lands,
And redistributes all amongst his poor.
He speaks of freedom, with his bloody hands,
And gathers followers behind his door.

KING
A wolf in sheep's disguise! A painted lie!
He seeks to beggar nobles, rob the rich,
And buy the common heart with empty cry
Of social justice, digging freedom's ditch!
He plays the populist, the people's friend,
To mask his tyranny, his lust for sway.
His revolution shall swiftly meet its end!
Gaius, prepare my armour for this day!
I shall march forth, and with my royal might,
Crush this rebellion ere it truly flower.
Let every loyal soldier join the fight,
And end this villain's brief and bloody hour!
My standard, Gilded Lion on the field,
Shall fly above the carnage we shall make!
His broken banners, to our power yield,
And know the wrath of kings, for honour's sake!

LADY ISOLDE
(Stepping forward, her voice a desperate whisper.)
Father, consider! To put on the steel,
And lead your armies forth into the fray,
Doth place your sacred person in peril real!
Your life is precious, for our kingdom's sway!
Let trusted generals command the host,
While you remain within your royal seat,
A beacon of stability, our boast,
And guide the strategy with wisdom sweet.

KING
(He turns, his eyes burning with resolve.)
No! This is not a common skirmish, child!
This is a challenge to my very throne,
A usurpation, barbarous and wild!
My presence on the field must make it known
That I, the King, do fight for every loyal heart,
For every stone that guards our ancient peace.
To hide behind my generals, were a part
Of weakness, that would see this struggle increase.
My people need to see their monarch's face,
To feel his courage, and to know his might.
My presence on the field shall bring forth grace
To loyal souls, and put the rebels to flight!
Gaius, send forth the orders! Let the drums
Begin their thunder, summoning the brave!
My royal wrath against Valerius comes!
Prepare to meet our foes beyond the grave!
(The King exits, followed closely by LORD GAIUS, whose face is grim but determined. LADY ISOLDE remains, alone in the chamber, her shoulders slumped in despair.)

LADY ISOLDE
(To herself, her voice filled with sorrow.)
Oh, cruel fate, that bids such conflict rise,
Between a king who holds his power dear,
And one who claims to open people's eyes,
And banish thence their ever-present fear!
My father, blinded by his royal pride,
And Valerius, driven by a righteous cause
(Or so he claims), now stand on either side
Of this abyss, defying nature's laws
Of peace and harmony. What shall become
Of this poor kingdom, caught between two wills?
A silent dread, a chilling, heavy hum,
Fills all the air, and every spirit stills.
To crush the voice, doth not subdue the heart.
To force allegiance, doth not make it true.
And now the kingdom tears itself apart,
As ancient loyalties give way to new.
(She walks to the window, looking out over the city.)
The sun that shines so brightly on this morn,
Shall set on fields of carnage, red with gore.
And many a mother's heart shall be forlorn,
And many a child shall weep, and ask no more
For father's love, or brother's kind embrace.
This is the "hope" my father cast aside.
This is the "justice" that hath left no trace
Of reason, drowned in anger's surging tide.
Valerius, though thy words are sharp and keen,
And touch a chord within my very soul,
Yet civil war's a monstrous, bloody scene,
That leaves behind a broken, empty whole.
And father, oh my father, canst thou not
Perceive the dangers of this iron hand?
Must all our future be so dearly bought,
With every drop of blood upon the land?
I fear for all. For father, for the realm.
And yes, Valerius, even for thy plight.
A ship without a helmsman at its helm,
Is tossed upon the sea's unending might.
Oh, for a touch of grace, a moment's pause,
To turn this tide of wrath, and calm the storm!
But now the die is cast, defying laws
Of mercy, bringing only war's grim form.
The clash of steel, the cries of dying men,
The burning villages, the silent tears.
These are the spectres that await us then,
To haunt our waking days, and fill our fears.
(A bell tolls mournfully in the distance. Isolde shudders.)
The bell of war. It rings a solemn sound.
A requiem for peace, now laid to rest.
And soon the earth shall drink the blood profound,
Of brother fighting brother, put to test.
May heaven lend some wisdom to this land,
And guide our footsteps from this tragic way.
But fate, it seems, hath seized us by the hand,
And drags us onward to a darker day.
(She sinks into a chair, her face buried in her hands.)

(SCENE CHANGE: VALERIUS'S CAMP. A rustic but organised camp outside the walls of Veridia. Common folk, some armed with makeshift weapons, others with rusty swords, mingle with more disciplined soldiers. VALERIUS stands on a raised platform, addressing the crowd. Beside him stands SEPTIMUS, a grizzled veteran with a scarred face, and LYRA, a young woman with fiery eyes, holding a crude banner emblazoned with a rising sun.)

VALERIUS
(His voice strong and clear, resonating with conviction.)
Good friends, good citizens, no longer slaves!
The hour is come to claim our birthright due!
The King, enthroned upon our father's graves,
Doth hear no cries, nor sees our sorrow true!
He feeds upon our toil, and casts us down
To serve his luxury, his pride, his lust.
We are the architects of this fair town,
And yet our bones are ground to common dust!
For generations, we have tilled the soil,
And forged the metal, built the very walls
That shelter him, and given endless toil,
While he in gilded chambers holds us thralls!
He calls us loyal subjects, yet he takes
Our sons for wars he wages, far from home.
He breaks our backs for taxes, for his sake,
And leaves our families to beg and roam!
He claims his power by divine decree,
Yet where is God in hunger, want, and pain?
Where is the heaven in this tyranny,
That showers sorrow down like bitter rain?
No! Heaven's grace doth shine on justice bright,
On freedom's cause, on equity and right!
We seek no power born of selfish might,
But common good, to banish endless night!
(A cheer rises from the crowd.)
He calls us rebels, traitors to the crown!
But I say nay! We are the truest heart
Of this fair kingdom, fighting to cast down
The ancient wrongs that tear our souls apart!
He calls this sedition, I call it truth!
He calls it chaos, I call it new birth!
He calls it danger to our nation's youth,
I call it justice blooming on the earth!
(Another, louder cheer. Many raise their weapons.)

A COMMONER
Aye, Valerius! Thy words are sharp and true!
My children starve, while royal coffers swell!
This burden placed upon us, old and new,
Hath made our lives a living, constant hell!

ANOTHER COMMONER
My brother died upon the northern field,
For battles fought for kings, and not for us!
No recompense, no comfort did they yield,
But only deeper sorrow, and thus
My father pines, my mother ever weeps!
The King forgets the blood that buys his peace!

VALERIUS
(Spreading his arms, embracing their pain.)
Hark, hear their voices! Are these not the cries
That pierce the heavens, begging for relief?
Are these not honest tears, from honest eyes,
That speak of suffering, and endless grief?
The King doth turn his ear from such plain sound,
And wraps himself in cloaks of kingly pride.
He sees no suffering upon this ground,
But only challenge, from this surging tide!
He sends his armies now, to crush our plea,
To silence truth, and put us back in chains.
He means to show us what his rule can be,
By spilling blood upon these fertile plains!
But we, my friends, we shall not bend nor break!
We stand for freedom, for a better way!
For every child, for every woman's sake,
We shall make strong our stand this very day!
(He draws a plain, unadorned sword. It gleams in the sunlight.)
This sword is not for conquest, nor for greed!
It is the sword of justice, finely honed!
It is the promise of a future freed,
From tyranny, from burdens disowned!
Septimus, my friend, what news doth fly?
Doth the King's host approach our city walls?

SEPTIMUS
(Stepping forward, his voice a gravelly rumble.)
They come, Valerius. The King himself doth ride
At their vanguard, with Gaius at his side.
Their numbers vast, their banners waving wide,
A sea of steel, a tide of vengeful pride.
Their cavalry hath been sighted on the plains,
Their footmen marching, like a shadowed cloud.
The royal fury, with its bitter rains,
Doth seek to drown our hopes amongst the crowd.
They seek to take Veridia, and to break
Our newfound spirit, ere it takes firm hold.
They mean to make an example, for their sake,
And leave our bodies, lifeless, stark, and cold.
But fear not, Valerius! Our walls are strong,
Our men are ready, though our numbers less.
Our hearts are true, our purpose clear and long,
To fight for freedom, banishing distress!

LYRA
(Stepping forward, raising her banner high.)
Aye! Let them come! For every stone they throw,
For every arrow that they loose with hate,
Shall meet a spirit they can never know,
A burning fire, to seal their cruel fate!
They fight for power, for a rusted crown!
We fight for dignity, for life, for breath!
Let us not falter, though they cast us down!
For freedom, we shall conquer even death!

VALERIUS
(Nodding, a grim determination on his face.)
Bravely spoken, Lyra! And true words, Septimus.
We know the odds, the might arrayed against us.
But what they lack in spirit, we possess,
A burning cause, a reason to be just!
They fight with gold, with numbers, and with might.
We fight with hearts ablaze, with righteous ire!
They fight for darkness, we for dawning light,
And freedom's flame shall cleanse their feudal fire!
To arms, my friends! To battlements and walls!
Let every man and woman play their part!
For when the trumpet's final, urgent calls
Resound, let courage guide each loyal heart!
We shall defend this city, stone by stone!
We shall defend our freedom, breath by breath!
Let it be carved in history, made known,
That we embraced our liberty, not death!
(Another mighty cheer erupts, fiercer than before. The crowd begins to disperse, heading towards the city walls, weapons in hand. Valerius watches them go, then turns to Septimus and Lyra.)

VALERIUS
The King's wrath is a tempest, fierce and grand.
But a tempest often spends its fury fast.
We must hold firm, and make our final stand,
Until his royal arrogance is past.
Septimus, ensure our stores are well secured.
The water sources, every single well.
Our archers ready, every arrow spurred,
To meet the foe, and send them back to hell.
Lyra, your banner, let it wave on high!
A symbol of our hope, our common bond.
Inspire the weary with its hopeful sky,
And lead our women, to their work beyond
The battle lines, preparing food and care.
For every soldier needs a helping hand.
Our strength is not just steel, but what we share,
The common purpose, for this troubled land.

SEPTIMUS
It shall be done, Valerius. Every man
Is at his post, or soon shall take his place.
We have devised a strong and cunning plan,
To hold the city, and slow down their pace.
The outer gates are barred with iron bolts,
The inner gates reinforced with heavy timber.
Our traps are set, for any sudden jolts
Of royal madness, for each daring climber.
We know the King's impetuosity.
He will attack with fury, without thought.
His pride will be his greatest enemy.
And by that flaw, his victory dearly bought.

VALERIUS
(A faint smile touches his lips, quickly vanishing.)
Aye, Septimus. He thinks his very name
Can shatter walls, and turn the tide of war.
But courage born of freedom's fervent flame,
Doth burn far brighter than a king's loud roar.
I pray to heaven that this bitter strife
Might end with reason, not with endless gore.
But if the King doth seek to take my life,
And crush the people's spirit to the floor,
Then let him come! For I shall stand and fight!
For every soul that suffers, yearns to breathe!
We shall defend our truth with all our might,
And for our freedom, victory achieve!
(He looks towards the horizon, where a distant dust cloud suggests the approaching royal army. The sound of distant trumpets, faint but ominous, can be heard.)
Hark! The King's trumpets. They announce his pride.
But our drums shall answer with a different beat.
The drum of freedom, with nothing left to hide,
A rhythm that foretells his swift defeat!
(He turns, and walks towards the city walls, his sword still in hand, his gaze fixed on the approaching conflict.)
The hour is come. Let history record
The choice we make, the stand that we embrace.
For freedom, we unsheath the righteous sword,
And claim our future, in this hallowed place!

(The trumpets grow louder, mixed with the distant rumble of marching feet. The sun, high in the sky, casts long shadows as the forces of the King draw ever closer to Veridia.)

(SCENE END)
(The play continues.)
