VALERIUS
My heart doth trouble, good Elijah, and my sleep
Is vexed by shadows, spectres of unrest.
The Duke, that serpent, Leofric, doth creep
With subtle poison 'round my peace and crest.
He courts young Seraphina, so 'tis said,
A maid whose lands abut mine ancient hold.
Should she but grace his ducal, gilded bed,
My sovereign sway were lost, my story sold
To vassalage beneath his grasping hand.

ELIJAH
My lord, I beg you, let not rumour's breath
Fan 'gainst your reason such a fiery brand.
The Lady Seraphina is but youth,
And Leofric, though potent, seeks perchance
A gentle match, not conquest by the ring.
His purpose may be love, not war's dread dance,
Nor yet a plot your sovereignty to sting.
Give credence not to every whispered tale.

VALERIUS
Nay, good Elijah, you are too benign,
Too apt to trust where treachery doth trail.
His smile is fair, but hides a sharp design.
Remember well the quarrel o'er the fens,
The disputed marsh, the borderland of old.
His tongue then promised peace, his hands held pens
That signed my loss, a deed both false and bold.
He is a wolf in lambkin's wool arrayed,
And Seraphina, innocently blind,
Will be the unwitting pawn in his parade
To bring my lineage low, and leave me pined.

ELIJAH
Yet hasty action, sir, breeds swift regret.
To challenge him without a cause in proof
Would mark you rash, a king's unheeding threat,
And drive your friends to stand then far aloof.
Let us observe, let patience be your guide,
And wisdom's torch illuminate the path.

VALERIUS
Patience. Nay, patience is for fools to hide
Their craven fear from coming storm and wrath.
I feel it here, within my very soul,
A chill foreboding, deeper than the tomb.
He moves to gain an unassailable goal,
And I, Valerius, stand within his gloom.
Hark, what sound is that, a knocking at the door?

ELIJAH
A servant, sir, with tidings, nothing more.

VALERIUS
Bid him enter. Let us hear what fresh dismay
The turning hour hath brought us on this day.

(Enter MESSENGER)

MESSENGER
My Lord Valerius, pardon this intrusion.
A message from the north, of swift import.
Lady Seraphina, by a strange delusion,
Hath left her manor for a distant port.
She sails at dawn, by Leofric's own ship,
To wed a foreign noble, so they say.

VALERIUS
What. By my soul, this news doth make me whip
My blood to fire. Seraphina. Away.
To wed a foreign lord. This is his game.
A deeper ploy, a feint, a master stroke.
He takes her not, yet shares her lands and fame
Through this alliance, a subtle, binding yoke.
He doth secure the north, and my domain
Is circled now, by this insidious art.
Elijah, see, my fears were not in vain.
This treachery doth strike me to the heart.
Prepare my horse. I ride this very night.
I'll not be trifled with, by man or fate.
This web of malice I shall tear with might,
And meet his cunning with a force more great.

ELIJAH
My Lord, I pray, consider well this deed.
A hasty ride, a rash and sudden quest,
May lead to perils that we do not need,
And put your honour to a grievous test.

VALERIUS
My honour, man, is reason to my sword.
I'll not stand idly by whilst villains scheme.
My patience ends. I am Valerius, Lord,
And I shall wake them from this bitter dream.
Away, Elijah. See my will be done.
The sun shall rise upon a new-born war.

(Exit VALERIUS, in a fury)

ELIJAH
(Solus)
Alas, when passion overrules the mind,
What tangled webs of sorrow do we find.
He sees a foe where no true foe doth dwell,
And by his wrath, he digs his own deep hell.

ELIJAH
(Solus)
Alas, when passion overrules the mind,
What tangled webs of sorrow do we find.
He sees a foe where no true foe doth dwell,
And by his wrath, he digs his own deep hell.
My Lord Valerius, blinded by a shade
Of ancient wrong, a phantom subtly made
By rumour's breath, doth rush unto his doom.
His noble heart, now cloaked in hasty gloom,
Doth take a rumour for a proven fact.
Seraphina wed. A binding, false pact.
To wed a foreign noble. So they say.
But who are 'they'? What whispers lead astray?
Leofric's ship. Aye, that part doth ring true.
But what design doth Leofric pursue?
Is he a spider, weaving threads of night,
Or but a pawn, caught in a darker light?
Valerius thinks him mastermind of ill,
A subtle serpent, bending all to will.
Yet Leofric, though stern and oft severe,
Hath ever prized his honour, held it dear.
The quarrel o'er the fens, a bitter cause,
Was settled by the letter of the laws,
Though Valerius felt the sting of loss.
Could Leofric then bear so foul a cross,
To traffic hearts for mere terrestrial gain,
And sow division through a gentle reign?
My loyal duty binds me to my lord,
But conscience bids me sheath his hasty sword.
I must not let him blindly charge ahead,
To wake a sleeping lion from its bed.
I'll send a rider swift, to trace her path,
To learn the truth, and cool Valerius' wrath.
Or better yet, myself shall take the road,
And lighten Valerius' unthinking load.
A quiet scout, unseen, unheard, unknown,
Before a storm of fury he hath sown.
(Exit ELIJAH, determined)

(A wild and rocky pass. The wind howls. VALERIUS, cloaked and mounted, rides furiously, his horse’s breath steaming in the cold night air.)

VALERIUS
Haste, noble beast, let not thy sinews tire!
Let every hoof-beat fan my burning fire!
The north awaits, where treachery doth brood,
And innocence by cunning is subdued.
Seraphina! My kinswoman, my blood!
To be ensnared within this hateful flood
Of Leofric's vile machinations deep!
While honest men like me are left to weep,
Or rage, as fury now doth grip my soul.
He seeks to bind my lands, to take his toll
Upon my lineage, crush my ancient name.
But by the heavens, he shall feel my flame!
He thinks me soft, a king of gentle hand,
Who lets his foes usurp his rightful land.
He thinks because I seek fair justice's sway,
I lack the iron will to seize the day.
But he shall learn Valerius is no child,
To be deceived by smiles, however mild.
This foreign wedding! What a subtle snare!
To tie the north, beyond all just compare,
To distant powers, hostile and unknown.
And leave my heart and kingdom overthrown.
Nay, I shall tear this fabric of deceit,
Before it wraps my realm in bitter sheet.
The dawn shall find me where his treachery sleeps,
And justice wake where villainy now creeps.
What shadows lurk upon the mountain's crest?
Are those his spies, come to obstruct my quest?
(He draws his sword, looking keenly into the gloom.)
Come forth, ye hirelings of a coward's hand!
Valerius fears no foe in any land!
(He waits, listening. The wind whistles. No one appears.)
A trick of night, a phantom of the mind,
Wrought by the fears which restless spirits find.
But fear I not. My purpose is my guide.
And righteous anger courses at my side.
(He spurs his horse onward, disappearing into the darkness.)

(A small, fortified watchtower in the northern marches. A few SOLDIERS huddle by a fire. A SENTINEL

SENTINEL
A bitter wind doth scour the stony peak
And gnaws the marrow from our frozen bone.
The night grows old, and every lonely shriek
Of mountain beast doth make the spirit groan.
What stirreth there? A shadow in the gloom?
Or but a fancy born of endless watch?

FIRST SOLDIER
More like a chill that seals us to our tomb
Than living thing. My fingers scarce can scratch
A spark from flint. This north doth hate our breath.

SECOND SOLDIER
Aye, Leofric's peace hath brought a living death
To those who guard it. Scant reward for toil.
They feast in halls, while we freeze on this soil.

SENTINEL
Hark now! Methought I saw a rider pass,
A figure swift, like spirit of the blast.
Too quick for mortal horse, across the grass
He flew, his steed no common earthly cast.

FIRST SOLDIER
A phantom, comrade. Fancy plays thee false.
No sane man rides these crags when darkness calls.
The road is treacherous, the passes deep.

SENTINEL
Yet 'twas no phantom that my eyes did keep.
A noble form, though cloaked against the air.
He rode as if pursued by black despair
Or driven by a fury all his own.
He rode to northward, whither danger's sown.

SECOND SOLDIER
The northern marches ever breed such tales.
They say that Leofric, whose power prevails,
Doth seek to bind the north with southern thread,
A marriage pact where hearts are left for dead.
A kinswoman of Valerius, I hear,
Is pledged to one whose name I know not, here
In these bleak lands, against her very will.

FIRST SOLDIER
Such rumours run, as water down a rill,
And gather force. Valerius, proud and stern,
Would not let kin for state's cold purpose burn.

SENTINEL
Then mark my words, if 'twas no ghost I spied,
But living man with passion for his guide,
Then trouble brews. A storm is on the way,
To break the peace of this most frigid day.

(A new day dawns, though still cold and grey. VALERIUS spurs his horse through a winding forest track, the animal clearly tiring. Valerius's face is grim and set.)

VALERIUS
Press on, my loyal friend! Though muscle aches,
And every breath a new exhaustion takes,
The goal is nigh! I feel it in the air,
The stench of false intent, a subtle snare
Woven by Leofric, whose smiling guise
Doth hide the venom of his serpent's eyes.
To barter Seraphina! My own blood!
A virgin flower, caught in treason's flood!
He means to tie these northern lords to him,
And make my rightful sway grow weak and dim,
By forcing kin to wed a stranger's hand.
What matter peace, when honour stains the land?
He speaks of statecraft, of a wider peace,
But 'tis his power he means to increase.
And I, Valerius, who hold the crown,
Am meant to watch my kingdom tumble down,
Piece by slow piece, until I stand alone,
A hollow king upon a crumbling throne.
Nay, by the saints! This day shall see me strike!
I'll tear the web, and break the ancient dyke
Of his ambition, ere it drowns us all.
My righteous anger answers duty's call.
(He dismounts, tying his horse to a stunted tree. He takes a moment to scan the distant horizon.)
Yonder, a tower rears its ancient head.
The seat, they say, where Seraphina's led,
A lamb to slaughter, by a shepherd false.
I'll scale these slopes, heedless of their falls,
And learn the truth. My sword shall be my tongue,
If peaceful pleas are scorned, or justice stung.
(He begins to climb a steep, rocky path, sword at his side, cloak flapping in the wind. He moves with a determined urgency.)

(Meanwhile, ELIJAH rides swiftly along a less arduous road, his horse fresher. He too is clad for travel, though less dramatically than Valerius. He stops at a small, isolated inn, its sign swinging precariously.)

ELIJAH
(To himself)
The wind howls stories, and the dust doth rise
From hurried hooves. Valerius's fierce eyes,
I know them well. He sees but one stark truth,
And charges forth with all the fire of youth,
Though wisdom's years should temper such hot haste.
He rides to judgment, lest the truth be waste.
But what if truth be not as he conceives?
What if the serpent hides within the leaves
Of Leofric's fair purpose? Or what more?
What if there is no serpent, but a door
To peace, misjudged by Valerius's fear?
I must unravel what is hidden here.
(He dismounts, and enters the inn.)

(Inside, the inn is dimly lit and sparse. AN OLD INNKEEPER wipes a wooden counter, his face lined with age and weariness. A lone TRAVELER sits by the hearth.)

ELIJAH
Good morrow to this house, though morn be grey.
I seek refreshment, and perchance to stray
Into the knowledge of these northern ways.
What news do idle tongues rehearse these days?

INNKEEPER
Good morrow, sir. The news is ever cold
As winter's breath. No joyful tales are told
In these parts now, save rumours of a wedding,
Which sets some hearts to hope, and others dreading.
A kinswoman, they say, of Valerius,
Pledged to a lord whose name sounds ominous
To southern ears. But Leofric doth swear
'Twill bring forth peace, and banish war's despair.

ELIJAH
A wedding? Aye. And where is this event
To take its place? Is all consentient
To such a match? Or do some voices cry
Against this union, as the moments fly?

INNKEEPER
It is at Castle Blackwood, far to north.
A day's ride hence, for those who hurry forth.
As for consent, I know not what men say
Beyond the whisperings that hold their sway.
Some call it wisdom, some a subtle snare.
But Leofric's will, 'tis said, hangs in the air
As heavy as the snow that caps the peak.
What choice has man but patiently to seek
To understand? Or else to hold his tongue.

ELIJAH
And has there been a notable one sprung
From southern lands, whose haste outruns the wind?
A noble, by his bearing, you might find
Memorable, had he passed this way?

INNKEEPER
Ah, such a one, but hours past this day.
He rode with fire, as if the very Fates
Had set his spirit on their fiery gates.
He asked no questions, took no drink or bread,
But spurred his horse as if by demons led,
Northward he rode, I marked it well, towards
The very stronghold of the northern lords.
He spoke no name, nor gave he any sign
Of purpose, save a fury most malign.

ELIJAH
(To himself)
Valerius, without a doubt! He speeds
To sow discord, and plant most bitter seeds
Where peace might bloom. I must make greater haste.
(To the INNKEEPER)
My thanks, good friend. Your words have quickly traced
The path I need. I take no drink today,
But here, a coin for comfort on your way.
(He tosses a coin on the counter, then swiftly exits.)

(ELIJAH remounts his horse, urgency now etching his features. He spurs the horse onward, following Valerius's trail.)

ELIJAH
My lord Valerius, blinded by his pride,
Doth ride to challenge what he thinks doth hide
A deep deceit. But if the truth be plain,
A marriage for the realm's long-sought-for gain,
Then will his hasty hand destroy all hope,
And leave the kingdom on a slippery slope
Of civil strife. What treachery is this,
That Leofric should seek to weave amiss
A plot against his king, his trusted lord?
And yet, his honour oft hath been adored
By those who serve him. Could ambition's sting
Corrupt a soul that once did truly sing
Of justice, and of virtue, and of peace?
I pray this fevered pace doth find surcease
Before the moment when his anger breaks.
I must gain tidings for Valerius's sakes,
And for the kingdom's. May my journey be
A light that shows him clearer sight to see.
(He rides off at speed.)

(A grand hall in Castle Blackwood. Tapestries adorn the walls. Lords and Ladies mill about, some engaged in quiet conversation, others observing the preparations for a wedding feast. At the head of a long table, LEOFRIC sits, a man of grave but not unkind demeanour. Beside him, SERAPHINA, pale and beautiful, sits with downcast eyes. A FOREIGN PRINCE, young and somewhat stiff, stands near her, attempting polite conversation.)

FOREIGN PRINCE
The customs of your northern lands are cold,
Compared to sunnier climes. Yet I am told
Your people's hearts burn with a fiercer flame
Of loyalty, and honour knows its name
Amongst your mountains. Is it so, fair maid?

SERAPHINA
(Softly, without looking up)
Our mountains are a shield, Prince Aldric. They
Guard ancient virtues, lest they fade away.
The warmth within, 'tis often kept concealed,
Until by trial, its true worth is revealed.

FOREIGN PRINCE
A noble sentiment. I hope to learn
To cherish such a warmth, and to discern
Its hidden depths. My kingdom seeks alliance,
Not mere dominion, nor a cold defiance
Of sovereign rights. My father, wise and old,
Doth see in this a future to unfold
Where northern strength and southern grace combine
To forge a power truly most divine.

LEOFRIC
(Smiling gravely)
Prince Aldric speaks with wisdom, Seraphina.
This union brings a peace long sought, I mean,
Across our borders, where for years the sword
Hath been the only arbiter or lord.
Valerius, our King, doth give assent,
And sees this pact as heaven-truly-sent.
A bond of nations, stronger than the stone
Of any fortress, built for peace alone.

SERAPHINA
(Still not meeting his gaze)
If it be so, my lord. If truly peace
Doth bloom from this, and all contentions cease.
My duty then, as kinswoman to king,
Is to embrace the fate these tidings bring.
But 'tis a heavy weight, this peace you claim,
To bear it solitary, in my name.

LEOFRIC
Such burden, Seraphina, is shared by all
Who labour for the realm. Though duty's call
Be stern, and happiness must sometimes yield
To greater purpose, on a wider field.
Your grace, your gentle spirit, will assuage
The prince's fears, and turn a brighter page
In both our histories. This is no chain,
But silken thread that binds for common gain.

(A LADY-IN-WAITING, ELARA, approaches Seraphina, carrying a rich garment.)

ELARA
My lady, here the bridal gown doth wait.
Its fabric shimmers, fit for queenly state.
The seamstresses have worked through all the night,
To deck you in this vision of pure light.

SERAPHINA
(Looks at the gown, then away with a sigh)
A gilded cage, however bright its hue,
Doth still enclose. Yet duty I pursue.
Leave it, Elara. I shall don it when
The final hour calls for women and for men
To witness what the Fates have preordained.
(Elara places the gown on a chair and bows, casting a sympathetic glance at Seraphina.)

(Leofric watches Seraphina with a thoughtful expression. He turns to a nearby NOBLEMAN.)

LEOFRIC
She struggles, good Sir Kael. The tender heart
Doth find it hard to play its given part
When private longings whisper in the ear.
But she is strong. Her spirit knows no fear
When honour bids. Valerius's line, though proud,
Hath ever bent its knee beneath the cloud
Of sovereign duty. He himself doth know
The heavy cost when seeds of passion grow
Beyond their measure, into foolish strife.
He understands the sacrifice of life,
And love, for state.

SIR KAEL
Indeed, my lord. The King
Doth rule with justice, and true counsel bring
To every matter. 'Tis his own command
That blesses this alliance for the land.
The northern lords, whose loyalties oft sway,
Are bound by this, to hold to him their way.
A clever move, to bind them to the crown
Through Seraphina's pledge, throughout the town
Of politics and power. Peace will reign.

LEOFRIC
And so it shall. The future we attain
Must be secure. Prince Aldric's royal kin
Are strong, and honest. They would never win
By foul deceit. Their word is firm as rock.
This marriage builds a bulwark, to withstand the shock
Of any foe who seeks to undermine
Our kingdom's peace, and shatter its design.
(Leofric looks towards Seraphina, then to Prince Aldric. A faint smile, not entirely free of worry, touches his lips.)

(Back on the rocky path, VALERIUS has reached a vantage point overlooking Castle Blackwood. He sees the activity, the banners, the arriving guests, and interprets it all through the lens of his suspicion.)

VALERIUS
Behold the viper's nest! The gaudy show
Of banners flaunting, where black treasons grow!
The guests assemble, like a carrion flock,
To witness this most base, most cruel mock
Of honour's name! See how the foreign flags
Unfurl against the sky, like villainous rags!
Prince Aldric, he whose name my ear hath spurned,
The very instrument by which is turned
My kinswoman to a political pawn!
To be consumed before the hateful dawn
Of this alliance, meant to crush my might!
Oh, Seraphina, caught in endless night!
I see the preparations for the feast,
A celebration for the ravening beast
Of Leofric's ambition! Every stone
Of that proud castle doth begin to groan
With silent protest at the coming wrong.
How long hath this deceitful plot been strong,
Beneath the cloak of peace, and duty's name?
He plays the loyal liegeman, free from blame,
While secretly he sharpens all his blades
To sever my connection to these glades
Of northern power, where my ancestors
Held sway for ages, through a hundred wars.
(He draws his sword, its steel glinting in the cold light.)
No more! My sword shall cut the Gordian knot
Of this vile treason! All that he hath got
By cunning hand, I'll tear it from his grasp!
Before this union seals her life's last gasp,
And binds my realm in chains of servitude.
I am Valerius! My spirit crude
To compromise with traitors! My heart cries
For justice, burning in these northern skies!
(He starts to descend the path towards the castle, his movements quick and determined. He seems to be in a trance of anger, heedless of any danger.)

(ELIJAH, still riding hard, pauses at a crossroads. He sees a peasant woman leading a mule.)

ELIJAH
Good woman, may I beg a moment's speech?
My journey leads to parts I scarce can reach
Without direction. Is this path the way
To Castle Blackwood, ere the close of day?

PEASANT WOMAN
Aye, sir, it is. But why such pressing speed?
There's naught but feasting there, and little need
To hurry so. Unless you seek the bride,
Or groom, whose foreign name doth cast a tide
Of whispers through the villages and farms.
They say Prince Aldric brings both wealth and harms.

ELIJAH
Harms, say you? What manner of ill do you mean?

PEASANT WOMAN
Oh, nothing certain, sir, but 'tis a scene
Of much ado. Lord Leofric, so grave,
Doth push this match. They say he seeks to save
Our lands from strife. But some old folk do fear
That foreign blood doth bring its dangers near.
They say King Valerius, far away in south,
Hath not truly blessed it with his mouth.
But Leofric, he rules this region now,
And bends the knee to none but his own brow.

ELIJAH
(To himself)
The plot thickens with each spoken word.
The peasant's fears, though oft by rumour stirred,
Reflect a doubt of Leofric's intent.
And Valerius's name is subtly rent
From full consent. He thinks my lord doth know
And doth approve. But this doth make me grow
More fearful for Valerius's rash design.
He sees a spider, where a statesman's sign
Of peace might lie. Or does the spider weave
So cunningly, that even I believe
His honest front?

(To the PEASANT WOMAN)
Good thanks, old friend. Your words
Have shed a light upon these warring lords
And their strange pacts. May heaven guide our way.
(He gives her a small coin, then again spurs his horse onward. His pace is now even more desperate, as if he races against an inevitable disaster.)

ELIJAH
Oh, for a tongue to speak with Leofric!
To probe his heart, to learn what subtle trick
He means to play. Or if his heart is pure,
To find the means Valerius to assure
Of honest purpose. But my king rides on,
His fury honed, his doubts already gone.
He trusts no reason, save his passionate plea
For Seraphina's freedom, and to see
His honour vindicated. What a coil
Of misunderstanding, brewing on this soil!
If Leofric indeed doth mean to bind
His loyalties through Seraphina, kind
And gentle soul, to a foreign sway,
Then Valerius's wrath holds righteous sway.
But if 'tis peace, a shield against the foe,
Then Valerius's charge will overthrow
The very ground on which our safety stands.
I must arrive before his vengeful hands
Unleash the storm. A whisper, or a sign,
Might yet prevent this tragic, grim design.
(He disappears over a rise, following the winding path that leads to Castle Blackwood.)

(Inside Castle Blackwood, in a private chamber. SERAPHINA is being dressed for the wedding by ELARA and another LADY. Seraphina's face is tear-stained.)

SERAPHINA
This silk, this lace, these jewels that gleam and shine,
Are but adornments for a soul in pine.
They speak of joy, of promise, and of grace,
But in my heart, they find no kindred place.
Elara, tell me, does my uncle, Leofric,
Truly believe this union holds no trick
For our own kingdom? Does he truly swear
That Valerius doth consent, and does not care
For my heart's freedom, or my woman's choice?
To be a pawn, without a single voice
To speak against this fate.

ELARA
My lady, he
Doth claim the King's consent. He swears to me
And all the court, this marriage is for good,
To seal a truce, long wished and understood
By mighty rulers. Prince Aldric, though austere,
Doth bear himself with honour, free from fear
Of censure. He would rule your people well.

SERAPHINA
And what of rule? What of the magic spell
Of love, that poets sing? Am I to be
A barren vessel, set upon the sea
Of cold diplomacy? My heart, it yearns
For warmth, for passion, such as truly burns
In bosoms free to choose their own sweet way.
But I am bound. My life begins this day,
Not with a lover's kiss, but duty's vow.
(She looks at herself in a polished metal mirror. Her reflection seems a stranger.)
My father's name, Valerius's high renown,
Demand this sacrifice. To wear this gown
Is to become a symbol, not a maid.
My private dreams are withered and decayed.

SECOND LADY
Yet, lady, think upon the good it brings!
No longer shall the border's bitter stings
Divide our peoples. Peace shall heal the scars
Of ancient quarrels. Yonder, from the stars,
Perhaps this union was foretold to be
A beacon light for all posterity.

SERAPHINA
A beacon forged from tears. Yet I obey.
To question Leofric is to betray
The trust he places. And my sovereign lord,
Valerius, whose righteous, noble word
Doth guide our realm, hath surely given leave.
He would not let my tender spirit grieve
Unless for greater purpose. Is it so?
Must all my secret, hidden passions go
Unmet, unheeded, for a kingdom's gain?
(A sigh escapes her lips, a sound of deep, resigned sorrow.)
Then let it be. My life I here resign
To fate's cold dictates. Make me brave, divine
And patient spirits. Let me play my part
With grace, though sorrow eats my bleeding heart.

ELARA
You are too gentle for this world's harsh strife,
My lady. Would that you might choose your life
As you desire. But royal blood must bear
The weighty burden, and the constant care
Of kingdom's need.

SERAPHINA
I know, I know. Yet still,
A secret hope, despite my strongest will,
Doth flicker faintly. Would that one could rise,
A champion of truth, beneath these skies,
To prove my uncle's motive true and plain,
Or else release me from this bitter pain.
But who would dare? Who would defy the great
And powerful Leofric, and challenge fate?
(A knock at the door.)

LEOFRIC
(From outside)
My dear kinswoman, are you ready now?
The hour approaches. It is time to bow
To sacred vows. The guests await below.

SERAPHINA
(Collecting herself, with effort)
I come, my lord. As ready as I'll be.
(She takes a deep breath, and lifts her head, a mask of noble resignation now in place. Elara adjusts her veil. Seraphina walks towards the door.)

(The grand hall, now filled with guests. Prince Aldric stands expectantly by an improvised altar. Leofric stands nearby, his expression solemn. A fanfare sounds, and Seraphina enters, escorted by her two ladies. A hush falls over the hall. Just as she approaches the altar, a sudden commotion is heard from the castle gates, followed by shouts.)

FIRST NOBLEMAN
What sounds are these? A most ill-timed alarm!

SECOND NOBLEMAN
Hark! 'Tis the clash of steel, and cries of harm!

(The doors to the hall burst open, revealing VALERIUS, sword in hand, his face a mask of furious determination. Behind him, a few of his loyal guards, who have evidently fought their way in, stand ready.)

VALERIUS
Stay this unholy rite! This foul charade!
No further step, before my sword is played
Against this treason! Leofric, you snake!
Your cunning plot, for Seraphina's sake,
I here denounce! You think to steal my kin,
To bind her fate, and sow dissent within
My very kingdom! But Valerius stands
To break your chains, and free her from your hands!

At bay in west.

KING

Good choices, Aethelred. Your foresight's clear.
But tell me, how will these raw levies bear
The sudden shock of battle? For these Picts,
Though savage, fight with courage, and their tricks
Of ambush, born from mountain and from glen,
Are known to overcome even seasoned men.
Have they been trained? Or are they merely sheep
Sent to the slaughter, while their mothers weep?

AETHELRED

My King, the fyrd, as you well know, are sons
Of plough and hammer, not of battle guns.
Yet every man, in English blood, doth feel
The ancient urge to guard his commonweal.
We set them to their drills, from dawn till dusk,
To stand in shield-wall, hard as an iron tusk,
To thrust with spear, and swing the axe with might.
Their captains, seasoned in the border fight,
Instruct them daily. Fear and ancient lore
Of Northmen raids, oft whispered on the shore,
Hath taught them much. They may not be the same
As your Housecarls, those fiery sons of fame,
But for their hearths, their wives, their cherished kin,
They'll stand as lions, and the battle win.
Their ardour now, kindled by holy ire,
Doth burn as fiercely as a cleansing fire.
They speak of vengeance for the desecrated shrine,
And swear by Christ, that every Pictish swine
Shall pay in blood for sacrilege profound.
This common wrath unites them on the ground.
Yet, arms are lacking, as my Lord Leofric said.
The common swords are blunt, the helmets dread
With rust and age. A thousand shields are split,
And few the hauberks that our smiths can fit
To every chest. This burden on our stores
Is great, my Liege, for war's devouring jaws
Consume all treasure, swift as hungry flame.
The land grows lean when war pronounces name.

KING

Aye, I have felt that chill. The royal chest,
Though once it overflowed, is now oppressed
By peaceful years. What stores of wheat and corn
Have we amassed, to feed our host forlorn?
For men fight well upon a full stomach,
But ill and weakly if their rations lack.

AETHELRED

Our granaries are sound, my King, for now.
The harvest yielded much, to fill the mow,
And careful stewards saved for leaner times.
But such a host as we must raise, consumes
A mountain swift. Our drovers herd the beasts
From distant pastures, for the hungry feasts
Of countless men. The bakers toil all night
To feed the fyrd, and keep their spirits bright.
Yet, even so, if this war lingers long,
Or winter's icy breath begins its song,
Then famine's gaunt hand might assist the foe,
And lay our proudest warriors cold and low.
The challenge, King, is not of men alone,
But of their sustenance, by seed and bone.

KING

A bitter truth. Leofric's words ring true,
We need swift gold, and stores both old and new.
And what of messengers? Who are these keen
And watchful men you spoke of? Have they seen
The Pictish camps? Or gathered any sign
Of Bran's dark purpose? Is his main design
To strike at London, or to ravage wide
Through open country, with no goal to guide
His savage host?

AETHELRED

My scouts are few, my King, but sharp of wit.
Young Cynewulf, a lad whose courage's lit
By loyal fire, hath ridden north alone,
Disguised as pedlar, heard a drunken groan
Of Pictish captains in a border inn.
He brings but whispers, clouded, thin, and grim.
They speak of gathering at a place called Cairn,
A hollowed hill, where ancient spirits burn,
North of the Wall, a meeting point of might,
Where painted tribes prepare for bloody fight.
Their numbers, by his guess, are past belief,
A locust swarm, beyond all words of grief.
But this is rumour, heard through ale-soaked haze.
He saw no banners, through the misty days.
He knows not Bran's true aim, but fears a blow
At unprotected lands, where hamlets grow
In quiet peace, to draw our forces thin,
Before a main assault begins to win
Our greater cities. He spoke of a witch,
A crone who whispers prophecies in pitch
Of night, whose counsel Bran doth hold as law.
A chilling tale, to make the spirit thaw.

KING

A witch, you say? These pagan devils cling
To darkness, and on wicked spirits swing
Their faithless hopes. This strengthens my resolve
To purge their evil, and their power dissolve.
The greater numbers, though they give me pause,
Cannot outweigh the justice of our cause.
For God is with us, and our English saints
Shall lend their strength, when mortal power faints.
But Cairn, you say? That ancient, cursed place,
Is known for ambush, where no man finds grace
Or easy passage. If they gather there,
They mean to march, and fill the common air
With terror. We must anticipate their move.
How many days, think you, ere they disprove
Our fragile peace?

AETHELRED

The journey from the Cairn to London's gates
Is many days, my King, if fortune waits
Upon their march. Perhaps a fortnight more,
If winter's chill holds back the savage roar
Of eager warriors. But they move with speed,
And live off plunder, as a hungry weed
Doth suck the soil. So ten days, or even less,
Might bring their terror to our peacefulness.
This gives us little time, to gather all
Our scattered strength, and answer honour's call.
The beacons must be lit, from hill to hill,
To rouse the sleeping land, and sound the shrill
Alarm of danger. Every able man,
From priest to peasant, must obey the plan,
And ready stand for England's dire defence.
The call for arms must echo with intense
And urgent plea, from every English mouth.

KING

Then let it be so. Send forth your swift men now,
With burning brands, and bids to bend and bow
No longer to the plough. Let every Thane
And noble Baron ride across the plain
Of Wessex, Mercia, and Northumbria's south,
And speak the summons with courageous mouth.
Let every shire contribute of its strength,
Its seasoned warriors, famed for breadth and length
Of spear and shield. Let beacons bright ignite
From hill to hill, across the fading light,
To wake the land, and bid her sons arise.
Our standing fyrd, though brave, cannot suffice
Against so vast a number. We must call
The levies forth, and answer honour's call.
And for the gold, my heart, though sore it aches,
Must steel itself, for England's honour's sakes.
Let messengers of speed and trusted word,
Ride to the Bishops, let their pleas be heard
In every church and monastery tall.
And to the merchants, let the message fall
Like iron hail, that they must yield their coin,
To save their profit, ere the Picts enjoin
Their wealth to plunder. From the royal mint,
Let every die be struck, without stint
Of labour. The royal treasury, though strained,
Must pour its last, till victory is gained.

AETHELRED

It shall be done, my King. No man shall rest,
Until the summons flies from east to west,
And north to south, across our English lands.
Our horses ride, our messengers make demands.

KING

And tell me, Aethelred, what of the mood
Amongst the common folk? Hath panic brewed
Its bitter potion in their simple minds?
Or do they stand, as English spirit finds
Its strength in peril, ready for the fray?
For common will can turn the darkest day.

AETHELRED

My Liege, fear sows its seeds in every heart,
But hope, and anger too, play equal part.
The news of God's house sullied, and the grave
Of ancient saints defiled, doth make them brave
With holy wrath. The priests, with fervent tongue,
Recount the horrors, and the war-song's sung
In every village green. The mothers weep
For sons who go to fight, but also keep
A burning pride. The older men recount
The ancient battles, and the brave account
Of English victory against the foe.
They see you, King, as shield against their woe.
They speak your name with reverence and trust,
And pray that God will grant you strength, as must
A King who fights for them. There is a fear,
A primal dread, when savage foe draws near,
But it is mingled with a steadfast will
To guard their homes, and climb the hardest hill
For England's sake. They only ask a leader bold,
To guide them through the night, as stories told
Of ancient heroes. And that leader's you.

KING

(A rare, grim smile touches the King's lips.)

Then I shall be that leader, Aethelred.
And by God's grace, no English blood is shed
In vain. Let every man, from highest Lord
To lowest peasant, draw his righteous sword.
Let every bell within the churches toll,
Not for a death, but for a nation's soul
Awakening. Let prayers rise to the sky,
For strength to stand, and courage, e'en to die
If need be. But our goal is victory,
To drive these painted demons to the sea.
Go, Aethelred. See to the beacons' light,
And send your scouts to pierce the darkest night.
Dispatch commanders, swift and true and keen,
To fortify our bulwarks, bold and green
With fresh-cut timbers. Let our fleet arise,
And sweep the ocean, to the salty skies.
And tell your Captains, tell your men, that I,
Their King, will ride with them, beneath the eye
Of Heaven. I shall not stand aloof and wait,
But lead the charge, and challenge cruel fate.
For honour calls, and vengeance spurs my hand.
No peace for Picts, while they defile our land.

AETHELRED

(Bowing deeply, his voice full of resolve.)

Aye, King. Your word is law, your courage bright.
We shall not fail you, in this desperate fight.
God save our King, and England!

(AETHELRED turns and exits with brisk steps. The KING stands alone again, his hand now firmly on the hilt of his sword, his gaze fixed on some distant horizon, as if already seeing the gathering storm. The sound of a distant, faint horn call, perhaps the first beacon, echoes from afar.)

KING

So let it be. The die is cast, the game
Is set. For England's honour, and her name.
Let every prayer, let every sword and shield,
Proclaim our wrath upon the battle field.
No quarter given, no mercy shown the foe,
Whose impious hands have laid our sacred low.
This land, this soil, made holy by our dead,
Shall drink their blood, for every tear we've shed.
The ancient spirits of this blessed isle
Shall rise with us, and banish every wile
Of heathen magic. By the Rood, I swear,
That England's vengeance shall pollute the air
With Pictish wails, and fill the northern fen
With painted corpses of their godless men.
Come, darkness, come! Bring forth the bloody morn!
For England's might, by righteous anger born,
Shall rise like thunder, and with fearful sound,
Shake every heathen from our hallowed ground.
The long ships shall be ready, sharp and swift,
A watery wall, a sovereign, precious gift
Of watch and ward, to scour the ocean wide,
And turn the serpent head of Northmen's pride,
Should they presume to touch our guarded shore,
And add their rapine to the Pictish war.
No weakness shall they find, no faltering hand,
But steadfast courage, throughout all the land.
My Thanes shall gather, with their loyal bands,
From every river, every fertile sands,
To rally to the banner, to the call
Of England's King, to stand and rise or fall.
No baron, no, nor lord, however proud,
Shall shirk his duty, hide within the shroud
Of false excuse. For every man must lend
His strength, his coin, his life, until the end
Of this dark conflict. Let the mints proclaim
A sudden surge, and stamp your royal name
On every coin, though base the metal be,
If need compel, for England's liberty.
The common folk, though fear may touch their soul,
Will find their spirit, make themselves whole
In unity, beneath their sovereign's gaze.
For in this hour, through these troubled days,
A nation's heart must beat as one strong drum,
To face the darkness, and the evils come.
I feel a strength, a holy fire within,
To purge this blight, and England's freedom win.
My ancestors, who fought upon this soil,
Whose blood and sweat relieved the ancient toil,
They watch me now. Their spirits urge me on,
To right the wrong, before the coming dawn
Breaks upon chaos. So, let all prepare!
Let trumpets call defiance to the air!
The wolf of war now howls upon our door,
And we, as lions, answer with a roar.
The sacred earth demands its vengeance due.
And by my crown, my people, I and you,
Shall pay that debt, in blood and fiery strife,
To guard our heritage, and English life.

(The stage darkens slightly, as if the shadow of war has fallen. The horn call sounds again, closer this time, and then fades.)
