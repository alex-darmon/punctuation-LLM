LORD ALARIC
(Alone, in a dimly lit chamber)
To weep alone, in this sad, shadowed room,
Becomes the spirit cloaked in endless night.
My heart, a sepulchre for joys departed,
Lies buried deep beneath this icy grief.
Young Arthur, my fair son, my rising sun,
Extinguished swift, before his noon could bloom.
What comfort now can mend a father's wound,
When all his hopes lie cold beneath the stone?
The world outside doth clamour, blind to sorrow,
But here, within these walls, my soul doth mourn,
And finds no solace in the empty air,
No echo of that laughter, sweet and clear.

MAUDLIN
(Entering softly, an old woman of kind mien)
My noble lord, why dost thou shroud thyself
In shadows deeper than the crypt itself?
The morn doth break, though clouds obscure its face,
And duties beckon, though thy spirit droops.
Thy people wait, thy lands require thy hand;
Must sorrow ever drown out reason's plea?

ALARIC
Peace, good Maudlin. Speak not of living worlds
When mine lies shattered at a child's cold tomb.
What solace can the sun afford these eyes,
Which saw their brightest star extinguished swift?
My only son, the heir to all my line,
Now but a whisper on the mournful wind.
Thy words are kind, but cannot mend the breach
That death hath torn within my bleeding breast.

MAUDLIN
Alas, my lord, I know thy anguish well,
For I did nurse him from his tender birth.
But life doth call, and though the heart may break,
The body yet must act, the will must serve.
Perchance in action, thou shalt find some balm,
To ease the fever of this bitter grief.
Dost thou not think thy son would wish thee thus,
A captive to despair, whilst honour calls?

ALARIC
Honour hath fled with Arthur to the grave.
What honour in a crown, when love is lost?
A hollow king, upon a barren throne,
Whose only kingdom is this vast despair.

To reach the riverbank, and seek the truth
Of ancient tales, to save a noble youth.
(To the PAGE.)
You, Page, remain here. Guard this humble home.
And pray for us, till we again do roam
These friendly halls. If Arthur should return,
Or Alaric, tell them we chose to burn
With righteous anger, not with helpless dread.
Tell them we fought, though few, against the dead
And living shadows that consume this land.
Now go, Sir Kaelan, swift upon my command!

SIR KAELAN
(Bows deeply.)
It shall be done, my Lady. Faith and speed!
(SIR KAELAN and PAGE exit.)

MAUDLIN
(Alone again, she draws a deep breath. Her hand goes to her heart.)
My heart, be steel! My fears, be put to flight!
For now, the Queen must venture into night.
A queen with naught but courage for her crown,
And loyalty, lest all our hopes fall down.
(She moves to a small chest, pulling out a simple cloak and hood, and a small, sharp dagger. Her movements are determined.)
This blade, though small, shall serve me in dire straits.
This cloak, to hide me from their hateful gates.
And God above, watch over Arthur's path,
And spare my Alaric from Thorne's foul wrath.
My Gareth, hold! Your Queen shall soon draw near!
No matter what the cost, no matter what the fear!
(She extinguishes the small lamp, plunging the lodge into deeper shadow, and exits with a resolute step.)

***

SCENE II

A dank and stone-walled dungeon within the Serpent's Coil. GAINSAY, a grizzled guard, stands watch by a heavy door. GORAN enters, a cruel smirk on his face, followed by THORNE, who carries himself with an air of cold, calculating power.

GORAN
See, Gainsay, how our royal prize doth fare!
Sir Gareth, once the kingdom's finest heir
To martial fame, now languishes in chains!
His spirit, like a caged bird, it complains
With silent scorn. But soon, that scorn shall break!
When Thorne's great justice for its vengeance wake!

THORNE
(Observing Gareth's cell with an impassive stare.)
Patience, Goran. Let the fruit grow ripe.
A hasty hand can spoil the bitter gripe
Of vengeance. Let him feel the slow decay
Of hope, until he sees no light of day.
His spirit, though it burns with stubborn fire,
Shall flicker low, consumed by vain desire.
Where is the information we require?
Hath he confessed the rebel plot entire?
Where hide they now, those loyal to the King?
Who lends them aid? What tidings do they bring
Of Alaric, that phantom lord who flees?

GAINSAY
My Lord, he answers naught but scorn and ire.
He spits upon your name, with burning fire
In his proud eye. He calls you 'traitor vile,'
And swears the King shall rise, ere little while,
And cast you down to hell. He speaks of fate,
And destiny, and Heaven's angry gate
That waits for you. No word of Alaric,
No whisper of the rebels, no sly trick
To gain his freedom. Just defiant pride.

GORAN
(Laughing harshly.)
Such foolishness! Such blind and loyal love
For shadows gone, and stars that shine above
A forgotten throne! He clings to dying hope.
But we, my Lord, have stronger means to cope
With stubborn wills. The rack can loose a tongue,
The slow starvation, till the spirit stung
By hunger's gnaw, doth beg for mercy's grace.
Shall we employ such measures in this place?

THORNE
(Raises a hand, silencing Goran.)
Not yet. His breaking must be witnessed, seen
By those who yet might harbour thoughts unclean
Of loyalty to Arthur. Let his fall
Be public, grand, a lesson to them all.
His body, yes, can break. His spirit too,
But let the method be refined and new.
He waits for Alaric. He trusts young Arthur's quest.
Let that hope sicken, put it to the test.
Send word to every village, market town,
That Alaric is routed, cast down,
And Arthur, captured, kneels before our might.
Let falsehoods sow despair, eclipse their light.
Then, when his hope is shattered, and his mind
Believes his King is lost, and left behind,
Then will he break. Then will his secrets flow.
And every rebel's hidden path we'll know.

GORAN
A subtle stroke, my Lord! A master's hand!
To poison hope before it grasps the land!
I shall dispatch swift riders, north and south.
With lying tongues, to fill each listener's mouth
With tales of doom for Arthur and his Lord.
The people's spirit, soon shall be deplored.

THORNE
(A thin smile plays on his lips.)
Good Goran. And ensure that Gareth hears
These whispered lies. Let all his deepest fears
Be fed by rumour. Let him feel the sting
Of his King's downfall, and the bitter thing
Of shattered faith. Maintain the strictest guard.
No message in, no comfort from a bard,
No friendly face, no solace for his pain.
Let only darkness and despair remain.

GAINSAY
It shall be done, my Lord. The watch is keen.
No living soul shall pass this wall between
The prisoner and his doom. My oath is sworn.

THORNE
(Turns to leave.)
Good. Let us leave him to his slow despair.
The Serpent's Coil now holds him in its snare.
Come, Goran. Plans for greater conquests call.
To crush the rebels, and to conquer all!
(THORNE and GORAN exit, leaving GAINSAY alone.)

GAINSAY
(He glances at Gareth's cell, a flicker of something, perhaps pity, in his eyes, quickly suppressed.)
Poor fool. He hopes for naught. The world has changed.
Thorne's might is vast, his power rearranged
The very fabric of this troubled land.
Loyalty's a dream, held in a dying hand.
(He shakes his head, then resumes his stoic watch, pacing slowly before the cell.)

(Within the cell, GAINSAY is visible through the bars, but Gareth himself is slumped against a far wall, obscured by shadow. Only his defiant breathing can be faintly heard.)

***

SCENE III

A dense forest, dark even by day, where the path is overgrown and winding. ARTHUR, dressed in plain traveler's clothes, leads his horse, looking weary but determined. The sun struggles to penetrate the canopy.

ARTHUR
(To himself, his voice a little hoarse.)
The sun doth hide its face, as if to say
That light itself hath fled this weary day.
This path is dark, and silence holds its breath,
Save for the wind that whispers tales of death
Among the leaves. My mother's words, they ring
Within my heart, like some sad, ancient thing:
"If he but reaches Alaric, if he,
Can rouse my lord to swift and fierce decree..."
But where is Alaric? This journey's long.
And every shadow hides a villain's song,
Or so she said. My spirit does not quail,
But weariness doth make my courage fail
A moment's space. This hunger, and this chill,
They gnaw upon my youth, despite my will.
I ride for king, for kingdom, and for thee,
My mother, who entrusts so much to me.
Oh, for a sign, a whisper in the breeze,
To guide my steps through these bewildering trees!

(A rustling in the undergrowth. Arthur stops, his hand on his dagger, pulling his horse closer.)

ARTHUR
Who's there? Declare yourself! A friend or foe?
Speak plainly now, before my temper flow!

(From the bushes emerges a figure, old and bent, clad in simple, patched garments, leaning on a gnarled staff. It is an HERMIT, with a long white beard and piercing eyes.)

HERMIT
Peace, youngling, peace. No foe am I, nor friend
To those who seek to cause the world to rend
With strife and malice. I am but a wretch,
Who seeks the peace the forest's arms do stretch
Around these ancient trees. Why wander you
So deep within these woods, where paths are few
And danger lurks in every shaded glen?
You have the look of one who seeks for men
Of noble purpose, or a cause forlorn.

ARTHUR
(Lowering his hand slightly, but still wary.)
Good sir, you speak with wisdom in your tone.
Indeed, I seek a lord, and not alone
Do I bear purpose. I pursue a quest
To mend a kingdom, put it to the test
Against a tyrant's rule. My name is Arthur.

HERMIT
(His eyes widen slightly, a knowing look passing over his face.)
Arthur, you say? A name of ancient kings,
Of legends whispered, and of soaring wings!
You seek your lord? And is this lord, perchance,
The valiant Alaric, whose noble lance
Once guarded justice, when the world was young?
The very one, of whom the bards have sung?

ARTHUR
(Surprised by the Hermit's knowledge.)
You know of him? My quest, you understand?
Then tell me, pray, old sir, where in this land
Doth Alaric now hold his loyal stand?
My mother bids me find him, with swift hand,
To rouse him to our aid, ere all is lost!
For Thorne doth rule, and counts not any cost
To crush our hopes, and seize the royal throne!

HERMIT
(Sighs, a profound sadness in his voice.)
Alaric, good youth, is far beyond your reach.
His spirit wanders, past the furthest beach
Of mortal sorrow. He is caught, I fear,
In webs of woe, that draw him ever near
To madness' edge. A grief too deep to tell,
Hath claimed his heart, and bound it in a spell.
His lady fair, his beloved, Lady Elaine,
Hath perished, so the bitter whispers rain,
In distant lands, where he did wage fierce fight.
The sorrow smote him, like a blinding light,
And left him broken, lost in deepest gloom.
He wanders aimless, courting his own tomb.

ARTHUR
(Stunned, stepping back.)
My mother spoke of storms! But not of this!
Elaine, his love? A joy transformed to hiss
Of serpent sorrow? This cannot be true!
My mission then, what hope remains anew?
To rouse a man already crushed by fate?
His grief might prove a heavier, colder gate
Than Thorne's own iron bars! What shall I do?

HERMIT
(Placing a hand on Arthur's arm, his gaze firm.)
Do not despair, young Prince. For even grief
Can be a forge, to temper, and relief
Can come from purpose, if the cause be true.
Your father's spirit still lives on in you.
A king's own blood, a queen's unbending will.
Perhaps your very presence, standing still
Before his sorrow, might recall his mind
To duties sworn, to loyalties enshrined.
But where he is, I cannot truly say.
He vanished from the fields of bloody fray,
When news of Elaine's death did reach his ear.
Some say he sought a solace, far from fear,
In ancient ruins, or a hermit's cave.
A broken knight, whom no man now can save,
But one who bears a message of true need.
One who can plant a potent, valiant seed.

ARTHUR
(His brow furrowed in thought.)
Then I must seek him still. Though hope now wanes,
And sorrow's icy grip his soul enchains,
I bear a message dire, from heart to heart.
My mother's plight, a kingdom torn apart,
Sir Gareth's capture, Thorne's encroaching might.
These are the spurs that drive me through the night!
Where might I find such ruins, or such cave?
Speak plainly, sir, if this poor realm you'd save!

HERMIT
(Points with his staff to the deeper forest.)
Beyond this wood, where ancient mountains rise,
And misty peaks do pierce the morning skies,
There stands a place, a fortress long undone,
The Keep of Shadows, touched by setting sun.
It holds a grief, a silence, dark and deep.
Perchance in such a place, he makes his sleep.
But heed my words, young Prince, the path is hard.
And solitude can leave a soul unscarred,
Or else destroy it. Go with gentle tread.
And wake his spirit, though his heart be dead.
For sometimes, hope can bloom from barren ground,
When loyal love and urgent need are found.

ARTHUR
(Bows to the Hermit.)
Your wisdom lights my way, though it brings pain.
I thank you, sir, and shall not speak in vain
Of your kind aid. I go to Keep of Shadows.
May righteous fate guide me through unknown meadows
And darkest paths, to rouse a sleeping might.
(He mounts his horse, his face grim, but newly resolved.)

HERMIT
(Watching him go, a wistful look in his eye.)
Go well, young King, whose crown is yet unseen.
The fate of Albion rests on what hath been,
And what shall be, if courage holds its sway.
May Arthur's star yet light this darkest day.
(The HERMIT watches ARTHUR disappear into the forest, then slowly turns and shuffles back into the undergrowth.)

***

SCENE IV

The riverbank near the Serpent's Coil. Moonlight filters through sparse trees. MAUDLIN, cloaked and hooded, kneels by the river's edge, inspecting the bank. SIR KAELAN stands guard with a small group of ten loyal, but weary, men, their swords drawn.

MAUDLIN
(Softly, to Kaelan.)
The current's strong, Kaelan, and the banks are high.
The legend spoke of where the waters lie
Against a stone, a carved and ancient sign.
I search for it, a hope, a fragile line.
(She runs her hand along the damp stones, peering closely.)
Here, where the roots of ancient willow cling,
And water murmurs, like a whispered thing.
This stone... it feels distinct. Not carved by nature's hand.
But worn by time, by currents and by sand.
(She pushes against a heavy, moss-covered stone.)
It moves! A fraction, yes, it yields its place!
The old tales hold a truth, a hidden grace!

SIR KAELAN
(Drawing closer, his men forming a tight defensive circle.)
My Lady, are you sure? The passage deep
Could prove a trap, where dangers softly sleep!
And if it leads to Thorne's dark dungeon's heart,
We risk our lives, and play a deadly part
In Thorne's cruel game. What if it is a ruse?
A secret passage, which his guards now use
To snare the unwary?

MAUDLIN
(Her voice firm.)
No, Kaelan. This passage predates Thorne's reign.
Built by a tyrant, whose memory doth wane,
But whose dark cunning left this hidden way.
It is our only chance to see the day
When Gareth walks free. Trust to my old lore!
This stone yields inwards. Help me push the door!

(Kaelan signals to two of his men. Together, they strain against the large stone. With a grinding sound, it slowly swings inward, revealing a dark, water-filled tunnel, barely wide enough for one person.)

FIRST SOLDIER
(Whispering in awe.)
By all the saints! A passage from the river!
The old men's tales did truly now deliver!

SECOND SOLDIER
And dark within! A chill upon the air!
A thousand horrors might be lurking there!

MAUDLIN
(Ignoring their fear, she peers into the darkness.)
The water runs deep, but shallow near the sides.
The current's strength, the very channel guides
Our wary feet. It smells of earth and stone,
And secrets kept, and darkness to be known.
I go within. Kaelan, you guard the entrance well.
If danger calls, if any tongue doth tell
Of our intrusion, then defend this place.
Give me what time you can, with speed and grace.

SIR KAELAN
(Alarmed.)
Lady, you cannot go alone! It is too dire!
Let me, or one of my more youthful fire,
Attempt this passage. You are far too prized!
Your royal life must not be jeopardized!

MAUDLIN
(Turns to him, her eyes shining in the faint light.)
Who else knows of the legends, Kaelan, pray?
Who else remembers tales from childhood's day,
Of where this passage leads, what doors it finds?
My mind alone possesses what it binds.
I am the only guide to this dark way.
My purpose calls me forth, I must obey!
Give me a torch, and let me light the gloom!
For Gareth's sake, I face this watery tomb!

(One of the soldiers hands her a small, well-wrapped torch. Maudlin takes it, her hand steady.)

SIR KAELAN
(His voice heavy with concern.)
Then take great care, my Queen. And may the light
Of Heaven guide you through this fearsome night.
We shall stand fast, till you again emerge,
Or send a signal, when your mission urge
Demands our aid. A signal swift and keen!

MAUDLIN
(Nods, a faint smile on her lips.)
A bird's soft call, three times repeated clear.
That shall be my sign, to banish all your fear.
If I can reach him, if I can but gain
A moment's breath within that cursed domain,
Then I shall know what aid is to be sent.
Now close the stone when I within have went.
(She steps into the dark tunnel, the water rising to her knees, the torch casting flickering shadows. The water splashes gently as she moves deeper. Her determined silhouette soon disappears into the blackness.)

SIR KAELAN
(To his men, his voice low but firm.)
Push the stone back! Conceal the entrance well!
And guard this place, as if our lives did swell
With loyalty's last breath! No living soul
Shall pass unseen, to make our purpose whole!
Be silent, men! And watch with eagle's eye!
For on this night, our hopes ascend or die!

(The soldiers strain, and with another heavy grinding sound, the stone door slides back into place, sealing the tunnel. The riverbank returns to its serene, moonlit appearance, hiding the secret passage. Kaelan and his men spread out, blending into the shadows, their eyes fixed on the surrounding trees, swords ready.)
(Faint splashing sounds echo from behind the stone for a short while, then fade to silence.)

***

SCENE V

Inside the dark, water-filled tunnel. MAUDLIN moves cautiously, the torch held high, revealing slimy, ancient stone walls. The air is damp and heavy.

MAUDLIN
(To herself, her voice a whispered echo.)
The darkness presses, thick and cold and deep.
What ancient evils might within here sleep?
This passage, long forgotten by the light,
Now serves a queen, in her most desperate plight.
For Gareth, yes. My loyal, valiant friend.
This bitter journey soon must reach its end.
The water chills my bones, but not my heart.
A mother's love, a queen's determined part,
Doth lend me strength, when mortal flesh would quail.
Let fear itself before my purpose fail!
(She reaches a point where the tunnel begins to slope upwards, and the water recedes, becoming merely damp earth.)
Ah, higher ground! The water's path doth cease.
A breath of hope, a moment's sweet release.
This tunnel narrows here, and winds its way.
Like some great serpent, from the light of day.
(She squeezes through a tight space, her cloak snagging on rough stone. She emerges into a small, dry chamber, where the air is still and dusty.)
A chamber, dry and still. What hidden lore
Doth linger here, behind this secret door?
(She sees another heavy, ancient stone door, set into the far wall.)
Another door! This one, more rough and crude.
A dungeon entrance, surely, I conclude!
How to unbar it? No obvious latch I see.
No handle wrought for easy liberty.
(She inspects the door, her torchlight dancing across its surface.)
The stones are mortared firm, no chink or crack.
Unless... unless... the mechanism sits aback
From common sight. A lever, or a weight?
A secret spring, concealed by cruelest fate?
(She runs her hands over the cold stone, searching. Her fingers brush against a loose block, hidden in a recess

recess.

MAUDLIN
(To herself, her breath catching)
A loose block here! A tremor in the stone!
A hollow sound, as if a sigh were shown
Within the cold, hard heart of ancient rock.
What hidden springs, what intricate padlock
Does this conceal? Let fortune now but smile,
And grant my purpose aid, though for a while
My fingers fumble, seeking out the way.
(She presses harder on the loose block. With a low, grinding rumble, a section of the wall beside the heavy door retracts inward, revealing a rusted iron lever.)
Ah, there it is! A lever, grim and old,
Its surface rough with rust, like tales untold
Of prisoners' tears, and jailors' heavy hand.
Now, by my will, and for this weary land,
This portal shall obey!
(She grasps the lever firmly, gathering all her strength. It resists at first, then yields with a protesting groan, pulling downwards. The sound echoes through the chamber, long and mournful. With a tremendous, scraping, grating roar, the great stone door slowly begins to slide inwards and downwards, revealing a chasm of absolute darkness beyond. A chill, musty air, laden with the scent of damp earth and decay, wafts out.)
The way is open! Yet what horror lies
Beyond this threshold, shrouded from our eyes?
No matter! Fear must be a banished guest,
My spirit steeled, my courage put to test.
For Gareth's sake, I brave this noxious gloom,
To snatch him from this cold and living tomb.
(She steps through the newly opened doorway, holding her torch aloft. The light weakly illuminates a narrow, winding passage, cut from rough stone, descending into the earth. The floor is slick with moisture, and the air grows heavier, pressing down upon her. The sounds of dripping water echo faintly from deeper within.)
The very air doth weep within this place,
A testament to sorrow and disgrace.
No joy, no light, no hope has touched these stones,
Only the echoes of forgotten groans.
This must be it, the dungeon's cruel domain,
Where light is banished, and shadows ever reign.
I must proceed with caution, step by step,
Lest some unseen pit or trap should keep
My mission from its end. My loyal heart,
Be strong, perform thy most courageous part!
(She descends the passage, her torchlight revealing more of the grim surroundings. The passage opens into a larger, cavernous space, a labyrinth of cells carved into the living rock. Iron bars, thick with rust, line the walls, forming cages within the natural stone. A pervasive, heavy silence hangs in the air, broken only by her footsteps and the drip of water. The stench of stale air and something metallic, like old blood, is strong here.)
A dreadful place, where agony has dwelt,
And hopeless souls their last lament have felt.
Each cell a tomb, each shadowed recess cries
With voiceless pain that never truly dies.
Is Gareth here? My heart, a frightened bird,
Doth beat against its cage, by hope deterred
And fear assailed. Gareth! My friend! Thy Queen
Doth seek thee in this most abhorrent scene!
(Her voice, though whispered, sounds loud in the oppressive silence. She moves from cell to cell, peering into the blackness within each. They all appear empty, save for scattered bones and rusty shackles clinging to the walls. Despair begins to gnaw at her resolve.)
No sign of him! Have they removed him hence?
To some more hidden, hellish recompense?
Or have I strayed, and wandered from the track,
Into a part from which there's no turning back?
No, I must search. He cannot be far off.
My instincts tell me, this is not a scoff
Of fate, but truth. He breathes within these walls,
Though deeply hid, beyond these stony halls.
(She presses on, her torch throwing dancing shadows that seem to mock her search. The passage turns sharply, leading to an even deeper level. Here, the air is colder, and a faint, rhythmic sound, like a slow, painful breath, reaches her ears. It is almost imperceptible, lost in the general dampness, but Maudlin catches it.)
Hark! What faint sound? A whisper from the gloom!
Not wind, nor water, but a living room
It occupies. A breath, so thin and weak,
As if a tortured spirit scarce could speak.
(She quickens her pace, her heart pounding. The passage opens into a circular chamber, larger than the others, with a single, heavily barred cell in its center. The rhythmic breathing is stronger here. Her torch illuminates the figure within: Gareth, chained to the damp stone wall, his head bowed. He is gaunt, his face streaked with grime, his clothes torn, but undeniably him.)

MAUDLIN
Gareth! By heaven's grace, I find thee here!
My heart doth break to see thy suffering clear!
(Gareth slowly raises his head, his eyes, though shadowed and weary, widen in disbelief. A flicker of hope, mingled with profound shock, crosses his face.)

GARETH
My Queen! Maudlin! Is this some fevered dream,
Born of my pain, a fleeting, cruel gleam
Of what once was? Or doth my senses lie,
And visions mock me, ere I come to die?
How cam'st thou here, to this most vile abyss?
Thy presence is a peril, not a bliss!

MAUDLIN
No dream, dear friend, but living, breathing truth!
I come to free thee, in the flower of my youth,
And with a will as firm as castle stone,
To right the wrongs that on this land are sown.
King Edward's mind, by Isolde's venom swayed,
Hath cast thee here, by foulest plots betrayed.
But fear not, Gareth, for thou shalt not pine
Within these chains, nor waste away and whine.
I bear a hope, a rescue strong and deep,
From this dark pit where cruelest secrets sleep.

GARETH
My Queen, thou speak'st with courage, bold and true,
Yet danger stalks each breath that thou dost do.
This place is watched, by Edward's loyal hounds,
Whose watchful ears detect the softest sounds.
How didst thou pass the guards, the locks, the gate?
Thy venture here doth tempt a dreadful fate!
And I, alas, a broken, weary man,
Cannot assist thee in this desperate plan.
My limbs are weak, my spirit almost spent,
By hunger, cold, and cruelest torment rent.

MAUDLIN
Speak not of weakness! For thy spirit's fire
Still burns within, beyond the torturer's pyre!
I passed unseen, by secret, winding way,
A path forgot, since many a bygone day.
Sir Kaelan guards the entrance, strong and true,
So none shall follow, nor discover you
Are sought. Now tell me, how may these chains yield?
This iron door, what keys doth it conceal?
We have but precious moments, ere they come,
To check their captive, hear his bitter hum
Of misery.

GARETH
Alas, my Queen, the chains
Are forged of iron, steeped in ancient stains
Of magic, wrought by Isolde's wicked hand.
No common key unlocks this cursed band.
And for the door, it hath no simple latch,
But draws its power from a hidden catch
Above, accessible by guards alone.
Unless a tremor shakes this prison stone,
Or some great magic, stronger than her spell,
Doth break its hold, I am condemned to dwell
Until my death.

MAUDLIN
(Inspecting the chains and the cell door closely, her torch revealing their formidable strength. The iron is thick, embedded deep in the stone.)
Magic, sayest thou? Accursed sorcery!
That binds thee fast, and holds thy spirit free
In cruelest thrall. But even magic yields
To greater power, or to sharpened steel's
Persistent bite. My blade is sharp and keen,
But not for this. This iron is a screen
Against all force.
(She paces before the cell, her brow furrowed in thought. Her eyes fall upon a small, intricate symbol carved into the stone lintel above the cell door, almost invisible in the gloom.)
What symbol's this? A serpent, twined and grim,
Its venomous fangs, projecting from the rim
Of ancient runes. A mark of Isolde's craft?
Her dark enchantments, here so subtly graft
Upon the stone, to hold her captives fast.
This must be where the chains' true power's cast.

GARETH
Aye, Queen, that sigil, carved with hateful care,
Holds all the strength of Isolde's foul despair.
'Tis but a focus for her wicked art,
To bind me here, and rend my soul apart.
No axe can sever, nor no fire melt,
The bonds that by her wicked spells are dealt.
I've tried in vain, with all my fading might,
To break these bonds and steal a glimpse of light.

MAUDLIN
Then not by force, but wit, must we proceed.
If magic holds, then magic can impede
Itself, or be unmade by counter-spell.
Alas, no such dark knowledge do I tell,
Nor bear with me. But oft, a simple trick,
A common fault in spells, however thick
With power, may be found. A hidden flaw.
(She examines the symbol more closely, running her fingers over the cold stone. She mutters to herself, recalling old wives' tales and fragments of forgotten lore. She notices a slight imperfection in the carving, a tiny chip at the serpent's eye.)
A chip! A flaw! So small, yet it might be
The very chink to set thy spirit free!
For every power, wrought by darkest hand,
Must have its weakness, somewhere in the land.
What if this symbol, meant to hold thee fast,
Can be reversed, or broken at the last,
By simple truth, or light, or honest touch?
(She hesitates, then places her hand firmly over the carved symbol, focusing her will. There is no immediate effect. She closes her eyes, concentrating. A faint warmth begins to emanate from the stone beneath her palm.)

GARETH
My Queen, take heed! Such rashness may rebound!
What if her magic, vengeful and profound,
Should strike thee down, for daring to defy
Her cursed will, beneath this hellish sky?

MAUDLIN
(Her voice strained, but resolute)
Fear not for me! My purpose is my shield!
Let truth and courage be this night revealed!
For love of thee, and for our troubled land,
I challenge Isolde's dark, unholy hand!
(She presses harder. The warmth grows, and the stone symbol begins to glow with a faint, pulsing light, like a dying ember. The light flickers, then intensifies, casting strange, shifting shadows across the chamber. A low hum emanates from the symbol, growing louder, vibrating through the rock. Gareth's chains begin to shimmer with the same faint light.)

GARETH
By heaven's grace! The sigil doth respond!
The chains grow warm! This is beyond the bond
Of mortal craft! What power dost thou wield,
That Isolde's foulest magic now doth yield?

MAUDLIN
(Opening her eyes, which now burn with an inner light)
No magic I, but purpose, pure and true!
A mother's love, a queen's deep loyalty too!
These are the forces that can break all spell,
And banish darkness to the deepest hell!
(With a sudden, sharp crack, like lightning rending stone, the glowing symbol shatters into a thousand shards of light, which then vanish into the air. Simultaneously, with a series of metallic clangs, Gareth's chains fall from the wall and from his wrists and ankles, clattering to the floor. The heavy iron door of the cell shudders, and with a groan of protest, the hidden latch mechanism above springs open with a loud thud, leaving the door ajar.)

GARETH
(Staring at his freed limbs, then at Maudlin, overwhelmed)
My Queen! My saviour! Thou hast wrought a feat
That gods might envy, bitter foes defeat!
I am unbound! My spirit lifts anew!
My thanks, my life, my soul, I pledge to you!
But quick! Ere Isolde senses this great loss,
And sends her hounds to nail us to a cross!

MAUDLIN
Indeed! No time for praises now, dear friend!
Our perilous journey doth not yet have end!
(She rushes to the cell door, pushing it open. It swings inward with a rusty shriek.)
Come, Gareth, quick! We must away from here!
Each moment's delay invites further fear!
Canst thou now walk? Thy strength, can it endure?
The path is hard, the passage insecure.

GARETH
My limbs are stiff, like branches in the frost,
But freedom's fire will heal what I have lost!
I'll follow thee, though every muscle ache,
And every step a tremor for thy sake!
(He stumbles out of the cell, leaning heavily against the doorframe, his legs trembling beneath him. He takes a deep breath, filling his lungs with the damp, stale air, which now tastes of freedom.)

MAUDLIN
Lean on me then, until thy strength returns.
The way we came, a secret passage burns
With hope. But first, to mend thy broken state,
A little sustenance, ere it's too late.
(She pulls a small flask of water and a piece of dried meat from a pouch within her cloak.)
Here, drink and eat. It is not much, I trow,
But it will lend thee strength, and swiftly so.

GARETH
(Takes the flask and meat, his hands shaking slightly. He drinks deeply, then tears into the meat with a hunger that speaks of long deprivation.)
A monarch's feast! Each drop, each morsel sweet!
Thou art my angel, sent with wings and feet!
(As Gareth eats, a faint clatter of heavy boots and the murmur of voices can be heard from a distant passage. The sounds are irregular, drawing closer then fading, suggesting a patrol.)

MAUDLIN
Hark! What was that? A sound from yonder way!
The guards! They come! This cannot be delay!
They must have felt the weakening of the spell,
Or heard the clang of chains that broke so well!
Quick, Gareth, hide! Within thy cell once more!
(She tries to push him back into the cell, but he resists.)

GARETH
Nay, Queen! I'll not return to that dark hole!
If I must fight, then I'll engage my soul
In battle's fury, though my body fails!
I'll draw their notice, whilst thy purpose sails
Away from here. Thou must escape alone!
Leave me to face them, on this bloodied stone!

MAUDLIN
What foolish talk is this? We rise or fall
Together, Gareth! By this dungeon wall,
I swear it! Now, a plan! We cannot fight
Against their numbers, in this dismal light.
The passage back, where I did enter in,
Is known to none. Our safety may begin
By hiding near it, till their watch has past.
Then swift escape! Our freedom holding fast!
(She looks around desperately. Her eyes light upon a shallow recess in the wall, half-hidden by a natural rock outcropping, not far from the secret passage's entrance.)
There! By yon rock! A shadow deep and wide!
We may conceal ourselves, and deftly hide
Until they pass. Be silent as the grave!
Our very lives, this stealth alone can save!
(Maudlin quickly helps Gareth, who is still weak but moving with renewed purpose, to the hidden recess. They press themselves into the shadows, barely breathing, as the sounds of the approaching guards grow louder. The heavy footsteps echo closer, accompanied by gruff voices.)

FIRST GUARD
Didst thou not hear it, then? A sound of iron,
As if a beast from hell did now environ
This cursed dungeon.

SECOND GUARD
Naught but rats, I say!
Or the wind's moan, that brings a colder day.
The King hath sworn, by Isolde's potent word,
That Gareth's cell can never be unstirred
By mortal hand. Her magic holds him fast.
His freedom's hour, a moment long since past.

FIRST GUARD
Still, 'twas a clang, like shackles giving way.
My blood ran cold, and sent my soul astray.
I heard it plain, a thunder in the stone.
Perhaps the earth itself begins to groan
Beneath our feet, at such a wicked deed.
This dungeon's air doth poison, and doth feed
Upon our souls.

SECOND GUARD
Enough of superstitious dread!
Let's check the prisoner. His wretched head
Will still be bowed, his spirit still contained.
Unless a demon hath his freedom gained!
(The two guards enter the circular chamber. Their torches cast flickering light, illuminating the empty cell where Gareth had been. They gasp in unison, their voices filled with shock and fear.)

FIRST GUARD
By all the saints! The cell is empty, friend!
The chains are broken! Is this earth's sad end?
The prisoner is gone! A miracle, or worse,
A demon's work, a hellish, evil curse!

SECOND GUARD
Impossible! The sigil! It is rent!
Its power gone, its wicked purpose spent!
This cannot be! Isolde's wrath will fall
Like fire from heaven, consuming one and all!
Search everywhere! He cannot have gone far!
He's weak, he's spent, beneath this evil star!
(The guards begin to search the chamber frantically, their eyes darting into every shadow, their swords drawn. Maudlin and Gareth remain perfectly still in their hiding place, their hearts pounding.)

MAUDLIN
(Whispering to Gareth, barely audible)
Be silent, friend! Our lives depend on this!
No sound, no breath, no whisper, no soft kiss
Of air must stir. Let them search on in vain!
Our path to freedom, we must now regain!

GARETH
(Whispering back, his voice hoarse)
Their fear doth aid us, and their blind despair.
They look for demons, in the empty air.
Let them believe some spirit wrought this deed,
Whilst we escape, like roots from planted seed.
(The guards move closer to their hiding spot, their frantic search bringing them within arm's reach. Maudlin can feel the heat of their torches, the rough weave of their tunics as they pass.)

FIRST GUARD
He must be here! He cannot fly unseen!
Unless some witch, some hellish, wicked Queen,
Did aid his flight!

SECOND GUARD
Silence! Such talk is treason!
The Queen is far, beyond all sense and reason!
It is some sorcery, a fiend from hell,
That broke his bonds, and cast this wicked spell!
(The guards circle the recess, their torchlight momentarily falling upon Maudlin's face, but she keeps her eyes downcast, her features obscured by shadows. Gareth, behind her, holds his breath. The guards pass by, moving deeper into the winding passages.)

FIRST GUARD
Let's search the upper cells! He cannot crawl
Too far, his strength must falter and must fall!

SECOND GUARD
Aye, come! We'll scour these tunnels, dark and deep!
No corner left, where he might hide and sleep!
(Their footsteps fade as they move away, their voices growing fainter, heading back the way Maudlin had come. Maudlin waits, counting her breaths, until the sounds are completely gone, swallowed by the dungeon's silence.)

MAUDLIN
(Exhaling slowly, relief washing over her)
They're gone, for now. The fool's pursuit begins!
They seek a phantom, whilst our freedom wins!
Come, Gareth, quick! We must not tarry here!
Each fleeting moment fills my heart with fear,
Lest they return, with others, strong and bold.
Our precious secret must not be untold!
(She helps Gareth from the recess. He is still weak, but the food and water, combined with the adrenaline of escape, have given him a temporary surge of strength.)

GARETH
My Queen, I shame myself to lean on thee,
But by my troth, I shall repay this plea
For aid, with loyal service, till I die!
Now lead the way, beneath this shadowed sky!
(Maudlin leads Gareth back towards the secret door she had opened. The passage, though still dark and damp, now seems to hold the promise of escape.)

MAUDLIN
The way is steep, and then the waters rise,
But courage, Gareth! Freedom is our prize!
(They reach the chamber where the secret door stands ajar. Maudlin looks back at the dungeon, a grim determination set upon her face.)
This evil place, it shall not hold us long.
Our rightful King, by power true and strong,
Shall claim his throne, and banish Isolde's blight.
But first, we must escape this endless night!
(Maudlin and Gareth step through the secret door, into the winding passage beyond. The door, freed from its magic, can now be pushed closed from within the chamber. Maudlin reaches for the lever that released the door, her plan to seal it shut behind them.)

GARETH
The lever, Queen! Should we not seal this way?
Lest they discover where we fled today?

MAUDLIN
Indeed, dear friend! Let no man trace our flight!
(She pushes the lever back into its original position. With another heavy groan and grind, the great stone door begins to slide back into place, slowly sealing off the dungeon once more. The sound reverberates through the passages, announcing their departure.)
Now let them search, until their hope is spent!
Their captive's gone, as if by heaven sent!
(The door clangs shut with a final, echoing thud, plunging the dungeon into a deeper silence. Maudlin turns her attention to the path ahead, the narrow, rising passage that will lead them to the river.)

MAUDLIN
Now up this path, where water once did creep.
And then the river, silent, cold, and deep.
The entrance true, where Kaelan watches keen,
Shall be our gate, from this most dreadful scene.
(They ascend the sloping tunnel. Gareth, though still stumbling, draws strength from Maudlin's unwavering resolve. The air gradually becomes less heavy, less fetid, and the dampness less oppressive. They hear the faint sound of rushing water ahead.)

GARETH
The river's voice! A sweet and welcome sound!
To think that freedom can be truly found
Beneath the earth, in paths so long concealed,
A secret hope, by courage now revealed.

MAUDLIN
Aye, Gareth! Hope, a fragile, tender thing,
Can grow in darkness, and its triumph sing.
But we are not yet free. The open air
Holds dangers too, beyond this prison's snare.
King Edward's court, a nest of vipers vile,
Will hunt us down, and seek to reconcile
Their failure here, with vengeance swift and sure.
Our journey's end is yet far to procure.
(They reach the point where the tunnel levels out, and the water begins to rise. Maudlin holds her torch high, revealing the murky depths.)

MAUDLIN
The water calls! Prepare thy weary feet!
The current's chill, a bitter, icy treat.
But fear it not, for freedom waits beyond!
(She steps into the water, which rises to her knees, then her waist. Gareth follows, gritting his teeth against the cold and the discomfort.)

GARETH
This water, cold, doth cleanse the dungeon's stain.
A fresh beginning, washing out the pain.
My strength returns, with every chilling stride,
As if the river aids us, as our guide.

MAUDLIN
(Her voice echoing slightly in the water-filled tunnel)
It lends us speed, and washes out our trace.
No guard will follow through this watery space.
We move unseen, unheard, beneath the moon,
To greet our allies, and restore our noon
Of justice to the land.
(They move deeper into the water-filled tunnel. The splashing sounds echo around them. The journey through the water is slow and arduous for Gareth, whose strength is still severely depleted, but Maudlin supports him, urging him onward with soft words of encouragement. The tunnel seems to stretch endlessly, the torchlight barely penetrating the gloom. Time seems to lose all meaning in the dark, cold confines.)

GARETH
(Struggling, his voice weakening)
The cold, my Queen, doth pierce me to the bone.
My former strength, alas, is truly gone.
I fear I falter, in this watery maze.
My body wearies, from these endless days
Of hunger, chains.

MAUDLIN
(Putting an arm around him, pulling him forward)
Nay, Gareth, not now! Not here!
Hold fast to hope, and banish every fear!
A little more! I feel the current's pull
Grow stronger now! The exit, swift and full
Of promise, waits! Just a few more strides!
Our faithful Kaelan, by the river hides!
(She strains, pulling him along, her own strength tested by his weight and the water's resistance. The torch flickers, threatening to extinguish, but she shields it carefully. They can now hear the more pronounced rush of the river outside, a powerful, welcoming roar that promises escape.)

GARETH
(With a surge of effort, drawing upon his last reserves)
The river's song! I hear it loud and clear!
The open sky, it banishes all fear!
(They press on, the current guiding them towards the concealed exit. The light from Maudlin's torch begins to interact with a faint luminescence from ahead, indicating the approaching exit.)

MAUDLIN
A light! A flicker! See, the moon's pale grace
Doth kiss the water, in this secret place!
We are almost there, my true and loyal friend!
Our bitter journey's trial is at its end!
(They reach the very edge of the tunnel entrance, where the current is strongest. The moonlit night outside is visible through the narrow opening, a shimmering portal to freedom. Kaelan and his men are not yet visible, but the familiar outline of the riverbank, and the dark, watchful trees, assures Maudlin of their position.)

MAUDLIN
(To Kaelan, her voice ringing out, though still somewhat muffled by the water)
Sir Kaelan! We are here! The prisoner's free!
Now let us taste the air of liberty!
(She pushes Gareth forward through the opening, into the cool night air. Gareth gasps, drawing in deep breaths of the clean, fresh air, his eyes adjusting to the moonlit landscape. He stumbles onto the riverbank, collapsing for a moment, before pushing himself up to his knees, utterly exhausted but undeniably free. Maudlin follows, emerging from the water, her cloak dripping, her face streaked with water and grime, but triumphant.)

SIR KAELAN
(Rushing forward from the shadows, his face a mixture of relief and awe)
My Queen! And Gareth! By all holy names!
You have achieved it! Doused the wicked flames
Of treachery! I feared, with every hour,
That fortune would deny your sacred power!
(His men, equally relieved, emerge from their hiding spots, their swords still drawn, but their faces showing genuine joy at the sight of their Queen and the rescued Gareth.)

FIRST SOLDIER
Sir Gareth! Welcome from that dismal tomb!
The kingdom wept, and waited for thy bloom
To rise again!

SECOND SOLDIER
The King shall learn this night,
That truth and loyalty conquer darkest might!

GARETH
(Looking at Kaelan, then at the soldiers, a faint smile gracing his weary face)
My friends, my loyal hearts! Your watch, so true,
Hath brought me back to light, and life anew!
And to my Queen, whose courage knows no bounds,
Whose spirit breaks all spells, all evil sounds,
I owe my life. My service, evermore,
Is hers to command, till I reach death's dark shore.

MAUDLIN
(Wiping water from her eyes, her gaze sweeping over her loyal retainers)
No thanks, dear Gareth. 'Twas a duty owed,
To mend the path that Edward's madness showed.
But we must move! The castle will have learned
Of our escape, and vengeful fires burned
Within Isolde's heart. We cannot stay.
The night provides its cover, by its way.
Kaelan, my plans? Are all our forces set?
Our messengers despatched, without regret?

SIR KAELAN
Aye, Queen! The word is sent, to every lord,
Who still retains his faith, and draws his sword
For rightful cause. The gathering of strength
Shall soon commence, and sweep across the length
And breadth of England, to reclaim the crown!
Our loyal forces gather, in each town
