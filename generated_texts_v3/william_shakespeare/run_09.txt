KING OSWIN
Vernon, my heart doth heavier grow each morn,
Than any crown this weary head hath worn.
Our Alaric, Eldoria’s rising sun,
Extinguished now; his noble course is run.
The wolves of Westmarch sniff our bleeding plains,
And southern lords murmur rebellious strains.
What counsel now, when hope itself doth fade,
And all my kingdom seems a dying glade?

LORD VERNON
My liege, despair is poison to the throne.
Whilst breath remains, a king is not alone.
Though Alaric sleeps, his spirit guards thy right,
And bids us seek new dawn from darkest night.
Thy grace, Duke Valerius' fair daughter, too,
Whose lands abut the marches, strong and true,
Holds half the key to this encroaching woe.
Lady Eloise, whose father fell so low
In battle, doth command a potent sway
Amongst her vassals, who would hear her say.

KING OSWIN
Aye, Eloise. A flower born of might.
Her father, valiant, vanished in the fight.
She comes of age; her father’s will proclaims
Her hand alone can bind those distant names
To Eldoria. But Vernon, tell me true,
What foreign prince would claim so choice a prize,
Yet bear our banners 'neath unclouded skies?

LORD VERNON
My liege, the Prince of Westmarch, Valerius,
Hath sent his envoys, pressing fast and furious,
To seek alliance; friendship's binding vow.
His might would stem the tide, if Eldoria now
Would yield a bride, a bond for lasting peace.

KING OSWIN
Valerius! His father's greedy son?
Whose realm hath ever eyed what we have won?
His friendship is a serpent, coiled to strike.
Yet, desperate straits demand a bitter pill.
Hark, summon Eloise. My royal will
Must bend her spirit to this harsh decree.
Go, Vernon, bring the lady unto me.

KING OSWIN
Oh, heavy burden of a monarch's plea!
To beggar love for state's security.
My soul doth sicken at the task I face,
To crush a maiden's heart for Eldor's grace.
Yet, must I choose betwixt a private tear
And public ruin, quelled by public fear?

LADY ELOISE
My sovereign lord, thou sent'st thy messenger,
To bid me hither. What doth trouble thee,
That thus the royal summons finds me out
In my lone chamber, cloaked in father’s grief?

KING OSWIN
Sweet Eloise, rise up. Thy sorrow's keen,
But greater sorrows vex this troubled scene.
Eldoria weeps; her Prince, her dearest son,
Hath fallen 'neath the spear, his life undone.
And now the foes, like vultures, circle nigh,
To feast upon our weakness, ere we die.
Thy father's lands, the bulwark of our west,
Must stand impregnable, put to the test.
And thou, his daughter, through thy hand alone,
Canst guard our borders and protect thy throne.

LADY ELOISE
My hand, my liege? What office can it hold,
When sorrow makes my very spirit cold?
I am a woman, frail and ill-equipped
To wield a blade, or guard a city stripped
Of its defenders. My poor strength doth wane.

KING OSWIN
Nay, not with steel, but with a golden chain,
A silken bond, doth Eldoria seek her peace.
The Prince of Westmarch, Valerius, would cease
His border skirmishes, his hungry gaze,
If joined in wedlock through thy maiden days.
He seeks thy hand, that Eldor may be strong.

LADY ELOISE
My hand? To Valerius? That cruel man?
Whose father's legions vexed my kinsmen's clan?
My liege, it cannot be! My faith is pledged,
My heart is given, my troth already edged
With sacred promise! To Sir Kai, my lord,
My soul hath sworn, my spirit doth accord.
We were but waiting for the moon to wane,
And then to wed, and wash away our pain.

KING OSWIN
Love is a flower, Eloise, that fades.
But duty is a rock, that naught invades.
Thy troth, a whisper 'gainst a cannon's roar!
Thy kingdom's plight doth ask for something more
Than maiden vows, or private, tender dreams.
Dost think thy Kai, with all his knightly beams,
Could hold the passes 'gainst Valerius' host?
Or save Eldoria, were her borders lost?
This alliance, Eloise, is Eldor's breath!
To shun it is to court a certain death.

LADY ELOISE
To wed a tyrant! To forsake my love!
My heart will break, my spirit cease to move!
What king would force a soul to such despair,
And bind a life with chains she cannot bear?
Have mercy, sire! Unbind this cruel decree!
My very being doth refuse to flee
From Kai, my true love. Let me rather die,
Than live a queen beneath a perjured sky!

LORD VERNON
Lady, the crown doth weigh with pressing care.
A monarch's choice is not for joy to share,
But for his people's safety. Look around!
The land doth groan with sorrow, 'neath the sound
Of enemies gathering. Is thy love's delight
So dear, that it would plunge us into night?
Consider well. The king speaks of thy duty.

LADY ELOISE
My duty? Is it duty to deny
The truth of my own heart, and stand thereby
A living corpse, bound to a hateful bed?
Is that the peace for which my father bled?
My father, who did teach me truth and grace,
To honor faith, and meet with honest face
My given word! How can I break it now,
And wear a crown upon a broken brow?

KING OSWIN
The honest face, sweet maid, is one that knows
The greater good, from whence true honor flows.
Thy father, were he here, would bid thee yield.
He saw the storm clouds rising from the field.
Thy Kai, a loyal knight, would understand
The sacrifice that saves this dying land.
Go, seek thy chamber. Let these words take root.
Consider all. The kingdom yields no fruit
From private sorrow, though I mourn with thee.
But Eldor's future rests, now, on thy knee.
I give thee but a day, no more, to choose.
To save thy people, or thy kingdom lose.

KING OSWIN
The crown is heavy, Vernon, is it not?
A weight of woes, a king's unenvied lot.
To break a heart, to bid a spirit bleed,
For seeds of safety, sown in bitter need.

LORD VERNON
Such is the price of kingship, sire, full oft.
The lion must be cruel, though lambs be soft.
She will assent. Her duty calls her forth.
And Eldoria will thank her for her worth.

KING OSWIN
Perchance she will. But what of my own soul?
To make a maiden's misery my goal?
Oh, Alaric, my son, wert thou but here,
This bitter draught would not have caused such fear.
But fate, unkind, hath drawn its cruelest line.
And I, a king, must drink this poison wine.

My life, my love, a holy sacrifice

NURSE. (Entering cautiously, having heard Lyra's laments)
My gentle mistress, what disquieting sound
Doth rend the air within these chamber's bound?
Your voice, a nightingale's in sorrow's strain,
Betrays a heart o'erwhelmed by bitter pain.
What fresh affliction doth perturb your grace?
A tear doth glimmer on your lovely face.
Speak, Lyra, child, your old Nurse stands by thee.
What grief, what burden, bows thy spirit free?

LYRA. Oh, Nurse, dear Nurse, a thousand thousand woes
Have built a dam against my spirit's flows!
My heart is rent, my very soul undone,
By fate's decree, beneath a cruel sun.
They bid me wed Valerius, that cold fiend,
Whose touch, whose gaze, my very spirit hath weened
To loathe and shun, as darkness shuns the light.
To be his bride, is to embrace eternal night!
The King, my father, driven by their plea,
Hath signed my doom, and sealed my misery.
He hath forgot the promises he made,
The sacred trust within his heart arrayed.
He sells me, Nurse, like cattle in the stall,
To save his kingdom from a feared downfall.
But what is Eldor, if its royal line
Must sacrifice its truth at sorrow's shrine?
What peace can bloom from such a bitter root?
What joy can follow, what delightful fruit?

NURSE. Alas, my lamb, that such a cruel decree
Should fall upon a maid so fair as thee!
Valerius, say you? The Baron of the North?
Whose fame for harshness often issues forth
From every tongue, with shudders and with dread?
They say his castle is a place of dead
And silent vows, where laughter never rings,
And sorrow's shadow over all things flings.
Oh, woe the day, that such a heavy hand
Should fall upon the fairest in the land!
But princess, child, these royal unions oft
Are forged by statecraft, though the heart be soft.
The King, your father, loves you, this I know,
But kingdom's welfare bids his duty flow
In channels strange, and sometimes harsh to bear.
He fears for Eldor, and its people's care.
The storm clouds gather, so the whispers say,
From southern borders, driving peace away.
A sturdy alliance, built on strong accord,
Might save the realm from rapine of the sword.
Yet still, my heart doth ache to see you thus,
A victim to such dire and direful truss.

LYRA. They speak of statecraft, Nurse, and kingdom's need,
But crush my spirit with each heartless deed!
They speak of peace, but wage a war on me,
And strip my soul of all its liberty.
Valerius! His name is poison to my ear!
His face, a mask of cold and creeping fear!
To share his bed, to bear his hateful seed,
To plant within my womb a serpent's creed!
No, Nurse, I cannot! By the heavens above,
My soul would wither, perish without love!
My heart belongs to Kai, my noble Kai,
Whose gentle spirit doth reflect the sky
In purest azure, whose eyes hold such grace,

To him I'm plighted, by a sacred vow,
That binds my essence, Nurse, even until now.
We spoke of futures, painted bright and bold,
Before this tale of misery unfold.
How can I break a promise, pure and deep,
And let my loyal, loving spirit weep
In chains of gold, a queen without a soul,
Her life a mockery beyond control?
My father's friend! He counsels him to crime!
To blight my youth, and steal my precious time!
He, who once smiled upon my childish play,
Now plots to cast my dearest hopes away!
Is there no justice in this bitter world?
No mercy left, no banner to unfurled
For innocence, for truth, for faithful love?
Must every joy be shattered from above
By politic schemes, and cold ambitious greed?
Must Eldor flourish from my heart's bleed?

NURSE. Hush, child, hush, your words are sharp as steel.
Such desperation, Lyra, doth reveal
A spirit bruised, but not yet broken quite.
Let not despair obscure your inner light.
I know of Kai, your love is pure and true,
A fairer knight the sun hath never knew.
But fate, my princess, doth a cruel game play,
And oft doth steal our dearest joys away.

She married for the kingdom, not for flame
Of ardent passion, though she learned to prize
The King, your father, in her gentle eyes.
Her duty called, her heart made brave the stand,
And peace descended on this troubled land.
Though love came after, slowly, softly born,
From shared endeavor, and from duty's morn.

LYRA. My mother's path was different, Nurse, by far!
Her King was kind, a true and guiding star.
But this Valerius, he is darkness' own,
A heart of malice, cold and carved in stone.
To speak of love, where only hatred dwells,
Is to invite the deepest, darkest spells.
I cannot learn to love what I despise,
Nor yield my truth to such abhorrent ties.
My mother chose a man of noble mien,
Not one whose cruelties are often seen
As shadows lurking, chilling all the air,
A viper's venom, held beyond compare.
And Kai, my Kai, he loves me for myself,
Not for my crown, my title, or my pelf.
He sees my spirit, pure and undefiled,
And cherishes me, as a parent doth a child.

For all the riches of the world beyond?
No, Nurse, I swear it, by my father's grave,
And by the life my heart doth seek to save
From utter ruin, I will not agree!
This monstrous union shall not come to be!

NURSE. My lamb, my dearest, curb this fiery zeal.

His anger, Lyra, when aroused, hath
A bitter sting, and consequences dire.
He holds the sceptre, wields the royal fire.
He sees the realm's salvation in this match,

He fears the rising tide of lawless writ,
The murmuring lords, the border's grim unrest.

A man misunderstood, or understood

My life, my honor, I will hold more dear

A princess, yes, but first a woman true,
Whose heart can break, but never be untrue

I'll find a way, though it be dark as night,
To break these bonds, or break my very heart.

My spirit yearns for freedom, and for love.
And guided by the stars that shine above,
I'll find my path, though thorny it may be.

(Rising slowly, his weariness evident)
Another message? More demands, I'll swear,
More subtle tortures, more oppressive care.
Give it to me, Sir Hector. Let us see
What further torments Valerius sends to me.
(Eldor takes the scroll, breaks the seal, and reads. His face grows pale, then darkens with anger. Lyra watches him, her own face reflecting anxiety.)

ELDOR
(Reading aloud, his voice trembling with contained rage)
"To Eldor, King of Valerius's new domain,
Whose daughter's hand will soothe my future reign:
Know this, old king, my patience wears full thin.
My troops now stand upon thy borders, kin
To my own realm, and wait my swift command.
The marriage pact thou owest to my hand,
If not agreed by morrow's setting sun,
Then by my might, thy kingdom shall be won.
No grain shall reach thy starving, desperate towns,
But fire and sword shall bring forth bitter frowns.
Thy walls shall fall, thy people feel my wrath,
And desolation carve a bloody path.
Choose wisely, Eldor, ere too late thou see,
The might of Valerius, and what shall be.
For I shall have thy kingdom, and thy child,
By treaty, or by conquest, fierce and wild."

(Eldor crumples the scroll in his hand, his eyes burning.)

ELDOR
The fiend! The dog! To threaten us with war,
When famine gnaws our vitals to the core!
He offers bread, then holds a sword to throat!
This is his promise, Lyra, by his note!
The choice is gone! He takes away my will!
He forces me, the kingdom he will kill
Unless I yield. Oh, Lyra, hear this threat,
This cruel ultimatum, sharply set!

LYRA
(Stepping forward, her fear now replaced by cold resolve)
He shows his true colours, father. Mark him well.
This is the future that I did foretell.
He offers nothing but a tyrant's grace,
A hollow mercy, masking his dark face.
So be it then! If Valerius seeks war,
Let him then face the courage of the poor!
Let him come forth, with all his mighty host!
He thinks us broken, but he knows no boast
Of spirit, father, that can never die.
We stand for freedom, beneath heaven's sky!
This forced submission, this vile, hateful pact,
Will yield no peace, but only greater act
Of tyranny. Let us resist him, then!
Let freedom's banner rise among our men!
Let us die fighting, rather than live bound!
Let our last breaths a righteous trumpet sound!

SIR HECTOR
(Stepping forward, his voice grave)
My Princess, I applaud your valiant heart,
And would with you stand bravely, play my part.
But Valerius's armies number ten for one,
Well-fed, well-armoured, their grim work begun
By swift encampments on our southern plain.
Our few poor guards, they could not long sustain
A single charge. Our walls are old and weak,
And famine's touch has made our people meek.
To fight them now, would be a senseless waste,
A rush to ruin, made in reckless haste.
Your father speaks the truth, however grim.
There is no army, Lyra, limb by limb,
To match Valerius. Our time for war
Was long since ended, years and years before.
Now, only wisdom, born of deepest pain,
Can hope to break this cruel and bitter chain.

LYRA
(Turning to Sir Hector, her voice sharp with desperation)
Wisdom, Sir Hector? Is it wisdom's plea
To bow to evil, bend the noble knee?
Is it wisdom to give up all we hold dear,
To live in bondage, year by bitter year?
If strength be lacking, where is courage then?
To face this tyrant, not as lesser men,
But as a people, valuing their soul,
More than the body, seeking freedom's goal?
He thinks he holds us fast, with threats of fire.
But fire can purify, can raise us higher!
If we must fall, then let us fall in light,
Not in the shadows of a tyrant's night!
There must be one, who still remembers might,
A noble champion, to set all right!

ELDOR
(Rising, his eyes fixed on Lyra, a terrible resolution forming in his gaze)
A champion? Lyra, dost thou still recall
The ancient tale, the legend told to all?
Of Aric, the Dragon-Slayer, hero bold,
Whose valour saved our kingdom, ages old?
He wielded Drakenfang, the sacred blade,
And from our enemies a rescue made.
He swore an oath, that in our darkest hour,
His lineage would rise, with dragon's power.
But Aric's line, Lyra, has long been lost,
Or so we thought, at such a dreadful cost.

LYRA
(Her eyes widening, a flicker of hope igniting)
The Prophecy! The line of Aric, true!
It spoke of one, with purpose strong and new,
To rise from slumber, when the shadow fell,
And break the tyrant's dark and wicked spell!
Father, I heard the minstrels sing this song,
Of Aric's blood, that would right every wrong!
Could this not be the hour, the very time,
For Aric's champion, from lore sublime?
Hath no one searched? Hath no one ever tried
To find this hero, whom the legends hide?

SIR HECTOR
(Shaking his head gravely)
My Princess, many a king, through troubled years,
Has sought this legend, to assuage his fears.
But Aric's lineage, so blurred by time,
Is naught but myth, a poet's fading rhyme.
The tales grew faint, the names all passed away,
No trace remains, to brighten our dark day.
Believe me, child, I've seen the kingdom search,
From mountain peak to every humble church,
For any sign, a token, or a name,
But all in vain, to quench this desperate flame.

ELDOR
(His voice low, almost a whisper, as if grappling with a forbidden thought)
Yet... there was one. A whisper, long ago.
A scholar spoke, in hushed tones, soft and low.
Of Aric's blood, still flowing, far away,
In humble veins, awaiting its true day.
A family banished, for some ancient slight,
Their name obscured, hidden from public light.
They settled north, beyond the Misty Crags,
Where wild winds howl, and danger ever lags.
The House of Thorne, I think the scholar said,
Their bloodline distant, mostly thought long dead.
But there was rumour, Lyra, I recall,
Of one true heir, who might yet save us all.
A boy, then. Now a man, if he yet lives.
But what a hope, that such a rumour gives
Against Valerius's sword, his crushing might!
A phantom promise, in this endless night.

LYRA
(Her face alight with sudden, fierce determination)
A phantom promise? No, a beacon bright!
A ray of hope, a spark amidst the night!
If Aric's blood still flows, then we must seek!
We must despatch, ere Valerius can wreak
His vengeance! Send forth riders, swift and bold!
Let every loyal heart be made to hold
This sacred quest! Let us not yield to fear,
While Aric's heir, perhaps, is drawing near!
Give me a horse, father, I will ride!
I will myself search every mountain side!
I cannot marry Valerius, nor can I
Allow our kingdom, and our people, die
Without a fight, without a single plea
To ancient prophecy, to destiny!

ELDOR
(Rising fully, his voice regaining some strength, yet still heavy with doubt)
Lyra, my child, thou speak'st with youthful fire,
But this is madness, born of wild desire!
To send thee forth, into the wilderness,
Where bandits roam, and dangers still oppress?
The northern wastes are cruel, the paths unknown,
Thou art a princess, not a warrior grown!
And how can one man, however brave and true,
Contend with Valerius and all his crew?
This is a desperate gamble, Lyra, fraught
With peril, and with ruin dearly bought.
The time is short. The sun will set tomorrow.
And then, my child, we face a deeper sorrow.
To cling to myths, when grim reality
Demands a choice, however painful it be.

SIR HECTOR
My liege, with due respect, perhaps a thought:
If any hope, however faintly wrought,
Can stay the hand of Valerius's decree,
Then it is worth a moment's scrutiny.
To ride to Valerius, to ask for grace,
To seek an extension, to delay this pace?
Or, if a quest, for Aric's promised heir,
Could buy us time, to breathe a fresher air?
The princess's spirit, though bold and rash,
May hold a truth, beyond the common dash
Of strategy. Desperation often sees
What cold calculation never could appease.

ELDOR
(Pacing, his mind a maelstrom of conflicting thoughts)
Delay? What plea could move so hard a heart?
He sees our weakness, plays his cruelest part.
He wants the princess, and he wants the crown,
And will not wait, to tear our kingdom down.
Yet... Lyra's words. The prophecy. The heir.
A single chance, against a full despair.
To yield to Valerius, is certain doom,
A slow decay, within a gilded room.
To seek this Aric, is a desperate throw,
A fragile hope, against a powerful foe.
But if it fails, Lyra, then all is lost.
Our kingdom plundered, at a bitter cost.
And thou, my daughter, still must bear the chain,
But in a land consumed by war and pain.

LYRA
Then let me seek him, father! Let me ride!
Let hope, however faint, become our guide!
If I must marry, let it be the last
Recourse, when every other hope is past.
Give me this chance, to save our kingdom whole,
To seek this hero, for our people's soul!
I fear not danger, nor the mountain's chill.
My spirit rises, to obey your will
In all things just, but not to yield to shame.
Let us call forth the heir of Aric's name!

ELDOR
(He stops pacing, looking from Lyra's determined face to Sir Hector's grave one. He draws a deep, shaky breath.)
So be it then. This desperate, final plea.
A single chance for our sad destiny.
Sir Hector, choose our swiftest, surest men,
To ride with Lyra, to that northern fen.
And search for traces, of this vanished line,
The House of Thorne, beneath the mountain's shrine.
Give them a token, Lyra, of our trust,
A royal ring, to crumble time's old dust.
But time is brief. Thou hast one day to ride,
And one to seek, and one to turn the tide.
By three days hence, if Aric's heir be found,
Let him return, to stand on sacred ground.
If not, my Lyra... then the dreadful choice,
Will still await thee, with its mournful voice.
And thou must then embrace thy bitter fate,
To save thy people, at this hateful gate.
Go now, my daughter. May the heavens guide
Thy perilous journey, through the wastes so wide.
May hope itself, become thy shield and might.
And bring us succour, from this crushing night.

LYRA
(Kneeling swiftly, her voice filled with emotion)
I shall not fail thee, father, nor this land!
I shall not rest, until a saving hand
I find, or perish in the noble quest!
My heart is steeled, my spirit truly blessed
By this resolve! I go to seek our hope!
To climb the mountain, down the desperate slope!
Farewell, dear father. May we meet again,
With victory's glad tidings, free from pain!
(Lyra rises, a new strength in her posture, and exits with a determined stride.)

ELDOR
(Sinking back into his chair, his hands clenching)
A desperate throw. A child's impassioned dream.
Against the tide, against the bitter stream
Of Valerius's power. Hector, tell me true,
Dost thou believe this ancient tale can woo
A phantom hero from the dust of years?
Or are we merely feeding childish fears
With empty hope, prolonging our sad doom?

SIR HECTOR
My liege, belief is sometimes all we own,
When stark reality has overthrown
All reason. This is not a common age,
But one where miracles must fill life's page.
If Aric's blood can still be found and stirred,
Then let us pray, his mighty name be heard.
And even if the quest should come to naught,
At least your daughter's spirit will have fought
For what she holds as precious, for her truth.
And in that fight, there lies a kind of youth,
A vital strength, that tyranny cannot break.
Perhaps, my liege, for honour's noble sake,
This quest, though vain, will grant a brief reprieve,
A moment's breath, a moment to believe.
I shall prepare the horses, and the guard.
May heaven watch our princess, keep from hard
And cruel misfortune, her adventurous way.
And bring us swiftly, to a brighter day.
(Sir Hector bows deeply and exits, leaving Eldor alone.)

ELDOR
(A soliloquy, his voice filled with weariness and a glimmer of uncertain hope)
A brighter day? Or deeper, darker night?
To send my child into the fading light
Of an old legend. What a desperate plea!
What mockery of kingly majesty!
Yet, in her eyes, I saw a living fire,
A spirit burning, with a pure desire
For freedom, for a justice yet unknown.
And in that fire, a seed of hope was sown.
If Valerius's might cannot be turned
By treaty, then by prophecy discerned,
Perhaps a miracle, a legend's hand,
Can save this dying, desolated land.
But oh, my heart! The risk, the cruel despair!
To cast my Lyra to the mountain air,
To unknown dangers, for a whispered name.
And if she fails, then I must bear the blame
For all of it. For kingdom, and for child.
A king's harsh burden, furious and wild.
(He looks towards the door Lyra exited, a single tear tracing a path down his weathered cheek.)
Go, Lyra, go. My hope, my only prayer.
May Aric's spirit guide thee, and thee bear
To victory. Or, if it cannot be,
Then let thy courage be our legacy.
For Valerius's triumph, though it brings
A temporary peace, forever stings
With bitter shame. But Lyra's noble fight,
Will cast a glimmer, in the endless night.
I pray, my daughter, that thy quest be swift,
And bring the saving, long-awaited gift.
(He closes his eyes, his hands clasped tightly.)

(SCENE CHANGE: Three days later. A desolate, windswept mountain pass. LYRA, dressed in practical riding clothes, but still bearing the grace of a princess, rides a weary horse. SIR HECTOR and two weary, travel-stained GUARDS accompany her. The sky is grey and bleak.)

LYRA
(Her voice hoarse from the wind and travel, but resolute)
Three days, Sir Hector, have we ridden hard.
Three days have passed, and every loyal guard
Has searched these peaks, these valleys, cold and deep.
No sign of Thorne, no secrets can we reap.
The villages are poor, their people shy,
And speak of legends, with a distant eye.
They know not Aric, nor his noble line,
Only the hardship, and the cold confine.
Our provisions dwindle, and our horses tire.
Dost thou believe we chase a phantom fire?

SIR HECTOR
(His face grim, his voice weary)
My Princess, we have followed every trace,
Explored each cranny in this desolate place.
The House of Thorne, if ever it did exist,
Has vanished utterly, like morning mist.
The mountain folk, they live a simple life,
Unburdened by such ancient, royal strife.
They speak of hermits, and of spirits old,
But not the champions that the bards have told.
My heart misgives me, Lyra, it is time
To turn our faces from this barren clime.
Your father waits, in anguish and despair.
The sun will set, and mark the final prayer.

LYRA
(Stopping her horse, looking out at the vast, empty landscape, a profound sadness in her eyes)
To turn back now? To tell my father, 'Nay,
No hero comes, no champion for the fray.
The prophecy is dust, the legend's lie.
And I must go, to Valerius, and die
A living death, for honour's broken pledge.'?
Oh, Hector, is this truly destiny's edge?
I cannot bear it. Not to find a sign,
No single token of that noble line?
My spirit aches, my very soul is torn.
To ride to slavery, as I was born
To freedom. Is there no small hope we missed?
No hidden cave, no secret, ancient tryst?

FIRST GUARD
(Pointing towards a faint trail leading up a steeper, more treacherous slope)
My Princess, look! A trail, though overgrown,
Leads to that craggy peak, that stands alone.
The villagers oft warn of evil sprites,
That guard its summit, through the lonely nights.
No living soul, they say, dares to ascend,
Lest ancient curses on their journey send
A swift misfortune. It is wild and steep.

LYRA
(Her gaze fixed on the peak, a spark rekindling in her eyes)
A craggy peak? A place of ancient fear?
Then it is there, Sir Hector, we draw near!
If Aric's line, so long was forced to hide,
They would choose ground where danger's shadows ride.
Lead on, good guard! We scale that forbidden height!
If evil sprites protect it, so they might
Protect a secret, hidden from plain view,
A final hope, to see our kingdom through!
I will not turn, until that peak is scaled!
Until all hope, completely has been failed!

SIR HECTOR
(Hesitantly)
Princess, it is most perilous, I warn.
The path is narrow, treacherous and worn.
And if these 'sprites' be bandits, sharp and grim,
Our numbers few, to face them limb by limb.
Consider, Lyra, what your father said.
Your life is precious, let us not be led
By desperate fancy, to a needless end.

LYRA
(Turning to Sir Hector, her eyes burning with conviction)
My life, Sir Hector, is a price well paid,
If by its loss, a kingdom can be swayed
From Valerius's grasp! Do not gainsay
My purpose now! My heart demands this way!
