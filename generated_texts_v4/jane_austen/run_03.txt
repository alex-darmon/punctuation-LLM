Eleanor watched the exchange, her lips pressed into a thin line. He was playing directly to Lydia's heart, her fears, her very human desire for comfort and reassurance, effectively sidestepping Eleanor's principled arguments by reframing them as cold, impractical, and ultimately unsympathetic. To claim one path is purely objective, purely 'authentic,' while the other is merely 'ego' or 'invention,' is, I suggest, a rather too simplistic dichotomy, especially when dealing with the profound complexities of cultural heritage."

His gaze held hers, an almost triumphant glint in his eyes. Eleanor felt a fresh wave of irritation, recognizing his classic rhetorical move: blurring definitions, expanding concepts until they lost all specific meaning. His "living entity" argument was a convenient philosophical cloak for practices she found ethically dubious.
