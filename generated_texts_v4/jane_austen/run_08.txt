He arrived punctually at the hour specified, a gentleman whose sense of his own importance was perfectly unassailable. Mrs. Godfrey, ever eager to please and easily delighted, met him at the parlour door with a smile of such genuine warmth that even Amelia, who sometimes found her hostess a trifle overwhelming, felt a momentary glow of affection. Miss Charlotte, by the window, merely offered a quiet curtsey.

“Mr. Finch,” cried Mrs. Godfrey, her voice a little too high pitched, “how very kind of you to brave the lanes this morning! We have something quite new to shew you; a little treasure, indeed, which arrived only yesterday from Bath.”

Mr. Finch made his bows with an air of polite condescension, declaring, “My dear Mrs. Godfrey, to decline such an invitation, or indeed to delay my arrival, would be an act of the utmost barbarity. I perceive a certain air of delightful anticipation about the room; pray, what wondrous object has found its way to your charming collection?” He directed his gaze towards Charlotte, whose cheeks immediately assumed a faint pink. Amelia thought she detected a spark of mischief in his eye (though his mouth maintained a most innocent curve).

Mrs. Godfrey, quite oblivious to any undercurrents, led them to a small inlaid cabinet. “It is a miniature, Mr. Finch, a truly exquisite piece. The very same artist, I am assured, who painted Sir Thomas Denham’s portrait last year; you recall, no doubt, its striking resemblance and delicate finish?”

I confess, my own establishment’s cook, though competent, lacks a certain… je ne sais quoi in her patisseries.”

Amelia watched the exchange, sipping her tea. He was a master of social manoeuvre, Amelia reflected, observing how his eyes darted from Mrs. Godfrey to Charlotte, then to herself, missing nothing, taking everything in. Charlotte, meanwhile, picked at a small almond cake, her movements precise and quiet.
